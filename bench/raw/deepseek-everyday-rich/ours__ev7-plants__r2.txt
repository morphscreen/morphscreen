# Houseplants That Are Hard to Kill

If you've ever accidentally loved a plant to death (overwatering is the #1 killer), these resilient species will forgive your forgetfulness, neglect, and imperfect light. Here's your survival lineup.

<Card title="The Unkillable All-Stars">
<Columns>
<Card title="Snake Plant (Sansevieria)">
- Tolerates **weeks of drought** — water every 2–3 weeks
- Thrives in **low light**, bright light, or direct sun
- Grows slowly, rarely needs repotting
- Purifies air (NASA study)
- **Warning**: toxic to pets if chewed
</Card>

<Card title="ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas)">
- Survives **months without water** — rhizomes store moisture
- Handles **near-darkness** and fluorescent office light
- Glossy leaves look fresh even when neglected
- Slow grower, pest-resistant
- **Warning**: toxic to pets if chewed
</Card>

<Card title="Pothos (Epipremnum)">
- **Forgives missed waterings** — leaves droop, then perk up
- Grows in **low to medium light**; variegated types need more
- Easy to propagate — cut a stem, root in water
- Vigorous vining growth, great for shelves
- **Warning**: toxic to pets if chewed
</Card>

<Card title="Spider Plant (Chlorophytum)">
- **Drought-tolerant** — let soil dry between waterings
- Adapts to **average indoor light** — no direct scorching sun
- Produces "spiderettes" (baby plants) you can share
- Non-toxic to pets
- Prefers slightly cooler rooms
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Pro Tips for Keeping Any Plant Alive">
<Steps>
<Step title="Choose the right pot" status="done">
Always use a pot with **drainage holes**. No plant likes sitting in soggy soil — that's how roots rot.
</Step>
<Step title="Water deeply, but rarely" status="done">
Water until it runs out the bottom, then **wait until the top 2–3 cm of soil is dry** before watering again. For snake and ZZ plants, wait until the soil is fully dry.
</Step>
<Step title="Match light to the plant" status="done">
Most of these tolerate low light, but they'll grow faster with **bright, indirect light**. Avoid harsh afternoon sun on leaves.
</Step>
<Step title="Skip the fertilizer" status="done">
These tough plants need **little to no fertilizer**. If you want, feed once in spring with a balanced houseplant food at half strength.
</Step>
<Step title="Dust the leaves" status="done">
Every few months, gently wipe leaves with a damp cloth. Dust blocks light and makes the plant work harder.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="What Could Go Wrong?">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Yellow leaves = too much water">
If leaves turn yellow and mushy, you're overwatering. Let the soil dry out completely before watering again, and check the pot isn't holding water.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="info" title="Brown tips = too dry or too much fluoride">
Brown leaf tips on spider plants often come from tap water chemicals. Use filtered water or let tap water sit out overnight before watering.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="neutral" title="Leggy growth = not enough light">
If your pothos or spider plant stretches with big gaps between leaves, move it closer to a window.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Get Your First Plant">
<Columns>
<Card title="Local nurseries & garden centers">
- Best for **healthy, established plants** in proper pots
- Staff can answer questions about your specific home
- Often cheaper than online
</Card>
<Card title="Online plant shops">
- Huge variety of sizes and rare varieties
- Look for **"beginner-friendly"** labels
- Check reviews for packaging quality — plants ship well
</Card>
<Card title="Friends & community swaps">
- **Free cuttings** of pothos and spider plants are easy to root
- Check local Facebook groups or plant swaps
- The best plants come with a story
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Start small, grow confident">
Pick **one** plant that matches your light and watering habits. Learn its rhythm for a few months, then add more. Before you know it, you'll have a jungle.
