# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon is a city of bold flavors — salted cod, custard tarts, grilled sardines, and hearty stews. Here's your practical guide to the must-try dishes and the best spots to find them.

## 1. Pastéis de Nata (Custard Tarts)

The iconic Portuguese tart: flaky, caramelized puff pastry filled with creamy egg custard, dusted with cinnamon. Best eaten warm, with espresso.

<Card title="Where to get them">
<Columns>
<div>
**Manteigaria** (Chiado & Time Out Market)
- The gold standard — crispy, not too sweet
- Watch them being made through the glass
- €1.50 each
</div>
<div>
**Pastéis de Belém** (Belém)
- The original since 1837, secret recipe
- Expect a queue, but it moves fast
- €1.70 each
</div>
</Columns>
<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Ask for "meia dúzia" (half dozen) and share. The cinnamon and powdered sugar are on the table.</Callout>
</Card>

---

## 2. Bifana (Pork Sandwich)

A humble but legendary sandwich: thin slices of marinated pork in a crusty roll, sometimes with mustard or piri-piri. Cheap, fast, and utterly satisfying.

<Card title="Where to get them">
<Columns>
<div>
**Casa Bifanas** (Rossio, Praça da Figueira)
- The most famous — pork simmered in a garlicky broth
- €4.00, open 24h
</div>
<div>
**O Trevo** (Alfama)
- A tiny local joint, big flavor
- €3.50
</div>
</Columns>

## 3. Bacalhau à Brás (Salted Cod with Potatoes & Eggs)

Portugal's beloved cod dish: shredded salted cod, matchstick potatoes, and scrambled eggs, topped with olives and parsley. Comfort food at its finest.

<Card title="Where to get it">
<Columns>
<div>
**Cervejaria Ramiro** (Intendente)
- Famous for seafood — the bacalhau here is exceptional
- €15.00, busy but worth it
</div>
<div>
**A Casa do Bacalhau** (Graça)
- A neighborhood spot with a loyal following
- €14.00
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

---

## 4. Grilled Sardines (Sardinhas Assadas)

From June to September, the city smells of charcoal and sardines. Simple, salty, and best eaten with bread and salad.

<Card title="Where to get them">
<Columns>
<div>
**Casa de Pasto** (Alfama)
- Traditional taverna, sardines grilled over charcoal
- €12.00
</div>
<div>
**Any street festival** (June, Santo António)
- Grilled on the street, served on bread
- €5.00 per plate
</div>
</Columns>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Seasonal">The freshest sardines are only from June to September. Outside that, look for "sardinha assada" on the menu anyway — it'll be frozen but still tasty.</Callout>
</Card>

---

## 5. Cataplana (Seafood Stew)

A copper pot filled with clams, prawns, fish, tomatoes, and herbs. It's a showstopper — the pot arrives at your table and is opened with drama.

<Card title="Where to get it">
<Columns>
<div>
**Uma Marisqueira** (Campo de Ourique)
- A classic, generous portions
- €25.00 for two
</div>
<div>
**Cervejaria Ramiro** (again!)
- Their cataplana is legendary
- €30.00 for two
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

---

## 6. Ginjinha (Sour Cherry Liqueur)

Not food, but essential: a sweet-sour cherry liqueur served in a small chocolate cup. One shot, one sip, one bite of the cup.

<Card title="Where to get it">
<Columns>
<div>
**A Ginjinha** (Rua das Portas de Santo Antão)
- The original since 1840, standing-room only
- €1.20
</div>
<div>
**Ginjinha Sem Rival** (Baixa)
- Less crowded, same quality
- €1.20
</div>
</Columns>

## 7. Pão de Deus (Sweet Bread)

A soft, fluffy bun topped with coconut — perfect for breakfast with coffee or a mid-day snack. Find it in any pastelaria (bakery).

<Card title="Where to get it">
<Columns>
<div>
**Pastelaria Alcôa** (Baixa)
- A classic, fresh daily
- €1.80
</div>
<div>
**Confeitaria Nacional** (Rossio)
- Historic since 1829, elegant setting
- €2.00
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Money-saving tips">
- Eat lunch at **Time Out Market** — many stalls, shared tables, €10–15 fills you up
- **Ginjinha** is cheaper at the original bar than in tourist shops
- Order **"prato do dia"** (daily special) for the best value
- Water is free if you ask for "uma jarra de água" (a jug of water)
</Callout>
