# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

Vancouver is a hiker's paradise—within an hour you can go from sea level to alpine ridges, old-growth forests, and glacier-fed lakes. These are the best day trips, picked for variety, scenery, and accessibility.

<Card title="1. Stawamus Chief — The Classic Scramble">
<Columns>
<div>
The Chief is a granite monolith rising 700 m above Howe Sound. You climb chains, ladders, and bare rock to three summits (First, Second, Third Peaks). The view from the top over the Sound and the Tantalus Range is one of the most iconic in BC.

**Why go:** The most rewarding view-to-effort ratio in the region, if you're fit and okay with heights.
**How to get there:** 45 min drive from Vancouver to Squamish, or take the bus to Squamish then a short taxi.
</div>
<div>
<Gallery>
![The granite face of Stawamus Chief towering over Howe Sound](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1506905925346-21bda4d32df4?w=800)
![View from the top of the Chief looking over the Sound](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1470071459604-3b5ec3a7fe05?w=800)
</Gallery>
</div>
</Columns>

<Steps>
<Step title="Park at the Chief Trailhead" status="done">Free parking lot off Hwy 99 in Squamish. Arrive before 8 am on weekends.</Step>
<Step title="Climb to First Peak" status="done">2.5 km, 500 m gain, 1.5-2 hrs. Chains and ladders on the last section.</Step>
<Step title="Continue to Second and Third Peaks" status="current">Add 2 km and 200 m more, another 1-1.5 hrs. The traverse is exposed but well-marked.</Step>
<Step title="Descend the same way" status="todo">Watch for loose rock. Poles help on the way down.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Heights and weather">The summit has sheer drops. Don't go in wet or windy conditions—rock is slippery when damp. Bring gloves for the chains.</Callout>

<Card title="2. Garibaldi Lake — Turquoise Alpine Water">
<Columns>
<div>
A steady, well-graded trail climbs through old-growth forest to a massive turquoise lake ringed by snow-capped peaks. The lake's colour comes from glacial rock flour. You can extend to Panorama Ridge (add 4 km, 300 m) for the classic view over the lake and Black Tusk.

**Why go:** The single most beautiful lake view in the region, and a good workout without technical difficulty.
**How to get there:** 1 hr drive to the Rubble Creek trailhead, or take the Greyhound bus to Squamish and a shuttle (summer only).
</div>
<div>
<Gallery>
![Turquoise Garibaldi Lake with snowy peaks behind](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1500530855697-b586d89ba3ee?w=800)
![Panorama Ridge view over the lake](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1469474968028-56623f02e42e?w=800)
</Gallery>
</div>
</Columns>

<Steps>
<Step title="Start at Rubble Creek trailhead" status="done">Elevation 300 m. The trail climbs steadily through forest for the first 6 km.</Step>
<Step title="Reach the lake at 1,500 m" status="current">9 km, 820 m gain, 3-4 hrs. There's a shelter and pit toilets near the shore.</Step>
<Step title="Optional: Panorama Ridge" status="todo">Add 4 km and 300 m more, another 2 hrs. The view is worth it if you have energy.</Step>
<Step title="Return the same way" status="todo">The descent is gentle but long—allow 3 hrs back.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Best time">The trail is snow-free from late July to October. In June, crampons may be needed near the top. The lake is usually ice-free by mid-July.</Callout>

<Card title="3. Joffre Lakes — Three Glacier Lakes in One Day">
<Columns>
<div>
This is the ultimate "wow" hike: three lakes, each more stunning than the last, with hanging glaciers and waterfalls along the way. The trail is well-built with boardwalks and stairs, making it accessible despite the elevation gain.

**Why go:** Maximum payoff per kilometre—you see three distinct alpine lakes, each a different shade of blue.
**How to get there:** 2.5 hrs drive on Hwy 99 past Pemberton, or take a shuttle from Vancouver (summer).
</div>
<div>
<Gallery>
![Middle Joffre Lake with glacier behind](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1500534314209-a25ddb2bd429?w=800)
![Lower Joffre Lake turquoise water](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1472214103451-9374bd1c798e?w=800)
</Gallery>
</div>
</Columns>

<Steps>
<Step title="Arrive at the trailhead" status="done">Day-use pass required in summer (free, book online 48 hrs ahead). Arrive before 9 am.</Step>
<Step title="Lower Lake (10 min)" status="done">Flat walk from the parking lot. Great photo spot.</Step>
<Step title="Middle Lake (1 hr)" status="current">2 km, 250 m gain, with a waterfall along the way. The classic view is from the log bridge here.</Step>
<Step title="Upper Lake (1 hr more)" status="todo">Another 1.5 km, 200 m gain. The glacier is right above—you can feel the cold air.</Step>
<Step title="Return" status="todo">1.5-2 hrs down.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Permit required">From late June to early September, you **must** have a free day-use pass for Joffre Lakes. Book it on the BC Parks website exactly 48 hours before. No pass = no entry, even if you drove 2.5 hours.</Callout>

<Card title="4. Quarry Rock — The Easy Classic with a View">
<Columns>
<div>
A short, family-friendly hike in Deep Cove that ends on a granite outcrop overlooking Indian Arm, a deep fjord. It's the most popular easy hike in the region—you'll see everyone from toddlers to grandparents.

**Why go:** Perfect for a quick escape, great for kids, and the view over the arm is gorgeous.
**How to get there:** 30 min drive to Deep Cove, or take the 212 bus from Phibbs Exchange.
</div>
<div>
<Gallery>
![View from Quarry Rock over Indian Arm](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1500534623283-312aade485b7?w=800)
![Deep Cove marina below the trail](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1500530855697-b586d89ba3ee?w=800)
</Gallery>
</div>
</Columns>

<Steps>
<Step title="Start at Deep Cove" status="done">Park at the lot near the dock (arrive early—it fills fast) or on the street.</Step>
<Step title="Climb through forest" status="current">1.5 km, 100 m gain, 30-40 min on a well-graded trail with stairs.</Step>
<Step title="Enjoy the viewpoint" status="todo">The rock outcrop has benches. Best at low tide when the water is calm.</Step>
<Step title="Return" status="todo">Same way, 30 min down.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="Perfect for beginners">This is the hike to take visitors, kids, or anyone who's new to hiking. The trail is wide and well-maintained, and the reward is a postcard view.</Callout>

<Card title="5. Bowen Lookout — The Local's Secret">
<Columns>
<div>
A lesser-known gem in Cypress Provincial Park that gives you a huge view of Howe Sound and Bowen Island with a fraction of the crowds of the Chief. The trail is part of the Howe Sound Crest Trail, so you get a taste of a classic multi-day route.

**Why go:** Big views, fewer people, and it's only 20 minutes from downtown Vancouver.
**How to get there:** 20 min drive to Cypress Mountain ski area parking lot, or take the #253 bus from downtown.
</div>
<div>
<Gallery>
![Bowen Lookout view over Howe Sound](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1470071459604-3b5ec3a7fe05?w=800)
![Forest trail on the Howe Sound Crest Trail](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1441974231531-c6227db76b6e?w=800)
</Gallery>
</div>
</Columns>

<Steps>
<Step title="Start at Cypress Mountain" status="done">From the main parking lot, follow the signs for Bowen Lookout (part of the Howe Sound Crest Trail).</Step>
<Step title="Climb through forest" status="current">4.5 km, 600 m gain, 2-2.5 hrs. The trail is steep but well-marked, with occasional views opening up.</Step>
<Step title="Reach the lookout" status="todo">A rocky viewpoint with benches. You can see Bowen Island, Gambier Island, and the Strait of Georgia.</Step>
<Step title="Return" status="todo">1.5 hrs down. The descent is steep—poles help.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Slippery when wet">The trail is mostly forested, so it stays muddy longer than exposed hikes. Wear grippy shoes, and expect to get dirty.</Callout>
