# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is absolutely achievable with the right technique and tools. The key is high heat, proper dough hydration, and quality ingredients. Let's break it down.

<Card title="What Makes It Neapolitan?">
The Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (VPN) has strict standards: the dough must be made with 00 flour, water, salt, and yeast only. The pizza must be cooked in a wood-fired oven at 485°C (905°F) for 60-90 seconds. The crust should be soft, elastic, and have leopard-spotted charring.

<KeyValue title="The VPN Specifications">
| Attribute | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Dough | 00 flour, water, salt, yeast only |
| Hydration | 55-62% |
| Rise | 8-24 hours at room temperature |
| Cooking temp | 485°C (905°F) |
| Cooking time | 60-90 seconds |
| Diameter | Max 35 cm (14 inches) |
| Crust | Soft, elastic, leopard-spotted |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="The Dough — Step by Step">
This recipe makes 4 pizzas of about 250g each. The process takes 8-24 hours, so plan ahead.

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix the dough" status="current" subtitle="5 minutes">
In a large bowl, mix 500g 00 flour, 325g water (65% hydration), 10g fine sea salt, and 2g instant yeast. Mix until shaggy, then let rest 20 minutes.
</Step>
<Step title="Knead" status="todo" subtitle="10-15 minutes">
Knead on a lightly floured surface until smooth and elastic, about 10-15 minutes. The dough should be tacky but not sticky.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="todo" subtitle="8-24 hours">
Place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and let rise at room temperature until doubled. For more flavor, use the longer end of the range. <Button ask="What's the difference between 8-hour and 24-hour fermentation?">Ask about fermentation</Button>
</Step>
<Step title="Divide and ball" status="todo" subtitle="15 minutes">
Divide into 4 equal pieces (~250g each). Shape each into a tight ball, then place on a floured tray, cover, and rest 2-4 hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch" status="todo" subtitle="2 minutes per pizza">
Flatten a ball with your fingers, then stretch from the center outward, leaving the rim thicker. The center should be thin enough to see through.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't use a rolling pin">
A rolling pin crushes the air bubbles that create the puffy crust. Stretch by hand only.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The Sauce and Toppings">
Neapolitan pizza is minimal. Don't overload it.

<Columns>
<div>

**Tomato Sauce**

- 400g can of San Marzano tomatoes
- Pinch of fine sea salt
- 1-2 fresh basil leaves (optional)

Crush the tomatoes by hand. Don't cook the sauce — it cooks on the pizza.

</div>
<div>

**Mozzarella**

- 250g fresh mozzarella (bufala or fior di latte)
- Tear into small pieces
- Drain well on paper towels for 30 minutes

The cheese should be applied sparingly, so it melts into pools rather than covering everything.

</div>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="neutral" title="The classic Margherita">
The only true topping combo is: tomato sauce, mozzarella, fresh basil, and a drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil. That's it. The pizza should be sauced to the edge but leave the crust rim bare.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Cooking Your Pizza">
The cooking process is fast and requires attention. This is where the magic happens.

<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat" status="current" subtitle="20-30 minutes">
If using a pizza oven, preheat to 450-500°C (840-930°F). If using a home oven with a steel, preheat to max for at least 1 hour, then switch to broil for 5 minutes before launching.
</Step>
<Step title="Assemble" status="todo" subtitle="1 minute per pizza">
On a floured peel, stretch the dough, add a few spoonfuls of sauce, distribute the mozzarella, and add basil. Don't over-sauce or the pizza will be soggy.
</Step>
<Step title="Launch and cook" status="todo" subtitle="60-90 seconds">
Slide the pizza onto the hot surface. In a pizza oven, rotate every 15-20 seconds using a turning peel. The pizza is done when the crust is puffed, charred in spots, and the cheese is bubbling.
</Step>
<Step title="Rest and serve" status="todo" subtitle="2 minutes">
Let the pizza rest for 1-2 minutes on a wire rack before slicing. This lets the cheese set and prevents the toppings from sliding off.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Watch for burning">
Neapolitan pizza burns quickly at 500°C. Keep the pizza moving and check every few seconds. The bottom should be charred but not black.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Problems and Fixes">
Even experienced home cooks hit issues. Here's how to troubleshoot.

<Compare title="Diagnosing Your Pizza">
| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy center | Too much sauce or cheese | Use less topping, drain mozzarella better |
| Crust not rising | Dough too cold or under-proofed | Bring dough to room temp before stretching |
| Bottom burned, top undercooked | Oven too hot or pizza too thick | Reduce heat slightly, stretch thinner |
| Dough tears when stretching | Not rested enough or too much flour | Rest dough longer, use less flour |
| Crust too tough | Over-kneaded or over-floured | Knead less, use a wetter dough |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="The Bottom Line">
Making proper Neapolitan pizza at home is about three things: quality ingredients, a hot oven, and patience with your dough. The first few attempts might not be perfect, but the process is forgiving and delicious.

<Callout tone="success" title="Start simple">
Master the Margherita first. Once you've got the dough and cooking down, experiment with toppings. The technique transfers.
</Callout>
