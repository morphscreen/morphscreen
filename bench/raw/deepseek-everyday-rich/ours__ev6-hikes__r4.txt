## 1. Stawamus Chief (The Chief)

<Card title="Stawamus Chief">
The Chief is a massive granite monolith in Squamish, about 45 minutes north of Vancouver. Three peaks offer panoramic views of Howe Sound, the Squamish Valley, and the surrounding mountains. The chain ladders and steep granite slabs make it thrilling — and crowded on weekends.

- **Distance:** 11 km round trip (all three peaks)
- **Elevation gain:** 700 m
- **Time:** 4–6 hours
- **Difficulty:** Challenging
- **Best time:** May–October (dry rock is safer)

## 2. Joffre Lakes

<Card title="Joffre Lakes" subtitle="Three turquoise lakes, one epic trail">
Joffre Lakes Provincial Park, about 2 hours from Vancouver, delivers the most Instagram-worthy payoff per kilometre in the region. The trail climbs past Lower, Middle, and Upper Joffre Lakes, each a stunning glacier-fed turquoise. The final viewpoint at Upper Lake sits beneath the Matier Glacier.

- **Distance:** 10 km round trip
- **Elevation gain:** 400 m
- **Time:** 3–5 hours
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Best time:** July–October (ice-free)

## 3. Garibaldi Lake

<Card title="Garibaldi Lake">
Garibaldi Provincial Park's namesake lake is a jaw-dropping turquoise gem surrounded by black tephra sand and towering peaks. The switchback climb through old-growth forest is relentless but rewarded with a huge alpine lake and views of Mount Garibaldi, Black Tusk, and the Sphinx Glacier.

- **Distance:** 18 km round trip
- **Elevation gain:** 900 m
- **Time:** 5–7 hours
- **Difficulty:** Moderate-Challenging
- **Best time:** July–October

## 4. Lighthouse Park

<Card title="Lighthouse Park" subtitle="Easy coastal forest walk">
If you want a short, family-friendly hike with big ocean views, Lighthouse Park in West Vancouver delivers. The trail winds through old-growth Douglas fir and cedar forest to the Point Atkinson Lighthouse, perched on granite bluffs above the Strait of Georgia.

- **Distance:** 5 km loop
- **Elevation gain:** 100 m
- **Time:** 1.5–2.5 hours
- **Difficulty:** Easy
- **Best time:** Year-round

## 5. Quarry Rock

<Card title="Quarry Rock">
This quick, popular hike in Deep Cove (North Vancouver) climbs through second-growth forest to a granite outcrop with sweeping views of Indian Arm, Deep Cove, and the surrounding peaks. It's a local favourite for a morning or sunset outing.

- **Distance:** 3.8 km round trip
- **Elevation gain:** 100 m
- **Time:** 1.5–2 hours
- **Difficulty:** Easy-Moderate
- **Best time:** Year-round (muddy after rain)

<Callout tone="warning" title="Parking nightmare">The trailhead parking lot fills by 8 AM on weekends. Arrive early or take the #211 bus from downtown Vancouver. The trail itself can be bumper-to-bumper on sunny days.</Callout>

## 6. Panorama Ridge (Garibaldi Park)

<Card title="Panorama Ridge" subtitle="The ultimate alpine reward">
This is the big one. Panorama Ridge offers arguably the best view in the Lower Mainland: a 360-degree panorama of Garibaldi Lake, Black Tusk, Mount Garibaldi, and the Coast Mountains. It's a long, grinding day, but the payoff is unforgettable.

- **Distance:** 30 km round trip
- **Elevation gain:** 1,500 m
- **Time:** 8–12 hours
- **Difficulty:** Very Challenging
- **Best time:** July–September (snow-free)

<Callout tone="danger" title="Serious commitment">This is a full-day alpine hike. Start before 6 AM, carry 3+ litres of water, and bring layers — weather can change rapidly above treeline. Check avalanche conditions in spring.</Callout>

## 7. Dog Mountain (Mount Seymour)

<Card title="Dog Mountain">
This short but steep hike in Mount Seymour Provincial Park offers expansive views of Vancouver, Burrard Inlet, and the North Shore mountains — all in under 3 hours. The summit is a smooth granite dome, perfect for a picnic.

- **Distance:** 5.8 km round trip
- **Elevation gain:** 300 m
- **Time:** 2–3 hours
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Best time:** June–October (snow-free)

<Callout tone="info" title="Snowshoe in winter">In winter, Dog Mountain becomes a popular snowshoe route. The trail is well-marked with orange markers, but bring microspikes and check the avalanche forecast.</Callout>

## 8. Bowen Lookout (Cypress Provincial Park)

<Card title="Bowen Lookout">
A moderate hike from the Cypress Mountain ski area, Bowen Lookout rewards you with sweeping views of Howe Sound, Bowen Island, and the Tantalus Range. The trail climbs through subalpine forest before opening onto a rocky viewpoint.

- **Distance:** 8 km round trip
- **Elevation gain:** 400 m
- **Time:** 3–4 hours
- **Difficulty:** Moderate
- **Best time:** June–October

## Essential Hiking Checklist

<Card title="Pack These Essentials">
<Checklist title="Day hike packing list" name="hiking-checklist">
- [ ] Hiking boots or trail runners (broken in)
- [ ] 2–3 litres of water per person
- [ ] High-energy snacks (trail mix, energy bars, fruit)
- [ ] Layers: base layer, fleece, waterproof shell
- [ ] Rain jacket (Vancouver weather changes fast!)
- [ ] Sunscreen and sunglasses
- [ ] First aid kit (blister plasters, painkillers)
- [ ] Headlamp with spare batteries
- [ ] Map and compass (or downloaded GPS route)
- [ ] Bear spray (for Garibaldi, Joffre, Cypress)
- [ ] Emergency blanket or bivy sack
- [ ] Whistle
- [ ] Portable charger for phone
- [ ] Garbage bag for your waste
</Checklist>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Leave no trace">Pack out everything you pack in. Many of these trails have no garbage bins — carry a small bag for your trash.</Callout>
</Card>
