# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two — A Complete Itinerary

<Badge tone="info">Best for: First-time visitors, culture + food lovers</Badge>

Tokyo is a city that rewards a good plan but also leaves room for happy accidents. This 5-day itinerary balances the iconic sights (Shibuya, Senso-ji, Tsukiji) with quieter neighborhoods (Yanaka, Shimokitazawa), and it's paced so you're never sprinting between two places in 20 minutes.

**A few ground rules before we start:**
- **Get an IC card** (Suica or Pasmo) at the airport — it works on all trains, buses, and most convenience stores.
- **Book a pocket Wi-Fi or eSIM** — Google Maps is your best friend here.
- **Cash is still king** at small shops and ramen counters; carry ¥10,000–¥15,000.

---

<Card title="Day 1 — Arrival + Shinjuku at Night" subtitle="Land, settle in, and get the neon out of the way">
<Callout tone="success" title="Pro tip">If your flight lands before 3 PM, swap Day 1's evening with Day 2's morning — do Shinjuku's daytime sights first, then hit the observation deck at sunset.</Callout>

**Morning/Afternoon:**
- Arrive at Narita (NRT) or Haneda (HND). Take the Narita Express or Keikyu Line into the city.
- Check into your hotel in **Shinjuku** or **Shibuya** — these are the most convenient bases for first-timers.
- If you land early, take a gentle walk around **Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden** (¥500 entry, closes at 4:30 PM). It's a quiet green break after the flight.

**Evening:**
- **Dinner:** Join the queue at **Ichiran Shinjuku** for tonkotsu ramen — it's a solo-booth experience that's oddly perfect for two.
- **Golden Gai:** Wander the narrow alleys of tiny bars (most seat 5–8 people). Pick one with an English menu and a friendly sign. It's an experience, not a drinking marathon.
- **Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane):** A smoky, lantern-lit alley of yakitori stalls. Grab skewers and a beer.

<Details title="Where to stay in Shinjuku">
- **Budget:** Book Tea Bed Shinjuku — capsule-style, clean, ~¥6,000/person/night.
- **Mid-range:** Hotel Gracery Shinjuku — the famous Godzilla head is on the roof.
- **Splurge:** Park Hyatt Tokyo (of *Lost in Translation* fame) — skyline views from the pool and bar.
</Details>

</Card>

<Card title="Day 2 — Shibuya, Harajuku & Omotesando" subtitle="The classic west-side day, but with a few locals-only detours">

**Morning — Shibuya:**
- **Shibuya Crossing:** Watch the scramble from the Starbucks by the station (2F) or the free observation deck at **Shibuya Sky** (book ahead, ¥2,000).
- **Hachiko Statue:** The meeting point for Tokyo's teens and tourists alike. Snap a photo, then move on.
- **Miyashita Park:** A new rooftop green space with a food hall and a climbing wall — a nice breather from the crowds.

**Lunch:** Head to **Uobei Shibuya** for conveyor-belt sushi. You order via a touchscreen, and your plates zoom to your seat on a mini expressway. Fun, fast, and ~¥2,000/person.

**Afternoon — Harajuku & Omotesando:**
- **Takeshita Street:** The heart of kawaii culture. Even if you're not shopping, walk it once for the chaos — crepes, colorful wigs, and 100-yen stores.
- **Cat Street:** A quieter parallel street with vintage shops and tiny cafes. Great for people-watching.
- **Omotesando Avenue:** Tokyo's Champs-Élysées — zelkova trees, designer stores, and beautiful architecture. Walk to the end and you're at **Meiji Jingu**.

**Late Afternoon — Meiji Jingu:**
- Enter through the massive torii gate and walk the forested path to the shrine. It's astonishingly quiet for being 10 minutes from Harajuku Station. Free entry; the inner garden costs ¥500.

**Evening:**
- **Dinner:** **Afuri** in Ebisu (a 5-min train ride) for yuzu-shio ramen — light, citrusy, and perfect after a day of walking.
- **Drinks:** **Sake Bar Sake** in Ebisu — a tiny, knowledgeable bar with 100+ sakes by the glass.

<Card title="Day 3 — Tsukiji, Asakusa & Sumida River" subtitle="Old Tokyo, street food, and the city's most photogenic temple">

**Morning — Tsukiji Outer Market:**
- Arrive by 9 AM to beat the crowds. The inner market (where the tuna auction happens) has moved to Toyosu, but the outer market remains a warren of food stalls.
- **Must-eat:** Tamagoyaki (grilled egg on a stick, ¥100), fresh uni (sea urchin) from a vendor, and grilled scallops with butter.
- **Coffee stop:** **Turret Coffee** — a tiny stand run by former fishmongers.

**Late Morning — Asakusa:**
- Take the Tokyo Metro Ginza Line from Tsukiji to Asakusa (15 min).
- **Senso-ji:** Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the **Kaminarimon** gate, down **Nakamise-dori** (shopping street with traditional snacks), and up to the main hall. The **five-story pagoda** is the classic photo.
- **Fortune-telling:** Draw an *omikuji* (fortune slip) at the temple — ¥100. If you get a bad one, tie it to the rack to leave the bad luck behind.

**Lunch:** **Onigiri Asakusa Yadoroku** — a Michelin-starred onigiri shop with only 8 seats. Go early (opens 11:30 AM) or be prepared to wait. Alternatively, grab a *melon pan* (sweet bun) from a street stall.

**Afternoon — Sumida River:**
- Walk along the river to the **Sumida Park** and cross the **Sakura-bashi** bridge for the classic view of Senso-ji's pagoda with the **Tokyo Skytree** behind it.
- **Skytree:** If you want the view, book a ticket for the **Tembo Deck** (¥2,100). It's the tallest structure in Japan, and on a clear day you can see Mt. Fuji.

**Evening:**
- **Dinner:** **Hoppy Street** (just north of Senso-ji) — a row of izakayas serving *horumon* (grilled offal) and *hoppy* (a beer-like shochu drink). It's a locals' scene, not touristy.
- **Night walk:** The **Skytree lights up** at dusk — find a spot along the river and watch it change colors.

<Card title="Day 4 — Yanaka, Ueno & Akihabara" subtitle="Shitamachi charm, museums, and otaku culture">

**Morning — Yanaka:**
- Take the JR Yamanote Line to **Nippori Station**, then walk into **Yanaka Ginza** — a shopping street that survived WWII bombing and feels like 1960s Tokyo.
- **Cat central:** Yanaka is known for its stray cats. Visit **Neko no Jikan** (cat cafe) or just look for felines lounging on temple steps.
- **Yanaka Cemetery:** It's a beautiful, tree-lined walking path with cherry blossoms in spring. Not morbid — genuinely serene.

**Lunch:** **Yanaka Beer Hall** — a craft beer bar in a converted bathhouse. Pair a local IPA with *menchi-katsu* (fried meat croquette).

**Afternoon — Ueno:**
- **Ueno Park:** Home to several museums. Pick one:
  - **Tokyo National Museum** (¥1,000) — samurai armor, ukiyo-e prints, and the best collection of Japanese art.
  - **National Museum of Nature and Science** (¥630) — a giant blue whale skeleton and a faithful robot.
- **Ueno Zoo** (¥600) — if you want to see the pandas. The giant panda is the star.

**Late Afternoon — Akihabara:**
- Take the Yamanote Line from Ueno to Akihabara (2 stops).
- **Electric Town:** Gawk at the arcades, maid cafes, and electronics shops. Even if you're not into anime, the **Super Potato** retro game store (5F) is a fun time capsule.
- **Don Quijote:** The chaotic discount store — perfect for souvenir shopping (matcha KitKats, ramune, tiny robot figures).

**Evening:**
- **Dinner:** **Gyukatsu Ichi Ni San** — a chain serving *gyukatsu* (breaded, deep-fried beef cutlet) that you finish on a mini grill at your table. Juicy, crispy, and very satisfying. Expect a line; go before 6 PM.
- **Drinks:** **Mikkeller Bar Tokyo** in Kanda (5 min south) — Danish craft beer in a sleek, minimalist space.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Akihabara etiquette">Maid cafes are fun but can be pricey (¥1,500+ cover charge). If you're curious, go to **@home cafe** — it's the most tourist-friendly. Avoid touts on the street who offer "massage" — they're scams.</Callout>

</Card>

<Card title="Day 5 — TeamLab, Odaiba & Departure" subtitle="Digital art, a giant Gundam, and a relaxed send-off">

**Morning — TeamLab Planets:**
- Book tickets weeks in advance for **teamLab Planets** (¥3,500, 90-min timed entry). It's a sensory experience — you walk barefoot through water, past digital flowers, and into a room of floating spheres. One of Tokyo's most memorable experiences.
- Go for the **first entry (9 AM)** to avoid crowds.

**Late Morning — Odaiba:**
- Take the Yurikamome Line from Toyosu to Odaiba (10 min). It's a driverless train with great views of Rainbow Bridge.
- **Unicorn Gundam Statue:** The 19.7-meter robot in front of DiverCity mall transforms at certain hours (check the schedule). Free to see.
- **Rainbow Bridge views:** Walk along the waterfront promenade or take a photo from the **Odaiba Seaside Park** beach.

**Lunch:** **DiverCity Tokyo** has a huge food court. Try **Katsukura** for tonkatsu, or **Tsukiji Gindako** for takoyaki.

**Afternoon (flexible):**
- If you have time before your flight, pick one:
  - **TeamLab Borderless** (in Toranomon, near the station) — the sister exhibit, more immersive but also more crowded.
  - **Tokyo Tower** — the classic red-and-white tower, with a great view of the city from the Main Deck (¥1,200).
  - **Shibuya Sky** — if you missed it on Day 2, this is the best sunset spot in the city.

**Departure:**
- From Odaiba, take the Yurikamome back to Shimbashi, then the Narita Express or Keikyu Line to your airport. Give yourself 90 minutes for the ride + check-in.

<Details title="If you have a late flight (after 8 PM)">
Add a last stop: **Roppongi Hills Mori Tower** — the 52F observation deck has the best skyline view in Tokyo, especially at dusk. It's ¥2,000 and open until 11 PM. Then grab a final meal of *sushi* at **Sushi Zanmai** (24/7, decent quality, no reservation needed).
</Details>

**Have an amazing trip!** The city will be overwhelming, wonderful, and weird in the best way. Just remember: bow slightly when thanking, don't eat while walking (it's considered rude), and always trust the vending machines — they sell hot coffee and cold beer, and both are exactly what you need.
