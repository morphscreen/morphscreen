# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two — Your Map-Powered Itinerary

Tokyo is a city of contrasts — neon alleys and quiet temples, world-class sushi and hidden ramen bars. This 5-day plan balances iconic sights, food stops, and romantic moments, all laid out on a map so you can move between them easily.

<Card title="Day 1 — Shinjuku, Harajuku & Shibuya" subtitle="The classic Tokyo energy">
<Steps>
<Step status="done" title="Morning: Meiji Shrine" subtitle="A forest in the city">
Start at the serene Meiji Shrine, a Shinto sanctuary tucked into a 170-acre forest. Walk the wide gravel paths under towering cedar trees — a quiet contrast to what comes later.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Late morning: Harajuku" subtitle="Street style and crepes">
Wander Takeshita Street for quirky fashion, vintage stores, and the famous rainbow crepes. Try a matcha soft-serve at <strong>Milk</strong>.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Afternoon: Shibuya" subtitle="The scramble crossing">
Stand at the iconic Shibuya Crossing, then head to the <strong>Shibuya Sky</strong> observation deck for a 360° view. Grab sushi at <strong>Uobei</strong> — a fun conveyor-belt spot.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Evening: Shinjuku night" subtitle="Golden Gai and Omoide Yokocho">
End in Shinjuku. Explore the tiny lantern-lit alleys of Golden Gai (200+ micro-bars) and Omoide Yokocho for grilled skewers (yakitori) and beer.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 2 — Asakusa, Ueno & Akihabara" subtitle="Old Tokyo and otaku culture">
<Steps>
<Step status="done" title="Morning: Senso-ji Temple" subtitle="Tokyo's oldest temple">
Arrive early to beat the crowds. Walk through the huge red <em>Kaminarimon</em> gate, browse Nakamise-dori's souvenir stalls, and try <strong>ningyo-yaki</strong> (small filled cakes).
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Midday: Ueno Park" subtitle="Museums and street food">
Stroll Ueno Park, visit the Tokyo National Museum (samurai armor, ukiyo-e prints), and eat <strong>amazake</strong> or <strong>taiyaki</strong> from street vendors.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Afternoon: Akihabara" subtitle="Electric town">
Explore multi-story arcades, retro game shops, and anime merchandise. Try a <strong>maid café</strong> if you're curious — or just watch from the street.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Evening: Asakusa riverside" subtitle="A calm finish">
Return to Asakusa for a riverside stroll along the Sumida River. Grab dinner at a traditional <strong>izakaya</strong> near the temple.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 3 — Tsukiji, Ginza & Odaiba" subtitle="Food, fashion, and futuristic views">
<Steps>
<Step status="done" title="Early morning: Tsukiji Outer Market" subtitle="The best breakfast in Tokyo">
Arrive by 8am for the freshest sushi. Try <strong>tamagoyaki</strong> (grilled egg on a stick) and uni (sea urchin) at a tiny counter. Skip the inner market — it moved to Toyosu.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Late morning: Ginza" subtitle="Luxury and art">
Window-shop along Chuo-dori (closed to cars on weekends), visit the <strong>Ginza Six</strong> rooftop garden, and see the facade of the <strong>Kabuki-za</strong> theater.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Afternoon: Odaiba" subtitle="Island of the future">
Take the Yurikamome monorail (great views of Rainbow Bridge). Visit <strong>teamLab Planets</strong> — an immersive digital art museum you walk through in water. Book tickets in advance.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Evening: Rainbow Bridge sunset" subtitle="A photo moment">
Stay for sunset from the Odaiba waterfront. The skyline lights up across the bay — a perfect couple's photo spot.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 4 — Shinjuku Gyoen & Golden Gai" subtitle="A slower, romantic day">
<Steps>
<Step status="done" title="Morning: Shinjuku Gyoen" subtitle="A garden for every season">
One of Tokyo's best parks — a mix of Japanese, French, and English gardens. Rent a bench, feed the koi, and enjoy the quiet. Cherry blossoms in spring, crimson maples in autumn.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Afternoon: Free exploration" subtitle="Pick your pace">
Choose one: the <strong>Samurai Museum</strong> (closed for renovation until 2025), the <strong>Tokyo Metropolitan Building</strong> (free observation deck), or a <strong>ramen cooking class</strong>.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Evening: Golden Gai" subtitle="Cocktails in a tiny bar">
Return to Golden Gai for a cozy bar-hopping night. Many bars seat only 6–8 people — perfect for a date. Try <strong>Death Match</strong> bar for creative cocktails.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 5 — Skytree & Yanaka" subtitle="Views and old-town charm">
<Steps>
<Step status="done" title="Morning: Tokyo Skytree" subtitle="634 meters up">
Take the elevator to the Tembo Deck (350m) for a jaw-dropping view. On clear days you can see Mt. Fuji. Go right at 9am to skip the queue.
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Lunch: Solamachi" subtitle="Shops and food under the tower">
Explore the Skytree's attached mall — try <strong>Tokyo Banana</strong> (a famous souvenir) and <strong>Ganso Curry Pan</strong> (a fried curry bun).
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Afternoon: Yanaka Ginza" subtitle="Old Tokyo that survived the war">
Wander this nostalgic shopping street with 70+ small shops. Visit the <strong>Yanaka Cemetery</strong> (beautiful cherry trees) and spot the resident cats. Try <strong>menchi-katsu</strong> (fried meat croquette).
</Step>
<Step status="done" title="Evening: Farewell dinner" subtitle="A last taste of Tokyo">
Head back to your hotel area for a final meal. If you're near Shinjuku, try <strong>Omoide Yokocho</strong> again — or go for upscale sushi at <strong>Uogashi Nihon-Ichi</strong> (standing sushi bar, affordable).
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Essential Tips for Two">
<Columns>
<Card title="Getting Around">
- Get a <strong>Suica</strong> or <strong>Pasmo</strong> IC card (tap in/out of trains, use in convenience stores)
- The <strong>JR Yamanote Line</strong> loops through all major areas — it's your best friend
- Taxis are expensive but useful late at night (flag them at designated stops)
</Card>
<Card title="Booking in Advance">
- <strong>teamLab Planets</strong>: book 2–3 weeks ahead
- <strong>Shibuya Sky</strong>: book 1 week ahead for sunset slots
- <strong>Tokyo Skytree</strong>: book same-day morning for best slots
- <strong>Restaurants</strong>: use <strong>Tablelog</strong> or ask your hotel to reserve
</Card>
