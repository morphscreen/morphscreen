# Should you rent or buy a flat?

With a £60k salary and £1,800/month rent, you're in a solid position to consider both options. The answer depends on how long you'll stay put and what you'd buy. Let's work through it.

<Card title="The key question: how long will you stay?">
Buying only starts to beat renting once the upfront costs (stamp duty, legal fees, moving costs) are spread over enough years. As a rough rule:

- **Under 3 years** → renting usually wins
- **3–5 years** → it's close; depends on price growth
- **5+ years** → buying typically wins if you can afford it

<Callout tone="info" title="Stamp duty for you">
As a first-time buyer in England, you pay **no stamp duty on the first £425,000** (up to a £625k purchase price). Above that, the normal rates kick in.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="What could you afford?">
Lenders typically let you borrow **4–4.5× your salary**, so around **£240k–£270k**. With a 10% deposit, that puts you in the **£270k–£300k** range.

<Callout tone="success" title="Cash flow">
A repayment mortgage on a £280k flat would cost **~£1,560/month** — £240 less than your rent. But you also need to budget for service charges, ground rent, and repairs (typically £100–£300/month on a flat).
</Callout>

<Card title="What you gain and give up">
<Columns>
<Card title="Buying">
- **Build equity** — each payment buys you more of the flat
- **Stability** — no landlord can evict you or hike rent
- **Decorate freely** — paint, drill, renovate
- **Leverage** — price growth multiplies your deposit
- **But:** you're tied down, responsible for repairs, and prices can fall
</Card>
<Card title="Renting">
- **Flexibility** — move for a job or partner easily
- **No hidden costs** — broken boiler? Landlord's problem
- **Invest elsewhere** — your deposit could grow in stocks
- **But:** rent rises with inflation, and you build no ownership
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="So, what should you do?">
<Callout tone="info" title="The honest answer">
**If you can see yourself staying put for 5+ years** and can scrape together a deposit, buying a flat around £250k–£280k is likely to beat renting financially — and you'll own something at the end.

**If you're not sure about the next few years**, keep renting and invest your savings instead. You're paying more each month, but you're buying freedom.
</Callout>
