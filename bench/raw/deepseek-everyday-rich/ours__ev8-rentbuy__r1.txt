## The core question: how long will you stay?

<Card title="Rent vs buy — the 5-year rule">
<Callout tone="info" title="The rule of thumb">Buying usually wins if you stay **5+ years**. Below that, the upfront costs (stamp duty, legal fees, moving costs, repairs) rarely pay off.</Callout>

Renting is cheaper month-to-month right now, but buying builds equity. Here's the trade-off:

<Columns>
<Card title="Renting">
- **Monthly cost:** £1,800 — fixed, predictable
- **No deposit needed** (but usually 1–2 months' rent upfront)
- **No maintenance costs** — landlord handles it
- **Flexible** — move with a month's notice
- **Money gone** — no equity, no asset
</Card>

<Card title="Buying">
- **Monthly mortgage:** roughly £1,300–£1,700 (with 15% deposit)
- **Deposit needed:** £37,500–£52,500
- **Ongoing costs:** maintenance (~1% of value/year), insurance, service charges
- **Tied in** — selling costs 2–3% of the price
- **Builds equity** — your money works for you
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="success" title="The honest verdict">If you can save a deposit and plan to stay **6+ years**, buying a flat is very likely the better financial move. If you might move in 2–3 years, keep renting.</Callout>
</Card>

## The real decision — a checklist

<Card title="Should you buy? Answer these 5 questions">
<Checklist title="Buying readiness" name="buy-checklist">
- [ ] I have (or can save) a **15–20% deposit** within 2 years
- [ ] I plan to stay in the same city for **5+ years**
- [ ] I have **£5,000–£10,000** spare for emergencies and repairs after buying
- [ ] My job is stable (or I'd be happy renting out the flat if I move)
- [ ] I understand leasehold costs (service charges, ground rent) if applicable
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="If you ticked 3 or fewer">Renting is the smarter move for now. Focus on saving a bigger deposit — it'll cut your mortgage rate and monthly payments later.</Callout>

<Callout tone="success" title="If you ticked 4–5">Buying is likely the right call. Start researching areas, get a mortgage in principle, and line up a solicitor.</Callout>

## Next steps if you decide to buy

<Steps>
<Step title="Save your deposit" status="current">Aim for 15–20% to get the best mortgage rates. Use a Lifetime ISA if you haven't bought before — the government adds 25% up to £1,000/year.</Step>
<Step title="Get a mortgage in principle" status="todo">Banks will lend roughly **4.5× your salary** = £270,000. With a £45,000 deposit, that gives you a £315,000 budget.</Step>
<Step title="Research areas and flats" status="todo">Look at transport links, schools (if relevant), and service charges. A £300k flat can cost £200/month in service charges alone.</Step>
<Step title="Make an offer and get surveys" status="todo">A full structural survey costs £500–£1,000 but can save you thousands on hidden issues.</Step>
<Step title="Complete and move in" status="todo">Budget 8–12 weeks from offer to completion. Then enjoy owning your own place.</Step>
</Steps>
