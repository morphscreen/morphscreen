# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making an authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is about mastering three things: a high-hydration dough, a screaming-hot oven, and restraint with toppings. Here's the complete guide.

<Card title="The 3 Golden Rules of Neapolitan Pizza">
1. **Dough**: 60-65% hydration, 24-48 hour cold ferment
2. **Heat**: 430°C (800°F) minimum — wood-fired is ideal, but you can get close at home
3. **Toppings**: San Marzano tomatoes, fresh mozzarella, fresh basil, olive oil — nothing more

<Callout tone="info" title="Why it matters">The high heat creates the signature leopard-spotted crust — puffy, charred, and chewy. You can't fake this with a home oven set to 250°C.</Callout>
</Card>

## Step 1: Make the Dough

<Card title="The Perfect Dough Recipe">
<Steps>
<Step title="Mix the dough" status="done">
Mix **500g "00" flour** (Caputo is the gold standard) with **325g water** (65% hydration), **10g fine sea salt**, and **2g fresh yeast** (or 1g instant). The dough should be soft and slightly sticky.
</Step>
<Step title="Knead" status="done">
Knead by hand for 10-15 minutes, or in a stand mixer with a dough hook for 8 minutes, until smooth and elastic. It should pass the windowpane test — stretch a small piece until translucent without tearing.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done">
Cover and rest at room temperature for 2 hours, then fold the dough once or twice. Refrigerate for 24-48 hours. This cold ferment develops the complex, slightly tangy flavor.
</Step>
<Step title="Shape the balls" status="done">
Remove from the fridge, divide into **4 equal pieces** (about 210g each), and shape into tight balls. Let rest at room temperature for 2-3 hours until doubled and relaxed.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't over-proof">If the dough balls feel too airy or collapse when touched, they're over-proofed. Use them within 2 hours of coming to room temperature.</Callout>
</Card>

## Step 2: Get the Heat Right

<Card title="Oven Options — Ranked">
<Columns>
<div>
**Option 1: Dedicated Pizza Oven** — Best results
- **Ooni Koda 16** or **Gozney Roccbox** reach 450°C+ in 20 minutes
- Cook time: 60-90 seconds
- Leopard spotting: ✅ Perfect

**Option 2: Home Oven with Pizza Steel** — Good compromise
- Preheat steel at 260°C (max oven temp) for 1 hour
- Switch to broil for the last 10 minutes
- Cook time: 4-5 minutes
- Leopard spotting: ✅ Some char, less puffy

**Option 3: Cast Iron Skillet** — For beginners
- Heat skillet on stovetop high for 10 minutes
- Finish under the broiler
- Cook time: 3-4 minutes
- Leopard spotting: ✅ Bottom char, top less so
</div>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="success" title="Best home setup">A **pizza steel** (not a stone) in your home oven gives the best crust-to-cost ratio. Steels transfer heat faster, giving you that dark, blistered bottom.</Callout>
</Card>

## Step 3: Assemble & Bake

<Card title="Assembly — Less is More">
<Steps>
<Step title="Stretch the dough" status="done">
Flatten the ball with your fingers, then stretch from the center outward, leaving a 1-2cm rim. The center should be thin enough to see through. Don't use a rolling pin — it destroys the air bubbles.
</Step>
<Step title="Add sauce" status="done">
Spread **2-3 tablespoons of crushed San Marzano tomatoes** (drained, with a pinch of salt) in a spiral, leaving the rim bare. Don't over-sauce — the pizza should be almost dry in the center.
</Step>
<Step title="Add cheese" status="done">
Tear **80g fresh mozzarella** (fior di latte) into small pieces and scatter. Use less than you think — Neapolitan pizza is about the dough, not the toppings.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="done">
Launch the pizza onto the hot surface. In a dedicated oven: 60-90 seconds, rotating every 20 seconds. In a home oven: 4-5 minutes until the rim is puffy and charred.
</Step>
<Step title="Finish" status="done">
Remove, drizzle with **extra-virgin olive oil**, add fresh basil leaves, and serve immediately. The pizza should be soft enough to fold — never crisp like New York style.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Troubleshooting

<Card title="Common Problems & Fixes">
<Columns>
<div>
**Dough too sticky**
- Use less water (60% hydration instead of 65%)
- Flour your work surface, not the dough

**Crust not puffing**
- Your oven isn't hot enough
- Dough is under-proofed — give it more time at room temp

**Soggy center**
- Too much sauce or cheese
- Mozzarella not drained
- Pizza not cooked long enough
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>
