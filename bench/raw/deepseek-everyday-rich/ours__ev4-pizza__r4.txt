# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

<Callout tone="info" title="The 3 pillars of Neapolitan pizza">
Authentic Neapolitan pizza (pizza napoletana) comes down to **dough** (high-hydration, long-fermented), **sauce** (San Marzano tomatoes, raw), and **heat** (a blazing-hot oven, 430°C+/800°F+). Master these three and you're 90% there.
</Callout>

<Card title="The dough: your foundation">
The dough is the heart of Neapolitan pizza. It's a simple mix of flour, water, salt, and yeast — but the magic is in the **hydration** (60-65%) and **fermentation** (24-48 hours in the fridge).

**You'll need:**
- 1 kg (about 8 cups) **00 flour** (Caputo Pizzeria or similar, protein ~12.5%)
- 600-650 ml (about 2.5-2.75 cups) **cold water**
- 25 g (about 4.5 tsp) **fine sea salt**
- 2 g (about 0.5 tsp) **fresh yeast** (or 0.7 g dry yeast — yes, that little!)

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done">Dissolve salt in the water, then add flour and crumbled yeast. Mix by hand until a shaggy ball forms.</Step>
<Step title="Knead" status="done">Knead for 10-15 minutes on a lightly floured surface until smooth and elastic. The dough should be tacky but not sticky.</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done">Cover and rest at room temperature for 2 hours, then refrigerate for 24-48 hours. This slow cold ferment develops flavor and structure.</Step>
<Step title="Ball and proof" status="done">Divide into 250 g balls (makes 4 pizzas). Let them proof at room temperature for 3-4 hours until doubled and pillowy.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The 00 flour question">
Use **00 flour** (finely ground Italian flour). It makes a softer, more extensible dough that's easier to stretch thin. All-purpose flour works in a pinch but gives a chewier, less authentic result.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The sauce: simple and raw">
Neapolitan sauce is **uncooked** — that's the secret. The heat of the oven does the cooking.

<KeyValue title="The classic recipe">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| San Marzano tomatoes (canned whole) | 1 × 400 g can |
| Fine sea salt | A pinch |
| Fresh basil | 3-4 leaves |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | A drizzle (optional) |
</KeyValue>

<Steps>
<Step title="Crush">Crush the tomatoes by hand or with a fork — don't blend them. You want a chunky, rustic texture.</Step>
<Step title="Season">Add salt and stir. Taste and adjust — some brands need more salt than others.</Step>
<Step title="Rest">Let the sauce sit at room temperature for 30 minutes so the flavors meld.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="San Marzano vs. regular canned tomatoes">
Real San Marzano tomatoes (from the Vesuvius region) are sweeter, less acidic, and fleshier. If you can't find them, use any good-quality whole peeled Italian tomatoes — Cento, Mutti, or La Valle are solid alternatives.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The cheese and toppings">
Authentic Neapolitan pizza uses **just a few** toppings. The classic is *Margherita*: tomato, fresh mozzarella, basil, and a drizzle of olive oil.

<Columns>
<div>
### Mozzarella di bufala
- **Best for:** Authentic flavor
- **Texture:** Soft, milky, creamy
- **Note:** Use it **fresh**, torn into pieces. It's wet, so pat it dry first or your pizza will be soggy.
</div>
<div>
### Fior di latte
- **Best for:** A drier, more manageable cheese
- **Texture:** Firm, stretchy
- **Note:** Made from cow's milk. Grate it or tear it. It's what most pizzerias in Naples use for Margherita.
</div>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't overdo it">
Neapolitan pizza is **not** a delivery-style American pizza. Use **sparingly**: about 80 g (1/3 cup) of cheese per pizza. Too much cheese = a soggy, heavy pie.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The oven: your biggest challenge">
This is where home pizza making gets tricky. Neapolitan pizza needs **430°C (800°F)** for 60-90 seconds. Most home ovens top out at 250°C (480°F).

<Columns>
<div>

</div>
<div>
<Stat label="Home oven max" value="250°C" change="Not enough" icon="warning-circle" tone="warning" />

<Callout tone="info" title="Your best home options">
1. **Ooni Koda 16** — gas-powered, hits 430°C in 20 minutes. ~$500.
2. **Gozney Roccbox** — gas or wood, portable. ~$500.
3. **Baking steel in a regular oven** — preheat for 1 hour at max. Your pizza will take 4-5 minutes and be *good*, but not *Neapolitan*.
</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat your oven" status="done">If using a pizza oven, preheat for 20-30 minutes. If using a baking steel, preheat for a full hour.</Step>
<Step title="Stretch the dough" status="done">Gently press the dough ball flat, then stretch from the center outward. Leave a slightly thicker rim (the cornicione). Don't use a rolling pin — it destroys the air bubbles.</Step>
<Step title="Top quickly" status="done">Sauce first, then cheese, then basil. Work fast — the dough should be in the oven within 60 seconds of being topped.</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="done">In a pizza oven: 60-90 seconds, rotating once. In a home oven: 4-5 minutes on the hottest setting, then 1-2 minutes under the broiler to char the top.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="The assembly: step by step">
<Steps>
<Step title="Warm the dough" status="done">Take your proofed dough balls out of the fridge 2 hours before baking. They should be at room temperature and nicely puffed.</Step>
<Step title="Flour your work surface" status="done">Use 00 flour or semolina. Don't use olive oil — it makes the dough slippery and hard to handle.</Step>
<Step title="Stretch by hand" status="done">Press the center down, then work outward with your fingertips. The rim should stay thicker. Gently pull and rotate until the disc is about 12 inches (30 cm) wide.</Step>
<Step title="Top it" status="done">Ladle about 3-4 tablespoons of sauce into the center, spread in a spiral. Add torn mozzarella, a few basil leaves, and a drizzle of olive oil.</Step>
<Step title="Launch and bake" status="done">Slide the pizza onto a well-floured peel, then into the oven. Watch it closely — it's done when the rim is puffed and charred, and the bottom is spotty.</Step>
<Step title="Rest and serve" status="done">Let it rest for 1 minute, then slice and eat immediately. No forks needed — fold it and enjoy.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common mistakes to avoid">
<Checklist title="Your pre-bake checklist" name="pizza-check">
- [ ] Dough is at room temperature (not cold from the fridge)
- [ ] Hydration is 60-65% (not lower — that makes a tough crust)
- [ ] Fermented 24-48 hours (not just 2 hours)
- [ ] Sauce is raw, not cooked
- [ ] Cheese is patted dry (not wet)
- [ ] Oven is screaming hot (not just "hot")
- [ ] Toppings are sparse (not piled on)
</Checklist>
</Card>
