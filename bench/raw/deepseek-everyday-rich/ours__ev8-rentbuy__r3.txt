# Rent or buy? Let's work through it

<Callout tone="info" title="Your situation">
You earn £60k a year and pay £1,800/month rent. On a £60k salary, a lender will typically let you borrow around **4–4.5× your income** — that's **£240k–£270k**. With a 10% deposit, you'd be looking at flats priced around **£267k–£300k**.
</Callout>

<Card title="The quick answer">
If you plan to stay in one place for **5+ years**, buying is usually the better financial move — you build equity instead of paying someone else's mortgage. If you might move sooner, renting wins because buying costs (stamp duty, solicitor, moving, selling) eat your gains.

That said, the exact numbers depend on your deposit, the flat's price, and how long you'll stay. Let's dig in.

<Card title="The real question: how long will you stay?">
The biggest cost of buying isn't the mortgage — it's the **one-time fees** you pay to get in and out. Here's what they look like:

<KeyValue title="One-time buying costs (on a £280k flat)">
| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Stamp duty (first-time buyer, £280k) | £0 |
| Solicitor & searches | £1,500–£2,500 |
| Survey | £400–£1,000 |
| Mortgage arrangement fee | £0–£1,500 |
| Moving costs | £500–£1,500 |
| **Total to get in** | **£2,400–£6,500** |
| Estate agent & legal to sell (when you leave) | £4,000–£8,000 |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The break-even rule of thumb">
Stamp duty is currently **£0 for first-time buyers** on flats up to £425k — that helps a lot. But you still face roughly **£7k–£15k** in combined buying and selling costs. Spread over 5 years, that's **£120–£250/month** you need to "earn back" before buying beats renting. After 5 years, buying almost always wins — you're building equity the whole time.
</Callout>
