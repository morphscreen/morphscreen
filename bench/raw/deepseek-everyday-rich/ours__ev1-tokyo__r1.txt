# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A balanced itinerary mixing iconic sights, hidden gems, great food, and romantic moments. <Badge tone="success">Perfect for first-timers</Badge>

<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
Get a **Suica or Pasmo IC card** at the airport — tap on/off trains, buses, and convenience stores. The Yamanote loop line covers most tourist areas.
</Callout>

<Card title="Day 1: Classic Tokyo — Asakusa & Sumida River">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Senso-ji Temple" status="done" subtitle="Tokyo's oldest temple">
Walk through the iconic **Kaminarimon Gate** and Nakamise shopping street. Try freshly baked *ningyo-yaki* (little doll cakes).
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Tempura near the temple" status="done" subtitle="Crispy, light, and affordable">
Head to **Tempura Daikokuya** — a local institution since 1887. The tendon (tempura rice bowl) is a must.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Sumida River cruise" status="done" subtitle="See Tokyo from the water">
Take the **water bus** from Asakusa to Hamarikyu Gardens. Cherry blossoms in spring, cool breezes in summer.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Tokyo Skytree at sunset" status="done" subtitle="634m of glittering views">
Book the **Tembo Deck** for golden hour. Watch the city light up below. Have dinner at the **Solomon** restaurant on floor 31.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 2: Youth Culture — Shibuya & Harajuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Meiji Shrine" status="done" subtitle="A forest in the city">
Walk the gravel path through 100,000 trees. Watch a traditional Shinto wedding if you're lucky. Write an *ema* (wish plaque).
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Harajuku street food" status="done" subtitle="Tiny bites, big flavors">
Try **crepes** from Marion Crepes, **taiyaki** (fish-shaped cakes) from Taco-ya, and **rainbow cotton candy** from Totti Candy Factory.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Takeshita Street & Omotesando" status="done" subtitle="Kawaii chaos to high-end chic">
Explore Harajuku's quirky shops (try a purikura photo booth!), then stroll down tree-lined Omotesando for designer stores.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Shibuya Crossing & dinner" status="done" subtitle="The world's busiest intersection">
Watch the scramble crossing from **Magnet by Shibuya 109** rooftop. For dinner, book **Uobei Shibuya** — sushi on a conveyor belt, each plate ¥120.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 3: Neon Nights — Shinjuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Shinjuku Gyoen Garden" status="done" subtitle="Three gardens in one">
A perfect blend of Japanese, French, and English landscape styles. Cherry blossoms in late March, autumn colors in November.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ramen in Omoide Yokocho" status="done" subtitle="Memory Lane, tiny stalls">
Squeeze into a 10-seat ramen bar. Try **Nagoshi** for rich tonkotsu broth.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building" status="done" subtitle="Free observation deck">
The 45th-floor observatory is **free** — see Mt. Fuji on clear days. Go before sunset for day-and-night views.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho" status="done" subtitle="Tiny bars, big character">
Explore the 200+ tiny bars in Golden Gai. For dinner, try **yakitori** at Isomaru Suisan. End with a nightcap at **Bar Albatross** (3rd floor, glowing skulls).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 4: Culture & Quirk — Ueno & Akihabara">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Ueno Park & Museums" status="done" subtitle="Art, history, and pandas">
Visit the **Tokyo National Museum** (samurai armor, ukiyo-e prints) or **Ueno Zoo** (giant pandas!). The park is stunning in spring.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ameya-Yokocho market" status="done" subtitle="Street food paradise">
Grab **yakitori** skewers, fresh fruit, and cheap Japanese snacks. Try the famous **Kaiyū** for grilled seafood.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Akihabara Electric Town" status="done" subtitle="Anime, gadgets, and arcades">
Browse multi-story electronics shops, visit **Mandarake** for retro toys, and play at **Taito Station** arcade.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Maid café experience" status="done" subtitle="Cheesy, fun, unforgettable">
Book **@home café** — the most popular maid café. They'll draw ketchup art on your omelette rice and sing songs. Very tourist-friendly with English menus.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 5: Futuristic Tokyo — Odaiba & TeamLab">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: TeamLab Planets" status="done" subtitle="Walk through digital art">
Book tickets **weeks in advance**. You'll wade through knee-deep water surrounded by floating flowers and light. Mind-blowing and romantic.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: DiverCity Tokyo Plaza" status="done" subtitle="Gundam statue & food court">
See the 1:1 scale **Unicorn Gundam** transform. Eat at the mall's food court — try **Katsukura** for tonkatsu.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Odaiba Seaside Park" status="done" subtitle="Mini Statue of Liberty & Rainbow Bridge">
Take a stroll on the beach, snap photos of the Rainbow Bridge, and ride the **ferris wheel** at Palette Town for panoramic views.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Farewell dinner in Ginza" status="done" subtitle="Tokyo's most elegant district">
Take the Yurikamome train back. For a splurge, try **Ginza Kojyu** (Michelin-starred kaiseki). Or keep it casual at **Ippudo Ginza** ramen.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Pro Tips">
<Columns>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Book ahead">
TeamLab Planets and popular restaurants need **2-4 weeks advance booking**. The Tokyo Skytree sells out on weekends.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="info" title="Best times to visit">
March-April for cherry blossoms, October-November for autumn colors. Avoid Golden Week (late April-early May) — everything's packed.
</Callout>
</Columns>
<Callout tone="success" title="Money-saving hack">
Get a **Tokyo Subway Ticket** (24/48/72-hour) — unlimited rides on Tokyo Metro & Toei lines for ¥800/¥1,200/¥1,500. Covers most tourist spots.
</Callout>
</Card>
