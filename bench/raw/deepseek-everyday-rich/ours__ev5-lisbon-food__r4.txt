# What to Eat in Lisbon, and Where

Lisbon's food scene is a delicious mix of fresh seafood, hearty stews, and sweet egg-based pastries. Here's your practical guide to the must-eats and the best spots to try them.

<Card title="The Iconic Bites">
<Columns>
<div>

### Pastéis de Nata
The famous custard tart with a caramelized, flaky crust. Eat it warm, dusted with cinnamon.

**Where:** **Manteigaria** (multiple locations, including Time Out Market) or the legendary **Pastéis de Belém** in Belém, the original since 1837.

</div>
<div>

### Bifana
A spicy, garlicky pulled-pork sandwich on a crusty roll. Simple, messy, and perfect with a cold beer.

**Where:** **Casa Batalhas** (near Praça da Figueira) or **O Trevo** for a classic street version.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Seafood & Main Dishes">
<Columns>
<div>

### Bacalhau à Brás
Shredded salted cod with onions, matchstick potatoes, and eggs. It's the national dish in a thousand forms — this is the most beloved.

**Where:** **Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau** (try their baked version in a pastry shell) or **Solar 31 da Calçada**.

</div>
<div>

### Arroz de Marisco
A rich, saffron-hued seafood rice stew loaded with prawns, clams, and lobster. Hearty and unforgettable.

**Where:** **Cervejaria Ramiro** — famous for seafood, expect a queue. Or **Marisqueira Um** for a more local vibe.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat: Neighborhoods">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Baixa & Chiado">
The historic center. Great for a first taste, but tourist-heavy.

- **Time Out Market** — a food hall with stalls from Lisbon's top chefs. Perfect for sampling many things at once.
- **Café A Brasileira** — historic café for a coffee and a pastel de nata.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Alfama">
The oldest quarter, winding streets and fado music. Best for traditional tascas.

- **A Travessa do Mercado** — intimate, family-run spot for grilled fish.
- **Casa de Pasto** — classic Portuguese dishes in a no-frills setting.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Príncipe Real & Bairro Alto">
Trendy and vibrant. Great for modern takes on classics and late-night bites.

- **A Cevicheria** — though Peruvian, it's a Lisbon institution for fresh fish.
- **Taberna da Rua das Flores** — small, seasonal menu, book ahead.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Sweet Treats & Drinks">
<Columns>
<div>

### Ginjinha
A sour cherry liqueur served in a shot glass, often with a piece of fruit at the bottom.

**Where:** **A Ginjinha** (near Rossio) — the original since 1840.

</div>
<div>

### Queijada de Sintra
A small, sweet cheese tart. Less famous than the nata, but just as good.

**Where:** **Piriquita** in Sintra (a short train ride away), or find them at **Manteigaria**.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Practical Tips">
- **Book ahead** for dinner at popular spots like Cervejaria Ramiro, especially weekends.
- **Lunch is the main meal** — many restaurants offer a "prato do dia" (daily special) for around €10-12.
- **Tap water is fine** to drink. Ask for "água da torneira" if you want to save money.
- **Tipping** is not expected, but rounding up or leaving 5-10% for great service is appreciated.
</Callout>
