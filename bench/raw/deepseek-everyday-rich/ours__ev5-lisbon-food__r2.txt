# What to Eat in Lisbon, and Where

Lisbon's food scene is a delicious mix of Atlantic seafood, hearty stews, and sweet pastries — all best enjoyed at small, family-run spots. Here's your essential eating guide.

<Card title="The Must-Try Dishes">
<Columns>
<div>

### Petiscos (Small Plates)
- **Amêijoas à Bulhão Pato** — clams in garlic, olive oil, and coriander
- **Chouriço assado** — flamed smoked sausage served in clay pots
- **Pastéis de bacalhau** — crispy salt cod fritters

</div>
<div>

### Main Courses
- **Bacalhau à Brás** — shredded salt cod with potatoes, eggs, and olives
- **Arroz de marisco** — rich seafood rice, like a Portuguese paella
- **Bifana** — spicy pork sandwich, the ultimate street food

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

- **Casa de Pasto** — tiny, authentic spot for grilled sardines and petiscos
- **Pois, Café** — charming café with excellent pastel de nata and coffee

<Card title="The Sweet Finish: Pastel de Nata">
No trip is complete without Lisbon's iconic custard tart. The best ones are served warm, with a sprinkle of cinnamon.

<Gallery>

**Where to get them:**
- **Manteigaria** (Chiado) — the gold standard
- **Pastéis de Belém** — the original recipe, near the monastery (expect a queue)
- **Fábrica da Nata** — excellent, with multiple branches

<Card title="Practical Tips">
<Callout tone="info" title="Reservations">
Popular spots like Ramiro and Taberna da Rua das Flores book out days ahead. Call or book online, especially for dinner.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Dining Hours">
Lunch runs 12:30–3 PM, dinner starts around 8 PM. Many kitchens close between 3–7 PM. Hungry at 5 PM? Grab a bifana or a pastry.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="success" title="Costs">
- Petiscos: €3–8 each
- Seafood mains: €15–25
- Pastel de nata: €1.20–1.50
- Coffee (bica): €0.80–1.20
</Callout>
</Card>
