# What to Eat in Lisbon, and Where

Lisbon is a city of bold flavors — grilled fish, custard tarts, and hearty stews that taste like the Atlantic. Here's your edible tour, neighborhood by neighborhood.

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Pastéis de Nata">
The iconic custard tart — crisp, caramelized top, creamy center. Best eaten warm with a sprinkle of cinnamon.

<Gallery>
![Pastel de nata on a small plate with cinnamon shaker](find)
![Pastel de nata with espresso on a café table](find)
</Gallery>

**Top spots:**
- **Pastéis de Belém** (R. de Belém 84) — the original since 1837, worth the tram ride; €1.30/tart
- **Manteigaria** (R. do Loreto 2) — the modern favorite, watch them being made; €1.20/tart
- **Confeitaria Nacional** (Praça da Figueira 18B) — historic, elegant, great coffee pairing

<Button ask="Tell me more about Pastéis de Belém">More on Belém</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bifana">
A humble but perfect sandwich: marinated pork in a crusty roll, with mustard or piri-piri. Street food royalty.

**Where:**
- **Casa Bifanas** (Praça da Figueira 10) — the classic, open since 1880s, €3.50
- **O Trevo** (Rua da Conceição 128) — a local institution, bigger portion, €4
- **Café Beira Gare** (Praça do Areeiro) — late-night favorite, bustling till 2am

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Ask for it "com piri-piri" if you want heat. Most places serve it plain.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bacalhau à Brás">
Portugal's national dish — shredded cod, thin-cut fries, onion, and scrambled egg, topped with olives. Salty, comforting, essential.

**Where:**
- **Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau** (Rua Augusta 275) — the cod cake version, €9.50
- **A Semente** (Travessa do Fala-Só 32B) — modern take, €14
- **Cervejaria Trindade** (Rua Nova da Trindade 20) — historic tiled hall, €16

<Button ask="What's the difference between bacalhau dishes?">Compare cod dishes</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Sardinhas & Grilled Fish">
Grilled sardines are a summer ritual — but any time of year, fresh fish from the Atlantic is the star.

**Where:**
- **Ponto Final** (Cais do Ginjal 70, Almada) — across the river, sunset views, €18 for sardines
- **Casa de Pasto** (Rua de São Bento 92) — no menu, chef's choice, €25
- **Marisqueira Ramiro** (Av. Alm. Reis 1) — the seafood temple, famous for garlic shrimp, €30-40

<Gallery>
![Grilled sardines on a platter at a riverside restaurant](find)
![Garlic shrimp at Marisqueira Ramiro, sizzling in oil](find)
</Gallery>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Ginjinha">
A shot of sour cherry liqueur — sweet, boozy, and a Lisbon birthright. Often served in a little chocolate cup.

**Where:**
- **A Ginjinha** (Largo São Domingos 8) — the original, since 1840, €1
- **Ginjinha Sem Rival** (Rua das Portas de Santo Antão 7) — stronger, more local, €1.20

<Callout tone="warning" title="Pace yourself">It's deceptively sweet — two or three is plenty. It's 20% ABV.</Callout>
</Tab>

<Card title="Before You Go">
<Checklist title="Foodie packing list" name="lisbon-food-checklist">
- [ ] Comfortable shoes — you'll walk up hills between meals
- [ ] Appetite — portions are generous
- [ ] Cash — small tascas don't take cards
- [ ] Reusable bag — for market finds
- [ ] Open mind — try the green wine, the tripe, the pig's ear
</Checklist>

<Callout tone="success" title="The golden rule">Eat where the locals queue. If a place has a line of Portuguese workers at lunch, you're in the right spot.</Callout>
