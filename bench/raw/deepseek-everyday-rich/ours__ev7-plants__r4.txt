# Houseplants That Are Hard to Kill

Some plants thrive on neglect. These hardy houseplants tolerate low light, irregular watering, and temperature swings — perfect for beginners or busy people.

<Callout tone="success" title="The golden rule">Most "unkillable" plants die from overwatering, not neglect. When in doubt, wait longer between waterings.</Callout>

### 1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)
- Tolerates weeks without water
- Thrives in low or bright light
- Grows slowly, needs repotting rarely

- Vining plant, great for shelves or hanging
- Tolerates low light and missed waterings

<Card title="Care cheat sheet">
| Plant | Water | Light | Fertilizer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snake | Every 2–4 weeks | Low to bright indirect | Twice a year |
| Pothos | When top 2 cm dry | Low to medium indirect | Monthly in spring/summer |
| ZZ | Every 3–4 weeks | Low to medium indirect | Twice a year |
| Spider | When top 2 cm dry | Bright indirect | Monthly in spring/summer |
| Cast Iron | Every 2–3 weeks | Low to deep shade | Twice a year |

<Callout tone="info" title="Signs you're overwatering">
Yellow, mushy leaves and soggy soil. Let the top 2–3 cm dry out before watering again — for snake and ZZ plants, let the soil dry completely.
</Callout>

<Card title="Learn the basics">
<Details title="How to water properly" open=false>
Water thoroughly until it drains out the bottom, then let the pot fully drain. Never let the pot sit in standing water. For snake and ZZ plants, wait until the soil is completely dry. For pothos and spider plants, wait until the top 2 cm are dry.
</Details>
<Details title="What about fertilizer?" open=false>
Feed during spring and summer only — once a month for fast growers, twice a year for snake and ZZ. Skip fertilizing in winter when growth slows.
</Details>
<Details title="What if leaves turn brown?" open=false>
Brown, crispy tips usually mean underwatering or too much fertilizer. Brown, mushy spots mean overwatering. Trim damaged leaves with clean scissors.
</Details>
</Card>
