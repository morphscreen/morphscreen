# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae, and some bacteria use to turn sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into food (glucose) and oxygen. It's the foundation of almost all life on Earth.

<Card title="The Big Picture">
Photosynthesis happens in two main stages, working together like a factory:

1. **Light-dependent reactions**: Capture sunlight and split water to make energy (ATP and NADPH) and release oxygen.
2. **Calvin cycle (light-independent)**: Use that energy to turn carbon dioxide into glucose.

The overall equation is simple:

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />

**In words:** Carbon dioxide + water + sunlight → glucose + oxygen.
</Card>

<Card title="Where It Happens">
Photosynthesis occurs in the **chloroplasts** of plant cells, especially in the leaves.

<Card title="Step-by-Step: The Light Reactions">
These happen in the thylakoid membranes when sunlight hits the leaf.

<Steps>
<Step title="Light hits chlorophyll" status="current" subtitle="Energy capture">
Chlorophyll absorbs photons, exciting electrons to a higher energy state.
</Step>
<Step title="Water is split" status="todo" subtitle="Oxygen release">
Enzymes split water (H₂O) into oxygen (released as waste), protons, and electrons.
</Step>
<Step title="Electron transport chain" status="todo" subtitle="Energy conversion">
Excited electrons pass down protein complexes, pumping protons across the membrane.
</Step>
<Step title="ATP and NADPH formed" status="todo" subtitle="Chemical energy">
The proton gradient powers ATP synthase to make ATP; electrons reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Oxygen comes from water, not CO₂">
The oxygen we breathe comes from splitting water, not from carbon dioxide. This was proven with radioactive isotope experiments in the 1940s.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Step-by-Step: The Calvin Cycle">
These reactions happen in the stroma and don't need light directly (though they need ATP/NADPH from light reactions).

<Steps>
<Step title="Carbon fixation" status="current" subtitle="CO₂ captured">
An enzyme called RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to a 5-carbon sugar (RuBP), forming a 6-carbon molecule that splits into two 3-carbon molecules (3-PGA).
</Step>
<Step title="Reduction" status="todo" subtitle="Building sugar">
ATP and NADPH from light reactions convert 3-PGA into G3P, a sugar phosphate.
</Step>
<Step title="Regeneration" status="todo" subtitle="RuBP rebuilt">
Most G3P is used to regenerate RuBP, so the cycle can continue. One G3P exits per three turns to make glucose.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="What the Plant Produces">
For every **6 molecules of CO₂** fixed, the plant makes:

- **1 glucose** (C₆H₁₂O₆) — used for energy or stored as starch
- **6 oxygen** (O₂) — released into the air

<Chart type="bar" title="Products per 6 CO₂ molecules" x="Product" y="Molecules">
| Product | Molecules |
|---|---|
| Glucose | 1 |
| Oxygen | 6 |
| ATP used | 18 |
| NADPH used | 12 |
</Chart>

<Callout tone="success" title="Why it matters">
Almost all food chains start here. Herbivores eat plants, carnivores eat herbivores — and the energy all traces back to photosynthesis. It also produces the oxygen in every breath you take.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Misconceptions">
| Myth | Truth |
|---|---|
| Plants "breathe" CO₂ at night | Plants respire 24/7, but photosynthesis only happens in light |
| Photosynthesis = respiration in reverse | They use different pathways and organelles |
| Chlorophyll is green because it reflects green light | Correct! It absorbs red and blue best, reflecting green |
</Card>
