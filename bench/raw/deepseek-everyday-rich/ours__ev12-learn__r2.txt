# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae, and some bacteria use to turn sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into food (glucose) and oxygen. It's the foundation of almost all life on Earth.

<Card title="The Big Picture">
<Columns>
<div>
**Overall equation:**
6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

**In words:** Carbon dioxide + water + sunlight → glucose + oxygen

**Where it happens:** In the chloroplasts of plant cells, mainly in the leaves.
</div>

## The Two Main Stages

<Card title="Light-Dependent Reactions (Thylakoid Membranes)">
These reactions need sunlight and happen in the thylakoid membranes inside chloroplasts.

<Steps>
<Step title="Light is absorbed" status="done" subtitle="Photosystem II">
Chlorophyll molecules in the thylakoid membrane absorb photons of light, exciting electrons to a higher energy level.
</Step>
<Step title="Water is split" status="done" subtitle="Photolysis">
The excited electrons are passed to an electron transport chain. To replace them, water molecules are split: 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻. Oxygen gas is released as a byproduct.
</Step>
<Step title="ATP is made" status="done" subtitle="Chemiosmosis">
As electrons move down the transport chain, protons (H⁺) are pumped into the thylakoid space. They flow back out through ATP synthase, which uses their energy to make ATP from ADP + Pᵢ.
</Step>
<Step title="NADPH is produced" status="done" subtitle="Photosystem I">
The electrons are re-energized by another light absorption and passed to NADP⁺, reducing it to NADPH.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Outputs:** ATP, NADPH, O₂ (released)
</Card>

<Card title="Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)">
These reactions don't need light directly — they use the ATP and NADPH from the previous stage. They happen in the stroma (the fluid filling the chloroplast).

<Steps>
<Step title="Carbon fixation" status="done" subtitle="Rubisco enzyme">
CO₂ is attached to a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP) by the enzyme rubisco, forming two 3-carbon molecules (3-PGA).
</Step>
<Step title="Reduction" status="done">
ATP and NADPH from the light reactions donate energy and electrons to convert 3-PGA into G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate), a sugar.
</Step>
<Step title="Regeneration" status="done">
Most G3P molecules are used to regenerate RuBP, keeping the cycle going. One G3P molecule exits per cycle to build glucose and other sugars.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Outputs:** Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), regenerated RuBP
</Card>

## What Happens to the Products?

<Card title="Glucose & Oxygen Fate">
<Columns>
<div>
**Glucose is used for:**
- Cellular respiration (energy)
- Building cellulose (cell walls)
- Starch storage (roots, seeds)
- Sucrose transport (through the plant)
</div>
<div>
**Oxygen is released:**
- Through stomata (pores in leaves)
- Into the atmosphere
- Used by animals and plants for respiration
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<div>
**Key points:**
- **Light intensity:** Rate increases until saturation — then it plateaus.
- **CO₂ concentration:** Same pattern — more CO₂ helps until a limit.
- **Temperature:** Rate rises with temperature until about 30°C, then falls as enzymes denature.
- **Water:** Lack of water closes stomata, reducing CO₂ intake.
</div>

## Check Your Understanding

<Quiz title="Photosynthesis Quiz">
<Question text="Where do the light-dependent reactions occur?" answer="Thylakoid membranes">
- Thylakoid membranes
- Stroma
- Mitochondria
- Cytoplasm
**Explanation:** The light reactions happen in the thylakoid membranes where chlorophyll and the electron transport chain are located.
</Question>
<Question text="What is the main purpose of splitting water in photosynthesis?" answer="To replace electrons lost by chlorophyll">
- To produce oxygen for animals
- To replace electrons lost by chlorophyll
- To create ATP directly
- To make glucose
**Explanation:** Water splitting (photolysis) replaces the electrons that chlorophyll donates to the electron transport chain. Oxygen is a byproduct, not the goal.
</Question>
<Question text="Which product of the light reactions is used directly in the Calvin cycle?" answer="ATP and NADPH">
- Oxygen and glucose
- ATP and NADPH
- Water and CO₂
- RuBP and G3P
**Explanation:** The Calvin cycle uses ATP for energy and NADPH for reducing power to fix CO₂ into sugar.
</Question>
<Question text="What is the role of rubisco?" answer="It fixes CO₂ onto RuBP">
- It splits water molecules
- It fixes CO₂ onto RuBP
- It makes ATP
- It transports electrons
**Explanation:** Rubisco catalyzes the first step of carbon fixation, attaching CO₂ to RuBP. It's the most abundant enzyme on Earth.
</Question>
</Quiz>
