# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system has eight planets orbiting the Sun, split into two groups: the four rocky **terrestrial planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the four gas/ice **giants** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Here's a tour, starting from the Sun.

## The Giant Planets (Outer Solar System)

**What it's like:** Jupiter is so massive that all other planets could fit inside it twice over. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. It has 95 known moons, including Europa, which hides a subsurface ocean that might host life. Its magnetic field is 20,000× Earth's.

It's an ice giant, with a mantle of water, methane, and ammonia. Its methane gives it a cyan colour. It's the coldest planet, with the most extreme seasons: each pole gets 42 years of daylight, then 42 years of darkness.

## What about Pluto?

<Callout tone="info" title="Pluto is a dwarf planet">
Pluto was reclassified in 2006 because it hasn't "cleared its neighbourhood" of other objects. It's now one of five known dwarf planets (with Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake). It's smaller than Earth's Moon and has a heart-shaped glacier.
</Callout>
