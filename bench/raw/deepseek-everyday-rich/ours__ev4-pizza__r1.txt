# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making a true Neapolitan pizza (VPN-certified style) at home is absolutely possible with the right technique and a few key tools. The goal is a soft, puffy, leopard-spotted crust with a wet, saucy center — not a crispy New York slice.

<Card title="The 4 Pillars of Neapolitan Pizza">
<Columns>
<div>
<Badge tone="info">1. Dough</Badge>
**00 flour, water, salt, yeast** — nothing else. High hydration (60-65%), long fermentation.
</div>
<div>
<Badge tone="info">2. Sauce</Badge>
**San Marzano tomatoes, salt, basil** — uncooked, crushed by hand.
</div>
<div>
<Badge tone="info">3. Cheese</Badge>
**Fresh mozzarella di bufala** (or fior di latte), torn, not shredded.
</div>
<div>
<Badge tone="info">4. Heat</Badge>
**485°C (900°F) for 60-90 seconds** — a wood-fired oven ideally, but a home oven hack works.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="The Dough Recipe" subtitle="Makes 4 x 250g pizzas">
<KeyValue title="Ingredients">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Caputo 00 flour (red bag) | 500g |
| Water (room temp) | 320g (64% hydration) |
| Fine sea salt | 10g |
| Fresh yeast | 2g (or 0.7g dry) |

**Method:**
1. Dissolve yeast in water.
2. Add 70% of flour, mix until shaggy. Rest 20 min (autolyse).
3. Add salt and remaining flour. Knead 10-15 min until smooth and elastic.
4. Bulk ferment 2 hours at room temp (24°C).
5. Divide into 4 balls, proof 8-24 hours in fridge (covered).
6. Take out 1 hour before baking — never roll, only stretch by hand.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Key tip">
The dough should be **wet and sticky** — resist adding extra flour. A slightly wetter dough gives the signature airy cornicione.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The Sauce & Assembly">
<Columns>
<div>
**Sauce (uncooked):**
- Crush 400g San Marzano tomatoes by hand
- Add 10g fine salt and 5-6 fresh basil leaves
- Let stand 30 min before using

**Assembly order:**
1. Stretch dough to 10-12" disc, thicker at edges
2. Ladle 80-100g sauce in center, spread in a spiral, leaving 1" border
3. Tear 100g mozzarella, distribute evenly
4. Drizzle extra-virgin olive oil
5. Bake immediately
</div>

<Card title="Baking Without a Wood Oven">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Home Oven Hack">
<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat" status="done" subtitle="30 min">Place a baking steel or 12mm thick cast-iron plate on the top rack. Heat oven to max (250-275°C) for at least 45 min. Turn on grill/broiler for the last 10 min.</Step>
<Step title="Launch" status="done" subtitle="90 seconds">Slide pizza onto the steel with a floured peel. Bake 4-5 min total, rotating halfway. The top should get char spots — if not, finish under the grill for 30 sec.</Step>
<Step title="Rest" status="current" subtitle="2 min">Let rest on a wire rack (not a plate — steam ruins the crust). Serve immediately.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Ooni / Roccbox (Portable)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Heat" status="done" subtitle="20 min">Fire up to 450-500°C on gas or charcoal. Wait until the stone is smoking hot.</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="current" subtitle="60-90 sec">Launch and turn every 15-20 sec with a turning peel. Look for leopard spots and puffed rim — that's done.</Step>
<Step title="Serve" status="todo">Slice and eat within 3 minutes — no plates needed.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout tone="success" title="The steel trick">
A baking steel retains heat far better than a stone. If you only have a stone, preheat it for a full hour and use the grill for the last minute to get char.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Checklist title="Check before you bake" name="pizza-check">
- [ ] Dough too cold — let it come to room temp (1 hour)
- [ ] Too much sauce — the center should be wet but not soupy
- [ ] Overworked dough — stretch gently, don't press the rim
- [ ] Oven not hot enough — steel must be screaming hot
- [ ] Cutting too soon — wait 2 minutes so the cheese sets
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="What You'll Need">
| Tool | Why | Budget option |
|---|---|---|
| 00 flour (Caputo) | Fine grind, high protein for elasticity | Any Italian 00 flour |
| Baking steel | Conducts heat like a stone oven | Cast-iron skillet inverted |
| Wooden peel | Launching without sticking | Cardboard sheet dusted with semolina |
| Infrared thermometer | Check steel temp (must be 250°C+) | Oven thermometer |
| San Marzano tomatoes | Sweet, low acidity | Any whole peeled Italian tomatoes |

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">
Use **semolina flour** (not flour) on the peel — it rolls like ball bearings and prevents sticking. Never use oil on the peel.
</Callout>
