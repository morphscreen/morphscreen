## The 5 key questions that decide it

<Steps>
<Step title="1. Can you save a deposit?" status="current" subtitle="You need at least 5–10% of the price">
Most lenders want 10% (£30k on a £300k flat). If you can save £1,000/month, that's 30 months. If you can only save £500/month, it's 5 years — renting might make more sense in the meantime.
</Step>
<Step title="2. How long will you stay?" status="todo" subtitle="The golden rule: 5+ years makes buying worth it">
Buying costs 2–5% of the price upfront (stamp duty, solicitor, surveys). You need to stay long enough for equity growth and savings to beat those costs. Under 3 years? Renting usually wins.
</Step>
<Step title="3. Can you handle the risk?" status="todo" subtitle="House prices can fall; repairs happen">
If prices drop 10%, a 10% deposit is wiped out. Can you sleep at night knowing your flat could be worth less than you paid?
</Step>
<Step title="4. What's the rent-vs-buy ratio in your area?" status="todo" subtitle="A rough rule: if annual rent < 15× the price, buy">
Your rent is £21,600/year. A flat priced at £300k gives a ratio of 13.9× — that favours buying. If flats in your area are £450k, the ratio is 20.8× — renting looks better.
</Step>
<Step title="5. What does your life look like in 5 years?" status="todo" subtitle="Job changes, relationships, family plans?">
If you might move cities, get married, or change jobs, renting gives you freedom. If you're settled, buying builds wealth.
</Step>
</Steps>

## What to do next

<Card title="If you lean toward buying">
<Checklist title="Your buying checklist" name="buying-checklist">
- [ ] Save a deposit (aim for 10%+)
- [ ] Check your credit score (free via Experian, Equifax)
- [ ] Get an Agreement in Principle from a mortgage broker
- [ ] Research areas — what do flats sell for?
- [ ] Factor in £3k–£8k of buying costs
- [ ] Get a survey before you commit
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="If you lean toward renting">
<Checklist title="Your renting checklist" name="renting-checklist">
- [ ] Consider a cheaper place to free up cash
- [ ] Invest the difference (e.g. index funds, ISA)
- [ ] Negotiate your renewal — rents often drop in winter
- [ ] Build an emergency fund (3–6 months of expenses)
</Checklist>
</Card>

## A final word

<Card title="The honest truth">
<Callout tone="neutral" title="There's no perfect answer">
Buying isn't always better — it depends on your timeline, your area, and your risk tolerance. At your income, though, £1,800/month rent is a lot. If you can bring that down or convert it into a mortgage payment, you'll have much more money working for you.
