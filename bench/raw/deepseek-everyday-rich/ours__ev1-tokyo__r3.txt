# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two — The Perfect Couple's Itinerary

Tokyo is a city that rewards slow mornings, long walks, and shared discoveries. This itinerary balances iconic sights with hidden gems, leaving room for spontaneity — because some of the best Tokyo moments happen when you get lost together.

<Callout tone="info" title="Best time to visit">
Spring (late March–April) for cherry blossoms or autumn (October–November) for crisp air and fall colors. Avoid Golden Week (late April–early May) when the city is packed.
</Callout>

## Day 1: Arrival & Shinjuku — Neon Lights and First Impressions

<Card title="Morning: Settle In">
Land at Narita or Haneda, take the Narita Express or limousine bus to Shinjuku. Check into your hotel, drop bags, and grab a casual lunch at a **ramen shop** — try Ichiran for solo-booth fun or Afuri for yuzu-shio ramen.
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Explore Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho">
Wander through the labyrinth of tiny bars in **Golden Gai** (over 200 micro-bars). Then head to **Omoide Yokocho** ("Memory Lane") — a smoky alley of yakitori stalls. Perfect for a late-afternoon snack of grilled skewers and beer.

<Gallery>
![Golden Gai's narrow alleys lined with tiny bars glowing at dusk](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1540959733332-eab4deabeeaf?w=800)
![Yakitori grilling over charcoal at Omoide Yokocho](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517248135467-4c7edcad34c4?w=800)
</Gallery>
</Card>

<Card title="Evening: Sky-High Views & Izakaya Dinner">
Head to the **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building** — free observation deck with stunning sunset views over the city. For dinner, book a seat at a local izakaya like **Zauo Shinjuku** (where you can fish your own dinner!) or simply wander into any lively spot.

## Day 2: Asakusa & Sumida — Old Town Charm and Digital Art

<Card title="Morning: Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise Street">
Start early at **Senso-ji**, Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the iconic Kaminarimon Gate, browse Nakamise-dori's food stalls (try freshly baked ningyo-yaki), and draw a fortune (omikuji) together.

<Gallery>
![Senso-ji Temple's five-story pagoda and main hall in morning light](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1528360983277-13d401cdc186?w=800)
![Nakamise shopping street leading to Senso-ji with colorful stalls](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1526481280648-69b3b0b6a1a3?w=800)
</Gallery>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Sumida River Cruise & teamLab Planets">
Take a **river cruise** down the Sumida River to Toyosu (about 40 minutes, great views of Tokyo Skytree). Then immerse yourselves in **teamLab Planets** — a sensory digital art museum where you walk through water and light. Book tickets in advance!

<Button ask="How do we book teamLab Planets tickets?">Get booking details</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Evening: Skytree at Night">
Watch the sunset from **Tokyo Skytree** (350m and 450m observation decks). For dinner, explore the **Solamachi** complex at its base — from conveyor-belt sushi to upscale teppanyaki.

## Day 3: Shibuya & Harajuku — Youth Culture and Trendy Eats

<Card title="Morning: Meiji Shrine & Harajuku">
Start with a serene walk through the forested grounds of **Meiji Shrine** — a tranquil escape from the city. Then explore **Harajuku's** Takeshita Street for quirky fashion and crepes. For a quieter vibe, stroll down **Cat Street** with its vintage shops and indie cafes.

<Gallery>
![Meiji Shrine's massive torii gate in a forested setting](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1524413840807-0c3cb6fa808d?w=800)
![Colorful crepe stalls along Takeshita Street in Harajuku](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517639493569-c5b2f2b7c5e4?w=800)
</Gallery>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Shibuya Scramble & Observation">
Experience the famous **Shibuya Crossing** — watch the chaos from the Starbucks second floor or the new **Shibuya Sky** observation deck (book sunset slots!). Grab lunch at **Uobei Sushi** (conveyor belt with high-speed delivery) or **Miyashita Park** food hall.

<Button ask="What's the best time to see Shibuya Crossing without huge crowds?">Get timing tips</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Evening: Dinner & Nightlife in Shibuya">
For dinner, try **Ushigoro** for premium yakiniku (Japanese BBQ) or **Tapas Molecular Bar** for a unique fine-dining experience. End the night at a **karaoke bar** — private rooms for two are perfect for a fun, silly sing-along.

## Day 4: Day Trip to Hakone — Mt. Fuji and Onsen Romance

<Card title="Morning: Scenic Train & Cable Car">
Take the **Odakyu Romancecar** from Shinjuku to Hakone (about 90 minutes). Then ride the **Hakone Tozan Railway** (switchback train), **cable car**, and **ropeway** for jaw-dropping views of Mt. Fuji and the volcanic valley of Owakudani — try the black eggs (they're said to extend your life by 7 years!).

<Gallery>
![Mt. Fuji towering over Lake Ashi in Hakone with clear blue sky](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1490806843957-31f4c9a91c65?w=800)
![Hakone ropeway gondolas crossing over volcanic terrain](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1533587851505-d119e13fa0d7?w=800)
</Gallery>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Lake Ashi Cruise & Onsen">
Take a **pirate ship cruise** across Lake Ashi, with Mt. Fuji as your backdrop. Then visit **Hakone Shrine** — the iconic red torii gate in the water. End your day with a soak in a **traditional onsen** (hot spring). Many ryokan offer day-use baths.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Onsen etiquette">
Full nudity is required, but there are separate men's and women's baths. If you're shy, book a private onsen (family bath) for two — many ryokan offer them.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Evening: Ryokan Dinner & Stay">
For the full experience, stay overnight at a **ryokan** (traditional inn) like Gora Kadan or Hakone Suishoen. Enjoy a multi-course kaiseki dinner in your room, sleep on futons, and wake up to a mountain view.

## Day 5: Ueno & Departure — Culture and Last-Minute Souvenirs

<Card title="Morning: Ueno Park & Museums">
Spend your final morning in **Ueno Park** — home to several world-class museums. Pick one or two: the **Tokyo National Museum** (Japanese art and history) or the **National Museum of Western Art**. Stroll the park's pond and visit the **Benten-do shrine**.

<Gallery>
![Ueno Park's Shinobazu Pond with lotus leaves in summer](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1551817958-4a4e2a6d8a8e?w=800)
![Tokyo National Museum's main hall with traditional Japanese architecture](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1604908176997-125f25cc6f3d?w=800)
</Gallery>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Ameya-Yokocho & Departure">
Explore **Ameya-Yokocho** market street — a chaotic, fun stretch of food stalls, clothing shops, and souvenir stands. Grab a final bowl of ramen or a monjayaki (Tokyo-style savory pancake) before heading to the airport.

## Practical Tips for Your Trip

<Card title="Getting Around">
| Transport | Best For | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Suica/Pasmo IC card** | All trains, buses, vending machines | ¥500 deposit + top-up |
| **Tokyo Metro 24/48/72-hour pass** | Unlimited subway rides | ¥800–¥1,500 |
| **JR Pass** | Only if traveling beyond Tokyo (e.g., Kyoto) | From ¥29,650 for 7 days |
| **Taxis** | Late nights, or when tired | ¥410 starting fare |

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">
Download **Google Maps** or **Japan Travel by NAVITIME** — they work perfectly for train routes and walking directions. Always carry cash; many small shops and restaurants don't accept cards.
</Callout>
</Card>
