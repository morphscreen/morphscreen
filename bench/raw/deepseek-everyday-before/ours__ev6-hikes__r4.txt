# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

<Badge tone="info">Curated for all fitness levels</Badge>

Vancouver is a hiker's paradise, with world-class trails within an hour's drive of downtown. Here are the best day hikes, from easy coastal strolls to challenging summit pushes.

<Card title="1. Quarry Rock (Deep Cove)" subtitle="Best for: Families, beginners, and quick afternoon escapes">
<Columns>
<div>
![Quarry Rock viewpoint overlooking Indian Arm](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1506905925346-21bda4d32df4)

**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Distance:** 3.8 km return  
**Elevation gain:** 100 m  
**Time:** 1.5–2 hours  
**Best season:** Year-round (muddy in winter)

This iconic North Shore trail starts at the Deep Cove parking lot and winds through lush coastal rainforest before opening to a breathtaking viewpoint over Indian Arm. The trail is well-maintained with boardwalks and stairs. Go early on weekends to avoid crowds and grab a famous Honey's Doughnuts afterward.
</div>
<div>
<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Panorama Drive, Deep Cove |
| Parking | Free lot (fills by 8 AM on weekends) |
| Dog-friendly | Yes (on leash) |
| Facilities | Washrooms at trailhead |
| Cell service | Mostly available |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Combine with a kayak rental in Deep Cove for a full day of adventure.</Callout>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="2. Stawamus Chief (Squamish)" subtitle="Best for: Experienced hikers and summit seekers">
<Columns>
<div>
![Stawamus Chief granite cliff face](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1519681393784-d120267933ba)

**Difficulty:** Moderate to Hard (choose your peak)  
**Distance:** 4–11 km return (depending on peak)  
**Elevation gain:** 500–700 m  
**Time:** 4–6 hours  
**Best season:** May–October (chain sections icy in winter)

The Chief is a massive granite monolith with three summits. First Peak offers the quickest reward; Second Peak is the most popular with the classic viewpoint; Third Peak is the most challenging. Expect steep sections with chains bolted into rock for safety. The view over Howe Sound and the surrounding peaks is unforgettable.
</div>
<div>
<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Shannon Falls parking lot |
| Parking | Free lot + overflow |
| Dog-friendly | No (chains and steep drops) |
| Facilities | Washrooms at trailhead |
| Cell service | Intermittent |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Safety note">The "Backside Trail" to the Chief is more difficult than the front side. Stick to the main route unless you're experienced with scrambling.</Callout>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="3. Joffre Lakes (Pemberton)" subtitle="Best for: Turquoise alpine lakes and Instagram-worthy views">
<Columns>
<div>
![Joffre Lakes turquoise water](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1500530855697-b586d89ba3ee)

**Difficulty:** Moderate  
**Distance:** 10 km return  
**Elevation gain:** 400 m  
**Time:** 3–5 hours  
**Best season:** July–October (snow lingers late)

Three glacially-fed lakes connected by a scenic trail. The colour of the water — a striking turquoise from glacial flour — is the star. The trail climbs steadily but gently, with the final stretch to the third lake being the steepest. Camping is available at the upper lake for overnight trips, but day hiking is equally rewarding.
</div>
<div>
<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Joffre Lakes parking lot (off Hwy 99) |
| Parking | Free, but fills by 8 AM |
| Dog-friendly | No (wildlife area) |
| Facilities | Pit toilets at trailhead and upper lake |
| Cell service | None after trailhead |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="success" title="Best time to go">Weekdays before 9 AM are blissfully quiet. The lakes are most vivid in July and August.</Callout>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="4. Garibaldi Lake" subtitle="Best for: Big alpine scenery without a summit push">
<Columns>
<div>
![Garibaldi Lake with mountain backdrop](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1519681393784-d120267933ba)

**Difficulty:** Moderate to Hard  
**Distance:** 18 km return  
**Elevation gain:** 820 m  
**Time:** 5–7 hours  
**Best season:** July–October (snow-free)

The trail climbs steadily through old-growth forest before opening to the vast turquoise Garibaldi Lake, ringed by jagged peaks and the Battleship Islands. The switchbacks are relentless but the payoff is immense. For a longer day, continue to Panorama Ridge for a 360° view of the region.
</div>
<div>
<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Rubble Creek parking lot |
| Parking | Free, fills early on weekends |
| Dog-friendly | Yes (on leash) |
| Facilities | Pit toilets at lake |
| Cell service | None after trailhead |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Bring layers — it's often 10°C cooler at the lake than in Vancouver.</Callout>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="5. The Grouse Grind" subtitle="Best for: Fitness buffs and quick summit rewards">
<Columns>
<div>
![Grouse Mountain summit view](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1500530855697-b586d89ba3ee)

**Difficulty:** Hard (it's a stairmaster in nature)  
**Distance:** 2.9 km one-way  
**Elevation gain:** 853 m  
**Time:** 1.5–2.5 hours up (gondola down)  
**Best season:** May–October (closed in winter)

Nicknamed "Mother Nature's Stairmaster," this trail is a relentless climb of 2,830 steps. It's not the most scenic hike — it's about the workout and the bragging rights. At the top, you'll find the Grouse Mountain resort with restaurants, wildlife refuges, and a free gondola ride down (included with your trail pass).
</div>
<div>
<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Grouse Mountain base |
| Parking | Paid lot ($8–10) |
| Dog-friendly | No |
| Facilities | Washrooms at base and summit |
| Cell service | Available |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Know before you go">You must purchase a "Grind Pass" (about $25) that includes the gondola descent. No refunds if you quit early — the gondola is the only way down.</Callout>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Essential Hiking Tips">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check trail conditions" status="done" subtitle="Parks BC website + AllTrails reviews">
Always check for closures, snow, or wildlife warnings before heading out.
</Step>
<Step title="Pack the 10 Essentials" status="done" subtitle="Even for short hikes">
Navigation, headlamp, sun protection, first aid, knife, fire starter, shelter, food, water, and extra layers.
</Step>
<Step title="Start early" status="done" subtitle="Before 8 AM on weekends">
Parking lots fill fast and afternoon thunderstorms are common in the mountains.
</Step>
<Step title="Leave no trace" status="done" subtitle="Pack out everything">
Pack out everything, stay on trail, and give wildlife space.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
