# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon is a seafood lover's paradise with deep-rooted culinary traditions. Here’s your essential food tour.

<Card title="The Must-Try Dishes">
<Columns>
<Card title="Seafood & Salt Cod">
- **Bacalhau à Brás** — shredded salt cod, onions, and matchstick potatoes bound with eggs. Try at **Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau** (a dedicated cod pastry spot) or **Cervejaria Ramiro** (famous for shellfish).
- **Sardinhas Assadas** — grilled sardines, best during the June festivals. Head to **A Tasca do Alfredo** or a local tasca in Alfama.
- **Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato** — clams in garlic, olive oil, and coriander. **Ramiro** and **Solar 31 Alfama** excel here.
</Card>
<Card title="Pastries & Sweets">
- **Pastel de Nata** — the iconic custard tart. The gold standard is **Pastéis de Belém** (since 1837). For a modern twist, try **Manteigaria** or **Casa Pasteis de Nata**.
- **Bolo de Bolacha** — a no-bake biscuit cake with coffee and buttercream, found at **Confeitaria Nacional**.
- **Ginjinha** — a sour cherry liqueur, sipped at **A Ginjinha** near Rossio.
</Card>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<Card title="Hearty Comfort Food">
- **Bifana** — a spicy pulled-pork sandwich. The legendary spot is **Casa Bifanas** at Rossio station.
- **Francesinha** — a layered sandwich with ham, sausage, steak, melted cheese, and a tomato-beer sauce. Though from Porto, **Café de São Bento** serves a solid Lisbon version.
- **Caldo Verde** — kale and potato soup with chouriço. Try at **A Cevicheria** (they do a superb version) or any traditional tasca.
</Card>
<Card title="Where to Eat Like a Local">
- **Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market)** — curated stalls from top chefs like **Henrique Sá Pessoa** and **Marlene Vieira**. Great for variety.
- **Alfama** — wander the alleys for tiny family-run tascas. Look for places packed with locals, like **Clube de Fãs** or **Cantinho do Aziz** (Afro-Lisbon fusion).
- **Cervejaria Ramiro** — a no-frills institution for giant tiger prawns, crab, and garlic bread. Arrive early; it’s always crowded.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Local Tip">
Don’t ask for “pasteis de nata” at Pastéis de Belém — the original recipe is called **Pastéis de Belém** there. Also, lunch is served from 12:00–15:00 and dinner often starts at 19:30; many tascas close on Sundays.
</Callout>

<Card title="Budget & Booking">
- **Budget eats**: Bifanas (~€4), pastel de nata (~€1.50), and a caldo verde (~€8) will keep you full for under €15.
- **Mid-range**: A seafood lunch at Ramiro runs €30–45 per person.
- **Fine dining**: Try **Belcanto** (2 Michelin stars) for a modern tasting menu (~€150+).
- **Reservations**: Essential for Ramiro, Solar 31 Alfama, and Belcanto. Book 2–3 days ahead for weekends.

<Card title="Dietary Notes">
- **Vegetarians**: The vegetable-focused tasca **A Cevicheria** (despite the name) and **O Asiático** offer creative meat-free options. Caldo verde and pastéis de nata are safe bets.
- **Vegans**: **Terra** and **Ao 26 – Vegan Food Project** are excellent. Ask for “sem carne” (without meat) at traditional spots.
- **Gluten-free**: Grilled fish and seafood are naturally GF. Watch out for flour in the francesinha sauce and bifana bread.
