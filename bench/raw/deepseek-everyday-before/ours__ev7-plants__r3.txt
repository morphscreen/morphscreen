# Which houseplants are hard to kill?

<Callout tone="success" title="Good news">
You don't need a green thumb to enjoy a thriving indoor jungle. These plants forgive missed waterings, low light, and general neglect — perfect for beginners or busy people.
</Callout>

<Card title="The best low-maintenance houseplants">
<Columns>
<Card title="🌿 Snake Plant (Sansevieria)">
- Tolerates weeks without water
- Thrives in low light or bright light
- Purifies air (NASA study)
- Grows slowly, rarely needs repotting
</Card>
<Card title="🪴 Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)">
- Droops when thirsty, perks up after watering
- Grows in almost any light (except direct sun)
- Easy to propagate from cuttings
- Trails beautifully from shelves or hanging pots
</Card>
</Columns>
<Columns>
<Card title="🍃 ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas)">
- Survives months of drought
- Handles low light and fluorescent office light
- Waxy leaves store water
- Very pest-resistant
</Card>
<Card title="🌱 Spider Plant">
- Produces baby "spiderettes" you can replant
- Tolerates irregular watering
- Prefers bright, indirect light
- Safe for pets (unlike many others)
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Why these are so forgiving">
<KeyValue title="Survival traits">
| Trait | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Thick, succulent-like leaves | Store water for dry weeks |
| Slow growth | Need fewer nutrients and less repotting |
| Native to understory | Adapted to low indoor light |
| Dormant growth in winter | Rest naturally, so less care needed |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Simple care rules for any of these">
<Steps>
<Step title="Water less than you think" status="current">
Stick a finger 2 inches into the soil. If it's dry, water. If damp, wait. Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose the right spot">
Most of these tolerate low light, but they'll look better with bright, indirect light — near a window but not in direct sun.
</Step>
<Step title="Use well-draining soil">
A cactus or succulent mix works for snake and ZZ plants. For pothos and spider plants, any all-purpose potting mix is fine.
</Step>
<Step title="Fertilize sparingly">
Once every 2-3 months during spring and summer is plenty. Skip fertilizing in winter.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="What to avoid">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Overwatering is the #1 killer">
These plants are drought-tolerant, not aquatic. Yellow, mushy leaves mean too much water. Always let the soil dry out between waterings.
</Callout>

- **Direct hot sunlight** — can scorch leaves, especially for pothos and spider plants
- **Cold drafts** — keep away from drafty windows in winter
- **Water with fluoride** — spider plants are sensitive; use filtered water if tips turn brown
</Card>

<Card title="Signs your plant is actually struggling">
| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow, soft leaves | Overwatering | Let soil dry out completely, reduce frequency |
| Brown, crispy edges | Underwatering or dry air | Water more, mist occasionally |
| Pale, stretched growth | Too little light | Move closer to a window |
| Leaves drooping | Needs water (pothos) | Water — it'll perk up within hours |
</Card>

<Card title="Where to start">
<Callout tone="info" title="Best first plant">
Begin with a **Pothos** — it gives clear signals when thirsty (droopy leaves) and bounces back quickly. It's also the easiest to propagate, so you can grow your collection for free.
</Callout>
