# Houseplants That Are Hard to Kill

Whether you're a beginner or just busy, these resilient houseplants thrive on neglect. Here's a guide to the most forgiving green companions.

<Card title="Top 10 Unkillable Houseplants">
<Columns>
<div>

### 1. Snake Plant (*Sansevieria*)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 2–4 weeks; let soil dry completely
- **Why it survives:** Tolerates drought, low light, and irregular watering
- **Bonus:** Purifies air, releases oxygen at night

### 2. ZZ Plant (*Zamioculcas zamiifolia*)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 3–4 weeks
- **Why it survives:** Stores water in rhizomes; shrugs off neglect
- **Bonus:** Glossy, architectural leaves

### 3. Pothos (*Epipremnum aureum*)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** When top 2" of soil is dry
- **Why it survives:** Fast-growing, forgiving of missed waterings
- **Bonus:** Great in hanging baskets or trailing shelves

</div>
<div>

### 4. Spider Plant (*Chlorophytum comosum*)
- **Light:** Bright indirect
- **Water:** When top inch is dry
- **Why it survives:** Produces baby plants, tolerates average humidity
- **Bonus:** Pet-safe and easy to propagate

### 5. Aloe Vera
- **Light:** Bright, indirect to direct sun
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; let soil dry fully
- **Why it survives:** Succulent leaves store water
- **Bonus:** Gel soothes minor burns

### 6. Cast Iron Plant (*Aspidistra elatior*)
- **Light:** Very low to medium
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks
- **Why it survives:** Lives up to its name—nearly indestructible
- **Bonus:** Handles cold drafts and dim corners

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="More Resilient Options">
<Columns>
<div>

### 7. Jade Plant (*Crassula ovata*)
- **Light:** Bright, direct sun
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; let soil dry
- **Why it survives:** Succulent stems and leaves store water
- **Bonus:** Can live for decades

### 8. Peace Lily (*Spathiphyllum*)
- **Light:** Low to medium indirect
- **Water:** When leaves droop slightly
- **Why it survives:** Dramatic drooping reminds you to water
- **Bonus:** Beautiful white blooms, air purifier

</div>
<div>

### 9. Philodendron (Heartleaf)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** When top inch is dry
- **Why it survives:** Trailing vines tolerate missed waterings
- **Bonus:** Fast-growing, easy to propagate

### 10. Chinese Evergreen (*Aglaonema*)
- **Light:** Low to medium indirect
- **Water:** Every 1–2 weeks
- **Why it survives:** Tolerates low light and dry air
- **Bonus:** Colorful variegated varieties available

</div>

<Card title="Care Tips for Success">
<Steps>
<Step title="Choose the right pot" status="done">
Use pots with drainage holes. Terracotta pots wick away excess moisture, helping prevent root rot.
</Step>
<Step title="Water less, not more" status="done">
Most of these plants prefer dry soil. Stick your finger 2 inches deep—only water if it feels dry. When in doubt, wait.
</Step>
<Step title="Match light to plant" status="done">
Snake plants and ZZ plants tolerate dark corners. Pothos and spider plants prefer brighter spots but adapt to less.
</Step>
<Step title="Skip the fertilizer" status="done">
These plants rarely need feeding. If you want, apply a balanced liquid fertilizer at half strength once in spring and once in summer.
</Step>
<Step title="Ignore them a little" status="done">
The biggest killer of these plants is overwatering. If you forget about them for a week or two, they'll thank you.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Overwatering is the #1 killer">
More houseplants die from too much water than too little. Always check soil moisture before watering, and ensure pots drain well.
</Callout>

<Details title="What if my plant has yellow leaves?" open="false">
Yellow leaves usually mean overwatering. Let the soil dry out completely before watering again. If leaves are mushy at the base, root rot may have set in—repot in fresh, dry soil.
</Details>

<Details title="What if my plant has brown, crispy tips?" open="false">
This usually indicates underwatering or low humidity. Water more consistently, and consider misting the leaves or placing a small humidifier nearby.
</Details>
</Card>

<Card title="Bottom Line">
If you want the absolute easiest start, get a **snake plant** or a **ZZ plant**—both tolerate low light, infrequent watering, and even some neglect. For a trailing look, add a **pothos**. These three together give you variety without the stress.

<Callout tone="success" title="Beginner's pick">
The Snake Plant is the most forgiving: it can survive weeks without water, thrives in dim corners, and even purifies your air while you forget about it.
</Callout>
</Card>
