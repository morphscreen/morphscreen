## Top Picks

<Columns>
<Card title="1. Stawamus Chief" subtitle="The iconic granite monolith">
![Stawamus Chief summit view](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1506905925346-21bda4d32df4?w=800)

**Difficulty:** <Badge tone="warning">Hard</Badge>  
**Distance:** 11 km round trip  
**Elevation gain:** 700 m  
**Time:** 5–7 hours

The Chief is Vancouver's most famous hike — three granite peaks overlooking Howe Sound. The trail is steep with chains and ladders on the final sections, but the panoramic views of the Sound and surrounding peaks are unforgettable.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Be prepared">The Chief is a strenuous climb. Bring plenty of water, wear sturdy boots, and start early to avoid the biggest crowds.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="2. Garibaldi Lake" subtitle="Turquoise alpine beauty">
![Garibaldi Lake turquoise water](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1519681393784-d120267933ba?w=800)

**Difficulty:** <Badge tone="warning">Moderate-Hard</Badge>  
**Distance:** 18 km round trip  
**Elevation gain:** 820 m  
**Time:** 6–8 hours

The payoff at Garibaldi Lake is one of the most stunning sights in BC — a massive turquoise lake ringed by snow-capped peaks. The switchback trail is relentless but well-maintained. Pack a lunch and spend time at the lake before heading back.

<Callout tone="info" title="Seasonal note">The trail is snow-covered until late June. The best time is July–October when wildflowers bloom.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="3. Quarry Rock" subtitle="Quick coastal forest escape">
![Quarry Rock viewpoint over Deep Cove](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1464822759023-fed622ff2c3b?w=800)

**Difficulty:** <Badge tone="success">Easy-Moderate</Badge>  
**Distance:** 3.8 km round trip  
**Elevation gain:** 100 m  
**Time:** 1.5–2 hours

A short, family-friendly trail in Deep Cove that ends at a granite outcrop with sweeping views over Indian Arm. The trail is mostly forested with some stairs and roots. Perfect for a half-day outing combined with a paddle or fish and chips in Deep Cove.

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Insider tip">Go early morning or late afternoon to avoid the crowds — this is one of the busiest trails in the region.</Callout>
</Card>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<Card title="4. Lynn Canyon Loop" subtitle="Suspension bridge and waterfalls">
![Lynn Canyon suspension bridge](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1508739773434-c26b3d09e071?w=800)

**Difficulty:** <Badge tone="success">Easy</Badge>  
**Distance:** 5 km loop  
**Elevation gain:** 100 m  
**Time:** 2 hours

A gentle loop through old-growth rainforest, past the famous (and free!) suspension bridge, roaring waterfalls, and emerald pools. The trail is well-marked and suitable for all ages. Combine with a visit to the Lynn Canyon Ecology Centre.

<Callout tone="success" title="Free alternative">Unlike Capilano Suspension Bridge, Lynn Canyon is completely free — same thrill, no entry fee.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="5. Bowen Lookout" subtitle="Hidden gem with Sound views">
![Bowen Island view from Cypress](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1500530855697-b586d89ba3ee?w=800)

**Difficulty:** <Badge tone="neutral">Moderate</Badge>  
**Distance:** 10 km round trip  
**Elevation gain:** 460 m  
**Time:** 3–4 hours

Starting from Cypress Mountain ski area, this trail climbs through forest to a rocky viewpoint overlooking Howe Sound, Bowen Island, and the Gulf Islands. Less crowded than the Chief but equally rewarding. The trail is well-signed and manageable for fit beginners.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Snow caution">The trail may have snow patches until June. Check conditions before heading up.</Callout>
</Card>
</Columns>

<Card title="Seasonal Guide">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Spring (Mar–May)">
- Lower elevation trails clear of snow: Quarry Rock, Lynn Canyon, Whyte Lake
- Higher trails (Chief, Garibaldi) may have snow/ice — check conditions
- Waterfalls at peak flow from snowmelt
- Wildflowers begin in April
</Tab>
<Tab title="Summer (Jun–Aug)">
- All trails accessible; best time for Garibaldi Lake
- Expect crowds on popular trails — start before 8 AM
- Mosquito repellent essential in forest areas
- Hot days: carry 2+ liters of water per person
</Tab>
<Tab title="Fall (Sep–Nov)">
- Ideal hiking weather — cool and clear
- Larch trees turn golden at Garibaldi (late Sep)
- Fewer crowds after Labour Day
- Watch for early season snow at higher elevations
</Tab>
<Tab title="Winter (Dec–Feb)">
- Low-elevation trails (Quarry Rock, Lynn Canyon) are snow-free
- The Chief is climbable but icy — traction devices essential
- Snowshoe options at Cypress and Seymour
- Shorter daylight hours — start by 9 AM
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

## Essential Planning Tips

<Card title="Getting There">
| Trail | From Downtown | Transit | Parking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stawamus Chief | 1 hr drive | Bus to Squamish + 15 min walk | Paid lot, fills early |
| Garibaldi Lake | 1.5 hr drive | Bus to Squamish + shuttle | Paid lot, arrives early |
| Quarry Rock | 40 min drive | Bus #211/212 to Deep Cove | Free lot, very limited |
| Lynn Canyon | 30 min drive | Bus #228/229 | Free lot, busy on weekends |
| Bowen Lookout | 30 min drive | Bus to Cypress (summer only) | Free at Cypress lot |

**Best bet:** Drive or carpool. Public transit works for Lynn Canyon and Quarry Rock, but the others need a car.
</Card>

<Card title="Gear Checklist">
<Steps>
<Step title="Footwear" status="done">Sturdy hiking boots or trail runners with good grip. The Chief and Garibaldi have rocky, rooty sections.</Step>
<Step title="Water & Food" status="done">At least 2 liters of water per person, plus high-energy snacks and a packed lunch for longer hikes.</Step>
<Step title="Layers" status="done">Coastal weather changes fast. Bring a waterproof shell, warm mid-layer, and sun protection.</Step>
<Step title="Navigation" status="done">Download offline maps (AllTrails or Gaia GPS). Trails are well-marked but fog can disorient.</Step>
<Step title="Safety" status="done">Tell someone your route and return time. Carry a basic first-aid kit and whistle.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Leave No Trace">
Pack out everything you bring in, stay on designated trails, and respect wildlife closures. These trails are in protected watersheds — keep them pristine.
</Callout>

## Final Word

For first-time visitors, **the Stawamus Chief** delivers the classic Vancouver experience — a real mountain climb with jaw-dropping views. If you're short on time or new to hiking, **Quarry Rock** or **Lynn Canyon** are perfect introductions. And for the full alpine experience, **Garibaldi Lake** is worth every switchback.

Whatever you choose, start early, pack smart, and enjoy some of the most accessible world-class hiking on the planet.
