# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon is a city of bold flavors, fresh seafood, and humble but brilliant comfort food. Here's your essential eating guide, from breakfast pastries to late-night snacks.

## The Icons You Must Try

<Card title="The Essentials">
<Columns>
<Card title="Pastel de Nata">
The legendary custard tart with a caramelized, crackly top and flaky pastry. Eat it warm, dusted with cinnamon.

**Where:** *Manteigaria* (multiple locations, but the Chiado one is iconic) or *Pastéis de Belém* — the original since 1837, near the Jerónimos Monastery.
</Card>
<Card title="Bifana">
A humble but mighty sandwich: thin slices of marinated pork in a crusty roll, often with a hit of spicy mustard or piri-piri.

**Where:** *Casa Bifanas* near Rossio station, or *O Trevo* in the center for a late-night version.
</Card>
<Card title="Bacalhau à Brás">
Shredded salted cod mixed with matchstick potatoes, onions, and scrambled eggs, topped with black olives. Salty, creamy, and utterly Portuguese.

**Where:** *Cervejaria Ramiro* (famous for seafood, but their bacalhau is superb) or *Casa da Comida* for a refined take.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Seafood — the City's Pride

<Card title="Fresh from the Atlantic">
<Columns>
<Card title="Cataplana">
A fragrant seafood stew steamed in a copper pot — clams, prawns, fish, and sausage with tomatoes, onions, and coriander. Perfect for sharing.

**Where:** *Pinoquio* (near the waterfront) or *Casa do Alentejo* for a rustic, traditional version.
</Card>
<Card title="Grilled Sardines">
In June, the whole city smells of these. Outside festival season, head to a proper *cervejaria* for charred, salty sardines with boiled potatoes and a simple salad.

**Where:** *Cervejaria Ramiro* — famous for its seafood, don't miss the *carabineiros* (giant scarlet prawns) either.
</Card>
<Card title="Arroz de Marisco">
A rich, soupy rice loaded with shrimp, clams, and crab — essentially a seafood risotto with a Portuguese soul. Order it as a main and share.

**Where:** *Marisqueira Uma* (tiny, no-frills, incredible) or *Cervejaria Trindade* in a former monastery.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Street Food & Petiscos (Portuguese Tapas)

<Card title="Graze Like a Local">
<Columns>
<Card title="Pregunto">
A juicy pork steak sandwich with a fried egg on top — messy, garlicky, and unforgettable. The Portuguese answer to a cheesesteak.

**Where:** *A Preguiça* in the Graça neighborhood, with a view over the city.
</Card>
<Card title="Chouriço Assado">
Grilled smoked sausage, flamed in aguardente at your table, served with crusty bread. Theater and food in one.

**Where:** *O Faia* (a fado house that also serves petiscos) or *Casa Independente* for a modern twist.
</Card>
<Card title="Peixinhos da Horta">
The original "fish and chips" — green beans battered and fried, crisp and golden. A vegetarian-friendly classic.

**Where:** *Cantinho do Avillez* (José Avillez's casual spot) or *Tasca da Esquina* in Campo de Ourique.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Where to Eat by Neighborhood

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Alfama & Mouraria">
The oldest districts, full of tiny tascas and family-run spots.

- **Casa de Pasto** — traditional Portuguese stews and grilled meats.
- **Maria da Mouraria** — a modern tasca run by a local celebrity chef, great for petiscos.
- **A Travessa do Fado** — fado music with excellent seafood.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Chiado & Bairro Alto">
The stylish heart — cafés, galleries, and buzzy restaurants.

- **Café a Brasileira** — historic café for a *bica* (espresso) and a pastel de nata.
- **Belcanto** — José Avillez's two-Michelin-starred flagship (book ahead).
- **Taberna da Rua das Flores** — tiny, seasonal, and wildly popular; arrive early.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Príncipe Real & Estrela">
Upscale and bohemian, with a great Sunday market.

- **A Cevicheria** — Peruvian-Portuguese fusion, famous for its octopus ceviche.
- **Prado** — farm-to-table with a focus on local ingredients.
- **O Velho Eurico** — a no-menu, market-driven kitchen with a cult following.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Cascais & Belém">
For a day trip: pastries, monuments, and beachside seafood.

- **Pastéis de Belém** — the original custard tarts.
- **Mar do Inferno** — cliffside seafood with a view.
- **Casa de Pasto O Pereira** — rustic, excellent grilled fish in Cascais.
</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Sweet Endings & Drinks

<Card title="Desserts & Nightcaps">
<Columns>
<Card title="Ginjinha">
A sour cherry liqueur served in a tiny chocolate cup. Sweet, tart, and dangerously easy to drink.

**Where:** *A Ginjinha* near Rossio — the classic spot since 1840.
</Card>
<Card title="Bolo de Bolacha">
A no-bake biscuit cake layered with coffee and buttercream — like a Portuguese tiramisu. Find it at any decent pastelaria.

**Where:** *Pastelaria Versailles* in Saldanha, a Lisbon institution.
</Card>
<Card title="Vinho Verde">
A light, slightly fizzy white wine from the north. Perfect with seafood. For something bolder, try a *tinto* from the Douro or Alentejo.

**Where:** *Grapes & Bites* wine bar or any *adega* (wine cellar) in the center.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Practical Tips

<Callout tone="info" title="Eating like a local">
- **Lunch is the big meal** — many restaurants offer a *prato do dia* (daily plate) for €8–12, including soup, main, drink, and coffee.
- **Dinner starts late** — most places open at 7:30 or 8 PM; reservations are wise for popular spots.
- **Tap water is free** — ask for *água da torneira*; it's perfectly safe and saves money.
- **Tipping** — 5–10% is appreciated but not mandatory; rounding up is fine.
- **Bread & olives** — often brought to the table unasked; you'll be charged for them if you eat them. Politely decline if you don't want them.
</Callout>

If you only eat one thing in Lisbon, make it a **pastel de nata** from *Manteigaria* — warm, fresh, and utterly perfect. Everything else is a bonus.
