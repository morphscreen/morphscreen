<Card title="Day-by-Day Plan">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Day 1 – Shinjuku & Shibuya">
<Steps>
<Step title="Arrival & Check-in" status="done" subtitle="Settle into your hotel near Shinjuku Station">
Land at Narita or Haneda, take the Narita Express or airport limousine bus to Shinjuku. Check in and freshen up.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ramen in Shinjuku" status="done" subtitle="Ichiran or Afuri for tonkotsu or yuzu shio ramen">
Line up early (before 12pm) to avoid the rush. Both shops have counter seating — perfect for two.
</Step>
<Step title="Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden" status="done" subtitle="A peaceful escape from the city">
Stroll through Japanese, French, and English gardens. Cherry blossoms in spring, maples in autumn. ~2 hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Crossing & Hachiko Statue" status="done" subtitle="The world's busiest pedestrian crossing">
Watch the scramble from the Starbucks second floor or the Shibuya Sky observation deck at sunset.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner & Nightlife in Golden Gai" status="done" subtitle="Tiny bars in narrow alleys">
Pick a themed bar (jazz, movies, anime). Most fit 5–8 people — great for meeting locals. Go early (6–7pm) to avoid queues.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 2 – Asakusa & Ueno">
<Steps>
<Step title="Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise Street" status="current" subtitle="Tokyo's oldest temple">
Arrive by 8:30am to beat crowds. Walk through the Kaminarimon Gate, explore the shopping street, and get your fortune (omikuji).
</Step>
<Step title="Sumida River Cruise" status="todo" subtitle="Boat ride to Hamarikyu Gardens">
Take a 40-minute cruise down the river — great views of Tokyo Skytree. Disembark at Hamarikyu for a matcha break.
</Step>
<Step title="Ueno Park & Museums" status="todo" subtitle="Museums, zoo, and street food">
Visit the Tokyo National Museum (samurai armor & ukiyo-e) or the Ueno Zoo. Try yakisoba or taiyaki from street stalls.
</Step>
<Step title="Ameyoko Market" status="todo" subtitle="Bustling market street">
Fresh seafood, spices, and bargain clothes. Grab an early dinner of yakitori or oden at a standing bar.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 3 – Harajuku & Shibuya">
<Steps>
<Step title="Meiji Shrine" status="todo" subtitle="A forested shrine in the city">
Walk the gravel path through the 100,000-tree forest. Watch a traditional wedding procession if you're lucky.
</Step>
<Step title="Takeshita Street" status="todo" subtitle="Kawaii culture & crepes">
Try a rainbow cotton candy or a crêpe from Marion Crepes. Browse vintage shops and character stores.
</Step>
<Step title="Omotesando & Cat Street" status="todo" subtitle="Architecture & boutique shopping">
Walk down the tree-lined boulevard with flagship stores (Prada, Dior). Cat Street has independent designers and cafés.
</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Sky at Sunset" status="todo" subtitle="360° views from 229m">
Book tickets online in advance for the sunset slot (4:30–5:30pm). The glass corner is a great photo spot.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Izakaya in Shibuya" status="todo" subtitle="Japanese pub food">
Try a local izakaya like Uoshin Nogizaka — fresh seafood, sake, and a lively atmosphere.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 4 – Day Trip: Nikko or Kamakura">
<Callout tone="info" title="Choose your adventure">
Both are 1.5–2 hours from Tokyo. Nikko is grander (UNESCO shrines, waterfalls); Kamakura is more relaxed (beach, smaller temples, great hiking).
</Callout>
<Steps>
<Step title="Option A: Nikko" status="todo" subtitle="UNESCO World Heritage shrines">
Take the Tobu Railway from Asakusa. Visit Toshogu Shrine (lavish mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu), Kegon Falls, and Lake Chuzenji. Return by early evening.
</Step>
<Step title="Option B: Kamakura" status="todo" subtitle="Beach town with the Great Buddha">
Take the JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo Station. See Kotoku-in (Great Buddha), Hase-dera Temple, and hike the Daibutsu Trail. Finish at Kamakura's Komachi-dori shopping street.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 5 – Ginza & Akihabara">
<Steps>
<Step title="Tsukiji Outer Market" status="todo" subtitle="Fresh sushi breakfast">
Arrive by 7:30am. Try tamagoyaki (egg omelette), fresh uni (sea urchin), and kaisendon (seafood rice bowl). Go early — many stalls close by 2pm.
</Step>
<Step title="teamLab Planets" status="todo" subtitle="Immersive digital art museum">
Book timed tickets online (advance is essential). Walk through water and mirror rooms — bring shorts or a change of socks.
</Step>
<Step title="Ginza Shopping & Lunch" status="todo" subtitle="High-end department stores & cafes">
Browse Ginza Six and Uniqlo's flagship store (12 floors!). Lunch at a basement food hall (depachika) or a sushi counter.
</Step>
<Step title="Akihabara Electric Town" status="todo" subtitle="Anime, electronics & arcades">
Visit Mandarake for retro games, Super Potato for consoles, and the multi-floor arcades (try the UFO catchers!). End with a maid café or a curry at Coco Ichibanya.
</Step>
<Step title="Farewell Dinner" status="todo" subtitle="Monjayaki or teppanyaki in Tsukishima">
Take the subway to Tsukishima Monja Street — cook your own savory pancake at the table. A fun, interactive final meal.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="Transportation Tips">
<Columns>
<Card title="IC Cards">
Get a **Suica** or **Pasmo** card at any station. It works on trains, buses, and at convenience stores. Load ¥3,000–5,000 to start. Refundable at the end.
</Card>
<Card title="JR Pass?">
Only worth it if you take the Nikko day trip AND plan another long-distance ride. Otherwise, pay per ride — Tokyo's subway is cheap ($1.50–$3 per trip).
</Card>

<Card title="Packing & Etiquette">
<Columns>
<Card title="Essentials">
- Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk 15k+ steps/day)
- Light jacket or umbrella (rain is common)
- Power adapter (Type A/B, 100V)
- Cash — many small shops are cash-only
- A small towel for public restrooms (some lack paper)
</Card>
<Card title="Etiquette Tips">
- No tipping — it's not customary
- Keep quiet on trains (no phone calls)
- Remove shoes when entering homes/traditional restaurants
- Don't eat while walking (stand at stalls instead)
- Bow slightly when greeting or thanking
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Money-Saving Tips">
<Callout tone="success" title="Smart Savings">
- Eat at **conbini** (7-Eleven, FamilyMart) for breakfast — onigiri, sandwiches, coffee under $5
- Use **depachika** (department store basements) for free samples and discounted bento after 7pm
- Visit shrines and parks — most are free
- Take the **Yamanote Line** loop for sightseeing from the train ($3)
- Buy a **Tokyo Metro 24-hour pass** ($6) for unlimited subway rides on busy days
</Callout>
</Card>
