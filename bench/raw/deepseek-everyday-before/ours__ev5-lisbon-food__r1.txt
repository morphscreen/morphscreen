# What to Eat in Lisbon, and Where

Lisbon is a seafood lover's paradise, a haven for pastry enthusiasts, and a city where simple ingredients shine. Below is your culinary roadmap, organized by what to eat and where to find the best versions.

<Card title="The Iconic Bites" subtitle="You haven't been to Lisbon without trying these">
<Columns>
<div>

### Pastéis de Nata
The famous custard tart with a caramelized, crispy top and flaky shell. Best eaten warm, dusted with cinnamon.

**Where:** Manteigaria (multiple locations) or Pastéis de Belém (the original, since 1837 — expect a queue).

</div>
<div>

### Bifana
A humble but mighty sandwich: thin slices of marinated pork in a crusty roll, seasoned with garlic and paprika.

**Where:** Casa Bifanas (Praça da Figueira) or O Trevo (near Cais do Sodré) for a late-night version.

</div>
<div>

### Bacalhau à Brás
Shredded salt cod with onions, thin fries, and scrambled eggs, topped with olives and parsley. Portugal's "faithful friend" prepared a thousand ways.

**Where:** Cervejaria Ramiro (also famous for shellfish) or a classic tasca like O Velho Eurico.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="The Seafood Scene" subtitle="From the Atlantic to your plate">
<Columns>
<div>

### Grilled Sardines
At their peak in June during the Santos Populares festivals, but available year-round. Charred, salty, and best eaten with your hands.

**Where:** A Cevicheria (upscale twist) or any local festival stall. For a sit-down spot, try Ponto Final across the Tagus river.

</div>
<div>

### Amêijoas à Bulhão Pato
Clams steamed in white wine, garlic, and cilantro — the perfect starter. The sauce begs for bread.

**Where:** Cervejaria Ramiro (iconic) or Marisqueira Uma, a tiny spot with one dish and a big reputation.

</div>
<div>

### Polvo à Lagareiro
Octopus roasted with olive oil and potatoes, tender and smoky. A Portuguese classic done right.

**Where:** Solar dos Presuntos (a mother-son institution) or Taberna da Rua das Flores for a modern take.

</div>
</Columns>

<Card title="Street Food & Quick Eats" subtitle="Fuel for exploring the hills">
- **Churro com chocolate** — Crunchy fried dough with thick hot chocolate. Try at **Churraria Lisboa** near the Sé.
- **Ginjinha** — A sour cherry liqueur, served in a small chocolate cup. Best at **A Ginjinha** (the original, 1840) on Largo de São Domingos.
- **Pão com chouriço** — Bread stuffed with cured sausage, baked in a wood oven. Look for the street carts near Alfama.
- **Azeitona** — Marinated olives, sold by the cup at markets and street stands. Grab a handful at **Mercado da Ribeira**.

</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat by Neighborhood" subtitle="Match the mood to the district">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Alfama">
The oldest quarter, full of fado music and cozy taverns.

- **O Velho Eurico** — Modern takes on tasca classics; book ahead.
- **Cantinho do Aziz** — A hidden gem mixing Portuguese and Goan flavors (a legacy of Portugal's colonial past).
- **Fado at Clube de Fado** — Dinner plus authentic fado singing (not touristy).

</Tab>
<Tab title="Príncipe Real">
Trendy, with a bohemian vibe and excellent small plates.

- **A Cevicheria** — Peruvian-Portuguese ceviche, famous for the octopus hanging from the ceiling.
- **Taberna da Rua das Flores** — Tiny, seasonal, and always packed; go early.
- **O Velho Eurico** — Also has a sibling spot here; both are great.

</Tab>
<Tab title="Cais do Sodré & Santos">
Nightlife hub by day, working-class eateries by night.

- **Cervejaria Ramiro** — The seafood palace; go for the tiger prawns, stay for the garlic bread.
- **O Trevo** — The bifana spot, open late.
- **Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market)** — A food hall with outposts of Lisbon's best chefs; great for sampling.

</Tab>
<Tab title="Belém">
Touristy but worth it for the pastry and riverside views.

- **Pastéis de Belém** — The original custard tart factory.
- **Ponto Final** — Across the water in Cacilhas (take a ferry); sunset views with grilled fish.

</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="A Sample Day of Eating" subtitle="Follow this to hit the highlights">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Pastry & coffee">
Start at **Manteigaria** in Chiado for a pastel de nata and a *galão* (milky coffee). Order two — one won't be enough.
</Step>
<Step title="Late Morning" status="done" subtitle="Market wander">
Head to **Mercado da Ribeira** and try a *prego* (beef sandwich) at **O Prego da Peixaria** stall, or a fresh oyster at the seafood counter.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch" status="done" subtitle="Seafood feast">
Go to **Ramiro** before 12:30 to beat the queue. Order shrimp, clams, and a *manteiga* (butter) with bread. Don't skip the garlic bread.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Wander & snack">
Walk off lunch in Alfama. Stop at a *ginjinha* stand for a shot in a chocolate cup. If you're peckish, split a *bifana* at a local tasca.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Dinner with a view">
Take the ferry to Cacilhas for **Ponto Final** at sunset. Order the grilled sardines or the *arroz de marisco* (seafood rice). Share the table — it's communal.
</Step>
<Step title="Night" status="done" subtitle="Sweet finish">
Return to the city and find a gelato at **Gelato Davvero** (Chiado) or a final *pastel de nata* from a 24-hour bakery.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Key Tips" subtitle="Eat like a local">
- **Portions are generous** — share starters and mains, or ask for *meia dose* (half portion).
- **Bread, olives, and butter** that appear on the table are not free — you pay for what you eat. Politely decline if you don't want them.
- **Lunch is 12–15h**, dinner starts late (20h+). Many kitchens close between 15h and 19h.
- **Cash is king** at small tascas; cards work fine at mid-range spots.
- **Drink the house wine** — often a local *vinho da casa* that's cheap and excellent.
- **Reservations** are essential for the famous spots (Ramiro, Uma, Taberna da Rua das Flores) — book a few days ahead.

</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Vegetarian?">
Lisbon is getting better for plant-based eaters. Try **Ao 26** (vegan tasting menu) or **Jardim das Cerejas** (vegetarian buffet). For a quick bite, most places can make a *sandes de queijo* (cheese sandwich) or a *salada* (salad).
