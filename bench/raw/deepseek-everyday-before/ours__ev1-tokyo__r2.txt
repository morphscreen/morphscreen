# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A balanced itinerary mixing iconic sights, hidden gems, great food, and romantic moments.

<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
Get a **Suica or Pasmo IC card** at the airport — it works on all trains, subways, and buses, and at most convenience stores. Download **Google Maps** and the **Japan Travel** app for offline use.

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Money-saving tips">
- **Stay in capsule or business hotels** (from $60/night) instead of luxury hotels
- **Eat at convenience stores** for breakfast — quality is surprisingly high
- **Buy a 72-hour Tokyo Metro pass** ($13) if you're mostly on the subway
- **Skip the airport taxi** — trains are far cheaper and just as fast
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Practical info" subtitle="Everything else you need to know">
<Columns>
<div>
**Packing essentials**
- Comfortable walking shoes (you'll do 15,000+ steps/day)
- Light jacket (evenings can be cool year-round)
- Power adapter (Type A/B, same as US)
- Small towel for public restrooms
- Cash — many small shops don't take cards

**Etiquette**
- No tipping — it's not expected
- Eat on the go is fine, but not on trains
- Remove shoes where indicated (tatami, some restaurants)
- Keep noise down on public transport
</div>
<div>
**Useful apps**
- Google Maps (essential)
- Google Translate (camera mode for menus)
- Japan Travel (offline maps)
- Japan Official Travel App (timetables)

**Emergency numbers**
- Police: 110
- Ambulance/Fire: 119
- Tourist hotline: 03-3201-3331 (English, 24h)
</div>

<Card title="Quick answers">
<Details title="Is 5 days enough for Tokyo?" open="false">
Yes — it's a great first visit length. You'll cover the major neighborhoods, do one day trip, and still have time to relax. You won't see everything, but you'll get a strong feel for the city.
</Details>
<Details title="What's the best area to stay?" open="false">
**Shinjuku** is the classic choice — great transport links, endless dining, and lively nightlife. **Shibuya** is trendier and more central. **Asakusa** is quieter, traditional, and cheaper but less convenient.
</Details>
<Details title="Is Tokyo expensive?" open="false">
It's comparable to New York or London. Expect $50–80/night for a business hotel, $8–15 for a casual meal, and $3–5 for a subway ride. Budget travelers can do $100/day; luxury travelers will spend much more.
</Details>
<Details title="Do I need to speak Japanese?" open="false">
Not at all — most signs are bilingual, and English is widely understood in tourist areas. Learning a few phrases (arigatou, sumimasen, kore kudasai) goes a long way and is appreciated.
</Details>
</Card>
