# Rent or buy? A look at your numbers

You earn **£60,000 a year** and pay **£1,800 a month** in rent. Here's a practical breakdown to help you decide.

<Callout tone="info" title="What this guide covers">
The decision depends on your savings, how long you'll stay put, and what you value. Below I've compared your current rent against typical buying costs, plus the key trade-offs.
</Callout>

| Annual salary | £60,000 |

| Current rent | £1,800/month |
| Rent as % of take-home | ~49% |
| Typical mortgage (85% LTV) | ~£1,600–£2,000/month |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Rent is high relative to income">
Lenders often cap mortgage payments at 4.5× salary, and your rent already eats nearly half your take-home pay. That's a sign you could afford a mortgage — but you'll need a deposit saved up.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Renting vs buying — side by side">
<Compare title="Rent vs buy">
| Factor | Renting | Buying |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | £1,800 fixed | ~£1,700–£2,100 variable |
| Deposit needed | None | £25k–£50k+ (10–20%) |
| Maintenance | Landlord's problem | Yours (≈1% of value/yr) |
| Flexibility to move | High | Low (stamp duty + fees) |
| Building equity | No | Yes |
| Price appreciation | No | Potential |
| Interest rate risk | None | Yes (if variable) |
</Compare>

<Callout tone="success" title="The rule of thumb">
If you'll stay in the same place **5+ years**, buying usually wins financially. If you might move sooner, renting is often cheaper once you factor in buying and selling costs.
</Callout>

That budget buys a 1–2 bed flat in most UK cities outside London, or a studio in central London. If you want a house or a London zone 1–2 flat, you'd need a bigger deposit or a longer term.

<Card title="The 5-year break-even test">
<Steps>
<Step title="Work out your deposit" status="done" subtitle="You'll need £25k–£60k saved">
If you have under £25k saved, buying is likely off the table for now. With £30k+, you can start.
</Step>
<Step title="Estimate buying costs" status="current" subtitle="Stamp duty, legal fees, surveys ≈ £5k–£15k">
These are sunk costs you don't get back if you sell quickly.
</Step>
<Step title="Compare total costs over 5 years" status="todo" subtitle="Rent £108k vs mortgage interest + fees">
Over 5 years, renting costs ~£108k with nothing to show. Buying might cost ~£90k in interest + fees, but you keep the equity.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide on stability" status="todo" subtitle="Can you commit to one place for 5 years?">
If your job, family or lifestyle might change, renting keeps you flexible.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="The verdict">
**If you have a £30k+ deposit and plan to stay put for 5+ years, buying is likely the better financial move.** You'd build ~£45k in equity while paying less than rent in interest. If you're unsure about the future or lack savings, renting remains sensible — but your rent is high enough that saving aggressively to buy should be a priority.
</Callout>
