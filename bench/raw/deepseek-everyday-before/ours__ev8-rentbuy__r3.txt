<Card title="What could you afford to buy?">
With a £60k salary, most lenders will offer around **4–4.5× your income**, so roughly **£240,000–£270,000**.

| Deposit | Max property price | Monthly mortgage (4.5%, 25yr) |
|---|---|---|
| £20,000 (8%) | £250,000 | ~£1,150 |
| £30,000 (12%) | £260,000 | ~£1,200 |
| £50,000 (20%) | £300,000 | ~£1,330 |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't forget the extras">
Add **~£200–£300/month** for buildings insurance, service charges (if leasehold), and maintenance. That pushes your true cost to **£1,350–£1,650/month** — still potentially less than your rent, but not by a huge margin.
</Callout>
</Card>

---

<Card title="The 5-year rule">
<Steps>
<Step title="Staying less than 3 years" status="current" subtitle="Rent is usually better">
Buying costs ~£5,000–£10,000 in fees and stamp duty upfront. If you move within 3 years, you likely won't recoup those costs, even with price growth.
</Step>
<Step title="Staying 3–5 years" status="todo" subtitle="It's a close call">
This is the grey zone. If prices rise even modestly (2–3%/year), buying can win. If they stagnate or fall, you'd have been better off renting and investing the difference.
</Step>
<Step title="Staying 5+ years" status="todo" subtitle="Buying usually wins">
Over 5+ years, you build equity, and your monthly cost is likely to be lower than renting — especially as rents rise over time while your mortgage stays fixed.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

---

<Card title="Three scenarios for you">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Rent and invest the difference">
If you buy, your monthly cost is roughly **£300–£500 less** than renting. Invest that difference in a low-cost index fund (e.g., S&P 500, historical ~7% return) and after 5 years:

- **£300/month invested** → ~£21,500 (at 7% annual return)
- **£500/month invested** → ~£35,900

You stay flexible, avoid maintenance headaches, and build wealth outside property.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Buy now">
With a £30,000 deposit on a £260,000 flat, your mortgage is ~£1,200/month. Even with £300/month for upkeep, you're at **£1,500** — £300 less than rent.

After 5 years, even at just **2% annual price growth**, your flat is worth ~£287,000. You've built ~£27,000 in equity from price growth alone, plus paid down ~£20,000 of the mortgage.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Wait 2 years, save more">
Keep renting, save aggressively toward a **20% deposit (£50,000)**. That gets you a better mortgage rate (lower LTV), lower monthly payments, and more choice of properties. You also give yourself time to be sure you're staying put.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Bottom line">
<Callout tone="success" title="My recommendation">
**If you plan to stay 5+ years and can put down at least £30,000, buying is likely the better financial move** — you'll probably save £300–£500/month versus renting, build equity, and hedge against rising rents.

**If you're unsure about staying put, or don't have a solid deposit yet, keep renting** and invest the difference. There's no shame in renting — it buys you flexibility, and you can always buy later when you're more certain.
</Callout>
