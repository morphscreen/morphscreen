## Inner Rocky Planets

These four planets are composed mostly of rock and metal, are smaller and denser, and lie closest to the Sun.

<Card title="Mercury — The Swift Planet">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 57.9 million km (0.39 AU)
- **Diameter:** 4,879 km
- **Day length:** 59 Earth days (rotation)
- **Year length:** 88 Earth days
- **Moons:** 0
- **Avg. temperature:** 167°C (varies from -173°C to 427°C)

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has virtually no atmosphere, so its surface is scarred with craters, and it experiences the most extreme temperature swings of any planet. A single day (sunrise to sunrise) lasts 176 Earth days, twice as long as its year!
</div>
<Gauge label="Surface Temperature" value="167" min="-173" max="427" unit="°C" tone="warning" />
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Venus — The Veiled Planet">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 108.2 million km (0.72 AU)
- **Diameter:** 12,104 km
- **Day length:** 243 Earth days (retrograde)
- **Year length:** 225 Earth days
- **Moons:** 0
- **Avg. temperature:** 464°C

Venus is nearly the same size as Earth, but that's where the similarity ends. A thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide with clouds of sulfuric acid traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect, making it the hottest planet in the solar system. It rotates **backward** (retrograde), so the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. Surface pressure is 92 times that of Earth — like being 900 m underwater.
</div>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Hottest planet">
Despite being farther from the Sun than Mercury, Venus's thick CO₂ atmosphere makes it the hottest planet at 464°C — hot enough to melt lead.
</Callout>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Earth — The Blue Marble">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 149.6 million km (1.00 AU)
- **Diameter:** 12,742 km
- **Day length:** 24 hours
- **Year length:** 365.25 days
- **Moons:** 1
- **Avg. temperature:** 15°C

Our home planet is the only known world to harbor life. About 71% of its surface is covered with liquid water, and its nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere shields life from harmful radiation while allowing the greenhouse effect to keep temperatures moderate. Earth's magnetic field, generated by its molten iron core, deflects solar wind and cosmic rays.
</div>
<Stat label="Oceans Cover" value="71%" icon="droplet" />
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Mars — The Red Planet">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 227.9 million km (1.52 AU)
- **Diameter:** 6,779 km
- **Day length:** 24.6 hours
- **Year length:** 687 Earth days
- **Moons:** 2 (Phobos & Deimos)
- **Avg. temperature:** -65°C

Mars gets its rusty color from iron oxide (rust) on its surface. It hosts the solar system's largest volcano, **Olympus Mons** (21.9 km tall — nearly 2.5× Everest), and the longest canyon system, **Valles Marineris** (4,000 km long). Ancient riverbeds and polar ice caps suggest water once flowed freely, and rovers like *Perseverance* are searching for signs of ancient microbial life.
</div>
<Callout tone="info" title="Mars moons">
Phobos orbits so close to Mars that it rises and sets *twice* every Martian day, and is slowly spiraling inward — it will either crash into Mars or break apart into a ring in about 50 million years.
</Callout>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Outer Gas & Ice Giants

Beyond the asteroid belt lie the giant planets — massive worlds with deep atmospheres and many moons.

<Card title="Jupiter — The Giant King">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 778.5 million km (5.20 AU)
- **Diameter:** 139,820 km
- **Day length:** 9.9 hours (fastest rotation)
- **Year length:** 11.9 Earth years
- **Moons:** 95+ (including Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system)
- **Avg. temperature:** -110°C

Jupiter is more massive than all other planets combined — 2.5× the mass of all of them put together. Its iconic **Great Red Spot** is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for at least 350 years. Jupiter's strong magnetic field is 20,000× stronger than Earth's, and its moon Europa hides a liquid ocean beneath its icy crust that may harbor life.
</div>
<Progress value="318" max="318" label="Mass (Earth = 1)" tone="info" />
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn — The Ringed Jewel">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 1.43 billion km (9.54 AU)
- **Diameter:** 116,460 km
- **Day length:** 10.7 hours
- **Year length:** 29.4 Earth years
- **Moons:** 146+ (including Titan, with a thick atmosphere)
- **Avg. temperature:** -140°C

Saturn's magnificent ring system spans 282,000 km but is only about 10 meters thick in places, made of countless chunks of ice and rock. Saturn is so light it would **float** in water (density 0.687 g/cm³). Its moon Titan has rivers and lakes of liquid methane and ethane, and is a prime target in the search for prebiotic chemistry.
</div>
<Callout tone="info" title="Rings are young">
Saturn's rings may be only 100–400 million years old — a blink in cosmic time — and could disappear within 300 million years as ring particles rain onto the planet.
</Callout>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Uranus — The Sideways Planet">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 2.87 billion km (19.2 AU)
- **Diameter:** 50,724 km
- **Day length:** 17.2 hours (retrograde)
- **Year length:** 84 Earth years
- **Moons:** 28
- **Avg. temperature:** -195°C

Uranus is tilted a stunning 98° on its side, likely from a colossal ancient collision. This means its poles experience 42 years of sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness. It's an ice giant, composed mostly of water, methane, and ammonia ices, and its methane gives it a pale cyan color. It's the coldest atmosphere in the solar system despite not being the most distant planet.
</div>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Coldest atmosphere">
Despite Neptune being farther out, Uranus holds the record for the coldest planetary atmosphere at -224°C, likely because its sideways tilt disrupts its internal heat flow.
</Callout>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune — The Windy Blue Giant">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Distance from Sun:** 4.50 billion km (30.1 AU)
- **Diameter:** 49,244 km
- **Day length:** 16.1 hours
- **Year length:** 165 Earth years
- **Moons:** 16 (including Triton, which orbits backward)
- **Avg. temperature:** -200°C

Neptune is the most distant planet, discovered by mathematics before it was seen through a telescope in 1846. It has the fastest winds in the solar system — supersonic gusts reaching **2,100 km/h**. Its moon Triton is the coldest known body in the solar system at -235°C and has geysers of nitrogen ice erupting from its surface. Neptune has completed only **one** full orbit since its discovery!
</div>
<Stat label="Fastest Winds" value="2,100" unit="km/h" icon="wind" />
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Why Pluto Isn't a Planet">
<Callout tone="info" title="2006 IAU Definition">
In 2006, the International Astronomical Union defined a planet as a body that: (1) orbits the Sun, (2) is massive enough to be round, and (3) has **cleared its neighborhood** of debris. Pluto fails criterion #3 — it shares its orbit with many other Kuiper Belt objects.
</Callout>
Pluto is now classified as a **dwarf planet**. There are five officially recognized dwarf planets: Pluto, Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake. Pluto is smaller than Earth's Moon and has a heart-shaped glacier (Sputnik Planitia) that fascinates scientists.
</Card>

<Card title="Notable Missions Now">
- **Juno** — Orbiting Jupiter since 2016, studying its atmosphere and magnetic field.
- **Perseverance** — Exploring Mars' Jezero Crater, caching samples for future return to Earth.
- **BepiColombo** — Joint ESA/JAXA mission en route to Mercury, arriving 2026.
- **Dragonfly** — NASA rotorcraft planned for Titan, launching 2028, arriving 2034.
</Card>

<Card title="Fun Facts">
- **Jupiter's Great Red Spot** has been shrinking — it was once 3× Earth's diameter, now only ~1.3×.
- **Venus rotates so slowly** that a Venusian year (225 days) is shorter than a Venusian day (243 days).
- **Saturn's moon Titan** has a methane cycle just like Earth's water cycle, with rain, rivers, and seas.
- **Neptune was found by math** — astronomers predicted its location from perturbations in Uranus's orbit before anyone saw it.
- **You weigh less on Mars** — about 38% of your Earth weight. A 70 kg person weighs just 26.6 kg there.
</Card>
