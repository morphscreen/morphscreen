# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making an authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is absolutely achievable — you don't need a wood-fired oven to get a soft, leopard-spotted crust with a puffy cornicione. Here's the complete guide.

<Card title="The Golden Rules" subtitle="What makes it 'Neapolitan'">
<Columns>
<div>
**1. The dough** — just flour, water, salt, and yeast. No oil, no sugar. It must be soft, wet, and fermented slowly.

**2. The heat** — the higher the better. A home oven at 250°C (480°F) with a steel or stone works; a pizza oven (Ooni, Roccbox) gets you closer to the 450°C (850°F) of Naples.

**3. The toppings** — San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte mozzarella, fresh basil, extra-virgin olive oil. That's it.
</div>
<div>
**4. The shape** — thin in the center, puffy at the edge (the cornicione). Stretch by hand, never roll.

**5. The bake** — 60–90 seconds in a wood-fired oven, 4–6 minutes in a home oven. The base should be soft and a little charred.

**6. The eat** — immediately, with a knife and fork if you're in Naples, folded in half if you're not.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

## The Dough — The Heart of It All

<Card title="Ingredients for 2 pizzas (250g each)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Type 00 flour (or bread flour) | 300g |
| Water (room temperature) | 180ml (60% hydration) |
| Fine sea salt | 6g (2%) |
| Fresh yeast (or 1g dry yeast) | 3g |
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done" subtitle="5 minutes">
Dissolve the yeast in the water. Add the flour and mix until a shaggy mass forms. Rest 20 minutes (autolyse), then add the salt and knead.
</Step>
<Step title="Knead" status="done" subtitle="10–15 minutes">
Knead by hand or with a stand mixer until the dough is smooth and elastic. It will be sticky — resist adding flour. It should pass the windowpane test: stretch a small piece thin enough to see light through without tearing.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done" subtitle="8–24 hours">
Cover the bowl and let it rise at room temperature for 2–4 hours, then refrigerate for 8–24 hours. Cold fermentation builds flavor. The dough should roughly double in size.
</Step>
<Step title="Shape into balls" status="done" subtitle="1–2 hours before baking">
Divide the dough into 2 pieces (about 250g each). Gently shape each into a tight ball, pinching the seams underneath. Place on a floured tray, cover, and let rest at room temperature for at least 2 hours. They should be relaxed and puffy.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch" status="current" subtitle="Just before baking">
Generously flour your work surface and the dough ball. Press from the center outward, leaving the rim untouched. Pick it up and stretch gently with your knuckles, rotating. Aim for a thin center (2–3mm) and a thicker edge (1–2cm). Don't worry if it's not a perfect circle — organic shapes are fine.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Hydration for beginners">
60% hydration (180ml water per 300g flour) is forgiving and easy to handle. As you get comfortable, you can push toward 65–70% for a lighter, more open crumb.
</Callout>

## The Sauce — Keep It Simple

<Card title="Tomato Sauce">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| San Marzano tomatoes (canned, whole) | 1 can (400g) |
| Fine sea salt | a pinch |
| Fresh basil | 2–3 leaves |

Crush the tomatoes by hand. Add a pinch of salt and the basil. That's it — no cooking, no garlic, no oregano. The sauce goes on raw and cooks in the oven.
</Card>

## The Toppings — Authentic Combinations

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Margherita">
The classic: tomato sauce, fior di latte mozzarella (torn, not grated), fresh basil, a drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil.

<Badge tone="success">The one to master first</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Marinara">
The oldest Neapolitan pizza: tomato sauce, garlic (thinly sliced), oregano, extra-virgin olive oil. No cheese — the original fast food.

<Badge tone="info">A great dairy-free option</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Diavola">
Spicy salami (like 'nduja or spicy soppressata) on top of the tomato, plus fior di latte and a drizzle of olive oil.

<Badge tone="warning">For when you want a kick</Badge>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't overload">
Neapolitan pizza is about balance. Too many toppings make the crust soggy and the center heavy. A few well-chosen ingredients beat a mountain of them every time.
</Callout>

## The Bake — How to Get the Heat

<Card title="Two ways to bake at home">
<Columns>
<div>
**Option A: Home oven (250°C / 480°F)**

- Place a pizza steel or baking stone on the top shelf 30–45 minutes before baking, with the broiler on for the last 10 minutes.
- Assemble the pizza on a floured peel.
- Launch it onto the steel, bake 4–6 minutes, rotating halfway.
- Finish under the broiler for 30–60 seconds to char the cornicione.
</div>
<div>
**Option B: Dedicated pizza oven (Ooni, Roccbox, Gozney)**

- Preheat to 400–450°C (750–850°F) — 20–30 minutes.
- Assemble quickly — the peel will get hot.
- Launch and bake 60–90 seconds, turning every 20–30 seconds.
- Watch for leopard spotting: charred bubbles on the rim, soft and chewy in the center.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The peel matters">
A wooden or metal peel dusted with semolina or flour is essential for launching. Shake the pizza gently before it goes in — if it sticks, lift the edge and blow a puff of flour underneath.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cold dough | It tears when stretched | Always rest the dough balls at room temperature for 2+ hours |
| Too much flour | Makes the crust dry and tough | Use a light dusting; brush off excess before topping |
| Wet mozzarella | Soggy center | Pat the mozzarella dry with paper towels before using |
| Overloading | The center never cooks properly | Less is more — 2–3 toppings max |
| Rolling with a pin | Kills the air bubbles | Stretch by hand, from the center outward |
| Not preheating enough | Pale, doughy crust | Give your steel/stone at least 45 minutes at full heat |

## The Full Recipe, Step by Step

<Card title="Complete Timeline">
| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| Day before, 6 PM | Mix dough, knead, bulk ferment at room temp 2–4 hrs, then refrigerate |
| Day of, 4 PM | Take dough out, divide, shape into balls, rest at room temp |
| Day of, 6 PM | Preheat oven/steel at max heat for 45+ min |
| Day of, 6:30 PM | Stretch first dough ball, top it, launch, bake |
| Day of, 6:35 PM | Eat immediately, repeat with the second pizza |
</Card>

<Card title="The Final Assembly">
1. Stretch the dough ball.
2. Spoon 2–3 tablespoons of sauce onto the center and spread in a spiral, leaving a 2–3cm border.
3. Tear the mozzarella over the sauce — about 80g per pizza.
4. Add basil leaves, then a drizzle of olive oil.
5. Launch onto the steel/stone and bake as directed above.
6. The pizza is done when the cornicione is puffed and charred, the cheese is bubbling, and the base is cooked through — lift an edge to check.
7. Slice (or don't), drizzle with a little more oil, and eat immediately.
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="First time?">
Don't stress about perfection. The first few pizzas will be lopsided and maybe a little doughy in spots — that's how everyone learns. The second batch will be better. By the third, you'll be making pizzas your friends will beg you to repeat.

And if you want to go deeper, the next step is experimenting with hydration, longer cold ferments, and different flours. The journey never really ends.
</Callout>
