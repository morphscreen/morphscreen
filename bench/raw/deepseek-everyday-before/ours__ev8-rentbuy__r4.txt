| Rent | £108,000 | £0 (plus what you saved) | You’ve paid someone else’s mortgage |

<Callout tone="success" title="The rule of thumb">If you’ll stay in the same place **5+ years**, buying usually wins — you build equity and your monthly cost is similar or lower. If you might move in under 3 years, renting is often cheaper once you add stamp duty, legal fees, and buying/selling costs.</Callout>

## What’s your situation?

<Checklist title="Quick self-check" name="rent-buy-check">
- [ ] I have a deposit of at least 10% (ideally 15–20%) saved
- [ ] I plan to stay put for 5+ years
- [ ] I have an emergency fund left after the purchase (3–6 months of costs)
- [ ] I’m comfortable with repair costs and DIY or paying tradespeople
- [ ] I’m ready to be tied to one property (less flexibility for jobs/relationships)
</Checklist>

- **If you have a 10–20% deposit and a 5‑year horizon** → buying is likely the better long‑term move.
- **If you don’t have a deposit yet, or might relocate soon** → keep renting, but aim to save aggressively toward a deposit.

<Callout tone="info" title="One more thing">With £60k salary, you could also consider **shared ownership** or a **longer mortgage term (30–35 years)** to lower monthly costs — but that means more interest paid overall. Run your own numbers with a mortgage calculator before deciding.</Callout>
