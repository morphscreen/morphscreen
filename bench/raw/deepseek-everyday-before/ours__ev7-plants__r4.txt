# Houseplants That Are Hard to Kill

Whether you're a busy professional, a frequent traveler, or just new to plant care, these resilient houseplants can survive (and even thrive) with minimal attention. Here's a guide to the most forgiving green companions.

<Card title="The Top 10 Most Unkillable Houseplants">
<Columns>
<div>

1. **Snake Plant (Sansevieria)** — Tolerates low light, irregular watering, and almost any condition. It's nearly indestructible.
2. **Pothos (Devil's Ivy)** — Grows in low light and bounces back quickly even if you forget to water it for weeks.
3. **ZZ Plant** — Stores water in its rhizomes, so it's fine with neglect and low light. Glossy, attractive leaves.
4. **Spider Plant** — Resilient and forgiving, with fun baby "spiderettes" that can be propagated easily.
5. **Peace Lily** — Dramatically droops when thirsty, but perks right back up after a drink. Tolerates low light.

</div>
<div>

6. **Jade Plant (Crassula)** — A succulent that stores water in its leaves; needs only occasional watering and bright light.
7. **Aloe Vera** — Hardy succulent that thrives on neglect, plus it's useful for soothing minor burns.
8. **Rubber Plant** — Thick, waxy leaves tolerate low light and missed waterings. Grows into a striking statement plant.
9. **Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra)** — Lives up to its name: survives low light, temperature swings, and severe neglect.
10. **Philodendron** — Similar to pothos but with heart-shaped leaves; very forgiving and easy to propagate.

</div>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="info" title="The #1 killer is overwatering">
Most people kill houseplants by loving them too much — watering too often leads to root rot. When in doubt, wait another week. These plants prefer dry soil over soggy roots.

</Callout>

<Card title="Beginner's Cheat Sheet">
<Steps>
<Step title="Choose your plant" status="current" subtitle="Pick one from the list above based on your light">
Start with a snake plant or pothos — they're the most forgiving.
</Step>
<Step title="Pick the right pot" status="todo" subtitle="Make sure it has drainage holes">
A pot without drainage traps water and causes root rot. Terracotta pots wick away extra moisture.
</Step>
<Step title="Water only when dry" status="todo" subtitle="Check with your finger — 2 inches deep">
Stick your finger in the soil. If it's dry, water. If it's damp, wait. Most of these plants prefer to dry out completely between waterings.
</Step>
<Step title="Find a stable spot" status="todo" subtitle="Avoid drafty windows and heat vents">
Sudden temperature changes stress plants. A spot with consistent, indirect light is ideal.
</Step>
<Step title="Fertilize sparingly" status="todo" subtitle="Once a month in spring/summer only">
These tough plants need very little food. A diluted balanced fertilizer during the growing season is plenty.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Overwatering">
The most common way to kill an "unkillable" plant. Always let the soil dry out between waterings.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="No drainage">
Ensure your pot has holes. If it doesn't, add a layer of pebbles or switch pots.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Direct scorching sun">
While jade and aloe tolerate some sun, most of these plants prefer bright, indirect light. Harsh afternoon sun can burn leaves.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="info" title="Cold drafts">
Keep plants away from air conditioners, drafty windows in winter, and heat vents. They prefer stable room temperatures (60–75°F / 15–24°C).
</Callout>
</Card>

If you want the absolute easiest plant, get a **snake plant**. It can go a month without water, survives in dim corners, and still looks elegant. For a more lush, trailing look, go with **pothos** — it's nearly as forgiving.
