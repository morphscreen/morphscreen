# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system is home to eight planets, orbiting the Sun. They are divided into two main groups: the four inner rocky planets and the four outer gas and ice giants.

<Card title="Key Facts to Remember">
<Steps>
<Step title="Inner vs. outer" status="done">
The four inner planets are small, rocky, and close to the Sun. The four outer planets are large, gaseous, and far apart.
</Step>
<Step title="Sizes vary hugely" status="done">
Jupiter could hold over 1,300 Earths inside it. Mercury is barely larger than our Moon.
</Step>
<Step title="All orbit in the same plane" status="done">
The planets formed from a spinning disk of gas and dust, so they all orbit the Sun in roughly the same flat plane, the ecliptic.
</Step>
<Step title="Pluto is not a planet" status="done">
In 2006, Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet because it hasn't cleared its orbit of other debris.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
