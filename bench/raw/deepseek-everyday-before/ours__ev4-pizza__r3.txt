# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Neapolitan pizza is defined by its soft, puffy cornicione (crust), leopard-spotted char, and simple, high-quality toppings. Getting authentic results at home requires the right technique, but it's absolutely achievable.

<Callout tone="info" title="The Neapolitan Standard">
True Neapolitan pizza (pizza napoletana) is certified by the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (AVPN). Key requirements: 00 flour, San Marzano tomatoes, fresh mozzarella (fior di latte or buffalo), cooked in a wood-fired oven at 485°C (905°F) for 60–90 seconds.
</Callout>

## The Essential Ingredients

<Card title="What you'll need">
<Columns>
<div>

**For the dough (makes 4 pizzas):**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Caputo "00" flour (or other Italian 00) | 500 g (4 cups) |
| Water | 325 g (1⅓ cups), room temp |
| Fine sea salt | 10 g (2 tsp) |
| Fresh yeast | 2 g (½ tsp) OR instant yeast 1 g (¼ tsp) |

**For the topping (per pizza):**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| San Marzano tomatoes | 80–100 g, crushed |
| Fresh mozzarella (fior di latte) | 80–100 g, torn |
| Fresh basil | 2–3 leaves |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | Drizzle |
</div>
<div>

**Equipment you'll need:**

- Pizza steel or stone (or cast-iron skillet)
- Pizza peel (wooden or metal)
- Digital kitchen scale
- Large mixing bowl
- Oven thermometer
- Spray bottle with water (optional, for steam)

<Callout tone="warning" title="Skip the rolling pin">
Never roll Neapolitan dough with a pin. It deflates the air bubbles that create the puffy crust. Stretch by hand only.
</Callout>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Step-by-Step Process

<Steps>
<Step title="Make the dough" status="current" subtitle="Day 1 — 15 minutes active">
In a large bowl, dissolve the yeast in the water. Add most of the flour and mix until shaggy. Add the salt and remaining flour, then knead for 10–15 minutes until the dough is smooth and elastic. It should pass the windowpane test — stretch a small piece without tearing. The dough will be sticky; resist adding more flour.
</Step>

<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="todo" subtitle="Day 1 — 8–12 hours at room temperature">
Cover the bowl with plastic wrap or a damp towel. Let it rise at room temperature (ideally 20–25°C / 68–77°F) for 8–12 hours, until doubled and domed. The longer the ferment, the more flavor.
</Step>

<Step title="Shape the balls" status="todo" subtitle="Day 1 — 10 minutes">
Gently turn the dough onto a floured surface. Divide into 4 equal pieces (about 210 g each). Fold each piece's edges into the center, then flip it seam-side down and roll it on the counter with cupped hands to form a tight ball. Place on a floured tray, cover, and rest 15–20 minutes.
</Step>

<Step title="Cold ferment" status="todo" subtitle="Day 1–2 — 24–72 hours in fridge">
For the best flavor, refrigerate the dough balls for 24–72 hours. This slow fermentation develops complex, tangy notes. Take the dough out 2–3 hours before baking to warm to room temperature.
</Step>

<Step title="Prepare the oven" status="todo" subtitle="Day 2 — 45 minutes before baking">
Place your pizza steel or stone on the top rack of your oven. Preheat to maximum (500°F / 260°C if your oven goes that high). Let it heat for at least 45 minutes. If you have a broiler, turn it on for the last 10 minutes to build extra top heat.
</Step>

<Step title="Stretch the dough" status="todo" subtitle="Day 2 — 2 minutes per pizza">
On a well-floured surface, press the dough ball flat with your fingertips, leaving the outer inch untouched. Pick it up and let gravity stretch it, rotating your hands, until it's about 10–12 inches across — thinner in the center, puffy at the rim. Don't overwork it.
</Step>

<Step title="Top and bake" status="todo" subtitle="Day 2 — 90 seconds per pizza">
Spoon crushed tomatoes into the center and spread in a spiral, leaving a 1-inch border. Add torn mozzarella, basil, and a drizzle of olive oil. Launch onto the hot steel. Bake 60–90 seconds, rotating once with the peel, until the crust is puffy and charred in spots. The cheese should be molten, not browned.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Broiler trick">
For a home oven, after the pizza is mostly baked, switch to broil for the last 30–60 seconds. This adds top heat that mimics a wood-fired oven's ceiling, giving you better cornicione rise and char.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting Common Problems

<Details title="Dough is too sticky to shape">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Sticky dough">
This is normal — Neapolitan dough is ~65% hydration. Work with well-floured hands and surface. If it's unmanageable, let it rest 10 minutes and try again. Don't add flour to the dough itself; that makes it tough.
</Callout>
</Details>

<Details title="Crust isn't puffing up">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Flat crust">
Your dough likely lost its air during shaping. Be gentler — don't press on the cornicione. Also check your dough was fully proofed (room temp, doubled) before shaping. A cold, underproofed dough won't puff.
</Callout>
</Details>

<Details title="Bottom is burnt but top is undercooked">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Uneven cooking">
Your steel is too hot, or the pizza sat too long. Reduce oven temp slightly, or bake higher in the oven. Also, make sure you're rotating the pizza during baking.
</Callout>
</Details>

<Details title="Crust is tough and chewy">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Tough crust">
This usually means too much flour was used during shaping, or the dough was overworked. Use a light dusting of semolina for the peel instead of flour — it doesn't absorb into the dough.
</Callout>
</Details>

## The Classic Margherita

<Card title="Pizza Margherita — the benchmark">
<Gallery>
![Margherita pizza with charred crust and basil](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1574071318508-1cdbab80d002?w=1200&h=800&fit=crop)
</Gallery>

The Margherita is the ultimate test of technique: just tomato, mozzarella, basil, and olive oil. If you can make a great Margherita, you can make any Neapolitan pizza.

**Pro tips:**
- Use **whole peeled San Marzano tomatoes** and crush them by hand — don't use pre-crushed or pureed.
- **Drain the mozzarella** well and tear it, don't slice. Sliced mozzarella releases too much water.
- Add the **basil after baking** if you want it bright green, or before if you want it crispy.
- A **pinch of salt** on the tomatoes balances their sweetness.
</Card>

## What makes it authentic?

<Card title="The AVPN rules">
The Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana has strict requirements for true Neapolitan pizza:

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| Flour | Type 00 wheat flour |
| Tomatoes | San Marzano or Roma tomatoes |
| Cheese | Fresh mozzarella (fior di latte or buffalo) |
| Oven | Wood-fired at 485°C (905°F) |
| Bake time | 60–90 seconds |
| Crust thickness | No more than 1–2 cm at the edge |
| Center thickness | No more than 0.25 cm |
| Diameter | No more than 35 cm (14 in) |
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="The final word">
You don't need a wood-fired oven to make excellent Neapolitan-style pizza at home. A hot steel, well-fermented dough, and quality ingredients will get you remarkably close. The most important factor is practice — each pizza teaches you something about dough handling and heat management.
</Callout>
