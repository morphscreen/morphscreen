# Should I rent or buy a flat?

Your income of £60k and rent of £1,800/month puts you in a common dilemma. Buying often beats renting long-term, but only if you're ready for the upfront costs and commitment. Let's break it down.

| Factor | Rent | Buy |
|---|---|---|

| Monthly cost | £1,800 fixed | ~£1,200–1,600 mortgage + bills |
| Maintenance | Landlord pays | You pay (~1% of value/year) |
| Freedom to move | Easy | Harder, costly to sell |
| Building equity | No | Yes, over time |
| Price risk | None | House prices can fall |

<Callout tone="info" title="Rough maths">
On £60k, most lenders offer ~4.5× salary = **£270k mortgage**. With a 10% deposit (£30k), you could buy around **£300k**. Your monthly mortgage at ~4.5% over 30 years would be ~**£1,370** — less than your rent, but you'd also pay ~£150–250 in insurance, service charges and upkeep.
</Callout>

<Card title="When buying makes sense">
- You plan to stay **5+ years** — selling costs (~3–5% of price) eat short-term gains
- You have a **stable job** and can handle rate rises
- You can save a **10–20% deposit** without emptying emergency funds

<Card title="When renting is better">
- You may **move for work** or family within 3–4 years
- You want **flexibility** and no repair headaches
- House prices in your area are **overheated** or falling
- Your deposit would leave you **cash-poor**

<Card title="A quick decision guide">
<Steps>
<Step title="Can you afford a 10% deposit?" status="current" subtitle="£30k for a £300k flat — plus £3–5k in fees">
If not, keep renting and save. Consider a Lifetime ISA for a 25% bonus.
</Step>
<Step title="Will you stay put for 5+ years?" status="todo" subtitle="Moving costs usually wipe out early equity">
If you might relocate soon, renting is cheaper overall.
</Step>
<Step title="Are you comfortable with maintenance and rate rises?" status="todo" subtitle="Budget ~£250/month extra on top of the mortgage">
If yes, buying builds wealth. If no, renting buys peace of mind.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="Bottom line">
If you have a **£30k+ deposit** and plan to stay **5 years or more**, buying likely wins — you'd pay less monthly and build equity. If you're unsure about the future or can't save the deposit within 2–3 years, **renting is financially sensible** — just make sure you're investing the gap.
</Callout>
