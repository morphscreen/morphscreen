# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making authentic Neapolitan pizza (VPN-style) at home is about mastering three things: the dough, the sauce, and the heat. While a wood-fired oven reaches 900°F, you can get surprisingly close with a home oven and a few tricks.

<Card title="The Golden Rules of Neapolitan Pizza" subtitle="What makes it 'proper'">
- **Dough**: High-hydration (60–65%), 24–48 hour cold fermentation, 00 flour
- **Sauce**: San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand, raw — never cooked
- **Cheese**: Fresh mozzarella (fior di latte) or buffalo mozzarella, torn, not shredded
- **Bake time**: 60–90 seconds at 800–900°F (or as close as you can get)
- **Shape**: Thin center (1–2 mm), puffy, leopard-spotted cornicione (crust)
</Card>

## Step 1: The Dough

<Card title="Ingredients" subtitle="For 4 pizzas (about 250 g each)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| 00 flour (Caputo Pizzeria or similar) | 1 kg |
| Water (room temperature) | 600–650 g |
| Fine sea salt | 20–25 g |
| Fresh yeast | 2 g (or 0.5 g dry yeast) |

**Method:**

1. Dissolve the yeast in a small amount of the water.
2. In a large bowl, mix the flour and salt. Add the yeast-water and remaining water.
3. Knead for 10–15 minutes until smooth and elastic (or use a stand mixer with a dough hook for 8–10 minutes).
4. Cover and let rest 1 hour at room temperature.
5. Divide into 4 balls (about 250 g each), place in an oiled container, and refrigerate for **24–48 hours**.
6. Take the dough out 2–3 hours before baking to come to room temperature.

<Callout tone="info" title="Why 00 flour?">
Caputo 00 flour has a fine grind and high protein quality designed specifically for Neapolitan pizza. All-purpose flour won't give you the same stretch and puff.
</Callout>

<Card title="Dough Troubleshooting">
<Details title="Dough won't stretch">
If the dough tears or springs back, it's too cold or under-fermented. Let it rest 30 minutes more at room temperature, and be gentle — don't use a rolling pin, stretch by hand from the center outward.
</Details>
<Details title="Crust too dense">
Your hydration might be too low, or the dough didn't ferment long enough. Aim for 62–65% hydration and a full 24-hour cold ferment.
</Details>
<Details title="Too sticky">
Use a bench scraper and lightly flour your work surface. Don't add too much extra flour to the dough itself — it will make the crust tough.
</Details>
</Card>

## Step 2: The Sauce and Toppings

<Card title="Classic Marinara" subtitle="The simplest, most authentic sauce">
- 1 can (400 g) **San Marzano tomatoes** (DOP if possible)
- 1 clove garlic, thinly sliced
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- Pinch of salt
- Fresh basil leaves (optional)

Crush the tomatoes by hand or with a potato masher. Add the garlic, oil, and salt. **Do not cook the sauce** — it goes on the pizza raw and cooks in the oven.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't over-sauce">
Use only 2–3 tablespoons per pizza. Neapolitan pizza is about balance — the sauce should complement, not drown, the dough and cheese.
</Callout>

<Card title="Topping Guide">
| Pizza | Topping |
|---|---|
| **Margherita** | Tomato sauce, fresh mozzarella (torn), basil, drizzle of olive oil |
| **Marinara** | Tomato sauce, garlic, oregano, olive oil — no cheese |
| **Diavola** | Tomato sauce, mozzarella, spicy salami |
| **Bufalina** | Tomato sauce, buffalo mozzarella (added after baking for freshness), basil |

For the mozzarella, tear it into 1-inch pieces and **drain** it on paper towels for 30 minutes before using — excess moisture will make your pizza soggy.

</Card>

## Step 3: Baking — The Heat Challenge

<Card title="Baking Options" subtitle="Ranked from best to most accessible">
| Method | Temperature | Bake Time | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood-fired oven | 900°F (480°C) | 60–90 sec | Perfect leopard spots, puffy crust |
| Pizza oven attachment (e.g., Ooni, Roccbox) | 800–950°F | 60–90 sec | Very close to authentic |
| Home oven + pizza steel/stone | 550°F max | 5–7 min | Good, but not identical |
| Home oven + broiler trick | 550°F + broiler | 4–5 min | Better browning on top |

<Callout tone="info" title="The broiler trick">
If you only have a home oven, preheat your steel/stone at 550°F for 45+ minutes, then switch to the broiler for the last 2 minutes of baking. This gives you a blast of top heat to char the crust.
</Callout>

<Card title="Baking Steps">
<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat" status="done">Preheat your steel or stone at maximum oven temperature for at least 45 minutes — longer is better.</Step>
<Step title="Prep the peel" status="done">Lightly flour a wooden or metal peel. Stretch the dough, top it, and slide it onto the peel — shake to make sure it moves freely.</Step>
<Step title="Launch" status="current">Slide the pizza onto the hot steel/stone with a quick forward-and-back motion. If using a home oven, switch to broil after 2–3 minutes.</Step>
<Step title="Rotate" status="todo">After 2–3 minutes, rotate the pizza 180° for even browning. Total bake: 5–7 minutes in a home oven, 60–90 seconds in a wood-fired or pizza oven.</Step>
<Step title="Finish" status="todo">The crust should be puffy with charred spots (leopard pattern). Top with fresh basil and a drizzle of olive oil, slice, and serve immediately.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Step 4: The Stretch and Shape

<Card title="Hand-Stretching Technique">
1. **Flour the dough ball** lightly and place it on a floured surface.
2. **Press** from the center outward with your fingertips, leaving a 1-inch border untouched (this becomes the cornicione).
3. **Stretch** by draping the dough over your knuckles and gently rotating it, letting gravity do the work.
4. **Don't use a rolling pin** — it flattens the air bubbles that create a puffy crust.

<Callout tone="danger" title="Common mistake">
Pressing all the way to the edge flattens the cornicione. Keep that border thick — it's what makes Neapolitan pizza distinctive.
</Callout>

</Card>

## Step 5: Serving Like a Neapolitan

<Card title="The Right Way to Eat It">
- **Don't cut it** with a knife — Neapolitans eat it with a knife and fork, folded in half (the *portafoglio* style).
- **Eat it immediately** — the crust goes stale within minutes.
- **Use your hands** if you must, but fold it lengthwise to keep the toppings in.
- **Pair with** a cold Italian lager or a light red wine like a Chianti.

## Equipment Checklist

<Card title="What You Need">
| Essential | Nice to Have |
|---|---|
| Pizza steel or stone | Pizza oven (Ooni, Roccbox, etc.) |
| Pizza peel (wooden or metal) | Digital scale |
| 00 flour | Infrared thermometer |
| San Marzano tomatoes | Stand mixer with dough hook |
| Fresh mozzarella | Bench scraper |

<Callout tone="success" title="The verdict">
A home oven + steel will get you 85% of the way to a true Neapolitan pizza. If you're serious, a dedicated pizza oven (they can be had for under $300) closes the gap almost entirely. The dough recipe and technique are the same either way — the heat is the only real difference.
</Callout>
</Card>
