# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

<Callout tone="info" title="The gold standard">
True Neapolitan pizza (pizza napoletana) is protected by strict rules from the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (AVPN). You can get very close at home with the right technique and equipment.
</Callout>

<Card title="What you'll need" subtitle="Essential equipment and ingredients">
<Columns>
<div>

### Equipment

- Pizza stone or steel (at least 1/4" thick steel is best)
- Oven capable of 500°F (260°C) minimum — ideally 550°F or higher
- Kitchen scale (grams are essential)
- Pizza peel
- Large mixing bowl
- Dough scraper
- Parchment paper (for launching)

</div>
<div>

### Ingredients (for 4 pizzas)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| "00" flour (Caputo Pizzeria is ideal) | 500 g |
| Water (room temp) | 325 g (65% hydration) |
| Fine sea salt | 10 g (2%) |
| Fresh yeast or active dry yeast | 1 g fresh or 0.5 g dry |
| San Marzano tomatoes | 1 can (400 g) |
| Fresh mozzarella di bufala | 250 g |
| Fresh basil | 1 handful |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | For drizzling |

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

## The Dough — The Heart of It All

<Card title="Making the dough" subtitle="Day before: mix, ferment, and shape">
<Steps>
<Step title="Mix the dough" status="done">
In a large bowl, dissolve the yeast in the water. Add the flour and mix until a shaggy mass forms. Let it rest 20 minutes (autolyse).
</Step>

<Step title="Add salt and knead" status="done">
Add the salt and knead the dough for 10–15 minutes until smooth and elastic. It should pass the windowpane test — stretch a small piece thin enough to see light through without tearing.
</Step>

<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done">
Cover the bowl and let the dough rise at room temperature for 8–12 hours, until it has doubled in volume. This slow fermentation builds flavor.
</Step>

<Step title="Divide and ball" status="done">
Turn the dough out onto a floured surface. Divide into 4 equal pieces (about 210 g each). Shape each into a tight ball by pulling the edges to the center and pinching. Place on a floured tray, cover, and refrigerate 12–24 hours.
</Step>

<Step title="Bring to room temp" status="current">
Remove the dough balls from the fridge 2–3 hours before baking. They should be relaxed, soft, and slightly puffy.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## The Sauce and Toppings

<Card title="The simplest sauce" subtitle="Less is more — no cooking required">
<Callout tone="success" title="Rule of thumb">
Neapolitan sauce is raw. Crush San Marzano tomatoes by hand, add a pinch of salt, and that's it. Never cook it before baking.
</Callout>

<Columns>
<div>

**The sauce:**

1. Drain the can of San Marzano tomatoes slightly
2. Crush them by hand or with a potato masher
3. Add a pinch of fine sea salt
4. That's it — no garlic, no oregano, no olive oil (the oil goes on top)

</div>
<div>

**The mozzarella:**

- Use **fresh mozzarella di bufala** if possible
- Tear it into 1-inch pieces
- **Drain it well** on paper towels for at least 30 minutes — excess moisture is the enemy of a crisp crust
- Regular fresh mozzarella (fior di latte) works well too

</div>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't overload">
A Neapolitan pizza should have *just* enough sauce and cheese. Too much topping makes the center soggy. Aim for about 3–4 tablespoons of sauce and 3–4 ounces of mozzarella per pizza.
</Callout>
</Card>

## Shaping and Baking

<Card title="Shaping the pizza" subtitle="The stretch, not the roll">
<Steps>
<Step title="Flour your work surface" status="todo">
Use "00" flour or semolina to dust. Take one dough ball and press it flat with your fingertips, starting from the center and pushing outward — leave the outer 1/2 inch rim untouched.
</Step>

<Step title="Stretch by hand" status="todo">
Pick up the dough and stretch it gently with your knuckles, rotating it like a wheel. The center should be thin (1/8 inch) while the rim stays thick. Don't use a rolling pin — it crushes the air bubbles.
</Step>

<Step title="Check the shape" status="todo">
The finished disc should be about 10–12 inches across. A slightly irregular, oval shape is authentic and charming.
</Step>

<Step title="Top it quickly" status="todo">
Spoon the sauce into the center and spread it outward in a spiral, leaving the rim clear. Scatter the mozzarella, add 2–3 basil leaves, and drizzle with olive oil.
</Step>
</Card>

<Card title="Baking" subtitle="The hottest oven you can get">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Heat is everything">
A real Neapolitan pizza bakes at 900°F (485°C) for 60–90 seconds. Home ovens top out around 550°F, so we compensate with a longer bake and a preheated stone or steel.
</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat aggressively" status="todo">
Place your pizza steel or stone on the **top rack**. Preheat the oven at maximum temperature (550°F or 260°C) for at least 45–60 minutes. Turn on the broiler for the last 10 minutes to get extra heat from above.
</Step>

<Step title="Assemble on parchment" status="todo">
Build the pizza on a sheet of parchment paper. This makes launching much easier — you can slide the whole thing onto the peel, then into the oven.
</Step>

<Step title="Launch and bake" status="todo">
Slide the pizza (on its parchment) onto the hot steel. Bake for 4–6 minutes, rotating once halfway through. The crust should get leopard-spotted char marks and the cheese should be bubbling.
</Step>

<Step title="Broiler finish (optional)" status="todo">
For the last 30–60 seconds, switch to broil to get more color on top. Watch it closely — it can burn fast.
</Step>

<Step title="Rest and serve" status="todo">
Remove from the oven, slide off the parchment, and let rest 2 minutes. The center should be soft and foldable — that's the mark of a true Neapolitan. Serve immediately.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## The Classic Margherita

<Card title="Pizza Margherita — the benchmark">
<Columns>
<div>

The **Margherita** is the ultimate test: tomato, mozzarella, basil, olive oil. Named after Queen Margherita of Savoy in 1889, its red, white, and green colors represent the Italian flag.

</div>
<div>

<KeyValue title="Margherita specs">
| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| Sauce | San Marzano tomatoes, crushed |
| Cheese | Fresh mozzarella di bufala |
| Finish | Fresh basil + EVOO |
| Bake time | 4–6 min at 550°F |
| Crust | Soft, leopard-spotted, foldable |

</KeyValue>

## Troubleshooting

<Card title="Common problems and fixes">
<Columns>
<div>

**Soggy center?**

- Too much sauce or cheese — reduce both
- Mozzarella not drained enough — pat it dry
- Oven not hot enough — preheat longer

</div>
<div>

**Dough not stretching?**

- It's too cold — let it warm up longer
- Not enough gluten development — knead more
- Let it rest 10 minutes before stretching again

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>
