## 1. Quarry Rock (Deep Cove)

<Card title="Quarry Rock">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Overview">
**Distance:** 8 km round trip  
**Elevation gain:** 150 m  
**Time:** 2–3 hours  
**Difficulty:** Easy–Moderate

A classic North Shore hike with panoramic views over Deep Cove and Indian Arm. The trail winds through mossy forest before opening onto a granite outcrop with postcard-worthy views.

<Badge tone="success">Family-friendly</Badge> <Badge tone="info">Great for beginners</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Trail details">
The trail starts behind the Deep Cove parking lot and climbs gently through Douglas-fir and cedar forest. The final section is a series of wooden stairs and boardwalks. The viewpoint at the top is a large flat rock with benches — perfect for a picnic.

**Best time:** Early morning or weekday to avoid crowds.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Getting there">
- **Drive:** 30 minutes from downtown Vancouver via Highway 1, exit at Deep Cove.
- **Public transit:** Bus 212 from Phibbs Exchange or the SeaBus and bus combo.
- **Parking:** Very limited — arrive before 8 am on weekends or use the shuttle from Parkgate Community Centre.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

## 2. Stawamus Chief (Squamish)

<Card title="Stawamus Chief">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Overview">
**Distance:** 11 km round trip (all three peaks)  
**Elevation gain:** 700 m  
**Time:** 4–6 hours  
**Difficulty:** Hard

The Chief is the granite monolith that towers over Squamish, offering some of the most dramatic views in the region. You can do one, two, or all three peaks.

<Badge tone="warning">Strenuous</Badge> <Badge tone="danger">Steep drop-offs</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Trail details">
- **First Peak:** Steep switchbacks with some chain-assisted sections. Views over Howe Sound.
- **Second Peak:** The classic route with the famous "chains" — metal cables to hold onto on exposed ledges.
- **Third Peak:** Smaller but quieter, with a view down the backside.

All three peaks are connected by a ridge trail. Many hikers do Peak 1 and 2, then descend.

**Note:** Not for those afraid of heights. Bring gloves for the chains if you have them.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Getting there">
- **Drive:** 1 hour north of Vancouver on Highway 99 (Sea to Sky).
- **Parking:** Large lot at the base, but fills by 9 am on weekends.
- **Shuttle:** Park Royal (West Vancouver) to Squamish on weekends in summer.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

## 3. Joffre Lakes (Pemberton)

<Card title="Joffre Lakes">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Overview">
**Distance:** 10 km round trip  
**Elevation gain:** 370 m  
**Time:** 4–5 hours  
**Difficulty:** Moderate

Three turquoise glacier-fed lakes connected by a trail that climbs through alpine meadows. The colour of the water is unreal — a vivid milky blue from glacial silt.

<Badge tone="success">Instagram-worthy</Badge> <Badge tone="warning">Very busy</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Trail details">
- **Lower Lake:** 5 minutes from the parking lot.
- **Middle Lake:** The main climb happens here, with a waterfall and suspension bridge.
- **Upper Lake:** The payoff — a huge turquoise lake with a glacier backdrop. There's a backcountry campground here if you want to stay overnight.

**Best time:** June–October. In spring, snow lingers on the upper trail.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Getting there">
- **Drive:** 2.5 hours from Vancouver, just past Pemberton on Highway 99.
- **Day-use pass required:** You must book a free day pass online 48 hours in advance (BC Parks). They sell out within minutes.
- **Parking:** The lot is tiny — the pass system limits visitors, but you still need to arrive early.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

## 4. Lynn Canyon Park (North Vancouver)

<Card title="Lynn Canyon Park">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Overview">
**Distance:** 5 km of trails (choose your own loop)  
**Elevation gain:** 100 m  
**Time:** 1.5–2.5 hours  
**Difficulty:** Easy

A lush rainforest canyon with a famous suspension bridge (free, unlike Capilano), waterfalls, and a 30-metre-deep pool called the "Twin Falls."

<Badge tone="success">Free entry</Badge> <Badge tone="info">Perfect for families</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Trail details">
The park has a network of well-marked trails. The most popular loop:

1. Cross the suspension bridge over the canyon.
2. Take the Baden-Powell Trail down to Twin Falls.
3. Climb back up through old-growth cedar and fir forest.
4. Finish at the Lynn Canyon Ecology Centre (free, great for kids).

**Swimming:** The pool at Twin Falls is cold but popular with brave swimmers in summer.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Getting there">
- **Drive:** 25 minutes from downtown via the Ironworkers Memorial Bridge.
- **Public transit:** Bus 228 from Lonsdale Quay or 230 from Phibbs Exchange.
- **Parking:** Free lot, but fills on sunny weekends. There's overflow parking nearby.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

## 5. Garibaldi Lake (Squamish Valley)

<Card title="Garibaldi Lake">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Overview">
**Distance:** 18 km round trip  
**Elevation gain:** 810 m  
**Time:** 6–8 hours  
**Difficulty:** Hard

A relentless but rewarding grind up to one of the most beautiful alpine lakes in BC. The water is a deep blue-green, surrounded by jagged peaks and a tiny volcanic island.

<Badge tone="danger">Long day</Badge> <Badge tone="success">Unforgettable views</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Trail details">
The trail is essentially a long, steady climb on a wide gravel path — not technical, but it never lets up. The last 2 km are on a gentle grade through meadows before the lake suddenly appears.

**Optional extra:** Continue 3 km to Panorama Ridge for a view over the lake and Black Tusk — add 2–3 hours and 500 m of elevation.

**Best time:** July–September. Snow lingers into June.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Getting there">
- **Drive:** 1.5 hours north of Vancouver on Highway 99, turn at the Rubble Creek exit.
- **Parking:** Large lot at the trailhead, but fills by 8 am on summer weekends.
- **Day-use pass:** Required from June to October (book 48 hours ahead on BC Parks).
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

## Essential tips for hiking near Vancouver

<Card title="Before you go">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Safety first">
- **Weather changes fast** in the mountains. Pack a warm layer and rain shell even on sunny days.
- **Tell someone your route** and expected return time.
- **No cell service** on most trails — download offline maps (AllTrails or Gaia GPS).
</Callout>

<Callout tone="info" title="Check before you drive">
- **Day-use passes** are required for Joffre Lakes, Garibaldi Lake, and Stawamus Chief (May–Oct). Book on BC Parks 48 hours ahead.
- **Trail closures** happen due to wildlife (bears), logging, or maintenance. Check the BC Parks website or local hiking groups.
- **Parking fills early.** Aim to arrive by 7 am on weekends for popular trails.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="success" title="Pack these">
- **2 litres of water** per person (more on hot days)
- **Bear spray** — bears are common on the North Shore
- **Sunscreen and a hat** — UV is strong at altitude
- **Snacks with protein** — nuts, energy bars, jerky
- **Headlamp** even for day hikes — you never know
</Callout>
</Card>

### Quarry Rock

It's not the most dramatic hike, but it's the perfect introduction to Vancouver's outdoor scene:

- **Manageable for most fitness levels**
- **Stunning coastal views** with zero technical difficulty
- **Close to the city** — you can be back in time for lunch
- **Great for groups** — no need for bear spray or advanced planning

Go on a clear weekday morning and you'll have the viewpoint nearly to yourself.
