# Neapolitan pizza at home

The real thing needs three things most home kitchens fight against: a **very hot oven**, a **very wet dough**, and **time**. You can win all three with a little planning.

<Callout tone="info" title="The honest version">
A home oven tops out around 260 °C. True Neapolitan bakes at 430–480 °C for 60–90 seconds. You can't fully match it, but a preheated steel or stone on the top rack plus a broiler gets you 80% of the way — a puffy, leopard-spotted, 3-minute pizza.
</Callout>

<Card title="What you actually need" subtitle="Four ingredients, one piece of gear">
<KeyValue title="The dough">
| Item | Amount (for 4 pizzas) |
|---|---|
| Flour (00 or bread flour) | 500 g |
| Water | 325 g (65% hydration) |
| Salt | 12 g |
| Fresh yeast | 1 g (or 0.4 g instant dry) |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Timeline" subtitle="Start the day before — this is the whole trick">
<Steps>
<Step title="Mix the dough" status="done" subtitle="5 minutes, no kneading skill needed">
Dissolve yeast in the water, add flour and salt, mix until no dry patches remain. It will look shaggy and sticky.
</Step>
<Step title="Rest 20 minutes, then fold" status="done" subtitle="Builds strength without kneading">
Wet your hand, grab one edge, stretch it up and fold over the middle. Rotate and repeat 4 times. Do this 3–4 times over an hour.
</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment 24–48 hours" status="current" subtitle="The flavor step">
Cover and refrigerate. This is where the dough becomes digestible, flavorful and easy to stretch. Don't skip it.
</Step>
<Step title="Ball and warm up" status="todo" subtitle="2–3 hours before baking">
Divide into 4 balls (~210 g each), tuck each into a tight ball, cover. Let them come to room temperature and puff.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="todo" subtitle="90 seconds to 3 minutes">
Preheat steel/stone at max for 45–60 min, switch to broiler just before baking.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="The bake" subtitle="Where most home attempts fail">
<Checklist title="Bake checklist" name="bake">
- [ ] Oven + steel/stone preheated at maximum for at least 45 minutes
- [ ] Broiler (top grill) switched on 10 minutes before the first pizza
- [ ] Dough ball stretched by hand from the center out, leaving a 2 cm rim untouched
- [ ] Never a rolling pin — it crushes the gas bubbles in the rim
- [ ] Sauce: canned San Marzano, crushed by hand, salt only. No cooking.
- [ ] Toppings light: 60–80 g mozzarella, drained and torn. Wet mozzarella floods the pizza.
- [ ] Flour the peel with semolina, not wet dough, or it sticks
- [ ] Slide onto the steel, bake 2–3 min, rotate once
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Fresh mozzarella">
Buffalo mozzarella is the classic, but it's wet. Tear it and let it drain on paper towels for 30 minutes, or the center of your pizza turns to soup.
</Callout>

<Card title="Common mistakes">
<Compare title="Do this, not that">
| Step | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Flour | 00 or strong bread flour | All-purpose (weak, tears) |
| Ferment | 24–48 h cold | Same-day only |
| Sauce | Raw, crushed tomato | Cooked, reduced sauce |
| Cheese | Drained, torn mozzarella | Shredded low-moisture |
| Stretch | Hands, from center out | Rolling pin |
| Oven | Max heat + broiler + steel | Middle rack on a tray |
| Bake time | 2–3 min, rotating | 10 min at 200 °C |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Signs you nailed it">
Puffy, airy rim with dark leopard spots, a soft and foldable base, no soggy center, and a bottom that's crisp but not burnt. If the rim is pale and flat, your oven wasn't hot enough or your dough was under-fermented.
</Callout>
