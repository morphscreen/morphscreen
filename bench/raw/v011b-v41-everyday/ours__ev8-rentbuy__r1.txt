# Rent vs buy in your situation

Short answer: at £60k income and £1,800/month rent, buying is usually worth it **if** you can raise a deposit of roughly £30–45k and plan to stay put for **at least 5 years**. Below that horizon, renting usually wins once you count fees, moving costs and the risk of a flat sale taking 6+ months.

<Callout tone="info" title="The one number that decides it">
Your rent is £21,600 a year — about 36% of your gross income. A mortgage on the same flat would likely cost **less per month** than your rent, but only after you've sunk a deposit and ~£5–10k of one-off costs. The question is really "how long until those costs are paid back by the monthly saving?"
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Read that middle row again">
On a like-for-like flat, the **monthly cash out is close to a wash** once service charge and maintenance are added. The gain from buying isn't the monthly saving — it's the ~£450–600/month of principal you're paying down instead of a landlord's mortgage, plus any price growth.
</Callout>

At £60k, most lenders cap you around £270k. Add a £30k deposit and you're looking at flats up to ~£300k. Existing debts (car finance, credit cards, student loan) cut that figure. A broker will tell you in an hour what you can really borrow.
</Callout>

<Card title="A realistic 12-month path if you decide to buy">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check your borrowing power" status="current" subtitle="Month 1">
Talk to a fee-free broker. Get an agreement in principle. This costs nothing and tells you your true ceiling.
</Step>
<Step title="Build the deposit to £30–45k" status="todo" subtitle="Months 1–9">
On £60k you're likely taking home ~£3,700/month. If you can save £800–1,000/month, you're there in 9–12 months — faster if you already have savings.
</Step>
<Step title="Clear small debts and tidy your credit file" status="todo" subtitle="Months 2–6">
Pay down cards below 30% utilisation, get on the electoral roll, don't open new credit.
</Step>
<Step title="Get the agreement in principle and start viewing" status="todo" subtitle="Months 9–12">
Now you can offer seriously. Budget £5–8k on top of the deposit for stamp duty, legal, survey and moving.
</Step>
<Step title="Offer, survey, mortgage, exchange" status="todo" subtitle="Months 12–15">
Expect 3–4 months from offer to keys. Don't give notice on your rental until you've exchanged.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="My read for you specifically">
At £60k with £1,800 rent, you're paying a lot for someone else's asset. If you have £30k+ sitting available and you're confident you'll be in the same area for 5 years, buying a flat in the £250–300k range is the better long-run move. If your deposit would be thin, or your life is in flux, keep renting and save hard — you lose almost nothing by waiting a year or two.
</Callout>
