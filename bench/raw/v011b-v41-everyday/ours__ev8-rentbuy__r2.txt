# Rent or buy? The honest numbers for £60k and £1,800/month

The short version: at £60k income and £1,800 rent, buying is usually cheaper **per month** than renting — but only if you can raise the deposit and stay put for 5+ years. Here's the arithmetic, then the parts that decide it.

<Callout tone="info" title="What I'm assuming">
£60k gross ≈ £4,050/month take-home (England, standard tax code, 5% pension). £1,800 rent = 44% of take-home. A £300k flat with a 10% deposit is roughly the ceiling most lenders will offer at this income.
</Callout>

<Card title="The monthly comparison" subtitle="£300k flat, 10% deposit, 5-year fix at 5%">
| Cost | Rent now | Buy (first 5 years) |
|---|---|---|
| Housing payment | £1,800 | £1,424 mortgage |
| Service charge / ground rent | £0 | £180 |
| Buildings insurance | £0 | £25 |
| Maintenance (1% of value/yr) | £0 | £250 |
| **Monthly total** | **£1,800** | **£1,879** |
</Card>

So month-to-month it's close — buying is £79/month *more* until you count the fact that £700 of that mortgage is paying down your own debt rather than a landlord's.

<Callout tone="warning" title="The affordability squeeze">
A £270k mortgage at 5% over 30 years is £1,424/month. Lenders typically cap you at ~4.5× income (£270k), so a £300k flat is right at your limit — and a 10% deposit is the minimum most will take. Any rate rise on a variable deal, or a service charge jump, eats your margin fast.
</Callout>

Buying wins on total cost from **around year 3**, once fees and stamp duty are absorbed and equity builds. Before that, renting is cheaper and far more flexible.

| Cost if you move in 2 years | Low | High |

<Card title="What would have to be true to buy">
<Checklist title="Buying readiness" name="buying-readiness">
- [ ] £35k in cash you can spend without touching your emergency fund
- [ ] A flat you'd genuinely stay in for 5+ years
- [ ] Service charge under ~£2,000/year and no cladding or major works looming
- [ ] Mortgage payment under 35% of take-home after the fix ends
- [ ] Emergency fund of 3–6 months left over after completion
- [ ] You're not planning a move, career break, or big income drop
</Checklist>
