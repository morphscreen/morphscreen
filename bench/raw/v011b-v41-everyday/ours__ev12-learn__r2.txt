# Photosynthesis, from sunlight to sugar

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria turn light energy into chemical energy. In one sentence: **light + water + carbon dioxide → sugar + oxygen**, powered by chlorophyll.

<Card title="The big picture" subtitle="Two stages, one after the other">
<Steps>
<Step title="Light hits the leaf" status="done" subtitle="Chlorophyll absorbs it">Pigments in the chloroplast trap photons, mostly red and blue light; green is reflected, which is why leaves look green.</Step>
<Step title="Light reactions split water" status="done" subtitle="In the thylakoid membrane">The energy is used to split H₂O, releasing O₂ as waste and producing ATP and NADPH.</Step>
<Step title="Calvin cycle builds sugar" status="current" subtitle="In the stroma">ATP and NADPH drive CO₂ into a cycle that ends with G3P, which becomes glucose.</Step>
<Step title="Sugar leaves the leaf" status="todo" subtitle="Exported as sucrose">Glucose is packed into sucrose and shipped through the phloem to the rest of the plant.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="The two stages side by side">
<Compare title="Light reactions vs Calvin cycle">
| Feature | Light reactions | Calvin cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Needs light directly | yes | no |
| Where | Thylakoid membrane | Stroma |
| Inputs | H₂O, light, ADP, NADP⁺ | CO₂, ATP, NADPH |
| Outputs | O₂, ATP, NADPH | G3P (sugar) |
| Runs at night | no | yes, if ATP is left |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="The overall equation">
<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light} \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Balanced equation for photosynthesis" />

Six carbon dioxide molecules and six water molecules, using light, make one glucose and six oxygen molecules. The oxygen comes from the water, not the carbon dioxide — a detail worth remembering.
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Why the curve flattens">
Past a certain light level the rate stops climbing. Something else becomes the bottleneck — usually CO₂ concentration or temperature, since enzymes in the Calvin cycle have an optimum.
</Callout>

<Card title="Terms to lock in">
<Flashcards title="Photosynthesis flashcards">
| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | The green pigment that absorbs light energy |
| Thylakoid | The membrane stack where the light reactions happen |
| Stroma | The fluid around the thylakoids where the Calvin cycle runs |
| ATP | The cell's energy currency, made in the light reactions |
| NADPH | The electron carrier that powers the Calvin cycle |
| G3P | The three-carbon sugar that becomes glucose |
| Stomata | Leaf pores that let CO₂ in and O₂ out |
</Flashcards>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The one-line takeaway">
Plants capture light, split water to get electrons, and use those electrons to bolt carbon dioxide into sugar — releasing the oxygen we breathe as a side effect.
</Callout>
