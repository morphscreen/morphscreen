# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae, and some bacteria use to turn **light energy into chemical energy** stored in sugar. In one sentence: they take in water and carbon dioxide, use sunlight to rearrange the atoms, and make glucose plus oxygen.

<Card title="The big picture" subtitle="One equation, two stages">
<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Overall reaction" />

Six carbon dioxide + six water + light energy → one glucose + six oxygen.
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="The key insight">
The oxygen gas plants release comes from **water**, not carbon dioxide. This was proven with heavy-oxygen labeling in the 1930s and surprises almost everyone.
</Callout>

## The two stages

<Card title="Stage 1 — Light-dependent reactions" subtitle="In the thylakoid membrane">
<Steps>
<Step title="Light hits chlorophyll" status="done">Pigments in the thylakoid membrane absorb photons, mostly red and blue light.</Step>
<Step title="Water is split" status="done">The oxygen-evolving complex tears water apart: 2 H₂O → 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ + O₂. The O₂ is waste to the plant.</Step>
<Step title="Electrons travel the chain" status="done">Excited electrons move through photosystem II → plastoquinone → cytochrome complex → photosystem I, losing energy at each step.</Step>
<Step title="Energy is banked" status="current">That energy pumps protons into the thylakoid lumen, then drives ATP synthase to make ATP. Meanwhile NADP⁺ picks up electrons to become NADPH.</Step>
</Steps>

Outputs: **ATP** and **NADPH** — the "energy currency" that stage 2 spends.
</Card>

<Card title="Stage 2 — The Calvin cycle" subtitle="In the stroma, no light needed directly">
<Steps>
<Step title="Carbon fixation" status="done">The enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to a 5-carbon sugar (RuBP), making two 3-carbon molecules.</Step>
<Step title="Reduction" status="done">ATP and NADPH from stage 1 convert those into G3P, a usable 3-carbon sugar.</Step>
<Step title="Regeneration" status="current">Most G3P goes back to rebuild RuBP so the cycle can run again. The leftover G3P becomes glucose, then starch, cellulose, or sucrose.</Step>
</Steps>

It takes **3 turns** of the cycle to net one G3P, and **6 turns** to build one glucose.
</Card>

<Compare title="The two stages side by side">
| Feature | Light reactions | Calvin cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Thylakoid membrane | Stroma |
| Needs light directly | yes | no |
| Inputs | Water, light, ADP, NADP⁺ | CO₂, ATP, NADPH |
| Outputs | O₂, ATP, NADPH | G3P (sugar) |
| Runs at night | no | yes, if ATP/NADPH remain |
</Compare>

## What changes the rate

<Card title="Factors that speed it up or slow it down">
<KeyValue title="Rate drivers">
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Light intensity | Rises, then plateaus when photosystems saturate |
| CO₂ concentration | Rises, until RuBisCO maxes out |
| Temperature | Rises to an optimum (~25 °C for most C3 plants), then falls as enzymes denature |
| Water supply | Drought closes stomata, CO₂ can't enter, rate drops |
| Chlorophyll amount | More pigment, more photons captured |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The photorespiration trap">
RuBisCO sometimes grabs O₂ instead of CO₂, wasting energy. Hot, dry conditions make this worse because stomata close. C4 and CAM plants evolved workarounds — that's why corn and cacti thrive where wheat struggles.
</Callout>

## Remember these

<Flashcards title="Photosynthesis terms">
| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Thylakoid | The membrane stack where light reactions happen |
| Stroma | The fluid around thylakoids where the Calvin cycle runs |
| RuBisCO | The enzyme that fixes CO₂ onto RuBP |
| Photolysis | Splitting water with light to release electrons and O₂ |
| G3P | The 3-carbon sugar the Calvin cycle produces |
| Photorespiration | RuBisCO grabbing O₂ instead of CO₂, wasting energy |
| Chemiosmosis | Using a proton gradient to drive ATP synthase |
</Flashcards>
