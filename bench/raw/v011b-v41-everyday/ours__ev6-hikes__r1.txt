# Best day hikes near Vancouver

The North Shore mountains sit right at the city's edge, so you can be on a summit trail within 30–45 minutes of downtown. Below are the hikes worth your day, from easy forest walks to a proper full-day grind.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you go">
Most trailheads here are reached via Highway 1 or the Sea-to-Sky corridor. Parking fills by 8–9am on weekends, and BC Parks now requires free day-use passes for some trailheads (Cypress, Garibaldi) in peak season. Always carry water, layers, and a headlamp — coastal weather turns fast.
</Callout>

<Tab title="Easy (under 2 hours)">
<Card title="Quarry Rock — Deep Cove" subtitle="The classic starter hike">
A well-graded forest trail through mossy second-growth, ending at a rock outcrop over Deep Cove with the Indian Arm fjord below. Ideal if you want a real viewpoint without a big day.

<KeyValue title="Quarry Rock details">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Panorama Park, Deep Cove (North Vancouver) |
| Round trip | 3.8 km |
| Elevation gain | ~100 m |
| Time | 1.5–2 hours |
| Parking | Street parking in Deep Cove, tight on weekends |
| Post-hike | Honey's Doughnuts in the village |
</KeyValue>

<Card title="Dog Mountain — Mount Seymour" subtitle="Best sunset hike near the city">
Short but rocky in places, with a small lake partway and a rocky bluff at the top looking back over Metro Vancouver and the Burrard Inlet. Go late afternoon and stay for the light.

<KeyValue title="Dog Mountain details">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Mount Seymour ski area upper lot |
| Round trip | 5 km |
| Elevation gain | ~200 m |
| Time | 2–2.5 hours |
| Note | Roots and rock; a headlamp is essential if you stay for sunset |
</KeyValue>

<Tab title="Moderate (half day)">
<Card title="St. Mark's Summit — Howe Sound Crest Trail" subtitle="The best views-per-effort on the North Shore">
Rolling ups and downs along the ridge from Cypress, with a few rope-assisted sections, ending at a granite bluff over Howe Sound and the Sunshine Coast. This is the one locals recommend to visitors who can handle a half day.

<KeyValue title="St. Mark's Summit details">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Cypress Mountain downhill parking, then follow the Howe Sound Crest Trail |
| Round trip | 11 km |
| Elevation gain | ~450 m |
| Time | 4–5 hours |
| Note | Muddy year-round; some hands-on scrambling near the summit |
</KeyValue>

<Card title="Norvan Falls — Lynn Headwaters" subtitle="The rainy-day pick">
A flat, shaded valley walk to a tall waterfall, mostly under canopy so it holds up in drizzle. Long for the payoff, but genuinely quiet compared with the North Shore's headline trails.

<KeyValue title="Norvan Falls details">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Lynn Headwaters Regional Park, end of Lynn Valley Road |
| Round trip | 14 km |
| Elevation gain | ~250 m |
| Time | 4–5 hours |
| Note | Creek crossings; check conditions after heavy rain |
</KeyValue>

<Tab title="Hard (big day)">
<Card title="The Chief — Squamish" subtitle="The most famous climb in the region">
Three summits above Squamish, reached by a relentless staircase of rock and chain sections. The first peak is the standard target; the third is a longer day. About an hour's drive from Vancouver.

<KeyValue title="The Chief details">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Stawamus Chief Provincial Park, Squamish |
| Round trip | 6.5 km (first peak) |
| Elevation gain | ~600 m |
| Time | 4–5 hours |
| Note | Steep and busy; ladders and chains near the top |
</KeyValue>

<Card title="Garibaldi Lake — Whistler corridor" subtitle="Turquoise water, big effort">
A long switchback climb to a glacial lake with Mount Garibaldi behind it. It's a 1.5-hour drive each way, so it's the longest day here — but the lake itself is the best single view on this list.

<KeyValue title="Garibaldi Lake details">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Trailhead | Rubble Creek, Highway 99 (near Whistler) |
| Round trip | 18 km |
| Elevation gain | ~820 m |
| Time | 6–7 hours |
| Note | Day-use pass often required in summer; no shortcuts |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Safety notes">
- Tell someone your plan and expected return time; cell coverage drops on the North Shore trails.
- Bear activity is common in Lynn Headwaters and Garibaldi — carry bear spray and make noise.
- Trail conditions change; check AllTrails or BC Parks the morning you go.
</Callout>
