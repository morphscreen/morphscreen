# Houseplants That Forgive You

The short answer: pick plants that store water in their own tissues, tolerate low light, and tell you clearly when they're thirsty. Here are the ones that survive neglect, overwatering, and the dark corner you forgot about.

<Callout tone="info" title="The one rule that kills most houseplants">
Overwatering, not underwatering. Nearly every plant below dies from sitting in soggy soil. When in doubt, wait a few more days.
</Callout>

<Card title="The tough nine" subtitle="Ranked by how much neglect they shrug off">
| Plant | Light | Water | Why it survives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snake plant | Low to bright | Every 2–3 weeks | Stores water in thick leaves; rot-resistant |
| ZZ plant | Low | Every 3–4 weeks | Rhizomes hold water; tolerates dark rooms |
| Pothos | Low to bright | Weekly, or when drooping | Tells you when thirsty; roots in plain water |
| Spider plant | Medium | Weekly | Bounces back fast from wilt |
| Cast iron plant | Low | Every 2 weeks | Named for a reason; hates direct sun |
| Peace lily | Low to medium | Weekly | Dramatically droops, then recovers in hours |
| Aloe vera | Bright | Every 3 weeks | Succulent; thrives on neglect |
| Jade plant | Bright | Every 2–3 weeks | Succulent leaves; easy to propagate |
| Chinese evergreen | Low | Every 2 weeks | Tolerates low light and dry air |

</Card>

<Card title="Pick your situation">
<Columns>
<Card title="Dark room, no window">
**Snake plant** or **ZZ plant**. Both handle a north-facing room or a hallway with no direct light. Water monthly.
</Card>
<Card title="Bright, sunny window">
**Aloe vera** or **jade plant**. They want the sun and will sulk in shade. Let soil dry completely between waterings.
</Card>
<Card title="I forget for weeks">
**Pothos**. It visibly droops before dying, and recovers within a day of a drink. Nearly impossible to lose.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Watering cheat sheet">
<KeyValue title="How often, roughly">
| Plant | Summer | Winter |
|---|---|---|
| Snake plant | Every 2–3 weeks | Every 4–6 weeks |
| ZZ plant | Every 3–4 weeks | Every 6–8 weeks |
| Pothos | Weekly | Every 10–14 days |
| Aloe / jade | Every 3 weeks | Every 4–6 weeks |
| Peace lily | Weekly | Every 10–14 days |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Signs you're doing it right">
<Checklist title="Plant health check" name="plant-check">
- [x] Soil is dry a finger's depth before you water
- [x] Pot has a drainage hole
- [x] Leaves are firm, not mushy (mushy = too much water)
- [ ] Brown crispy tips (usually tap water or dry air — harmless)
- [ ] Yellow lower leaves (usually overwatering — let it dry out)
- [ ] New leaf unfurling (you're doing great)
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="Start here if you own zero plants">
<Steps>
<Step title="Buy a snake plant" status="current">One plant, one pot with a hole. Ignore it for two weeks.</Step>
<Step title="Add a pothos" status="todo">Hang it or let it trail. Water when the leaves start to droop.</Step>
<Step title="Add a ZZ plant" status="todo">For the darkest corner. Water once a month, no more.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Two things that still kill these">
**No drainage hole** — roots sit in water and rot, no matter the plant. **Cold drafts** — below about 10°C, even a snake plant struggles.
</Callout>
