# Neapolitan pizza at home

The real thing comes down to four things: a **soft, wet dough**, a **very hot oven**, **San Marzano tomatoes**, and **as little handling as possible**. Home ovens can't hit 900°F, so the plan below works around that.

<Callout tone="info" title="The one rule that matters most">
Get your oven as hot as it will physically go, with a steel or stone preheated for a full hour. A cold stone is the #1 reason home pizza comes out pale and soggy.
</Callout>

<Card title="What you need" subtitle="Makes 4 pizzas (about 250 g dough each)">
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| "00" flour (or bread flour) | 500 g |
| Water, room temp | 325 g (65%) |
| Fine sea salt | 12 g |
| Fresh yeast (or 1 g dry) | 2 g |
| San Marzano tomatoes, canned | 1 can (400 g) |
| Fresh mozzarella (fior di latte) | 300 g |
| Fresh basil | a handful |
| Extra virgin olive oil | for finishing |
</Card>

<Card title="The method" subtitle="Start the night before">
<Steps>
<Step title="Dissolve the yeast" status="done" subtitle="2 minutes">
Warm the water slightly (tepid, not hot). Stir in the yeast until it disappears.
</Step>
<Step title="Mix flour and salt, then the water" status="done" subtitle="5 minutes">
Combine flour and salt in a bowl. Pour in the yeasted water and mix with a hand or spatula until no dry flour remains. It will look shaggy and sticky — that's correct.
</Step>
<Step title="Rest, then fold" status="done" subtitle="30 minutes + 3 folds">
Cover and rest 30 minutes. Then do 3 sets of stretch-and-folds, 15 minutes apart. The dough goes from shaggy to smooth and elastic.
</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment overnight" status="current" subtitle="18–24 hours">
Cover and refrigerate. This is where flavor and digestibility come from. Don't skip it — a same-day dough tastes flat.
</Step>
<Step title="Ball the dough" status="todo" subtitle="2 hours before baking">
Divide into 4 pieces (~250 g each). Shape each into a tight ball with a smooth top. Let them come to room temperature, covered, for 2 hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Prep the toppings" status="todo" subtitle="10 minutes">
Crush the tomatoes by hand (never a blender — it whips in air and makes them watery). Salt lightly. Tear or slice the mozzarella and drain it on paper towel for 20 minutes so it doesn't flood the pizza.
</Step>
<Step title="Preheat hard" status="todo" subtitle="1 hour">
Oven at max (usually 250–290°C / 500–550°F) with a baking steel or stone on the middle-upper rack. Let it soak heat for a full hour.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch, top, bake" status="todo" subtitle="6–8 minutes each">
Press the ball flat from the center out, leaving a 2 cm rim untouched. Stretch over your knuckles to ~28 cm. Top lightly: sauce, mozzarella, nothing else yet. Bake 6–8 minutes until the rim is spotted and puffed. Add basil and a drizzle of oil after it comes out.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Why each step matters">
<Compare title="What changes what">
| Step | If you skip it | If you do it |
| Cold ferment | Flat, bland, gummy | Deep flavor, easy to digest |
| 65% hydration | Dry, cracker-like | Airy, blistered rim |
| Hand-crushed tomato | Watery, soupy center | Sweet, thick, bright sauce |
| Hour-long preheat | Pale, no leopard spots | Puffy charred rim |
| Light toppings | Heavy, steaming, soggy | Balanced, crisp base |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="Troubleshooting">
<KeyValue title="Common failures">
| Symptom | Cause and fix |
|---|---|
| Soggy center | Too much sauce/cheese, or wet mozzarella — drain it, go lighter |
| No char on the rim | Stone not hot enough — preheat longer, use steel if you can |
| Dough tears when stretching | Under-rested gluten — let it relax 15 more minutes |
| Dense, bready crumb | Not enough fermentation — give it the full overnight rest |
| Cheese pools and separates | Fresh mozzarella cooked too long — add it later or use less |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="No pizza oven? Two workarounds">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Cast-iron skillet">
Preheat the skillet in the oven until screaming hot. Stretch the dough into the pan, top it, and bake on the stovetop for 2 minutes to set the base, then under the broiler for 3–4 minutes. The base crisps like a steel.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Grill">
Get the grill as hot as it goes with the lid down. Oil the grates, lay the stretched dough directly on, cook 2 minutes until it bubbles, flip, top the cooked side, and close the lid for 2–3 minutes.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The test of a proper one">
It should be soft enough to fold in half and eat walking, with a rim that's puffed, spotted, and slightly chewy — never crunchy all the way through.
</Callout>

<Checklist title="Before you start" name="pizza-prep">
- [ ] Flour weighed, not scooped
- [ ] Yeast dissolved and active
- [ ] Dough folded three times
- [ ] Overnight cold ferment done
- [ ] Balls rested 2 hours at room temp
- [ ] Mozzarella drained
- [ ] Stone/steel preheated 1 hour
- [ ] Tomatoes crushed by hand
</Checklist>

<Quiz title="Check your understanding">
<Question text="Why crush the tomatoes by hand instead of blending them?" answer="Blending whips air into the sauce and makes it watery" explain="A blender aerates the pulp, turning a thick sauce into a thin, watery one that floods the base. Hand-crushing keeps it thick and sweet.">
- To make the sauce sweeter
- Blending whips air into the sauce and makes it watery
- Blending removes the seeds, which are bitter
- Hand-crushing cooks the tomatoes slightly
</Question>
<Question text="Your pizza comes out pale with no leopard spots. What's the most likely cause?" answer="The stone wasn't preheated long enough" explain="A stone needs a full hour to soak heat. If it's only warm, the base never chars and the dough dries instead of puffing.">
- Too much salt in the dough
- The stone wasn't preheated long enough
- The dough was over-fermented
- The mozzarella was too wet
</Question>
<Question text="What does the overnight cold ferment mainly give you?" answer="Flavor and a more digestible dough" explain="Slow cold fermentation builds flavor compounds and lets enzymes break down the dough, so it tastes deeper and sits lighter.">
- A faster bake
- Flavor and a more digestible dough
- A crispier crust
- A thicker rim
</Question>
