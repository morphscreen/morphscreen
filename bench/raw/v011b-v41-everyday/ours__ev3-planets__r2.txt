# The Planets of Our Solar System

Eight planets orbit the Sun, held by gravity. They fall into two families: the **four rocky inner planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the **four giant outer planets** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006.

<Card title="The four rocky planets" subtitle="Small, dense, solid surfaces">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Mercury">
The smallest planet and closest to the Sun. It has almost no atmosphere, so temperatures swing from about **430 °C** in daylight to **−180 °C** at night. Its surface is heavily cratered, much like our Moon.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Venus">
Similar in size to Earth but wrapped in thick carbon dioxide clouds. A runaway greenhouse effect makes it the **hottest planet**, around **465 °C** — hotter than Mercury despite being farther out. It spins backwards, and a single day lasts longer than its year.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Earth">
The only planet known to support life. Liquid water covers about 71% of its surface, and its atmosphere is 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen. Its magnetic field shields us from solar radiation.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Mars">
The "Red Planet," colored by iron oxide dust. It hosts **Olympus Mons**, the tallest volcano in the solar system (about 22 km high), and **Valles Marineris**, a canyon system over 4,000 km long. It has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="The four giant planets" subtitle="Huge, mostly gas and ice, no solid surface to stand on">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Jupiter">
The largest planet — more massive than all the others combined. Its **Great Red Spot** is a storm that has raged for centuries, wider than Earth. It has 95 known moons, including the four large Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Saturn">
Famous for its bright ring system, made of ice and rock. It is the least dense planet — it would float in water. Its moon **Titan** has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Uranus">
An ice giant tipped on its side, likely from an ancient collision. It rotates at roughly a **98° tilt**, so it essentially rolls around the Sun. Methane in its atmosphere gives it a pale blue-green color.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Neptune">
The farthest planet, and the windiest — gusts reach about **2,100 km/h**. It was the first planet found by mathematical prediction rather than observation, in 1846. Its moon Triton orbits backwards.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Why Pluto isn't on the list">
In 2006 the International Astronomical Union defined a planet as a body that orbits the Sun, is round under its own gravity, and has **cleared its neighborhood** of other debris. Pluto shares its orbit with many other icy objects, so it became a dwarf planet.
</Callout>
