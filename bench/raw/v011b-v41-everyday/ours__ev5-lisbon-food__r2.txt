# Eating in Lisbon: what and where

Lisbon's food splits into a few clear lanes: grilled fish and seafood, the *petiscos* (small plates) tradition, the pastelaria counter, and the modern tasting-menu scene. Below is what to order, where to go, and a plan you can act on.

<Callout tone="info" title="How to eat here">
Lunch runs 12:30–15:00, dinner rarely starts before 20:00. Many good places are small and don't take reservations — go early or expect a queue. Couvert (bread, olives, cheese) is charged if you eat it; say no and it comes off the bill.
</Callout>

<Card title="What to eat" subtitle="The dishes worth planning a day around">
| Dish | What it is | Where it shines |
|---|---|---|
| **Bacalhau à Brás** | Shredded salt cod with egg, onion, straw potato | Tascas everywhere |
| **Sardinhas assadas** | Grilled sardines on bread | Alfama, June festivals |
| **Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato** | Clams in garlic, coriander, white wine | Cervejarias |
| **Arroz de marisco** | Wet seafood rice, soupy and rich | Cascais, Setúbal |
| **Bifana** | Pork sandwich in garlic sauce | Old-school tascas |
| **Pastéis de nata** | Custard tart, blistered top | Belém and every pastelaria |
| **Queijo da Serra** | Runny sheep's cheese | Any petiscos table |
| **Ginjinha** | Sour cherry liqueur, one shot | A hole in the wall in Rossio |
</Card>

<Card title="Where to go" subtitle="By neighborhood and style">
| Place | Neighborhood | Style | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time Out Market** | Cais do Sodré | Food hall | A tour of many stalls at once |
| **Cervejaria Ramiro** | Intendente | Seafood institution | Garlic prawns, clams, crab |
| **Taberna da Rua das Flores** | Chiado | Modern petiscos | Whatever's on the board |
| **O Velho Eurico** | Alfama | Tiny, no-frills | Bacalhau, pork, daily specials |
| **Pastéis de Belém** | Belém | Pastelaria | Nata, warm, with cinnamon |
| **A Cevicheria** | Príncipe Real | Peruvian-Portuguese | Ceviche, octopus |
| **Ponto Final** | Cacilhas (ferry) | Riverside grill | Grilled fish, sunset |
</Card>

<Card title="A one-day eating plan">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Belém" status="todo" subtitle="Pastéis de Belém, then the monastery">
Nata fresh out of the oven, cinnamon on top, standing at the counter. Walk it off along the river.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Cais do Sodré" status="todo" subtitle="Time Out Market">
Graze across stalls — one plate of clams, one of bacalhau, share everything.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: ginjinha in Rossio" status="todo" subtitle="One shot, standing">
A tiny doorway, a euro, a cherry. Then wander Chiado.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Intendente" status="todo" subtitle="Cervejaria Ramiro">
Arrive by 19:30 or take a number. Garlic prawns, clams à Bulhão Pato, crab, then a steak sandwich to finish.
</Step>
<Step title="Nightcap: Alfama" status="todo" subtitle="O Velho Eurico or a fado bar">
If you still have room, a small plate and a glass of vinho verde.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Order like a local">
<KeyValue title="Phrases and customs">
| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| "A conta, por favor" | The bill, please |
| "Sem couvert" | No bread/olives charge |
| Tipping | Round up; 5–10% for good service |
| Water | "Água sem gás" = still |
| Coffee | "Uma bica" = an espresso |
| Reservations | Only the mid-to-upmarket places take them |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="If you only do three things">
Cervejaria Ramiro for seafood, Pastéis de Belém for the nata, and a petiscos dinner in Chiado or Alfama. That's the whole city on one plate.
</Callout>
