# Hard-to-Kill Houseplants

The short answer: pick plants that evolved to survive neglect — low light, infrequent water, and dry air. Here are the toughest ones, plus a way to match one to your actual room.

<Callout tone="success" title="The rule behind every pick">
Almost every "unkillable" plant dies from **overwatering**, not neglect. If you water less than you think you should, you're already ahead.
</Callout>

<Card title="The toughest tier" subtitle="Survives weeks of neglect, low light, and dry air">
| Plant | Light | Water | Why it's forgiving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snake plant (*Sansevieria*) | Low to bright | Every 2–3 weeks | Stores water in leaves; rots only if soaked |
| ZZ plant (*Zamioculcas*) | Low to medium | Every 2–3 weeks | Rhizomes hold water for a month |
| Pothos | Low to bright | When top soil is dry | Wilts dramatically, then recovers fully |
| Spider plant | Medium | Weekly-ish | Tolerates missed weeks; makes babies to replace itself |
| Cast iron plant (*Aspidistra*) | Low | Every 2 weeks | Named for surviving dim rooms and neglect |
</Card>

<Card title="Second tier" subtitle="Easy, but wants a bit more attention">
| Plant | Light | Water | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peace lily | Medium, indirect | Weekly | Droops visibly when thirsty — a built-in reminder |
| Rubber plant (*Ficus elastica*) | Bright, indirect | Every 1–2 weeks | Hates being moved; pick a spot and leave it |
| Aloe vera | Bright, direct | Every 2–3 weeks | Needs sun; rots fast in shade + wet soil |
| Chinese evergreen (*Aglaonema*) | Low to medium | Every 2 weeks | Cold drafts |
| Heartleaf philodendron | Low to medium | When dry | Very hard to actually kill |
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Skip these if you want easy">
Fiddle-leaf fig, calathea, maidenhair fern, and most succulents in low light. They look great in photos and punish small mistakes.
</Callout>

<Card title="The 3 habits that keep any of them alive">
<Steps>
<Step title="Water only when the top 2–3 cm of soil is dry" status="current">Stick a finger in. If it's damp, wait. This one habit saves more plants than any other.</Step>
<Step title="Give drainage">A pot with a hole, and never let it sit in a saucer of water. Roots need air.</Step>
<Step title="Don't repot or move it constantly">Most plants sulk for weeks after a move. Let them settle.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Quick reference">
<KeyValue title="Care at a glance">
| | |
|---|---|
| Safest first plant | Snake plant or ZZ plant |
| Best for a dark room | Cast iron plant, snake plant, pothos |
| Best for a bright window | Aloe, rubber plant |
| Most dramatic when thirsty | Peace lily (great if you forget, then notice) |
| Pet-safe picks | Spider plant, calathea (but calathea is fussy) |
| Toxic to cats/dogs | Pothos, ZZ, snake plant, philodendron, aloe |
</KeyValue>
</Card>
