# The Planets of Our Solar System

Eight planets orbit the Sun, held in place by gravity. Four are small and rocky, four are giants of gas and ice. Here's the whole family, in order from the Sun.

<Card title="A few things that surprise people">
<Callout tone="info" title="Jupiter outweighs the rest">
Jupiter is more massive than all the other seven planets combined — about 2.5 times over.
</Callout>

- **Mercury** has almost no atmosphere, so it swings from about 430 °C in daylight to −180 °C at night.
- **Venus** is the hottest planet at around 465 °C — its thick CO₂ blanket traps heat far better than Mercury's bare rock does.
- **Earth** is the only planet with liquid water on the surface, and the only one known to host life.
- **Mars** is home to Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the solar system at roughly 22 km high.
- **Saturn**'s rings are mostly water ice, and they're only tens of metres thick in places.
- **Uranus** is tipped on its side, so it effectively rolls around the Sun.
- **Neptune** has the fastest winds, gusting past 2,000 km/h.

</Card>
