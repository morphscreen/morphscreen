# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

Late October is one of the best windows: mild days, low rain, and the first hints of autumn colour. This plan keeps days geographically tight so you spend time in places, not on trains.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you land">
Get a **Suica or Pasmo** (now available as a digital card in Apple Wallet) and load ~¥5,000 each. Add a **eSIM** for data. Most temples and gardens are cash-only at the gate, so carry ¥10,000–20,000 in cash. Two people, 5 days, mid-range: budget roughly **¥180,000–260,000** excluding flights and hotel.
</Callout>

<Tab title="Day 1">
### Arrive and settle — Shinjuku
<Steps>
<Step title="Land, clear customs, get cards" status="done" subtitle="Narita ~60–90 min to Shinjuku; Haneda ~40 min">Load Suica, buy a data eSIM if you haven't.</Step>
<Step title="Check in, drop bags" status="done">Shower, change, walk 10 minutes to wake up.</Step>
<Step title="Omoide Yokocho for dinner" status="current">Tiny yakitori and ramen alleys. Order at 2–3 stalls, not one.</Step>
<Step title="Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatory" status="todo">Free, open to 22:00 (north tower). Clear night views over the city.</Step>
<Step title="Golden Gai nightcap" status="todo">Bars seat 4–8 people. Look for places with English menus or no cover charge.</Step>
</Steps>

<Tab title="Day 2">
### Old Tokyo — Asakusa and Ueno
<Steps>
<Step title="Senso-ji early" status="current" subtitle="Arrive by 07:30">The temple and Nakamise street are near-empty before 08:00. By 10:00 it's shoulder to shoulder.</Step>
<Step title="Breakfast: melon pan and matcha" status="todo">Kagurazaka or the stalls just off Nakamise.</Step>
<Step title="Sumida River walk to Skytree" status="todo">30 minutes along the river, or a short river bus.</Step>
<Step title="Ueno Park and Ameyoko market" status="todo">Museums if it rains; Ameyoko for cheap seafood, snacks and souvenirs.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: monjayaki in Tsukishima" status="todo">You cook it on the griddle at the table. Get the mentaiko mochi version.</Step>
</Steps>

<Tab title="Day 3">
### Markets and modern Tokyo
<Steps>
<Step title="Toyosu Market, 05:30–06:30" status="current" subtitle="Free observation deck; auctions need a lottery">Go early, eat sushi at the market for a fraction of dinner prices.</Step>
<Step title="teamLab or a digital art museum" status="todo">Book timed tickets online weeks ahead — they sell out.</Step>
<Step title="Ginza for lunch and browsing" status="todo">Depachika food halls in Mitsukoshi or Matsuya are a meal in themselves.</Step>
<Step title="Akihabara in the afternoon" status="todo">Arcades, retro games, gachapon. Super Potato for old consoles.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner in Ebisu or Daikanyama" status="todo">Quieter, more grown-up than Shinjuku. Good yakitori and wine bars.</Step>
</Steps>

<Tab title="Day 4">
### Day trip — Nikko or Kamakura
<Steps>
<Step title="Pick your direction" status="current" subtitle="Nikko: mountains, shrines, 2h each way. Kamakura: seaside, temples, 1h each way">Nikko in late October is at the start of autumn colour.</Step>
<Step title="Nikko: Toshogu Shrine and Kegon Falls" status="todo">Buy the Tobu Nikko All Area Pass. Wear real shoes.</Step>
<Step title="Kamakura: Great Buddha, Hasedera, Enoshima" status="todo">Enoden tram along the coast is the highlight.</Step>
<Step title="Return by 19:00, easy dinner near the hotel" status="todo">You'll be tired — konbini dinner and an early night is a legitimate plan.</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Practical notes">
<Details title="Trains, cards and passes" open="true">
- Suica/Pasmo works on trains, buses, konbini and most vending machines.
- A 24/48/72-hour **Tokyo Metro** pass pays off only on days with 4+ rides. Days 2 and 3 qualify; day 1 and 5 usually don't.
- The JR Pass is **not** worth it for a Tokyo-only trip.
- Last trains run roughly 00:30–01:00. Miss it and a taxi across town is ¥4,000–7,000.
</Details>
<Details title="Etiquette that actually matters">
- Don't eat while walking on residential streets; do eat at stalls and markets.
- No tipping anywhere.
- Queue on the marked platform lines; stand left on Tokyo escalators, right in Osaka.
- Cash trays at registers — put money in the tray, not in the cashier's hand.
- Tattoos may bar you from some onsen; look for "tattoo-friendly" or book a private bath.
</Details>
<Details title="What to pack for late October">
- Layers: days ~20°C, evenings ~12°C.
- One smart-casual outfit for a nicer dinner.
- Comfortable shoes — 15,000+ steps a day is normal.
- A small coin purse; ¥100 and ¥500 coins pile up fast.
- A compact umbrella or buy a ¥500 one at any konbini.
</Details>
</Card>
