# What to eat in Lisbon, and where

Lisbon eating runs on three things: grilled fish, pork sandwiches, and custard tarts. Here's the short list, with the classic spots for each.

<Callout tone="info" title="How Lisbon meals work">
Lunch is 12:30–14:30, dinner rarely before 20:00. Many tascas close Sunday and all of August. Petiscos (small plates) are the local version of tapas — order 3–4 per person.
</Callout>

<Card title="The dishes you shouldn't leave without trying">
| Dish | What it is | Rough price |
|---|---|---|
| Bacalhau à Brás | Shredded salt cod with egg, onion, straw potato | €14–18 |
| Sardinhas assadas | Grilled sardines on bread, June festival food | €8–12 |
| Bifana | Pork cutlet in a garlicky roll | €3–5 |
| Pastéis de nata | Custard tart, cinnamon optional | €1.50–2 |
| Arroz de marisco | Seafood rice, soupy, shared | €18–25 |
| Caracóis | Garlic snails, a summer beer snack | €6–9 |
</Card>

<Card title="Three days of eating, mapped out">
<Steps>
<Step title="Day 1 — Baixa and Chiado" status="done" subtitle="Start central, walk everywhere">
Morning pastel de nata at Manteigaria, bifana at O Trevo for lunch, petiscos in Bairro Alto at night.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 2 — Belém and Alcântara" status="current" subtitle="West along the river">
Pastéis de Belém in the morning, then Cervejaria Ramiro for a long shellfish lunch. Walk it off by the river.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 3 — Alfama and Graça" status="todo" subtitle="Old town, tascas, fado">
Grilled sardines at a street grill, bacalhau à Brás at A Merendinha do Arco, fado with dinner in Alfama.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Practical notes">
<KeyValue title="Need to know">
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tipping | Not expected; round up or leave 5% |
| Bookings | Essential at Ramiro and A Cevicheria; walk-in at tascas |
| Vegetarian | Harder in tascas — look for prato vegetariano or go to Ao 26 or Kong |
| Best months | April–June and September; August is packed |
| Cash | Some tascas are cash only |
