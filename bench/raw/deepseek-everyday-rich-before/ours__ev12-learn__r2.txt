# Photosynthesis: How Plants Turn Light into Life

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It's the foundation of nearly all life on Earth — providing oxygen and the base of the food chain.

<Card title="The Big Picture">
Photosynthesis happens in two main stages: the **light-dependent reactions** (which capture sunlight) and the **light-independent reactions** (the Calvin cycle, which builds sugar). The overall equation is:

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

In simple terms: carbon dioxide and water, powered by sunlight, become glucose and oxygen.
</Card>

<Card title="Where It Happens: The Chloroplast">
Photosynthesis occurs inside **chloroplasts**, tiny organelles found mainly in leaf cells. Key parts:

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| **Thylakoid membrane** | Site of light-dependent reactions; contains chlorophyll |
| **Stroma** | Fluid around thylakoids; site of the Calvin cycle |
| **Chlorophyll** | Green pigment that absorbs light (mostly red and blue) |
| **Stomata** | Tiny pores on leaves that let CO₂ in and O₂ out |

<Callout tone="info" title="Why leaves are green">
Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light most strongly, but **reflects green light** — that's why leaves look green to us.
</Callout>
</Card>

## The Two Stages in Detail

<Steps>
<Step title="Light-Dependent Reactions" status="done" subtitle="In the thylakoid membrane, requires sunlight">
1. **Light absorption** — Chlorophyll absorbs photons, exciting electrons to a higher energy level.
2. **Water splitting** — Enzymes split water (H₂O) into oxygen (O₂), protons (H⁺), and electrons. Oxygen is released as a byproduct.
3. **Electron transport chain** — Excited electrons pass through proteins, pumping H⁺ into the thylakoid space, building a gradient.
4. **ATP and NADPH production** — The H⁺ gradient powers ATP synthase to make ATP; electrons reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH.

**Outputs:** ATP, NADPH, and O₂ (released to the air).
</Step>

<Step title="Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)" status="done" subtitle="In the stroma, no light needed directly">
1. **Carbon fixation** — The enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to RuBP (a 5-carbon molecule), forming two 3-carbon molecules (3-PGA).
2. **Reduction** — Using ATP and NADPH from the first stage, 3-PGA is converted to G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate), a sugar.
3. **Regeneration** — Most G3P goes to regenerate RuBP, so the cycle continues; one G3P exits per three turns to build glucose.

**Inputs:** CO₂, ATP, NADPH. **Outputs:** G3P (used to make glucose and other carbohydrates).
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Key Factors That Affect Photosynthesis Rate">
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Light intensity** | Increases rate up to a saturation point |
| **CO₂ concentration** | Higher CO₂ boosts rate until other limits kick in |
| **Temperature** | Enzyme activity rises with warmth, but too hot denatures RuBisCO |
| **Water availability** | Shortage closes stomata, limiting CO₂ intake |
</Card>

<Details title="Common misconceptions" open="false">
- **Photosynthesis ≠ respiration** — Plants also respire (break down glucose) all the time, but during daylight photosynthesis exceeds respiration.
- **Not just leaves** — Green stems, some roots, and even certain fruits can photosynthesize.
- **C3, C4, and CAM plants** — Some plants (corn, pineapple) use modified pathways to conserve water or concentrate CO₂.
</Details>

<Card title="Try It Yourself">
A simple experiment: place two identical potted plants in a sunny window. Cover one with a dark box for a week. The covered plant will yellow and stop growing — because without light, the light-dependent reactions stop, and the plant can't produce new sugars.

<Callout tone="success" title="Takeaway">
Photosynthesis is a two-stage process: capture light energy to make ATP and NADPH, then use that energy to fix CO₂ into sugar. Master this and you understand the engine of life on Earth.
</Callout>
