# Photosynthesis: How Plants Turn Light into Life

<Card title="What Is Photosynthesis?" subtitle="The process that powers nearly all life on Earth">
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to create glucose (a sugar) and oxygen. It's the foundation of the food chain and produces the oxygen we breathe.

The overall chemical equation:
**6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**

In words: Carbon dioxide + Water + Sunlight → Glucose + Oxygen
</Card>

<Card title="The Two Main Stages">
<Columns>
<div>
#### 1. Light-Dependent Reactions
Happen in the **thylakoid membranes** of chloroplasts. Require light.

- **Inputs:** Light energy, water
- **Outputs:** ATP, NADPH, and oxygen (released as a byproduct)
- Water molecules are split, releasing oxygen gas

#### 2. Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)
Happen in the **stroma** (fluid around thylakoids). Don't need light directly.

- **Inputs:** CO₂, ATP, NADPH (from stage 1)
- **Outputs:** Glucose (sugar)
- Uses the energy from ATP/NADPH to "fix" carbon into sugar
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Step-by-Step Breakdown">
<Steps>
<Step title="Sunlight is absorbed" status="done" subtitle="Photosystems II and I">
Chlorophyll pigments in the thylakoid membranes capture photons. This excites electrons, which are passed along an electron transport chain.
</Step>
<Step title="Water is split" status="done" subtitle="Oxygen is released">
Enzymes split water molecules (H₂O) into oxygen, protons, and electrons. The oxygen is released as a byproduct — this is the O₂ we breathe.
</Step>
<Step title="ATP and NADPH are produced" status="done" subtitle="Energy carriers">
The excited electrons power pumps that create ATP (via ATP synthase) and reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH. Both carry energy to the next stage.
</Step>
<Step title="Carbon fixation" status="done" subtitle="Calvin cycle begins">
In the stroma, the enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP), forming a 6-carbon compound that immediately splits into two 3-carbon molecules (3-PGA).
</Step>
<Step title="Reduction and regeneration" status="done" subtitle="Making sugar">
Using ATP and NADPH, the 3-PGA is converted to G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate). Most G3P is used to regenerate RuBP, but some leaves the cycle to form glucose and other sugars.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Key Players">
<KeyValue title="Essential components">
| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | Pigment that absorbs light (mainly red and blue, reflects green) |
| Chloroplast | Organelle where photosynthesis occurs |
| Thylakoid | Membrane sac where light reactions happen |
| Stroma | Fluid where the Calvin cycle happens |
| RuBisCO | Enzyme that fixes CO₂ (most abundant protein on Earth) |
| ATP & NADPH | Energy-carrying molecules |
| Stomata | Pores in leaves that let CO₂ in and O₂ out |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="info" title="Limiting factors">
Like a chain, the slowest step limits the whole process. At night, light is the limiting factor; in bright sun, CO₂ or temperature may limit it.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Why It Matters">
<Columns>
<div>
- **Oxygen production:** Nearly all atmospheric O₂ comes from photosynthesis
- **Food source:** Glucose is the base of most food chains
- **Carbon sink:** Plants absorb CO₂, helping regulate climate
- **Raw materials:** Wood, fibers, and fuels (like fossil fuels) trace back to photosynthesis
</div>

<Card title="Common Misconceptions">
<Details title="Plants don't 'breathe' like we do" open=false>
Plants respire (break down glucose for energy) all the time, but photosynthesis only happens in light. During the day, photosynthesis usually outpaces respiration, so plants are net O₂ producers.
</Details>
<Details title="Photosynthesis isn't just about leaves" open=false>
Green stems, unripe fruits, and even some roots (if exposed to light) can photosynthesize. Cacti do it in their stems, and some algae do it in their entire body.
</Details>
</Card>
