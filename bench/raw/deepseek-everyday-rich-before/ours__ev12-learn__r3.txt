# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy, producing glucose and oxygen. It's the foundation of nearly all life on Earth.

## The Chemical Equation

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light energy} \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />

In words: six carbon dioxide molecules plus six water molecules, powered by light, yield one glucose molecule and six oxygen molecules.

<Card title="The Two Stages">
Photosynthesis happens in two linked stages, each in a different part of the chloroplast.

<Steps>
<Step title="Light-Dependent Reactions" status="done" subtitle="In the thylakoid membranes">
Light energy splits water molecules (photolysis), releasing oxygen gas. This energy also charges up ATP and NADPH — the energy carriers that power the next stage.
</Step>
<Step title="Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent)" status="todo" subtitle="In the stroma">
Using the ATP and NADPH from stage one, carbon dioxide is fixed into glucose through a series of enzyme-driven reactions. This stage doesn't need light directly, but it depends on the products of the light stage.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## A Closer Look

<Card title="Inside the Chloroplast">
<KeyValue title="Chloroplast anatomy">
| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Thylakoid | Disc-like membranes where light reactions occur; contain chlorophyll |
| Grana | Stacks of thylakoids that maximize light capture |
| Stroma | Fluid space around thylakoids; site of the Calvin cycle |
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment that absorbs red and blue light, reflecting green |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Light-Dependent Reactions (Thylakoid)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Light absorption" status="done" subtitle="Photosystem II">
Chlorophyll molecules absorb photons, boosting electrons to a higher energy level. Water is split to replace them, releasing O₂ as a byproduct.
</Step>
<Step title="Electron transport chain" status="done" subtitle="Thylakoid membrane">
High-energy electrons pass through a series of carrier proteins, pumping H⁺ ions into the thylakoid space, building a concentration gradient.
</Step>
<Step title="ATP synthesis" status="done" subtitle="Chemiosmosis">
H⁺ ions flow back out through ATP synthase, driving the production of ATP from ADP and phosphate.
</Step>
<Step title="NADPH formation" status="done" subtitle="Photosystem I">
Electrons (re-energized by more light) combine with NADP⁺ and H⁺ to form NADPH.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Calvin Cycle (Stroma)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Carbon fixation" status="done" subtitle="Rubisco enzyme">
Each CO₂ molecule combines with RuBP (a 5-carbon sugar), producing two 3-carbon molecules (3-PGA).
</Step>
<Step title="Reduction" status="done" subtitle="Using ATP and NADPH">
3-PGA is converted to G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate) using the energy from ATP and NADPH.
</Step>
<Step title="Glucose synthesis & regeneration" status="done" subtitle="Output and recycling">
Most G3P molecules go on to regenerate RuBP, but one out of every six G3P molecules exits the cycle to form glucose and other sugars.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Why It Matters

<Callout tone="success" title="Oxygen production">
Photosynthesis releases the oxygen we breathe. Nearly all atmospheric O₂ comes from this process, mostly from ocean algae rather than land plants.
</Callout>

<Card title="Key Takeaways">
| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Location | Chloroplasts in mesophyll cells of leaves |
| Pigment | Chlorophyll a and b, plus carotenoids |
| Light spectrum | Red and blue light are most effective; green is reflected |
| Products | Glucose (energy source), oxygen (waste for plants, vital for us) |
| Rate limiters | Light intensity, CO₂ concentration, temperature, water availability |
| Global impact | Produces ~100 billion tonnes of biomass per year |
</Card>

<Card title="Common Misconceptions">
<Compare title="Myths vs. reality">
| Statement | Reality |
|---|---|
| Plants "eat" soil for energy | Soil provides minerals; energy comes from sunlight via photosynthesis |
| Photosynthesis only happens during the day | Light reactions need light, but the Calvin cycle can run in darkness using stored ATP/NADPH |
| Plants don't respire | Plants respire all the time, using glucose for energy; photosynthesis and respiration are complementary |
| Oxygen comes from CO₂ | Oxygen actually comes from splitting water molecules in the light reactions |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Details title="C3, C4, and CAM plants" open="false">
Most plants use the C3 pathway (the Calvin cycle directly). But in hot, dry climates, C4 plants (corn, sugarcane) concentrate CO₂ to reduce water loss, and CAM plants (cacti, succulents) open their stomata at night to store CO₂ for daytime photosynthesis. These adaptations make photosynthesis more efficient under stress.
</Details>
