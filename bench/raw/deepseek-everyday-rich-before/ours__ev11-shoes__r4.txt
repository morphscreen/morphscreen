<Card title="How to choose">
<Steps>
<Step title="Know your stride" status="done">
If you're unsure whether you overpronate, check the wear pattern on an old pair of shoes. Most runners are neutral, and all four picks above work for neutral gaits.
</Step>
<Step title="Match cushioning to your runs" status="done">
Plush (Cumulus) for easy miles, balanced (Pegasus) for variety, soft (Ghost) for recovery, firm (Ride) for long steady runs.
</Step>
<Step title="Get the right size" status="done">
Running shoes should have a thumb's width of space between your longest toe and the shoe's end. Try them on at the end of the day when feet are slightly swollen.
</Step>
<Step title="Break them in" status="current">
Start with short runs and increase distance over 2–3 weeks. If you feel hot spots or rubbing, return them early — most stores accept worn returns within 30 days.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Sizing and fit tips">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't trust your casual shoe size">
Running shoes typically run half a size larger than your everyday shoes. Always try before you buy, or order two sizes and return one.
</Callout>
