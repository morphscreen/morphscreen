# 10 Basic Spanish Phrases for Your Trip

<Card title="Essential Phrases" subtitle="Pronunciations included to help you sound natural">

<Columns>
<div>

### 1. Hola — Hello
**Pronunciation:** *OH-lah*

The universal greeting. Works any time of day, with anyone.

</div>
<div>

### 2. Gracias — Thank you
**Pronunciation:** *GRAH-see-ahs*

Add "muchas" (MOO-chahs) before it to say "thank you very much."

</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>

### 3. Por favor — Please
**Pronunciation:** *por fah-VOR*

Use it at the end of requests: "Una cerveza, por favor" (One beer, please).

</div>
<div>

### 4. ¿Dónde está...? — Where is...?
**Pronunciation:** *DOHN-deh es-TAH*

Follow with "el baño" (el BAH-nyoh) for the bathroom, or "la playa" (lah PLY-ah) for the beach.

</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>

### 5. ¿Cuánto cuesta? — How much does it cost?
**Pronunciation:** *KWAHN-toh KWES-tah*

Essential for markets, taxis, and souvenirs.

</div>
<div>

### 6. No entiendo — I don't understand
**Pronunciation:** *noh en-tee-EN-doh*

Say this politely when you need someone to slow down or repeat.

</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>

### 7. ¿Hablas inglés? — Do you speak English?
**Pronunciation:** *AH-blahs een-GLES*

A lifesaver when you're stuck. Add "un poco" (oon POH-koh) — "a little" — if you want to practice Spanish anyway.

</div>
<div>

### 8. La cuenta, por favor — The check, please
**Pronunciation:** *lah KWEHN-tah por fah-VOR*

Use this at restaurants to ask for the bill. The check is "la cuenta."

</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>

### 9. ¿Me ayuda? — Can you help me?
**Pronunciation:** *meh ah-YOO-dah*

More polite than just "Ayuda" (help). Combine with "por favor" for extra courtesy.

</div>
<div>

### 10. ¡Salud! — Cheers!
**Pronunciation:** *sah-LOOD*

Also means "health." Use it when toasting with new friends — or when someone sneezes.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Pronunciation tip">
Spanish is phonetic — words are pronounced exactly as they're written. The "ll" sounds like a "y" (as in "yellow"), and the "j" sounds like an "h" (as in "hotel"). The upside-down question mark (¿) signals a question coming, so your voice should rise at the end.
</Callout>

<Card title="Put Them Together">
**Example conversation at a café:**

- **You:** Hola, una café con leche, por favor. *(Hello, a coffee with milk, please.)*
- **Waiter:** ¿Algo más? *(Anything else?)*
- **You:** No, gracias. ¿Cuánto cuesta? *(No, thanks. How much does it cost?)*
- **Waiter:** Tres euros.
- **You:** Gracias. *(You pay.)* ¡Adiós! *(Goodbye!)*

**Pro tip:** Even a small effort in Spanish goes a long way. Locals will appreciate your attempt, and you'll get warmer service almost everywhere.
</Card>
