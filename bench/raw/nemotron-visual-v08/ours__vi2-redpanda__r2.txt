<Card title="Where It Lives" icon="map-pin">
Red pandas inhabit the temperate forests of the **Eastern Himalayas** and southwestern China.

| Region | Countries | Elevation |
|---|---|---|
| Eastern Himalayas | Nepal, Bhutan, India (Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh), Myanmar | 2,200–4,800 m |
| Hengduan Mountains | China (Sichuan, Yunnan, Tibet) | 1,800–4,000 m |

**Habitat:** Cool, moist broadleaf and coniferous forests with dense bamboo understory. They are arboreal and spend most of their time in trees.
</Card>

<Card title="What It Eats" icon="leaf">
Red pandas are **primarily herbivorous** but technically omnivores.

| Food Type | Examples | % of Diet |
|---|---|---|
| **Bamboo** | Leaves, shoots, stems (*Arundinaria*, *Thamnocalamus*) | 85–95% |
| **Other plants** | Fruits, berries, acorns, roots, lichens | 5–10% |
| **Occasional protein** | Insects, bird eggs, small rodents | <1% |

**Fun fact:** They have a modified wrist bone ("false thumb") that helps them grip bamboo stems, much like giant pandas.
</Card>

**Major threats:**
- Habitat loss from deforestation, agriculture, and infrastructure
- Poaching for fur and the illegal pet trade
- Climate change shifting bamboo distributions
- Human-wildlife conflict and domestic dog predation

**Protections:** Listed on CITES Appendix I (international trade banned). Protected in all range countries. Conservation efforts focus on habitat corridors, community-based monitoring, and anti-poaching patrols.
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Did you know?">
Despite the name, red pandas are not closely related to giant pandas. They are the only living member of the family **Ailuridae**, while giant pandas are bears (Ursidae). Their closest relatives are raccoons, weasels, and skunks.
</Callout>
