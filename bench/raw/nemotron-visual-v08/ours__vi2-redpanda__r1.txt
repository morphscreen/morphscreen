<Card title="Where It Lives" icon="map-pin">
**Native range:** Eastern Himalayas and southwestern China

| Region | Countries | Elevation |
|---|---|---|
| Eastern Himalayas | Nepal, Bhutan, India (Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, West Bengal) | 2,200–4,800 m |
| Hengduan Mountains | China (Sichuan, Yunnan, Tibet) | 1,800–4,000 m |

**Habitat:** Temperate broadleaf and coniferous forests with dense bamboo understory. They prefer north-facing slopes with fallen logs and tree hollows for denning.

<Callout tone="info" title="Range fragmentation">Populations are isolated in patches across this range due to habitat loss. Fewer than 10,000 mature individuals remain in the wild.</Callout>
</Card>

---

<Card title="What It Eats" icon="leaf">
**Primary diet:** Bamboo (98% of intake) — mainly leaves and tender shoots of *Arundinaria*, *Thamnocalamus*, and *Phyllostachys* species.

**Supplemental foods:**
- Fruits (berries, apples, grapes)
- Acorns and roots
- Insects, bird eggs, small rodents (occasionally)
- Flowers and lichens

**Feeding behavior:** Crepuscular foragers. They use a modified wrist bone ("false thumb") to grasp bamboo stems, stripping leaves with their mouths. An adult eats 1–2 kg of bamboo daily — roughly 20–30% of body weight.

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Low nutritional payoff">Bamboo is protein-poor and hard to digest. Red pandas have a simple gut and short retention time, so they must eat large volumes and rest extensively to conserve energy.</Callout>
</Card>

**Major threats:**
1. **Habitat loss & fragmentation** — logging, agriculture, infrastructure, livestock grazing
2. **Poaching** — pelts, pet trade, bycatch in snares
3. **Climate change** — bamboo die-offs, upslope range shifts
4. **Free-roaming dogs** — predation and disease (canine distemper)

**Protections:** CITES Appendix I (trade banned), national protection laws in all range countries, ~35 protected areas within range.
