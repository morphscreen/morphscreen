## Recommended One-Day Route

<Card title="Best Order for a Day" icon="map-pin">
<Steps>
<Step title="7:30 – Fushimi Inari Taisha" status="todo" subtitle="Start early to beat crowds at the torii tunnels">
Arrive at opening. Hike to the summit (2–3 hrs round trip) or stop at Yotsutsuji intersection for city views. The lower gates are busiest; crowds thin as you climb.
</Step>
<Step title="10:30 – Kinkaku-ji" status="todo" subtitle="Golden Pavilion, 30 min by bus/taxi from Fushimi Inari">
Walk the circuit around the pond for the classic reflection shot. The garden is compact—45 minutes is plenty. Bus 101 or 205 from Kyoto Station area.
</Step>
<Step title="12:00 – Lunch near Kinkaku-ji / Ryoan-ji area" status="todo" subtitle="Nishijin or Kitano district">
Try yudofu (simmered tofu) at a temple-adjacent restaurant, or soba in the Nishijin textile district.
</Step>
<Step title="13:30 – Ryoan-ji" status="todo" subtitle="10 min bus/taxi from Kinkaku-ji">
Sit before the rock garden—15 stones, only 14 visible from any angle. Allow 45–60 min for the garden, pond stroll, and tea house.
</Step>
<Step title="15:00 – Kiyomizu-dera" status="todo" subtitle="Bus 101/205 to Gojo-zaka, then 15 min uphill walk">
Explore the main hall's wooden stage, Jishu Shrine (love deity), and Otowa Waterfall. Stay for sunset over Higashiyama if visiting in autumn/spring.
</Step>
<Step title="17:30 – Evening in Higashiyama" status="todo" subtitle="Sannenzaka / Ninenzaka preserved lanes">
Wander the stone lanes, browse craft shops, grab matcha sweets. Perfect end to the day.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Quick Tips

<Columns>
<Card title="Transport" icon="calendar">
Buy a **Kyoto City Bus & Subway One-Day Pass** (¥900) for unlimited rides. All four temples are on the bus network.
</Card>
<Card title="Timing" icon="chart-line">
Weekdays are quieter. Avoid Japanese holidays (Golden Week, Obon, New Year) when domestic tourism peaks.
</Card>
<Card title="Etiquette" icon="users">
Speak softly, no photos inside worship halls, remove hats. At Fushimi Inari, don't walk under the center of torii—step to the side.
</Card>
</Columns>
