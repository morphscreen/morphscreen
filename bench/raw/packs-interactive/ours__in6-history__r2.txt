# French Revolution Revision

## Key Dates

<Card title="5 May 1789" subtitle="Estates-General convenes at Versailles">
Louis XVI summons the three estates to address the fiscal crisis — the first meeting since 1614.
</Card>

<Card title="17 June 1789" subtitle="National Assembly declared">
The Third Estate, joined by some clergy, proclaims itself the National Assembly, claiming sole authority to represent the nation.
</Card>

<Card title="20 June 1789" subtitle="Tennis Court Oath">
Locked out of their meeting hall, deputies swear not to separate until France has a constitution.
</Card>

<Card title="14 July 1789" subtitle="Storming of the Bastille">
Parisian crowds seize the royal fortress-prison, a flashpoint that becomes the Revolution's enduring symbol.
</Card>

<Card title="4 August 1789" subtitle="Abolition of feudalism">
In a night session, the Assembly sweeps away seigneurial dues, tithes, and noble privileges.
</Card>

<Card title="26 August 1789" subtitle="Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen">
The Assembly adopts a universal charter of liberty, equality, and popular sovereignty.
</Card>

<Card title="5–6 October 1789" subtitle="Women's March on Versailles">
Market women force the royal family to return to Paris, placing them under the capital's watch.
</Card>

<Card title="21 September 1792" subtitle="Monarchy abolished, First Republic proclaimed">
The new National Convention votes unanimously to end the monarchy.
</Card>

<Card title="21 January 1793" subtitle="Execution of Louis XVI">
The former king dies by guillotine on the Place de la Révolution.
</Card>

<Card title="27 July 1794" subtitle="Fall of Robespierre (9 Thermidor)">
The Terror's architect is arrested and executed, ending the most radical phase.
</Card>

<Card title="9 November 1799" subtitle="Coup of 18 Brumaire">
Napoleon Bonaparte overthrows the Directory, establishing the Consulate and effectively ending the Revolution.
</Card>

## Period Summary

<Card title="The French Revolution (1789–1799)">
The French Revolution was a decade of radical political and social upheaval that dismantled the *ancien régime* — the absolute monarchy, feudal privileges, and the Catholic Church's dominance — and replaced it with a succession of governments rooted in the principles of **liberty, equality, and fraternity**.

**Phases:**
- **1789–1791: Constitutional Monarchy** — The Estates-General becomes the National Assembly; feudalism is abolished; the Declaration of Rights is adopted; a constitution creates a limited monarchy.
- **1792–1794: The First Republic & the Terror** — War and internal revolt radicalise the Revolution; the monarchy falls; the Committee of Public Safety, led by Robespierre, rules through the Terror (~16,000 executed).
- **1794–1799: The Directory** — A moderate, corrupt five-man executive struggles with war, inflation, and royalist/Jacobin plots.
- **1799: The Consulate** — Napoleon's coup ends the Revolution and begins his authoritarian rule, later the Empire.

**Legacy:** The Revolution spread nationalist and democratic ideas across Europe, inspired later revolutions (1830, 1848, 1917), and established the template for modern political ideologies — liberalism, socialism, conservatism — all defined in reaction to it.
</Card>
