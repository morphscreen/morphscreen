# Water Cycle Quiz

Test your knowledge of the water cycle! Each question has one correct answer.

<Quiz title="Year 7 Science – Water Cycle">
<Question text="What is the process called when water changes from a liquid to a gas (water vapour)?" answer="Evaporation" explain="Evaporation is the process where heat energy causes water molecules to move faster and escape into the air as invisible water vapour. This happens from oceans, lakes, rivers, and even puddles.">
- Evaporation
- Condensation
- Precipitation
- Collection
</Question>

<Question text="When water vapour cools and changes back into tiny liquid droplets, forming clouds, what is this process called?" answer="Condensation" explain="Condensation occurs when water vapour loses heat energy and changes back into liquid form. The tiny droplets gather around dust particles in the air to form clouds.">
- Evaporation
- Condensation
- Precipitation
- Transpiration
</Question>

<Question text="What do we call water falling from clouds as rain, snow, sleet, or hail?" answer="Precipitation" explain="Precipitation is any form of water that falls from clouds to Earth's surface. The type depends on temperature: rain (liquid), snow (ice crystals), sleet (ice pellets), or hail (larger ice balls).">
- Evaporation
- Condensation
- Precipitation
- Infiltration
</Question>

<Question text="What is the name for the process where plants release water vapour from their leaves?" answer="Transpiration" explain="Transpiration is like plants 'sweating' – they take up water through their roots and release it as vapour through tiny pores (stomata) in their leaves. This helps pull water up through the plant.">
- Evaporation
- Condensation
- Precipitation
- Transpiration
</Question>

<Question text="After precipitation falls, water soaks into the ground and fills spaces between rocks and soil. What is this called?" answer="Infiltration" explain="Infiltration is the process of water seeping into the ground. It replenishes groundwater supplies (aquifers) and eventually flows back into rivers, lakes, and oceans to continue the cycle.">
- Evaporation
- Condensation
- Runoff
- Infiltration
</Question>
</Quiz>
