# French Revolution Revision

## Key Dates

<Card title="1789 — Estates-General & Tennis Court Oath" subtitle="May–June 1789">
Louis XVI summons the Estates-General to address the fiscal crisis. The Third Estate declares itself the National Assembly and swears the Tennis Court Oath (20 June), vowing not to separate until a constitution is established.
</Card>

<Card title="14 July 1789 — Storming of the Bastille" subtitle="Symbolic start of the Revolution">
Parisian crowds seize the Bastille fortress-prison, a symbol of royal absolutism. The event triggers the Great Fear across rural France and forces the king to recognise the National Assembly.
</Card>

<Card title="26 August 1789 — Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen" subtitle="Foundational document">
The National Assembly adopts a charter of individual liberties and popular sovereignty, inspired by Enlightenment ideals and the American Revolution.
</Card>

<Card title="1791 — Constitution & Flight to Varennes" subtitle="June 1791">
Louis XVI attempts to flee Paris but is captured at Varennes, destroying trust in the monarchy. The Constitution of 1791 creates a constitutional monarchy with a Legislative Assembly.
</Card>

<Card title="10 August 1792 — Storming of the Tuileries" subtitle="Fall of the monarchy">
Insurrectionaries attack the Tuileries Palace. The monarchy is effectively suspended; the Legislative Assembly calls for a National Convention elected by universal male suffrage.
</Card>

<Card title="21 September 1792 — Republic Proclaimed" subtitle="First day of Year I">
The National Convention abolishes the monarchy and declares the French Republic.
</Card>

<Card title="21 January 1793 — Execution of Louis XVI" subtitle="Regicide">
The former king is guillotined on the Place de la Révolution after a trial by the Convention. War with European coalitions intensifies.
</Card>

<Card title="1793–1794 — The Terror" subtitle="Committee of Public Safety">
Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety rule through revolutionary tribunals. ~16,000–40,000 executions. Ends with Robespierre’s fall on 9 Thermidor Year II (27 July 1794).
</Card>

<Card title="1795 — Constitution of Year III & the Directory" subtitle="Thermidorian Reaction">
A new constitution establishes the Directory, a five-man executive. The period is marked by corruption, war, and reliance on the army.
</Card>

<Card title="9 November 1799 — Coup of 18 Brumaire" subtitle="Napoleon seizes power">
Napoleon Bonaparte overthrows the Directory and establishes the Consulate, effectively ending the revolutionary decade.
</Card>

## Period Summary

<Card title="The French Revolution (1789–1799) — Overview">
The French Revolution was a decade of radical political and social upheaval that dismantled the *ancien régime*—the absolute monarchy, feudal privileges, and the dominance of the Catholic Church—and replaced it with a succession of republican governments.

**Phases**
1. **Moderate (1789–1791)**: Constitutional monarchy; Declaration of Rights; feudalism abolished.
2. **Radical (1792–1794)**: Republic declared; war with Europe; the Terror under the Committee of Public Safety.
3. **Thermidorian & Directory (1794–1799)**: Reaction against the Terror; unstable five-man Directory; reliance on military.
4. **Consulate (1799 onward)**: Napoleon’s coup ends the revolutionary period proper.

**Key themes**
- **Popular sovereignty** replaces divine-right monarchy.
- **Legal equality** ends noble/clerical privilege.
- **Secularism** reduces Church power (Civil Constitution of the Clergy, dechristianisation).
- **War** drives radicalisation and centralisation.
- **Violence** — from Bastille to Terror to Vendée — accompanies each stage.

**Legacy**
The Revolution spread nationalism, republicanism, and rights-based citizenship across Europe and the Atlantic world. Its symbols (tricolour, *Marseillaise*, 14 July) remain central to French identity.
</Card>
