# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A walking-and-trains plan that loops through the city neighborhood by neighborhood, so you spend your time seeing things, not riding. Dates below assume you land on a Monday.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you book">
Get a **Suica or Pasmo card** at the airport (or add Suica to Apple Wallet) — it works on every train, subway, bus and convenience store. A 72-hour Tokyo Subway Ticket (~¥1,500) pays off if you ride 4+ times a day.
</Callout>

<Card title="The shape of the trip" subtitle="Each day is one cluster, so you never backtrack">
<Steps>
<Step title="Day 1 — Shinjuku & Shibuya" status="current" subtitle="Arrival, neon, the famous crossing">
Land, drop bags, then ease in with the west side. Jet lag actually helps here: the lights are best after dark.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 2 — Asakusa & Ueno" status="todo" subtitle="Old Tokyo, temples, museums">
The historic east side. Senso-ji early (before 8am it's nearly empty), then Ueno's park and museums.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 3 — Tsukiji, Ginza & Imperial Palace" status="todo" subtitle="Breakfast, then the center">
Tsukiji Outer Market for breakfast, walk to Ginza, then the Imperial Palace East Gardens.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 4 — Harajuku, Omotesando & Meiji Shrine" status="todo" subtitle="Forest, fashion, then Shinjuku at night">
Shrine first thing, then the shopping streets, then a night out in Golden Gai or Omoide Yokocho.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 5 — Day trip: Nikko or Kamakura" status="todo" subtitle="Or stay in town for teamLab + Odaiba">
Pick one. Nikko is shrines and mountains; Kamakura is the Great Buddha and the sea.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Nikko instead?">
Nikko needs the full day and a 2-hour train each way. Worth it for Toshogu Shrine and the Kegon Falls, but it's a long day. Kamakura is 1 hour and much easier on tired legs.
</Callout>

<Card title="Practical notes">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Trains stop around midnight">
No 24-hour trains. Plan your last ride, or budget for a taxi — they're expensive but honest.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="neutral" title="Tipping and trash">
No tipping anywhere. Almost no public bins — carry a small bag for trash and take it back to the hotel.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="success" title="Two people, one rule">
Split up when you want to. Tokyo is safe and easy to navigate alone; meet back at a station.
</Callout>
</Card>
