# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A walkable, low-stress itinerary that keeps each day in one area so you spend time seeing things, not riding trains. Everything below is on the map at the end.

<Callout tone="info" title="How to move around">
Get a **Suica or Pasmo** card at the airport (or add Suica to Apple/Google Wallet). Load ¥5,000 each and tap in and out of every train, subway and bus. A 24/48/72-hour **Tokyo Subway Ticket** is only worth it on heavy subway days — Days 2 and 3 here.
</Callout>

<Card title="Day-by-day plan">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Day 1 — Shinjuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Land and check in" status="done" subtitle="Afternoon">Drop bags at your Shinjuku hotel. Freshen up, then walk.</Step>
<Step title="Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building" status="current" subtitle="Free observation deck, 202 m">Go before sunset for daylight views, stay for the lights coming on. Two towers, both free.</Step>
<Step title="Omoide Yokocho" subtitle="Dinner">Tiny yakitori alleys under the tracks. Pick a counter with locals, order skewers and beer.</Step>
<Step title="Kabukicho + Golden Gai" subtitle="Night">Walk the neon, then squeeze into a 6-seat bar in Golden Gai. Expect a small seating charge.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 2 — Asakusa & Ueno">
<Steps>
<Step title="Senso-ji and Nakamise Street" subtitle="Early, before 9am">The temple is best before the crowds. Buy ningyo-yaki and melon pan on the way in.</Step>
<Step title="Ueno Park and museums" subtitle="Midday">Tokyo National Museum for the samurai and ceramics. Park itself is free.</Step>
<Step title="Ameyoko market" subtitle="Lunch">Under the JR tracks: seafood, fruit, snacks, chaos.</Step>
<Step title="Akihabara" subtitle="Late afternoon">Electronics, retro games, gachapon. Super Potato for old consoles.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 3 — Shibuya & Harajuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Meiji Jingu" subtitle="Morning">Forest shrine in the middle of the city. Free, quiet, 30–45 min walk.</Step>
<Step title="Takeshita Street, Harajuku" subtitle="Midday">Crepes, cat cafes, vintage shops. Then Cat Street for calmer browsing.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Crossing and Hachiko" subtitle="Afternoon">Cross it twice, then watch from the Starbucks or Mag's Park rooftop.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Sky" subtitle="Sunset">Book online in advance. Best sunset view in Tokyo.</Step>
<Step title="Nonbei Yokocho" subtitle="Dinner">Drinking alley next to the station, quieter than Golden Gai.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 4 — Day trip">
<Steps>
<Step title="Pick one" subtitle="Hakone, Nikko, or Kamakura">Hakone for onsen and Mt Fuji views; Nikko for shrines and fall color; Kamakura for the Great Buddha and the coast.</Step>
<Step title="Leave by 7am" subtitle="Full day">About 1–2 hours each way. Buy a round-trip limited express or a Hakone Free Pass.</Step>
<Step title="Return by evening" subtitle="Optional">Back in Tokyo by 8pm for a late bowl of ramen.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 5 — Tsukiji & Ginza">
<Steps>
<Step title="Tsukiji Outer Market" subtitle="Breakfast, 7–9am">Tamagoyaki on a stick, uni, fresh tuna, matcha. Go early; stalls close by noon.</Step>
<Step title="Ginza" subtitle="Midday">Uniqlo flagship, Itoya stationery, department store food halls (depachika).</Step>
<Step title="Imperial Palace East Gardens" subtitle="Afternoon">Free, closed Mondays and Fridays. Check before you go.</Step>
<Step title="Last dinner" subtitle="Evening">Splurge on sushi, or go for tonkatsu. Book ahead for anything famous.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Two things people get wrong">
**Don't** try to see Shinjuku, Asakusa and Shibuya in one day — you'll spend it underground. **Do** carry cash; many small counters and shrines still don't take cards.
</Callout>
