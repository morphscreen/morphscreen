# Hard-to-kill houseplants

The trick isn't finding a plant that never dies — it's finding one that forgives you when you forget it for two weeks. Here's what actually survives neglect.

<Card title="The short answer" subtitle="Ranked by how badly you can mistreat them">
| Plant | Light | Water | Survives neglect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snake plant | Any, even a dim corner | Every 2–3 weeks | 4–6 weeks |
| ZZ plant | Low to bright indirect | Every 2–3 weeks | 4–6 weeks |
| Pothos | Low to bright indirect | Weekly | 2–3 weeks |
| Spider plant | Bright indirect | Weekly | 2 weeks |
| Cast iron plant | Low light | Every 2 weeks | 3–4 weeks |
| Aloe vera | Bright, direct | Every 2–3 weeks | 3–4 weeks |
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The one rule that matters most">
Overwatering kills far more houseplants than forgetting them. When in doubt, wait. A plant that's thirsty droops and recovers; a plant that's drowned rots and doesn't.
</Callout>

<Card title="What kills them anyway" subtitle="Four ways to lose an easy plant">
<Steps>
<Step title="Watering on a schedule" status="todo">
Water when the top inch of soil is dry, not every Sunday. Seasons change how fast soil dries.
</Step>
<Step title="Letting it sit in a full saucer" status="todo">
Roots rot in standing water. Empty the saucer 20 minutes after watering.
</Step>
<Step title="Putting a low-light plant in direct sun" status="todo">
"Tolerates low light" doesn't mean "loves a south window." Leaves will scorch.
</Step>
<Step title="Repotting into a huge pot" status="todo">
A pot far bigger than the root ball holds wet soil the roots can't reach. Go up one size only.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="If you travel a lot">
Snake plant and ZZ plant are the two to buy. Both store water in their tissues and can go a month without you. Group them away from radiators and air vents, which dry soil faster.
</Callout>
