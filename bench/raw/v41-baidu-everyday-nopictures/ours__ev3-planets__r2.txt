# The planets of our solar system

Eight planets orbit the Sun, plus a dwarf planet (Pluto) and countless smaller bodies. They fall into two very different families: the small rocky worlds inside the asteroid belt, and the giant gas and ice worlds beyond it.

<Callout tone="info" title="Two families, one line between them">
The four inner planets are small, dense and rocky. The four outer planets are huge, made mostly of gas and ice, and have rings and many moons. The asteroid belt sits roughly between them.
</Callout>

<Card title="Planet by planet">
<Steps>
<Step title="Mercury" subtitle="Smallest, fastest, closest">
Tiny and airless, scorched by day and frozen by night. It laps the Sun every 88 days.
</Step>
<Step title="Venus" subtitle="Hottest planet">
A thick carbon-dioxide atmosphere traps heat, keeping the surface near 465 °C — hotter than Mercury. It spins backwards, and slowly.
</Step>
<Step title="Earth" subtitle="The only known home to life">
Liquid water, a protective atmosphere and a magnetic field make it uniquely habitable so far.
</Step>
<Step title="Mars" subtitle="The rusty one">
Iron oxide dust gives it its red colour. It has the tallest volcano (Olympus Mons) and a canyon system that dwarfs the Grand Canyon.
</Step>
<Step title="Jupiter" subtitle="The giant">
More massive than all other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth that has raged for centuries.
</Step>
<Step title="Saturn" subtitle="The ringed world">
Its rings are mostly ice chunks, wide but astonishingly thin. It is the least dense planet — it would float in water.
</Step>
<Step title="Uranus" subtitle="The tipped-over one">
It rotates on its side, likely knocked over by an ancient collision, so its poles get decades of continuous sunlight and darkness.
</Step>
<Step title="Neptune" subtitle="Farthest and windiest">
Winds reach 2,100 km/h, the fastest in the solar system. It was found by maths before it was seen through a telescope.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
