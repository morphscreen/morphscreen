# The Planets of Our Solar System

Eight planets orbit the Sun, plus dwarf planets like Pluto. They fall into two families: the small rocky worlds close in, and the giant gas and ice worlds farther out.

<Callout tone="info" title="Why Pluto isn't on the list">
In 2006 the IAU redefined "planet" to require that a body clear its own orbital neighborhood. Pluto shares its orbit with many other icy objects, so it was reclassified as a dwarf planet.
</Callout>

<Card title="How the planets formed">
<Steps>
<Step title="A cloud of gas and dust" status="done">A giant molecular cloud collapses under gravity, spinning into a flattened disk.</Step>
<Step title="The Sun ignites" status="done">The dense center heats until hydrogen fusion begins, lighting up the solar system.</Step>
<Step title="Dust sticks together" status="done">Grains clump into pebbles, then boulders, then planetesimals.</Step>
<Step title="Rocky planets close in" status="done">Near the Sun, only rock and metal can stay solid, forming Mercury through Mars.</Step>
<Step title="Giants sweep up gas" status="done">Beyond the frost line, ice and gas build Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.</Step>
<Step title="Leftovers remain" status="current">Asteroids and comets are the material that never joined a planet.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
