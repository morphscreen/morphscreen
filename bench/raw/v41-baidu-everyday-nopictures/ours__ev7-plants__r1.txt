# Hard-to-kill houseplants

The plants below survive neglect, low light, and irregular watering. They're the ones to start with if you've killed plants before.

<Card title="The shortlist" subtitle="Ranked by how hard they are to kill">
| Plant | Light | Water | Why it forgives you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snake plant (Sansevieria) | Low to bright | Every 2–3 weeks | Stores water in its leaves; rot is the main risk, not drought |
| ZZ plant (Zamioculcas) | Low to bright | Every 2–3 weeks | Rhizomes hold water; tolerates a dark corner |
| Pothos (Epipremnum) | Low to bright | Weekly | Wilts visibly to tell you it's thirsty, then bounces back |
| Spider plant (Chlorophytum) | Medium | Weekly | Sends out babies you can pot up; hard to drown |
| Cast iron plant (Aspidistra) | Low | Every 2 weeks | Named for a reason; tolerates neglect and shade |
| Peace lily (Spathiphyllum) | Low to medium | Weekly | Dramatically droops when dry, recovers within hours |
| Aloe vera | Bright | Every 3 weeks | Wants to be ignored; only dies from overwatering |
| Rubber plant (Ficus elastica) | Medium to bright | Every 1–2 weeks | Thick leaves; adapts to most rooms |
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="The one rule that matters most">
Overwatering kills more houseplants than neglect. When in doubt, wait. Stick a finger an inch into the soil — if it's damp, don't water.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Toxic to cats and dogs">
Snake plant, pothos, ZZ plant, and peace lily are all toxic if chewed. If you have pets, choose spider plant, cast iron plant, or a calathea instead — or keep the plant on a high shelf.
</Callout>

<Card title="A simple care routine" subtitle="Ten minutes a month covers most of these">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check the soil before watering" status="current">
Push a finger an inch down. Dry means water; damp means wait a few more days.
</Step>
<Step title="Water thoroughly, then let it drain">
Water until it runs out the bottom, then empty the saucer. Never let the pot sit in water.
</Step>
<Step title="Feed lightly in spring and summer">
A diluted liquid fertilizer once a month. Skip it entirely in winter.
</Step>
<Step title="Wipe the leaves now and then">
Dust blocks light. A damp cloth every few weeks keeps them working.
</Step>
<Step title="Repot only when rootbound">
If roots come out the drainage holes, go one pot size up. Otherwise leave it alone.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common ways people kill these anyway">
| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Watering on a schedule | Root rot | Water when the soil is dry, not on a calendar |
| No drainage hole | Root rot | Always use a pot with holes, or a nursery pot inside a cachepot |
| Moving it constantly | Dropped leaves, stress | Pick a spot and leave it there |
| Full sun on a low-light plant | Scorched, bleached leaves | Bright indirect light, not direct midday sun |
| Cold draughts or radiators | Crispy edges | Keep away from vents and windows in winter |
</Card>
