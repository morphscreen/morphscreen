# Rent or buy? The honest maths on £60k

Short answer: **buying wins on a 5+ year horizon, but only if you can raise the deposit without emptying your savings.** On a £60k salary, lenders will typically offer you around **£270k–£300k**, which is the real constraint — not the rent-vs-buy maths itself.

<Callout tone="info" title="The rule of thumb, applied to you">
Renting usually wins under 4 years. Buying usually wins past 6–7 years. Between 4 and 7 it's close enough that **how long you'll stay** matters more than the numbers.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The bit people forget">
The mortgage payment is not the cost of owning. **Maintenance, insurance and service charges add roughly 10–15%** on top of the mortgage — here that's about £325 a month, which more than wipes out the £230 you save on the payment itself.
</Callout>

## Where the money actually goes

<Chart type="bar" x="Year" y="GBP">

Rent is a pure outflow. A mortgage payment is part interest (outflow) and part principal (yours). At 5% on £270k, roughly **£1,125/mo is interest and £445/mo is equity** in year one — and the equity share grows every year.

This is the crossover. Past here, owning wins clearly — assuming you stay put.

## The three questions that decide it

<Checklist title="Decide before you commit" name="rent-buy-check">
- [ ] Am I staying in this city for **at least 5 years**?
- [ ] Can I raise a deposit **and** keep 3–6 months of expenses untouched?
- [ ] Would I be happy in a smaller or less central flat than I rent now?
- [ ] Is my job stable enough to carry a mortgage through a rate rise?
- [ ] Have I got a mortgage in principle to know my real budget?
</Checklist>

<Callout tone="danger" title="The trap to avoid">
Do not use your entire savings as the deposit. On £60k with £1,800 rent, you have little slack — **a boiler, a roof or a rate rise with no buffer is how people lose the flat.** Keep at least £10k back.
</Callout>

## What I'd actually do

<Card title="A concrete plan">
<Steps>
<Step title="Get a mortgage in principle" status="current" subtitle="This week">
Free, takes 20 minutes, and tells you your true borrowing ceiling instead of guessing.
</Step>
<Step title="Save hard for 12–18 months" status="todo" subtitle="Target £40k–£50k">
On £3,850 take-home with £1,800 rent, you need to be deliberate. £40k deposit gets you to ~£310k at 5% down plus fees and a buffer.
</Step>
<Step title="Consider a cheaper rental for one year" status="todo" subtitle="The accelerator">
Dropping to £1,400/mo rent frees £400/mo — about £5k a year toward the deposit, and it's the single biggest lever you have.
</Step>
<Step title="Buy at year 5+, not year 1" status="todo" subtitle="Then stay">
Buy when you're confident of staying. The maths only rewards you if you don't sell early.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Verdict">
**Buy — but not yet.** Renting is not wasted money if it buys you the deposit and the certainty. Aim to buy in 2–3 years with a £40k+ deposit, keep £10k in reserve, and plan to stay at least 5 years. If your life is likely to move city or change shape before then, keep renting without guilt.
</Callout>
