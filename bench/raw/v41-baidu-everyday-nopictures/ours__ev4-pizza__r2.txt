# Neapolitan pizza at home

Real Neapolitan pizza is 4 things: a soft, high-hydration dough, a very hot oven, a simple uncooked tomato, and 60–90 seconds. Home ovens can't hit 900°F, but you can get 90% of the way there.

<Callout tone="info" title="The honest expectation">
A home oven maxes out around 500–550°F (260–290°C). You'll get a great pizza in 4–6 minutes, not the leopard-spotted 60-second bake of a wood oven. The two tricks that close the gap: a preheated steel/stone and a broiler finish.
</Callout>

## The dough

<Card title="Dough formula" subtitle="4 balls, ~250g each — scale as needed">
| Ingredient | Baker's % | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Flour (W300–W320, e.g. Caputo Pizzeria) | 100% | 600 g |
| Water (cool, ~60°F) | 62% | 372 g |
| Sea salt | 2.8% | 17 g |
| Fresh yeast | 0.2% | 1.2 g |
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Yeast amount is not a typo">
0.2% fresh yeast is correct for a 24–48 hour room-temperature ferment. More yeast = fast rise = flat, bland, bready dough. Time is the flavor.
</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step title="Dissolve salt in the water" status="done">
Stir the salt into the water until fully dissolved. Never dump salt directly onto the flour — it damages gluten.
</Step>
<Step title="Add ~10% of the flour, then the yeast" status="done">
Whisk in a handful of flour, then crumble in the yeast. This protects the yeast from the salt.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the rest of the flour" status="done">
Mix with a spoon, then your hands, until no dry flour remains. It will look shaggy and sticky. That's right.
</Step>
<Step title="Rest 20 minutes (autolyse)" status="done">
Cover and walk away. The flour hydrates and gluten forms on its own.
</Step>
<Step title="Slap and fold 10–15 minutes" status="current">
On a lightly wet counter, no flour. Lift, slap down, fold over. The dough goes from sticky to smooth and silky. Windowpane test: stretch a piece thin enough to see light through.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment 2 hours at room temp" status="todo">
Covered. It should grow ~50%, not double.
</Step>
<Step title="Ball it" status="todo">
Divide into 4 × ~250g. Fold each into a tight ball, seam down, pinching the bottom closed.
</Step>
<Step title="Proof 18–24 hours" status="todo">
In a covered tray or containers at cool room temp (65–68°F). They should roughly double and be jiggly, not collapsed.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The sauce and the toppings

<Card title="Sauce" subtitle="Do not cook it">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Whole peeled San Marzano tomatoes | 400 g can |
| Salt | 3 g |
| Olive oil | 1 tsp |
</Card>

Crush the tomatoes by hand or pulse twice in a blender — you want texture, not purée. Add salt and oil. That's it. No garlic, no herbs, no simmering.

<Card title="Toppings, in order">
| Order | Item | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sauce | 2–3 oz per pizza, spread thin with the back of a ladle |
| 2 | Fresh mozzarella | Torn, drained on paper towel 30 min — wet cheese = soggy pizza |
| 3 | Basil | Added *after* the bake for Margherita |
| 4 | Olive oil | A thin swirl before baking |
| 5 | Parmigiano | Optional, a light dusting |
</Card>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Never do this">
Don't pre-shred mozzarella, don't use jarred sauce, don't pile toppings high, and don't roll the dough with a pin. A rolling pin crushes the gas bubbles that make the cornicione puff.
</Callout>

## Shaping and baking

<Card title="Shaping">
<Steps>
<Step title="Flour your bench with semolina or 00 flour" status="done" />
<Step title="Press from the center outward with your fingertips" status="done">
Leave a 1-inch rim untouched — that's your cornicione.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch over your knuckles, rotating" status="current">
Never press the rim. Gravity does the work. Stop at 10–12 inches.
</Step>
<Step title="Transfer to a floured peel" status="todo">
Shake the peel to confirm the dough slides before you top it.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Bake">
<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat steel or stone at max temp, 45–60 min" status="done">
Place it in the upper third of the oven. Full preheat is not optional.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake 4–5 min on the steel" status="current">
Top rack, as hot as the oven goes.
</Step>
<Step title="Switch to broiler for the last 60–90 seconds" status="todo">
This is what gives you the char and the melted top. Watch it — it goes from perfect to burnt in 20 seconds.
</Step>
<Step title="Rest 1 minute, then eat" status="todo">
It's best in the first 3 minutes. This is not a make-ahead food.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Troubleshooting

<Compare title="What went wrong">
| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| Dough springs back when stretching | Under-proofed, gluten too tight | Rest 20 min, then stretch again |
| Dough tears easily | Over-proofed or too much water | Use less water, shorter proof, handle gently |
| Soggy center | Wet mozzarella, too much sauce | Drain cheese, use less sauce, bake on steel |
| Pale, no char | Oven not hot enough | Longer preheat, broiler finish |
| Dense, bready crumb | Too much yeast, short ferment | Cut yeast, ferment 24–48 h |
| No puff in the rim | Rolled it, or pressed the rim | Fingertips only, never a pin |
</Compare>

<Callout tone="success" title="The one thing that matters most">
Long, cool fermentation. Everything else is technique you'll pick up in 3 or 4 bakes. The flavor comes from time, not from ingredients.
</Callout>

<Checklist title="Before your first bake" name="pizza-prep">
- [ ] Flour with W300+ protein (Caputo Pizzeria or similar)
- [ ] Fresh yeast or a tiny amount of instant dry
- [ ] San Marzano canned tomatoes
- [ ] Fresh mozzarella (or fior di latte), drained
- [ ] Pizza steel or baking stone
- [ ] Pizza peel (or a rimless sheet pan + parchment)
- [ ] Digital scale — baker's percentages don't work by volume
- [ ] 24 hours of patience
</Checklist>
