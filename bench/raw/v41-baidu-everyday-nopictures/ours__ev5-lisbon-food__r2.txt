# Eating in Lisbon: what to order, and where to go

Lisbon rewards grazing. The best meals are cheap, salty, and eaten standing up or at a counter. Here's what to eat, then where to find it.

<Card title="What to eat" subtitle="The dishes worth planning a day around">
| Dish | What it is | Order it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Pastéis de nata** | Custard tart, blistered top, still warm | Breakfast, always |
| **Bacalhau à Brás** | Salt cod shredded with egg, onion, straw potatoes | Lunch |
| **Sardinhas assadas** | Grilled sardines on bread, olive oil | June–September, dinner |
| **Bifana** | Pork cutlet in a papo seco roll, garlic and mustard | Any time you're walking |
| **Arroz de marisco** | Wet seafood rice, tomato and coriander | Dinner, for two |
| **Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato** | Clams in garlic, olive oil, coriander | As a starter |
| **Queijadas de Sintra** | Small cheese-and-almond cakes | If you go to Sintra |
| **Ginjinha** | Sour cherry liqueur, one shot | After dinner, standing at a counter |
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="The rhythm of a Lisbon day">
Lunch runs 12:30–15:00 and is the big meal. Dinner starts late — 20:00 is early, 21:30 is normal. Between 15:00 and 19:00 most kitchens close, so plan a *pastelaria* stop for that gap.
</Callout>

<Card title="A one-day eating route" subtitle="Walkable, roughly 4 km">
<Steps>
<Step title="Pastel de nata and bica" status="done" subtitle="Morning, Baixa">
Manteigaria on Rua do Loreto. Two natas, one espresso. Stand at the counter.
</Step>
<Step title="Market graze" status="done" subtitle="Late morning, Cais do Sodré">
Time Out Market — but go to the edges, where the actual vendors are, not the central bars.
</Step>
<Step title="Bifana stop" status="current" subtitle="Midday, Baixa">
O Trevo or a random tasca. Mustard, no cutlery, eat it walking.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch in Alfama" status="todo" subtitle="13:30">
Pick a tasca with a handwritten menu and no English version. Order the prato do dia.
</Step>
<Step title="Ginjinha" status="todo" subtitle="18:00, Rossio">
A Ginjinha, one shot, at the counter. Then walk it off uphill.
</Step>
<Step title="Seafood dinner" status="todo" subtitle="20:30">
Cervejaria Ramiro: clams, garlic prawns, then the steak sandwich to finish. Book or arrive at 19:45.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Things to know before you order">
<Checklist title="Lisbon eating checklist" name="lisbon-food">
- [ ] Carry cash — many tascas are cash-only
- [ ] Say *um café* for espresso; *uma bica* in Lisbon
- [ ] Bread, olives, and cheese on the table are charged — say no if you don't want them
- [ ] *Prato do dia* is the cheapest real meal, usually lunch only
- [ ] Tipping is optional; round up or leave 5%
- [ ] Book dinner for Friday and Saturday, or eat at 19:30
- [ ] Sardines are best June–September; bacalhau is year-round
</Checklist>
</Card>
