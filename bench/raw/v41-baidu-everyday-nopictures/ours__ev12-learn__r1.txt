# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae, and some bacteria turn **light energy into chemical energy** — they build sugar out of air and water, using sunlight as the power source. Every food chain on Earth ultimately runs on it.

<Card title="The one-line version" subtitle="The overall reaction">
Plants take in **carbon dioxide** and **water**, use **light** to split them apart, and produce **glucose** (sugar) plus **oxygen** as a by-product.

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light} \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Overall equation" />

Read it as: 6 carbon dioxide + 6 water + light energy → 1 glucose + 6 oxygen.
</Card>

<Card title="Where it happens" subtitle="The chloroplast, the plant's solar factory">
A leaf is packed with **chloroplasts** — tiny organelles with two working areas:

<KeyValue title="Parts of a chloroplast">
| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Thylakoid membranes | Capture light; run the light-dependent reactions |
| Stroma (fluid around them) | Run the Calvin cycle; build sugar |
| Chlorophyll | The green pigment that actually absorbs light |
| Grana (stacks of thylakoids) | Packed stacks that increase surface area for light capture |
</KeyValue>

Chlorophyll absorbs **red and blue** light and reflects **green** — which is why leaves look green to us.
</Card>

<Card title="The two stages">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Stage 1 — Light-dependent reactions">
**Where:** thylakoid membranes
**Needs:** light, water
**Makes:** ATP and NADPH (energy carriers), plus oxygen

Light knocks electrons off chlorophyll. Those electrons travel down a chain of proteins, and the energy released pumps protons across the membrane. Water is split to replace the lost electrons — and that splitting is where the **oxygen** comes from. The proton gradient then drives **ATP synthase**, the same kind of turbine your own cells use.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Stage 2 — Calvin cycle">
**Where:** stroma
**Needs:** CO₂, ATP, NADPH
**Makes:** glucose (sugar)

This stage doesn't need light directly — it runs on the ATP and NADPH from stage 1. An enzyme called **RuBisCO** grabs CO₂ from the air and attaches it to an existing molecule. Using the energy carriers, the plant reduces that into sugar and rebuilds its starting material so the cycle can run again.

<Callout tone="info" title="Why it's called a cycle">The molecule that captures CO₂ is regenerated each turn, so the process loops rather than stopping after one sugar.</Callout>
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Follow the atoms" subtitle="Where each product actually comes from">
This is the part most people get wrong:

<Compare title="Source of each output">
| Output | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Oxygen (O₂) | Split water (H₂O) |
| Carbon in sugar | Carbon dioxide (CO₂) from the air |
| Hydrogen in sugar | Water (H₂O) |
| Energy in sugar | Sunlight |
</Compare>

So the oxygen you breathe was never the CO₂ — it was the water.
</Card>

<Card title="What changes the rate" subtitle="The three limiting factors">
<Chart type="bar" x="Factor" y="Effect on rate">
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Light intensity | Rises, then plateaus once saturated |
| CO₂ concentration | Rises, then plateaus |
| Temperature | Rises to an optimum, then falls as enzymes denature |
</Chart>

Whichever of these is in shortest supply becomes the **limiting factor** — raise anything else and nothing improves.
</Card>

<Card title="Remember these">
<Flashcards title="Key terms">
| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment that absorbs red and blue light |
| Thylakoid | Membrane where light-dependent reactions occur |
| Stroma | Fluid where the Calvin cycle builds sugar |
| RuBisCO | Enzyme that captures CO₂ into the cycle |
| ATP synthase | Protein turbine driven by the proton gradient |
| Limiting factor | The input in shortest supply, capping the rate |
</Flashcards>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The big picture">Photosynthesis runs on two coupled stages: light splits water and stores energy, then that energy is spent assembling CO₂ into sugar. Light in, sugar and oxygen out — and nearly all life on Earth eats the proceeds.</Callout>
