# Neapolitan pizza at home

The short version: it's four ingredients and a very hot oven. The hard part is patience — the dough needs 24–48 hours cold, and the bake needs ~450–500 °C for 60–90 seconds. Most home ovens can't do that, but there are workarounds.

<Callout tone="info" title="What makes it 'Neapolitan'">
Four rules do most of the work: a long cold ferment, a soft high-hydration dough handled gently, a raw crushed-tomato sauce (never cooked first), and a bake fast enough that the crust puffs but doesn't dry out.
</Callout>

<Card title="The dough" subtitle="Makes 4 balls of ~250 g — enough for four 30 cm pizzas">

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Tipo 00 flour | 600 g | 100% |
| Water (cold) | 390 g | 65% |
| Fine sea salt | 18 g | 3% |
| Fresh yeast | 1.5 g | 0.25% |
| *or* dry yeast | 0.5 g | 0.08% |

<Callout tone="warning" title="That yeast number is not a typo">
At 24–48 hours cold, you need a tiny amount. Too much yeast and the dough over-proofs, turns soupy, and tastes of alcohol. If you only have 8 hours, use ~3 g fresh / 1 g dry and ferment at room temperature.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Method" subtitle="Hands-on time is about 30 minutes, spread over two days">

<Steps>
<Step title="Dissolve the salt in the water" status="todo">
Cold water straight from the tap. Stir until no crystals remain — salt left undissolved will spot-burn the dough.
</Step>
<Step title="Add ~10% of the flour and the yeast" status="todo">
Whisk to a loose slurry. This hydrates the yeast evenly instead of letting it clump.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the rest of the flour and mix" status="todo">
In a bowl with a spoon, then by hand, until no dry flour shows. It will look shaggy and sticky. That's correct.
</Step>
<Step title="Rest 20 minutes, then knead" status="todo">
Cover and wait — the flour hydrates and the dough becomes far easier to handle. Then knead 8–10 minutes until smooth and slightly tacky, not sticky.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment 1 hour at room temperature" status="todo">
Covered. It should grow a little, not double.
</Step>
<Step title="Ball it and refrigerate 24–48 hours" status="todo">
Divide into four 250 g balls. Tuck each into a tight sphere, seam down, into a lightly oiled tray with room between them. Cover. Fridge.
</Step>
<Step title="Pull out 2–3 hours before baking" status="current">
Let them come to room temperature. Cold dough tears when you stretch it. They're ready when a poke springs back slowly and leaves a faint dent.
</Step>
<Step title="Heat the oven as hot as it goes" status="todo">
See the oven section below. Start heating at least 45 minutes ahead — steel and stone need to soak up heat, not just reach temperature.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch, top, bake" status="todo">
One pizza at a time. Details below.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Tabs>
<Tab title="The oven">
<Card title="Getting to 450 °C in a home kitchen">

| Setup | Top heat | Bake time | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baking steel, top rack, grill on | ~300 °C | 3–4 min | Closest to real. Best all-rounder. |
| Pizza stone, max oven | ~250 °C | 6–8 min | Good, but crust dries before it chars |
| Cast iron skillet on the hob | ~280 °C | 4–5 min | Great char, awkward shape |
| Ooni / Gozney / gas pizza oven | 450 °C | 60–90 s | The real thing |

**The steel method, step by step:**
1. Steel on the highest rack the oven allows. Preheat at max for 45–60 minutes.
2. Switch the grill (broiler) on high, door closed, 10 minutes before baking.
3. Slide the pizza onto the steel. Grill on, door closed, 2 minutes.
4. Open, rotate 180°, grill another 1–2 minutes until the leopard spots appear.

<Callout tone="success" title="The single biggest upgrade">
A 6 mm baking steel costs less than a pizza stone and beats it badly. It holds far more heat, so the base sets before the toppings steam the crust.
</Callout>
</Card>
</Tab>
<Tab title="The sauce">
<Card title="Raw, uncooked, three ingredients">
Do not cook this. Cooking concentrates it and makes the pizza taste stewed.

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Whole peeled San Marzano tomatoes | 400 g tin |
| Fine sea salt | 4 g |
| Fresh basil | 3 leaves, torn |

Crush the tomatoes by hand — squeeze each one — or pulse twice in a blender, no more. You want texture, not purée. Stir in salt and basil. Use 3–4 tablespoons per pizza, no more, or the centre floods.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Drain the tin">
If the tomatoes come in thick juice, drain them for 10 minutes first. Watery sauce is the most common cause of a soggy middle.
</Callout>
</Card>
</Tab>
<Tab title="The cheese">
<Card title="Mozzarella, handled properly">
Fresh buffalo mozzarella is the classic, but it's wet. Tear it into pieces an hour ahead and rest them on paper towel, changing the towel once. Otherwise the water pools and drowns the pizza.

- **Best:** buffalo mozzarella (DOP), torn, drained
- **Easiest:** low-moisture mozzarella block, grated — melts evenly, no pooling
- **Avoid:** pre-shredded bagged cheese (starch coating, greasy melt)

Add cheese *after* the sauce, in blobs rather than an even blanket, so sauce still shows through.
</Card>
</Tab>
<Tab title="The bake">
<Card title="Stretch, top, launch">

1. **Flour the bench**, not the dough. A handful of semolina or 00.
2. **Press from the centre out** with your fingertips, leaving a 2 cm rim untouched — that rim is your cornicione and it must stay fat with air.
3. **Pick it up and drape it over your knuckles**, letting gravity stretch it. Rotate. Never use a rolling pin — it crushes every gas bubble you spent two days making.
4. **Stop at ~30 cm.** If it springs back, rest it 5 minutes and continue.
5. **Sauce, then cheese, then basil**, then a thin thread of olive oil. Keep the middle sparse.
6. **Launch onto the steel.** Semolina on the peel, a quick confident shove.
7. **Bake, rotate, eat immediately.** Neapolitan pizza is at its peak for about 90 seconds out of the oven.

<Callout tone="danger" title="The pizza stuck to the peel">
Shake the peel *before* you top it, while it's still bare dough. If it slides, you're safe. If it doesn't, lift the edge and throw more semolina under it. Once sauce is on, you can't fix it.
</Callout>
</Card>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="What goes wrong, and why">

<Compare title="Troubleshooting">

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy centre | Too much sauce, wet mozzarella, underheated steel | Less sauce, drain cheese, preheat longer |
| Pale, bready crust | Oven too cool | Steel + grill, or a dedicated pizza oven |
| Dense, no air in the rim | Rolled it out, or under-fermented | Fingertips only, longer cold ferment |
| Tastes sour or alcoholic | Over-proofed | Less yeast, shorter cold time |
| Tears when stretching | Dough still cold | 2–3 hours at room temperature first |
| Burnt base, raw top | Steel too close to the bottom element | Move up a rack, use the grill for top heat |
| Cheese greasy and split | Pre-shredded cheese, or baked too long | Block mozzarella, faster bake |

</Compare>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="If you want to start right now">
There's a same-day version: 3 g fresh yeast, 4 hours bulk at room temperature, ball and proof 2 hours. It's a decent pizza — just less flavour and less puff. Make the long version next time and you'll taste the difference immediately.
</Callout>

<Checklist title="Before you start" name="pizza-prep">

- [ ] Tipo 00 flour (or bread flour as a fallback — not plain flour)
- [ ] A scale that reads to 1 g
- [ ] Baking steel or stone, preheated 45+ minutes
- [ ] A pizza peel, or a rimless baking sheet as a stand-in
- [ ] Semolina or extra flour for the peel
- [ ] San Marzano tomatoes, whole peeled
- [ ] Fresh mozzarella, drained on paper towel
- [ ] Fresh basil
- [ ] Space in the fridge for a tray of dough balls

</Checklist>
