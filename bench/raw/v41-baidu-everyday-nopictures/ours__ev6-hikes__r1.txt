# Best day hikes near Vancouver

The North Shore mountains sit 20–30 minutes from downtown, so a real hike is an easy half-day trip. Below are the standouts, grouped by how much effort you want to spend.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you go">
Most trailheads on the North Shore need a **parking pass** (free, book online) in summer, and the alpine trails hold snow into June. Check conditions, tell someone your plan, and carry the 10 essentials even on short hikes.
</Callout>

<Card title="The hikes" subtitle="Easiest to hardest">
<Steps>
<Step title="Quarry Rock, Deep Cove" status="done" subtitle="3.8 km · 1.5 h · easy">
The classic starter hike. A shaded forest walk on the Baden-Powell Trail ending at a rocky bluff over Deep Cove. Go early — the parking lot fills by 9am on weekends. Reward yourself with a doughnut in the village after.
</Step>
<Step title="Dog Mountain, Mount Seymour" status="done" subtitle="5 km · 2 h · easy–moderate">
Short but rooty and muddy most of the year. The top is open granite with a wide view over the city and Mount Baker on clear days. Snowshoes replace boots from roughly December to April.
</Step>
<Step title="St. Mark's Summit, Cypress" status="current" subtitle="11 km · 4 h · moderate">
The best effort-to-view ratio on the North Shore. Follow the Howe Sound Crest Trail through old-growth to a ledge looking straight down Howe Sound. Some hands-on scrambling near the end; the last stretch holds snow late into spring.
</Step>
<Step title="Garibaldi Lake, Whistler" status="todo" subtitle="18 km · 6–7 h · hard">
A full day out, but the turquoise lake is the single most spectacular destination on this list. Steady switchbacks the whole way up, then a flat walk to the shore. Start before 8am so you're not descending in the dark.
</Step>
<Step title="Diez Vistas, Buntzen Lake" status="todo" subtitle="15 km · 5–6 h · hard">
Ten viewpoints over Indian Arm on a ridge loop above Buntzen Lake. Less crowded than the North Shore trails and a good shoulder-season option when the alpine is still snowed in.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Season matters">
Between November and May, the North Shore alpine trails (St. Mark's, Dog Mountain) are snow-covered and often icy. Stick to Quarry Rock, Buntzen Lake's lakeshore, or the Lower Falls Trail in Golden Ears if you're hiking in that window.
</Callout>
