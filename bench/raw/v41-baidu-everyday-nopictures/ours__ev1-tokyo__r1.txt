# Tokyo in 5 Days, for Two

Late October is one of the best windows in Tokyo: mild, dry, and the first hints of autumn colour in the parks. This plan keeps days geographic (one area per day) so you spend time walking, not riding trains.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you go">
Book the two big-ticket items now: **teamLab Planets** (timed entry, sells out days ahead) and any **observation deck** at sunset. Everything else in this plan can be done on the fly.
</Callout>

<Card title="The shape of the week" subtitle="One district per day, roughly">
<Steps>
<Step title="Day 1 — Shinjuku & Shibuya" status="todo" subtitle="Neon, crossings, night views">
Arrive, drop bags, walk Shibuya Crossing, then up to an observation deck for dusk.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 2 — Asakusa & Ueno" status="todo" subtitle="Old Tokyo, temples, museums">
Senso-ji early, then Yanaka backstreets and the Ueno park museums.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 3 — Tsukiji, Ginza & Imperial Palace" status="todo" subtitle="Food, shopping, gardens">
Breakfast at the outer market, then Ginza and the East Gardens.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 4 — teamLab & Odaiba" status="todo" subtitle="Digital art and the bay">
Timed entry at teamLab Planets, then the waterfront and a sunset cruise.
</Step>
<Step title="Day 5 — Harajuku, Meiji & Shimokita" status="todo" subtitle="Green, quirky, relaxed">
Meiji Shrine, Omotesando, then Shimokitazawa for the last evening.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day by day" subtitle="Times are suggestions, not rules">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Day 1">
**Morning–afternoon:** Land, check in, shake off the flight with a walk.

**Late afternoon:** Shibuya Crossing, Hachiko statue, then the Shibuya Sky deck — book the 16:30–17:30 slot so you catch daylight turning to neon.

**Evening:** Omoide Yokocho or Golden Gai in Shinjuku for yakitori and tiny bars. Two people, two stools, one grill.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 2">
**Early:** Senso-ji at opening (around 6:00) — nearly empty, and the best photos of the trip.

**Midday:** Walk north through Yanaka's cemetery-and-craft-street neighbourhood, lunch on the way.

**Afternoon:** Ueno Park — pick one museum, not three. Tokyo National Museum is the safe pick for a first visit.

**Evening:** Ameyoko market for cheap seafood and beer under the tracks.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 3">
**Morning:** Tsukiji Outer Market — tamagoyaki, uni, and a knife shop if you're feeling spendy.

**Midday:** Ginza for the flagship stores and the free rooftop garden on the Ginza Six building.

**Afternoon:** Imperial Palace East Gardens (closed Mondays and Fridays — check before you go).

**Evening:** A proper sit-down dinner; this is the night to book something.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 4">
**Morning:** teamLab Planets in Toyosu — allow 90 minutes, wear shorts or trousers you can roll up, you walk through water.

**Afternoon:** Odaiba: teamLab Borderless if you want a second one, or the Gundam statue and the beach.

**Evening:** Tokyo Bay sunset cruise, then dinner back in the city.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 5">
**Morning:** Meiji Shrine forest walk, then Omotesando for coffee and architecture.

**Afternoon:** Harajuku's Cat Street and Takeshita Street — busy, but worth 45 minutes.

**Evening:** Shimokitazawa: vintage shops, live houses, curry. The most relaxed night of the five.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Getting around">
<KeyValue title="Practical bits">
| Thing | Detail |
|---|---|
| Airport to city | Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner, ~60 min |
| Best card | Suica or Pasmo, tap in and out |
| Rush hour | Avoid 7:30–9:00 and 17:30–19:00 on the Yamanote line |
| Taxis | Clean and honest, but pricey; use for late nights only |
| Walking | Most days here are 12,000–18,000 steps |
| Tipping | Not a thing. Don't. |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Two things that trip people up">
**Museums and gardens close on specific weekdays** — the Imperial East Gardens and many museums shut Mondays. Check each one the night before. **Restaurants in the same building often share one closing day**, so have a backup within a few minutes' walk.
</Callout>
