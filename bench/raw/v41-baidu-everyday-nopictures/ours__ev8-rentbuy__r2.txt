# Rent vs buy on £60k

Short answer: **on £60k, with £1,800 rent, you can buy — but only if you have a deposit and you plan to stay put for 5+ years.** The maths is closer than most people think, because £1,800/month rent is already roughly what a mortgage on a £300–350k flat costs. The thing that decides it is your deposit and how long you'd stay.

<Callout tone="info" title="The one number that matters most">
Your rent is £21,600 a year — 36% of your £60k gross. A lender will typically offer you **4–4.5× income**, so roughly **£240,000–£270,000**. Add your deposit and that's your ceiling.
</Callout>

## The real comparison

<Card title="Monthly cost, like for like" subtitle="A £280,000 flat, 10% deposit, 25-year term">
<KeyValue title="What you'd actually pay">
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Mortgage (5.0% on £252,000) | £1,473/mo |
| Service charge + ground rent | £150/mo |
| Buildings insurance | £25/mo |
| Maintenance sinking fund | £150/mo |
| **Total** | **~£1,798/mo** |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

That's almost exactly your rent. So the monthly cash is a wash — which means the decision turns on **deposit, time, and risk**, not on monthly cost.

<Callout tone="warning" title="What that comparison hides">
- **Deposit:** £28,000 for a 10% deposit, plus ~£4,000 in fees (survey, legal, stamp duty if applicable). You need roughly **£32,000 in cash** before you start.
- **Stamp duty:** first-time buyers pay nothing up to £300,000 in England — a big help at this price point.
- **Sunk costs:** the first ~5 years of payments are mostly interest, not equity. At 5%, you build equity slowly at first.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="success" title="Rule of thumb">
If you'd stay **more than 5 years**, buying usually wins. Under 3 years, renting almost always wins — the transaction costs eat you alive.
</Callout>

## What changes the answer

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Leans toward buying">
- You have **£30k+ in cash** for deposit and fees.
- You expect to stay **5+ years** (job, relationship, family all stable).
- You want a fixed monthly payment instead of annual rent rises.
- You're happy owning a flat, not a house — service charges are the trade-off.
- You'd like the forced-savings effect: equity you can't spend.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Leans toward renting">
- Deposit is under ~£20k, or you'd have no emergency fund left.
- You might move city, job, or partner within 3 years.
- Your income is variable (bonus, freelance, commission).
- You want flexibility and no maintenance bills.
- Flat prices in your area are flat or falling.
</Tab>
<Tab title="The risks to weigh">
- **Rate risk:** at 5% your payment is £1,473. At 6% it's £1,624 — a £150/month swing.
- **Negative equity:** if prices fall 10% and you sell in year 2, you lose your deposit.
- **Service charge rises:** leasehold charges have been climbing well above inflation.
- **Cladding / EWS1:** some flats are unmortgageable. Always check before offering.
</Tab>
</Tabs>

## What to do next

<Steps>
<Step title="Check your deposit reality" status="current">
Add up savings, any help from family, and what you could free up. If it's under £25k, focus on saving for 12–18 months first.
</Step>
<Step title="Get an affordability decision in principle" status="todo">
Free, soft-search, no credit impact. This tells you your true ceiling instead of guessing.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide your minimum stay" status="todo">
Write down honestly: could you see yourself in the same flat in October 2031? If not, keep renting.
</Step>
<Step title="Compare against your actual rent rises" status="todo">
If your rent goes up 5% a year, renting costs £119,700 over 5 years — not £108,000. That shifts the break-even in buying's favour.
</Step>
<Step title="Talk to a broker before a lender" status="todo">
Brokers see deals you can't get direct, and they'll flag leasehold problems early.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="One thing people forget">
Keep **3–6 months of mortgage payments** in cash after buying. A boiler, a service charge demand, or a lost job with no cushion is how buyers get forced into a bad sale.
</Callout>
