# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

Vancouver is spoiled — you can be above treeline or on a coastal bluff within an hour of downtown. Below are the hikes locals actually repeat, sorted by how much effort they ask of you.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you go">
Most North Shore trails sit in **BC Parks / Metro Vancouver** land — dogs on leash, no drones, and parking lots fill by 8:30am on weekends. Bring water: the "easy" ones still climb.
</Callout>

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Easy — 2 hours or less">
<Card title="Quarry Rock" subtitle="Deep Cove, North Vancouver">
The classic starter. A well-graded forest trail over roots and boardwalks to a granite ledge looking down at Indian Arm. Go early or on a weekday — it's the busiest trail on the North Shore.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | 3.8 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | ~100 m |
| Time | 1.5 hours |
| Trailhead | Baden Powell Trail, Deep Cove |
| Post-hike | Honey's Doughnuts in the village |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Parking">
Deep Cove parking is permit-only in summer and tickets are enforced. Take the #212 bus from Phibbs Exchange if you can.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Dog Mountain" subtitle="Mount Seymour, North Vancouver">
Short, but not a walk in the park — roots, rocks and mud. The payoff is a flat granite bluff over the Seymour valley with a view straight down at the city. Snow lingers into June.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | 5 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | ~200 m |
| Time | 2 hours |
| Trailhead | Mount Seymour ski area parking lot |
| Note | Microspikes useful Nov–May |
</KeyValue>
</Card>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Moderate — half a day">
<Card title="Norvan Falls" subtitle="Lynn Headwaters, North Vancouver">
The best bad-weather hike near the city: almost entirely under canopy, so rain doesn't ruin it. Follow Lynn Creek up a flat, wide valley to a 30 m waterfall. Long but gentle.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | 14 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | ~250 m |
| Time | 4–5 hours |
| Trailhead | Lynn Headwaters Regional Park |
| Note | The last creek crossing can be tricky in high water |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="St. Mark's Summit" subtitle="Cypress Provincial Park, West Vancouver">
The best view-to-effort ratio on this list. You climb steadily through old-growth to a rocky outcrop above Howe Sound — the islands and the Sunshine Coast unfold below you.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | 11 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | ~600 m |
| Time | 4–5 hours |
| Trailhead | Cypress Mountain downhill parking, via Howe Sound Crest Trail |
| Note | Snow patches until July; the summit is exposed |
</KeyValue>
</Card>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Hard — full day">
<Card title="Garibaldi Lake" subtitle="Garibaldi Provincial Park, Squamish">
The one people put on their bucket list. A relentless switchback climb through forest, then the trees open onto a turquoise glacier-fed lake ringed by volcanic peaks. Start before 8am — the Rubble Creek lot fills fast.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| | |
|---|---|
| Distance | 18 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | ~820 m |
| Time | 6–7 hours |
| Trailhead | Rubble Creek, Highway 99 (1h 45m from downtown) |
| Note | Day-use pass required in summer; no campfires |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Don't swim casually">
The lake is glacier-fed and stays near 5°C even in August. Cold-shock risk is real — wade, don't swim.
</Callout>
</Card>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout tone="success" title="If you only have one day">
**St. Mark's Summit.** Under an hour's drive, half a day on foot, and a view that beats hikes twice its length. Bring a windproof layer — the summit is exposed.
</Callout>
