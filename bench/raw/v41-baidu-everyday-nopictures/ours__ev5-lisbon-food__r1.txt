# Eating in Lisbon

Lisbon eats late, eats cheap, and eats very well. The pattern that works: **petiscos** (small plates) with a drink before 8pm, a proper sit-down dinner after 9pm, and at least one pastel de nata still warm from the oven.

<Callout tone="info" title="The rhythm of a Lisbon day">
- **Breakfast (8–10am):** coffee standing at the counter + a pastry. Cheap, fast, everywhere.
- **Lunch (12:30–2:30pm):** the *prato do dia* — a daily set plate, often €8–12 with soup, bread and wine.
- **Snack (5–7pm):** a bifana or a pastel de nata.
- **Dinner (9–11pm):** when the city actually fills up. Book ahead for the good ones.
</Callout>

<Card title="What to eat" subtitle="The short list, in order of priority">

<Columns>
<Card title="Pastel de nata">
Egg-custard tart, blistered on top, cinnamon optional. Eat it warm, standing up, with an espresso.
</Card>
<Card title="Bacalhau à Brás">
Shredded salt cod with egg, onion and matchstick potato. The most comforting dish on any menu.
</Card>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<Card title="Bifana">
Thin pork cutlet in a crusty roll, drowned in garlicky sauce. The classic late-night sandwich.
</Card>
<Card title="Sardinhas assadas">
Grilled sardines on bread, salt and olive oil. Peak season is June, but good ones run all summer.
</Card>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<Card title="Arroz de marisco">
Seafood rice, soupy rather than dry. Order it for two and share.
</Card>
<Card title="Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato">
Clams in garlic, coriander and white wine. The best thing to eat with a cold beer at 6pm.
</Card>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<Card title="Queijadas & travesseiros">
Sintra's pastries, if you day-trip out. Travesseiro is almond-cream in puff pastry.
</Card>
<Card title="Ginjinha">
Sour-cherry liqueur, €1.50 a shot, drunk at a tiny doorway counter. With or without the cherry.
</Card>
</Columns>

</Card>

<KeyValue title="Concrete picks">

| Place | What to get |
|---|---|
| Manteigaria (Chiado, Cais do Sodré) | Pastel de nata, hot, at the counter |
| Time Out Market (Cais do Sodré) | Many kitchens, one roof — good first night |
| Cervejaria Ramiro (Intendente) | Garlic prawns, clams, then a steak sandwich to finish |
| A Cevicheria (Príncipe Real) | Seafood, no reservations, go early |
| O Tasca do Chico (Bairro Alto) | Petiscos plus live fado, tiny, cash-friendly |
| Taberna da Rua das Flores (Chiado) | Daily-changing small plates, no bookings |
| Pastelaria de Belém (Belém) | The original pastel de nata, plus travesseiro nearby |
| Ponto Final (Almada, across the river) | Grilled fish with the best skyline view of Lisbon |

</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Two traps">
**Menus with photos and a tout outside** in Baixa and Alfama are almost always mediocre and overpriced. And **"couvert"** — the bread, olives and cheese that appear unbidden — is charged. You can send it back, no offence taken.
</Callout>

<Card title="Ordering cheat sheet">

<Flashcards title="Tap to flip">

| Portuguese | What it is |
|---|---|
| Prato do dia | Daily set lunch, usually soup + main + drink |
| Petiscos | Small plates, Portugal's answer to tapas |
| Couvert | Bread/olives brought to the table — charged |
| A conta, por favor | The bill, please |
| Sem gelo | No ice |
| Uma cerveja / um copo de vinho | A beer / a glass of wine |
| Está incluído? | Is service included? (usually yes, 10% is fine to add) |
| Bem passado / mal passado | Well done / rare |

</Flashcards>

</Card>

<Card title="Check before you go">

<Checklist title="Lisbon eating prep" name="lisbon-food">

- [ ] Book Cervejaria Ramiro and any Alfama fado dinner (they fill up days ahead)
- [ ] Note that many tascas close Sunday and Monday — check hours
- [ ] Carry some cash; small tascas and ginjinha counters are often card-shy
- [ ] Learn "sem gelo" and "a conta, por favor"
- [ ] Save room for a pastel de nata on the last morning

</Checklist>
