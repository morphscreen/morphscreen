# Eating in Lisbon: A Local's Guide

Lisbon is a food lover's paradise — think fresh seafood, custard tarts, grilled sardines, and hearty petiscos (Portuguese tapas). Here's where to go and what to order, from iconic classics to hidden gems.

<Card title="The Must-Try Dishes" subtitle="Start with these, then explore">
<Columns>
<div>
<Badge tone="info">Iconic</Badge>

**Pastel de Nata** — The famous custard tart with a caramelized, flaky crust. Best eaten warm with a sprinkle of cinnamon.

**Bacalhau à Brás** — Shredded salted cod with onions, thinly sliced potatoes, and eggs, topped with olives.

**Sardinhas Assadas** — Grilled sardines, a summer staple, often served with roasted peppers and bread.

</div>
<div>
<Badge tone="success">Local Favorites</Badge>

**Bifana** — A spicy pulled-pork sandwich, perfect for a quick bite.

**Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato** — Clams in a garlic-cilantro white wine sauce, great for sharing.

**Ginjinha** — A cherry liqueur served in a tiny cup, often with a piece of fruit at the bottom.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat by Neighborhood" subtitle="From touristy to local, here's the breakdown">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Baixa & Chiado">
The historic center — convenient but touristy. Go for the atmosphere, not the deals.

**Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau** — Upscale bacalhau and pastel de nata with fado music. <Badge tone="warning">Touristy</Badge>

**A Brasileira** — A legendary café for coffee and people-watching. The pastel de nata is good, the history is better.

**Cervejaria Trindade** — A beautiful former monastery serving excellent seafood and beer.

<Callout tone="info" title="Tip">Skip restaurants with staff standing outside trying to pull you in — they're usually overpriced.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Alfama">
The oldest quarter — narrow streets, fado houses, and honest food.

**Ramiro** — Actually in nearby Intendente, but worth the walk. Famous for giant tiger prawns and garlic bread. <Badge tone="success">Must-visit</Badge>

**Casa de Pasto** — A tiny, no-frills spot serving traditional petiscos to locals.

**Pois, Café** — A charming café with great breakfast and brunch.

</Tab>
<Tab title="Bairro Alto & Cais do Sodré">
Nightlife central — eat late, eat well.

**O Trevo** — A classic bifana spot, open until 2 AM. Perfect after a night out.

**Sea Me** — Peixaria (fish market) meets restaurant. Pick your fish, they cook it. <Badge tone="info">Modern</Badge>

**Time Out Market** — A food hall with 24+ stalls from top chefs. Great for variety, but it's a tourist magnet. Go early or late.

</Tab>
<Tab title="Belém">
Home of the original pastel de nata.

**Pastéis de Belém** — The birthplace of the custard tart, since 1837. Eat them warm with cinnamon. <Badge tone="success">Iconic</Badge>

**Taberna dos Ferreiros** — A modern tavern with excellent petiscos and a great wine list.

**Casa Portuguesa** — Another branch of the bacalhau spot, right by the monastery.

</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="A Walkable Food Route" subtitle="Start in Chiado, end in Alfama — about 3 hours with stops">
<Steps>
<Step title="Stop 1: Café a Brasileira" status="done" subtitle="Chiado">Coffee and a pastel de nata to fuel up. Order a "bica" (espresso) with it.</Step>
<Step title="Stop 2: Mercado da Ribeira" status="done" subtitle="Cais do Sodré">Browse the fresh market for produce and local goods. Grab a bifana from a stall.</Step>
<Step title="Stop 3: O Trevo" status="current" subtitle="Bairro Alto">A proper bifana sandwich with mustard and piri-piri. Quick and satisfying.</Step>
<Step title="Stop 4: Ramiro" status="todo" subtitle="Intendente">The big one — order tiger prawns, garlic shrimp, and steak sandwiches. Share everything.</Step>
<Step title="Stop 5: Ginjinha Espinheira" status="todo" subtitle="Alfama">End with a cherry liqueur shot from a hole-in-the-wall bar.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Practical Tips">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Watch out for">
- **Tourist-trap menus** — If the menu is in 5 languages and has pictures, it's probably overpriced.
- **Bread & olives** — They're not free. If you don't want them, say "não, obrigado" when they arrive.
- **Service charge** — Usually included, but a 5-10% tip for good service is appreciated.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="info" title="Good to know">
- **Lunch is 12:00–15:00**, dinner starts at 19:30–20:00. Many places close between.
- **Reservations** are a must for Ramiro and Sea Me, especially on weekends.
- **Fresh fish** is best on Tuesdays and Fridays when the market restocks.
</Callout>
