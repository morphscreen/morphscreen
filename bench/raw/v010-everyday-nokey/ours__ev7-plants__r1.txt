<Card title="How to Start (Even If You've Killed Everything)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Pick one plant" status="done" subtitle="Start with a Snake Plant or Pothos — the most forgiving">
Choose a single plant before expanding your collection. One survivor builds confidence.
</Step>
<Step title="Find the right spot" status="todo" subtitle="Bright indirect light is ideal for most">
Most of these tolerate low light, but they'll grow faster with bright, filtered light — not direct afternoon sun, which can scorch leaves.
</Step>
<Step title="Set a watering rhythm" status="todo" subtitle="Use a reminder or check soil weekly">
<Checklist title="Watering checklist" name="watering">
- [ ] Check soil moisture with finger (2 inches deep)
- [ ] Water only when soil feels dry
- [ ] Use room-temperature water
- [ ] Drain excess water from saucer after 30 minutes
</Checklist>
</Step>
<Step title="Watch for signs" status="todo" subtitle="Yellow leaves = too much water; crispy tips = too little">
Each plant tells you what it needs. Learn the language and you'll rarely lose one again.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Overwatering — the #1 killer">
Roots need air as much as water. Soggy soil suffocates them and invites rot. Always use a pot with drainage holes.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Sudden light changes">
Moving a plant from a dark corner to a sunny window shocks it. Transition gradually over a week.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="neutral" title="Ignoring dust">
Dusty leaves block light absorption. Wipe leaves with a damp cloth monthly — your plant will thank you.
</Callout>
</Card>
