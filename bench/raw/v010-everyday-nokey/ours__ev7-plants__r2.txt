# Houseplants That Are Hard to Kill

If you're new to plants or just don't have a green thumb, these resilient houseplants can survive neglect, low light, and irregular watering. Here's a rundown of the toughest options, plus how to care for them.

<Card title="The Top 7 Unkillable Houseplants">
<Columns>
<div>
### 1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; let soil dry completely
- **Why it survives:** Stores water in thick leaves; tolerates drought and dim corners
</div>
<div>
### 2. Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Weekly; droops when thirsty, perks back up after watering
- **Why it survives:** Grows fast, forgiving of missed waterings, adapts to most spots
</div>
<div>
### 3. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 2–4 weeks; likes to dry out fully
- **Why it survives:** Nearly indestructible; survives weeks without water and low light
</div>
</Columns>
<Columns>
<div>
### 4. Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)
- **Light:** Bright indirect, tolerates some shade
- **Water:** Weekly; don't let it sit in water
- **Why it survives:** Robust, produces baby plants, bounces back from neglect
</div>
<div>
### 5. Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum)
- **Light:** Low to medium indirect
- **Water:** Weekly; droops dramatically when thirsty, recovers fast
- **Why it survives:** Tells you exactly when it needs water—no guessing
</div>
<div>
### 6. Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior)
- **Light:** Very low to medium
- **Water:** Every 1–2 weeks
- **Why it survives:** Lives up to its name; tolerates extreme neglect and poor light
</div>
</Columns>
<Columns>
<div>
### 7. Succulents (e.g., Echeveria, Haworthia)
- **Light:** Bright light, some direct sun
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; soak and let dry completely
- **Why it survives:** Built to store water; only dies from overwatering, not underwatering
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="The Golden Rule: Don't Overwater">
<Callout tone="warning" title="The #1 killer of houseplants">
More houseplants die from too much water than too little. If in doubt, wait another day. Most of these plants prefer to dry out completely between waterings.
</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step title="Check the soil" status="done" subtitle="Stick your finger 2 cm into the soil">
If it feels damp, don't water yet. If it's dry, it's time.
</Step>
<Step title="Water thoroughly" status="done" subtitle="Until water drains from the bottom">
Then let the pot drain fully—never leave the plant sitting in a saucer of water.
</Step>
<Step title="Wait for it to dry again" status="done" subtitle="This is the hard part">
Most of these plants want 1–3 weeks between waterings, depending on light and season.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Ready to start?">
<Columns>
<div>
<Callout tone="info" title="My recommendation for absolute beginners">
Start with a **Pothos** or **Snake Plant**. Both forgive missed waterings, thrive in most rooms, and are widely available at garden centers and big-box stores for under $15.
</Callout>
</div>
<div>
<Callout tone="success" title="Set yourself up for success">
Buy a pot with drainage holes and use a well-draining potting mix. That single choice prevents most plant deaths.
</Callout>
