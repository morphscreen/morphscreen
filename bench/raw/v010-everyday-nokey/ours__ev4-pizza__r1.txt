# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making an authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is absolutely possible — you just need the right dough, heat, and technique. Here's the complete guide.

<Card title="The Core Principles" subtitle="What makes it Neapolitan">
<Callout tone="info" title="The 3 pillars">
1. **00 flour** — finely milled Italian flour with high protein (12-13%)
2. **High heat** — 430-480°C (800-900°F) ideally, but we'll work with what you have
3. **Short, wet dough** — 60-65% hydration, fermented 8-24 hours
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The Recipe" subtitle="Makes 4 pizzas (about 250g dough each)">
### Ingredients
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Caputo 00 flour (or other 00 flour) | 1000g |
| Water (room temperature) | 600-650ml |
| Fresh yeast (or 1g dry yeast) | 3-5g |
| Fine sea salt | 20-30g |
| **For the topping (per pizza)** | |
| San Marzano tomatoes | 100g |
| Fresh mozzarella (fior di latte) | 80-100g |
| Fresh basil leaves | 3-4 |
| Extra virgin olive oil | 1 tbsp |
| Fine sea salt | Pinch |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't skip the 00 flour">All-purpose flour won't give you the same stretch and crispness. Caputo Pizzeria 00 is the gold standard, but any 00 flour with 12%+ protein works.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Step-by-Step Process">
<Steps>
<Step title="Make the dough" status="done" subtitle="8-24 hours before baking">
Dissolve the yeast in the water, then add the flour and mix until just combined. Add the salt and knead for 10-12 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough should be quite wet and sticky — that's correct. Cover and let it rest 15 minutes, then do a few stretch-and-folds.
</Step>

<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done" subtitle="Room temperature, 2-4 hours">
Let the dough rise at room temperature (20-24°C) until doubled. Then divide into 4 balls of about 250g each. Place on a floured tray, cover, and refrigerate 8-24 hours. Cold fermentation develops flavor.
</Step>

<Step title="Bring to room temperature" status="current" subtitle="1-2 hours before baking">
Take the dough balls out of the fridge and let them warm up. They should be soft and pliable, not cold and stiff.
</Step>

<Step title="Shape the pizza" status="todo">
Flour your hands and the dough ball. Press it flat, then stretch from the center outward, leaving the edges thicker (the cornicione). Don't use a rolling pin — it destroys the air bubbles. You want a thin center, about 3-4mm, with a 1-2cm raised edge.
</Step>

<Step title="Top it" status="todo">
Add a thin layer of crushed San Marzano tomatoes (just puree them with a pinch of salt, don't cook). Add torn mozzarella, a few basil leaves, and a drizzle of olive oil. Don't overload — less is more.
</Step>

<Step title="Bake" status="todo" subtitle="The critical part">
Bake at the highest temperature your oven can reach, ideally 250°C+ (500°F+). If you have a pizza stone or steel, preheat it for at least 45 minutes. Bake for 5-8 minutes until the crust is puffed, charred in spots, and the cheese is bubbling. For wood-fired results, consider a pizza oven attachment (see below).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Checklist title="Check your technique" name="pizza-mistakes">
- Overloading with toppings — the dough won't cook through
- Using cold dough straight from the fridge — it tears
- Rolling with a pin — kills the air
- Opening the oven too often — heat loss
- Not preheating long enough — soggy bottom
- Using wet mozzarella — drain it and pat dry
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="Troubleshooting">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Soggy middle">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Too much moisture">Drain the mozzarella well, use less sauce, and make sure the oven is screaming hot. Consider pre-cooking the base for 60 seconds before topping.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Burnt crust, raw middle">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Too hot, too fast">Lower the rack, reduce the heat slightly, or use less dough in the center. Also check that you're not adding too much sauce.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Dough doesn't stretch">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Not enough hydration or rest">Your dough may be too dry or cold. Let it warm up for 2+ hours and be more generous with flour when shaping.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Dough tears easily">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Overworked or underdeveloped">Knead less, let it rest more. The dough needs time to develop gluten. If it tears, let it rest 15 minutes and try again.</Callout>
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>
