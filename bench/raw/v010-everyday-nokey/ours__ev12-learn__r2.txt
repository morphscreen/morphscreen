# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae, and some bacteria turn sunlight into food — and in the process, they produce the oxygen we breathe. Here's the whole story, from the big picture down to the molecules.

<Card title="The one-sentence version">
Plants take **water** (from roots) and **carbon dioxide** (from air), use **sunlight** to power a chemical reaction, and produce **glucose** (sugar for energy) and **oxygen** (released as a waste product).
</Card>

<Card title="The balanced equation">
<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />
</Card>

---

## The two main stages

Photosynthesis happens in two connected stages, each with a specific job.

<Columns>
<Card title="1. Light-dependent reactions" subtitle="In the thylakoid membranes">
- **Input:** sunlight, water
- **What happens:** Light energy splits water molecules (photolysis), releasing oxygen gas
- **Output:** ATP and NADPH (energy-carrying molecules), plus O₂
- **Where:** Thylakoid membranes inside chloroplasts
</Card>

<Card title="2. Light-independent reactions" subtitle="The Calvin cycle">
- **Input:** CO₂, ATP, NADPH (from stage 1)
- **What happens:** Carbon from CO₂ is fixed into glucose through a series of enzyme-driven steps
- **Output:** Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), and recycled ADP + NADP⁺
- **Where:** Stroma (the fluid around the thylakoids)
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="info" title="Key insight">
The "dark reactions" (Calvin cycle) don't literally need darkness — they just don't need *light directly*. They run as long as the ATP and NADPH from stage 1 are available.
</Callout>

## Step by step through the process

<Steps>
<Step title="Light hits the chlorophyll" status="done" subtitle="Thylakoid membrane">
Chlorophyll molecules in photosystem II absorb photons. This boosts electrons to a higher energy level.
</Step>

<Step title="Water is split" status="done" subtitle="Photolysis">
The energized chlorophyll pulls electrons from water molecules, splitting them into O₂ (released), H⁺ (used later), and electrons (passed along).
</Step>

<Step title="Electrons travel the chain" status="done" subtitle="Electron transport chain">
The excited electrons move through a series of proteins, losing energy at each step. That energy pumps H⁺ ions into the thylakoid space, building a gradient.
</Step>

<Step title="ATP is made" status="done" subtitle="Chemiosmosis">
H⁺ ions flow back out through ATP synthase, spinning it like a turbine to make ATP from ADP + phosphate.
</Step>

<Step title="NADPH is produced" status="current" subtitle="Photosystem I">
A second light hit re-energizes the electrons, which then combine with NADP⁺ and H⁺ to form NADPH.
</Step>

<Step title="Carbon gets fixed" status="todo" subtitle="Calvin cycle in the stroma">
CO₂ is attached to RuBP by the enzyme Rubisco, then ATP and NADPH power the steps that produce G3P — which becomes glucose.
</Step>
</Steps>

## What actually happens in the Calvin cycle

<Card title="The Calvin cycle in three phases">
<Chart type="bar" title="Carbon atoms per molecule at each phase" x="Phase" y="Carbons">
| Phase | Carbons |
|---|---|
| Carbon fixation (CO₂ joins RuBP) | 6 |
| Reduction (G3P formed) | 6 |
| Regeneration (RuBP rebuilt) | 3 |
</Chart>
</Card>

<Card title="The details">
| Phase | What happens | Key molecule |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Fixation | CO₂ combines with RuBP (5C) via Rubisco → unstable 6C → splits into two 3-PGA (3C) | Rubisco |
| 2. Reduction | ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA → G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate) | G3P |
| 3. Regeneration | Most G3P is recycled to regenerate RuBP; one G3P leaves per 3 CO₂ fixed | RuBP |

**Net result:** For every 3 CO₂ molecules, you get **1 G3P** — and two G3Ps combine to form one **glucose** (C₆H₁₂O₆).
</Card>

## Where it all happens: the chloroplast

<Card title="Anatomy of a chloroplast" subtitle="A quick tour">
| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Outer & inner membranes | Envelope controlling what enters/leaves |
| Thylakoid | Stacked discs where light reactions occur |
| Stroma | Fluid filling the chloroplast; site of Calvin cycle |
| Granum | Stack of thylakoids, maximizing light capture |
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment that absorbs light (mostly red and blue) |
</Card>

## Quick facts to remember

<Card title="Key numbers">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Oxygen source | Water (not CO₂!) |
| Carbon source | CO₂ from the air |
| Energy currency made | ATP and NADPH |
| Final sugar | Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) |
| Key enzyme | Rubisco (most abundant protein on Earth) |
| Wavelengths absorbed | Mostly red & blue; green is reflected (why leaves look green) |
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Why it matters">
Every breath you take uses oxygen produced by photosynthesis. Every calorie you eat traces back to glucose made this way. It's the foundation of nearly all life on Earth.
</Callout>
