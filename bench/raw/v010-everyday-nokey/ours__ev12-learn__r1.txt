# Photosynthesis: How Plants Turn Light into Food

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae, and some bacteria use to convert sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose (food) and oxygen. It's the foundation of almost all life on Earth.

<Callout tone="info" title="Why it matters">
Without photosynthesis, there would be no oxygen in our atmosphere and no food for herbivores, carnivores, or us. Every bite of food and every breath you take traces back to this process.
</Callout>

## The Big Picture

<Card title="The Photosynthesis Equation">
In words: **Carbon dioxide + Water + Light energy → Glucose + Oxygen**

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Balanced equation" />

The plant takes in carbon dioxide through tiny pores on its leaves (stomata), absorbs water through its roots, and captures sunlight with a green pigment called **chlorophyll** — that's what makes leaves green.
</Card>

## Where It Happens

<Card title="Inside the Leaf">
<KeyValue title="Key parts of a leaf">
| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| **Stomata** | Pores that let CO₂ in and O₂ out |
| **Chloroplasts** | Tiny factories where photosynthesis occurs |
| **Chlorophyll** | Green pigment that absorbs light energy |
| **Thylakoids** | Disc-like structures inside chloroplasts where the light reactions happen |
| **Stroma** | Fluid around the thylakoids where the Calvin cycle happens |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

## The Two Main Stages

Photosynthesis happens in two linked stages, like a two-step assembly line.

<Steps>
<Step title="Light-Dependent Reactions" status="current" subtitle="In the thylakoids — needs sunlight">
Sunlight hits chlorophyll, which energizes electrons. This energy splits water molecules (releasing oxygen as a byproduct) and produces two energy-carrying molecules: **ATP** and **NADPH**.
</Step>
<Step title="Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)" status="todo" subtitle="In the stroma — no light needed directly">
Using the ATP and NADPH from step 1, the plant "fixes" carbon dioxide into glucose. This step can happen in the dark, but it depends on the energy made in step 1.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="Quick analogy">
Think of stage 1 as charging a battery (making ATP/NADPH) and stage 2 as using that battery to assemble a product (building glucose from CO₂).
</Callout>

## The Process in Detail

<Card title="Step-by-Step Walkthrough">
**Stage 1: Light-Dependent Reactions (in the thylakoid membrane)**

1. **Light absorption** — Chlorophyll absorbs photons, exciting electrons to a higher energy level.
2. **Water splitting** — Enzymes split H₂O into oxygen (released as O₂), protons, and electrons.
3. **Electron transport chain** — Excited electrons pass through proteins, releasing energy that pumps protons across the membrane, building a gradient.
4. **ATP synthesis** — Protons flow back through ATP synthase, powering ATP production.
5. **NADPH formation** — Electrons finally reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH.

**Stage 2: Calvin Cycle (in the stroma)**

1. **Carbon fixation** — The enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP), forming two 3-carbon molecules (3-PGA).
2. **Reduction** — ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA into G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate).
3. **Regeneration** — Most G3P is recycled to regenerate RuBP, while some exits to form glucose and other sugars.
</Card>

## What the Plant Does with Glucose

<Card title="Products and Uses">
| Product | What the plant does with it |
|---|---|
| **Glucose** | Used for cellular respiration (energy) |
| **Starch** | Stored energy for later |
| **Cellulose** | Builds cell walls |
| **Fats & oils** | Stored in seeds for energy |
| **Amino acids** | Building blocks for proteins (requires nitrogen from soil) |
| **Oxygen** | Released into the atmosphere — the O₂ we breathe |
</Card>

## Factors That Affect Photosynthesis Rate

<Card title="What Speeds It Up or Slows It Down">
<Compare title="Key factors">
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Light intensity** | More light = faster rate, up to a saturation point |
| **CO₂ concentration** | More CO₂ = faster rate, up to a limit |
| **Temperature** | Warmer speeds enzymes, but too hot (>40°C) denatures them |
| **Water availability** | Shortage closes stomata, slowing CO₂ intake |
| **Chlorophyll levels** | Deficiencies (e.g., nitrogen shortage) reduce capture |
</Compare>
</Card>

## Common Misconceptions

<Card title="Clearing Up Confusion">
- **"Plants only photosynthesize during the day"** — True, but the Calvin cycle can continue briefly in darkness using stored ATP/NADPH.
- **"Plants breathe in CO₂ and breathe out O₂"** — They don't "breathe" — they passively exchange gases through stomata. At night, they consume O₂ and release CO₂ via respiration.
- **"Photosynthesis is the opposite of respiration"** — They're complementary, but not exact opposites. Respiration breaks glucose fully to CO₂ and water, while photosynthesis builds glucose from CO₂ and water.
</Card>
