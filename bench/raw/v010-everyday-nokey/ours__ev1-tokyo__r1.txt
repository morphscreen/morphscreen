# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A balanced itinerary mixing iconic sights, hidden gems, great food, and romantic moments—designed for a comfortable pace without rushing.

<Callout tone="info" title="Best time to visit">
October–November or March–April offer mild weather and seasonal beauty (autumn colors or cherry blossoms). You're visiting in late October—perfect timing for crisp air and fall foliage.
</Callout>

## Day 1: Arrival & Shinjuku Evening

<Card title="Morning: Arrive & settle in">
<Steps>
<Step title="Arrive at Narita (NRT) or Haneda (HND)" status="done">Take the Narita Express (N'EX) or Keisei Skyliner to central Tokyo (60–90 min). From Haneda, the Keikyu Line or Tokyo Monorail gets you there in 30–60 min.</Step>
<Step title="Check in to your hotel" status="done">Drop bags in Shinjuku or Shibuya. Aim to be out by early afternoon.</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ichiran Ramen (Shinjuku)" status="done">Solo booths, perfect for a first meal—order the classic tonkotsu with extra chashu.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Explore Shinjuku">
- **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building** (free observation deck, 45th floor)—panoramic views of Mt. Fuji on clear days.
- **Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden**—a peaceful blend of Japanese, French, and English gardens. Autumn colors are stunning in late October.
- **Omoide Yokochō (Memory Lane)**—narrow alleys of tiny yakitori bars; perfect for a pre-dinner drink.

<Gallery>
![Shinjuku Gyoen](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1542051841857-5f90071e7989?w=800)
![Tokyo Metropolitan Building](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1540959733332-eab4deabeeaf?w=800)
</Gallery>
</Card>

<Card title="Evening: Golden Gai & dinner">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Golden Gai tip">Over 200 tiny bars in six alleys—many seat only 5–8 people. Go early (6–7 PM) to avoid lines. Most bars have a cover charge (¥500–¥1,000) that often includes a small snack. Some bars are private or require reservations.</Callout>

- **Dinner: Omoide Yokochō**—grilled skewers (yakitori) with beer.
- **Golden Gai**—bar-hop through tiny themed bars (jazz, rock, film). Try "Death Match in Hell" for a quirky experience.
- **Late-night dessert: Harbs (Shinjuku)**—their famous strawberry cake is a must.

## Day 2: Tsukiji, Ginza & Asakusa

<Card title="Morning: Tsukiji Outer Market & Ginza">
<Steps>
<Step title="6:30 AM: Tsukiji Outer Market" status="done">Beat the crowds for fresh sushi breakfast. Try **Daiwa Sushi** or **Sushi Dai** (arrive by 5:30 AM for the latter, or go to less-crowded stalls like **Yamachō** for uni and ikura bowls).</Step>
<Step title="9:00 AM: Ginza" status="done">Stroll the upscale shopping district. Visit **Itōya** (stationery heaven), **Uniqlo Ginza** (12 floors), and **Kabukiza Theatre** (even just the exterior).</Step>
<Step title="11:00 AM: Art & design" status="done">Check out **TeamLab Planets** (book tickets in advance) or **Ginza Six** for its rooftop garden and art installations.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Asakusa & Senso-ji">
- **Senso-ji Temple**—Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the iconic **Kaminarimon** gate and **Nakamise-dori** shopping street.
- **Nakamise-dori**—try **ningyo-yaki** (little cakes filled with sweet bean paste) and **senbei** (rice crackers).
- **Sumida River cruise**—take the water bus from Asakusa to Odaiba or Hamarikyu Garden (¥1,200–¥1,700, 40–60 min).

<Gallery>
![Senso-ji Temple](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1565967511849-76a60a516170?w=800)
![Nakamise-dori](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1578271887552-5ac3a72752bc?w=800)
</Gallery>
</Card>

<Card title="Evening: Sumida River & Tokyo Skytree">
- **Hoppy Street** (near Asakusa station)—cheap beer and fried chicken in a retro alley vibe.
- **Tokyo Skytree**—book a sunset time slot (around 4:30–5:00 PM) to see the city light up. The **Tembo Deck** (350m) is the classic view; the **Tembo Galleria** (450m) is worth the extra ¥1,000.

<Callout tone="success" title="Pro tip">If the Skytree line is long, head to the **Asakusa View Hotel** rooftop bar (29th floor) for free views with fewer crowds.</Callout>
</Card>

## Day 3: Shibuya, Harajuku & Meiji Shrine

<Card title="Morning: Meiji Shrine & Harajuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="9:00 AM: Meiji Shrine" status="done">A serene forest in the heart of the city. Walk the gravel paths, see the giant torii gates, and write an ema (wooden wish plaque).</Step>
<Step title="10:30 AM: Harajuku's Takeshita Street" status="done">Kawaii culture central—crepes (try **Marion Crepes**), quirky shops, and street style. Go early to beat the crowds.</Step>
<Step title="11:30 AM: Omotesando" status="done">The Champs-Élysées of Tokyo—tree-lined boulevard with high-end shops and beautiful architecture. Stop at **Omotesando Koffee** for a perfect espresso.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Shibuya">
- **Shibuya Crossing**—the world's busiest intersection. Watch from the **Shibuya Sky** observation deck (rooftop, book sunset slot) or the Starbucks on the second floor of the QFRONT building.
- **Hachikō Statue**—the loyal dog memorial, a classic meeting spot.
- **Shibuya Parco**—check out the **Nintendo Tokyo** store and **Pokémon Center Shibuya**.

## Day 4: Day Trip to Hakone (Mt. Fuji Views)

<Card title="Morning: Travel to Hakone">
<Steps>
<Step title="7:30 AM: Depart from Shinjuku" status="done">Take the **Odakyu Romancecar** to Hakone-Yumoto (85 min, ¥2,470 one-way). Book seats in advance online.</Step>
<Step title="9:00 AM: Hakone Open-Air Museum" status="done">Sculpture gardens with Mt. Fuji views on clear days. The Picasso Pavilion and the hot-spring foot bath are highlights.</Step>
<Step title="11:30 AM: Hakone Ropeway" status="done">Ride from Sounzan to Togendai (30 min) with spectacular views of Mt. Fuji and Lake Ashi. If visibility is poor, skip and do the lake cruise instead.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Afternoon: Lake Ashi & Owakudani">
- **Owakudani** (volcanic valley)—try the famous **black eggs** (boiled in sulfur springs, said to extend life by 7 years).
- **Lake Ashi cruise**—pirateship-style boats from Togendai to Hakone-machi (40 min, ¥1,000–¥1,600).
- **Hakone Shrine**—the iconic red torii gate rising from the lake.

## Practical Tips

<Card title="Before you go">
<Checklist title="Packing checklist" name="packing-list">
- [ ] Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk 15,000–20,000 steps/day)
- [ ] Light jacket or sweater (October temps: 15–22°C)
- [ ] Portable charger (you'll use your phone for maps and translations)
- [ ] PASMO/Suica IC card (get at airport or any station)
- [ ] Pocket WiFi or eSIM (rent at airport, ~¥1,000/day)
- [ ] Cash (¥10,000–¥20,000) for small shops and street food
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="Getting around">
- **IC cards** (Suica/Pasmo) work on all trains, subways, and buses. Tap in and out—no tickets needed.
- **Tokyo Metro** and **Toei Subway** cover most sights. Use Google Maps or **Japan Travel by NAVITIME**.
- **Taxis** are expensive (start ~¥500) but handy late at night.
- **JR Yamanote Line** is the loop—great for orientation.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Late-night transport">Trains stop around midnight–1 AM. If you're out late, the last train is your friend—or budget for a taxi (¥2,000–¥4,000 from central Tokyo).</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Cultural etiquette">
- **Bow** when greeting and thanking.
- **No tipping**—it's not practiced and can be seen as rude.
- **Quiet on trains**—silence your phone, avoid loud conversations.
- **Shoes off** in traditional restaurants, homes, and some temples.
- **Recycle correctly**—separate burnable, plastic, and cans/bottles.
</Card>
