# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

<Callout tone="info" title="The Golden Rule">
Authentic Neapolitan pizza (VPN-certified) is defined by its soft, puffy, leopard-spotted crust — not a crispy cracker. The key is high heat (at least 430°C / 800°F) and 48–72 hour cold-fermented dough.
</Callout>

## The Essential Setup

<Card title="What You'll Need" subtitle="The non-negotiables">
<KeyValue title="Equipment">
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Pizza steel or stone (≥1 cm thick) | Retains and radiates intense heat |
| Oven thermometer | Your home oven dial is a lie — verify actual temp |
| Kitchen scale | Precision is everything in dough |
| Pizza peel | Wooden for launching, metal for turning |
| Paddle/pizza cutter | A knife crushes the crust |

**If you have a gas or charcoal grill:** use it. A grill can reach 500°C+ with the lid down, which is far closer to a wood-fired oven than any home oven.
</KeyValue>
</Card>

## The Dough Recipe (Makes 4 × 250g pizzas)

<Card title="Ingredients">
| Ingredient | Amount | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Caputo "00" flour (red or blue bag) | 500 g | Blue bag is the classic VPN choice |
| Water (room temp, 20–22°C) | 320 g | 64% hydration — forgiving for home bakers |
| Fine sea salt | 10 g | 2% of flour weight |
| Fresh yeast | 2–4 g | Or 1–1.5 g dry yeast |
| (Optional) Sugar | 1 pinch | Helps yeast in cold water — not traditional, but harmless |
</Card>

<Card title="Dough Method" subtitle="Start 3 days before you want to eat">
<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done" subtitle="Day 0, evening">
Dissolve yeast in water. Add flour and salt. Mix with a spoon until shaggy, then knead 10–15 minutes until smooth and elastic (windowpane test: stretch a piece thin without tearing).
</Step>
<Step title="First rise" status="done" subtitle="Room temp, 2 hours">
Cover with a damp towel. Let it rise at 20–24°C until doubled. Then punch down, divide into 4 balls of ~250g each.
</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment" status="current" subtitle="Days 1–2, fridge">
Place balls in a lightly oiled, airtight container. Refrigerate at 4°C for 48–72 hours. This develops flavor and digestibility.
</Step>
<Step title="Temper" status="todo" subtitle="Day 3, 2–3 hours before baking">
Take dough out of fridge. Let it come to room temperature, covered, until soft and pliable — but not warm or over-proofed.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't skip the cold ferment">
The 48–72 hour cold ferment is what gives Neapolitan dough its signature flavor and that airy, irregular crumb. Same-day dough will be dense and taste like bread, not pizza.
</Callout>

## The Sauce & Toppings

<Card title="Simple Tomato Sauce" subtitle="Less is more">
- 1 can (400g) **San Marzano tomatoes** (DOP if possible)
- Pinch of fine salt
- 1–2 fresh basil leaves (torn, not chopped)

Crush tomatoes by hand. **Do not cook the sauce.** Add salt and basil. That's it. The heat of the oven will do the cooking.

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Topping rules">
- **Mozzarella:** Use fresh fior di latte or buffalo mozzarella. Tear into small pieces, pat dry with paper towel — excess moisture = soggy pizza.
- **Oil:** A drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil on top, just before baking, is traditional.
- **Keep it minimal:** Classic Margherita = sauce, mozzarella, basil. That's it. Overloading kills the crust.
</Callout>
</Card>

## The Bake — Two Approaches

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Home oven (max 250–300°C)">
<Card title="Home Oven Method">
1. **Preheat** your steel/stone on the **highest rack** for at least 45–60 minutes. Use the broiler for the last 10 minutes to push temps higher.
2. **Stretch** the dough by hand — never use a rolling pin. Start from the center, push outward, leave a 1–2 cm rim (cornicione).
3. **Assemble** on a floured peel: sauce first, then toppings.
4. **Launch** with a quick, confident shove. Bake 4–6 minutes, turning 180° halfway, until the crust is puffed and spotted.
5. **Finish** under the broiler for 30–60 seconds for leopard spots — watch it like a hawk.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Expect limitations">
A home oven maxes out around 300°C. Your crust will be softer and less charred than a wood-fired one. That's okay — it's still delicious, but it's not "true" Neapolitan. For the real thing, use the grill method.
</Callout>
</Card>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Grill (300–500°C)">
<Card title="Grill Method — The Closest to Wood-Fired">
1. **Set up** your gas or charcoal grill for indirect heat: all burners on high, or coals pushed to one side. Place a pizza steel on the grate over the heat.
2. **Preheat** with the lid closed for 30 minutes. Target 400°C+ (use a thermometer).
3. **Stretch, assemble** on a peel as usual.
4. **Bake** with the lid closed for 60–90 seconds. Turn 90° every 20–30 seconds with a metal peel.
5. **Watch for** the crust to puff and char in spots — that's the leopard look.

<Callout tone="success" title="Why this works">
At 400°C+, the pizza bakes in under 2 minutes — just like a wood-fired oven. The crust puffs, the sauce caramelizes, and the mozzarella melts into creamy pools without burning.
</Callout>
</Card>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

## The Assembly Sequence

<Card title="Step-by-Step Assembly">
<Steps>
<Step title="Stretch the dough" status="done" subtitle="By hand only">
Flour the ball. Press from the center out, leaving the rim thick. Then drape over your knuckles and gently stretch, rotating — you want a 30–33 cm circle, thinner in the middle.
</Step>
<Step title="Sauce first" status="done" subtitle="A thin layer">
Use a ladle or spoon. Spread in a spiral, leaving the rim bare. You want ~2–3 tablespoons per pizza — not a pool.
</Step>
<Step title="Add mozzarella" status="done" subtitle="Pat dry first">
Tear into small, dry pieces. Distribute evenly, not too close to the edge.
</Step>
<Step title="Drizzle oil & basil" status="done" subtitle="Last touch">
A few drops of EVOO and 1–2 basil leaves. Don't add basil before baking if you want it fresh — add after.
</Step>
<Step title="Launch & bake" status="done" subtitle="Quick and confident">
Slide onto the hot steel/stone with a quick forward motion. Don't hesitate or it'll stick.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Card title="Troubleshooting">
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy center | Too much sauce or wet mozzarella | Pat mozzarella dry; use less sauce |
| Dough tears when stretching | Not enough rest or overworked | Let it rest 15 min before stretching |
| Crust too brown, center raw | Oven not hot enough | Preheat longer; use grill method |
| Pizza sticks to peel | Not enough flour on peel, or slow launch | Dust peel generously; launch quickly |
| Flat, dense crust | Over-proofed or under-proofed dough | Check fridge temp (4°C); use within 72h |
| Bitter char | Too much oil or oven too hot | Reduce oil; lower grill heat slightly |
</Card>

## The Classic Margherita — Your First Try

<Card title="Recipe Summary" subtitle="One pizza, made right">
**Dough:** 250g ball (from recipe above)  
**Sauce:** 2–3 tbsp crushed San Marzano + pinch salt  
**Toppings:** 60–80g fresh mozzarella, torn & dried  
**Finish:** EVOO drizzle, fresh basil after bake

<Callout tone="success" title="Success check">
A proper Neapolitan Margherita should have: a puffy, leopard-spotted cornicione (rim), a soft and chewy interior that folds, a thin, saucy center with creamy mozzarella pools, and a faint hint of char — not burnt bitterness.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Practice Makes Perfect">
<Checklist title="Your First Attempt Checklist" name="pizza-checklist">
- [ ] Made dough 3 days ahead (cold ferment in fridge)
- [ ] Brought dough to room temp 2–3 hours before baking
- [ ] Preheated steel/stone for 45+ minutes
- [ ] Used hand-stretched, not rolled, dough
- [ ] Pat mozzarella dry before topping
- [ ] Used a quick, confident launch
- [ ] Turned pizza during bake (home oven) or every 20s (grill)
- [ ] Let pizza rest 1 minute before slicing
</Checklist>
</Card>
