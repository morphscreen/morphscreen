# Should you rent or buy? Let's work through it

The honest answer: **it depends on how long you'll stay, what you can put down, and what prices look like in your area.** Let's break it down with your numbers.

<Callout tone="info" title="Your situation">
You earn £60k a year, pay £1,800/month rent, and you're deciding whether to keep renting or buy a flat. The big question is whether buying makes financial sense *for you*, not just in general.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't forget the extras">

And then you're responsible for **maintenance** — about 1% of the property value per year is a common rule of thumb.
</Callout>

## The real deciding factor: how long will you stay?

<Card title="Rent vs buy — the break-even point">
The biggest single factor is **how long you'll live there**. Buying has high upfront costs (stamp duty, fees, moving), so it usually only pays off if you stay long enough to spread those costs out.

<Steps>
<Step title="Under 2 years" status="todo" subtitle="Probably keep renting">The upfront costs of buying (fees, stamp duty, moving) will likely outweigh any savings. You also risk selling in a dip.</Step>
<Step title="2–5 years" status="todo" subtitle="It's a close call">You might break even, but only if prices rise or you're in a cheaper area. Renting gives you flexibility.</Step>
<Step title="5+ years" status="current" subtitle="Buying usually wins">The longer you stay, the more you benefit from fixed mortgage payments (vs rising rents) and building equity.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## So — what should you do?

<Card title="Your decision guide">
Here's a simple checklist to help you decide:

<Checklist title="Signs you should buy" name="buy-signs">
- [ ] You plan to stay in the same city for 5+ years
- [ ] You have at least a 10% deposit saved (plus fees)
- [ ] You can comfortably afford the mortgage plus maintenance
- [ ] You're okay with less flexibility (harder to move quickly)
- [ ] You want to build equity instead of paying a landlord
</Checklist>

<Checklist title="Signs you should keep renting" name="rent-signs">
- [ ] You might move for work or lifestyle within 2–3 years
- [ ] You don't have enough for a deposit plus fees yet
- [ ] You value being able to leave with a month's notice
- [ ] You don't want to deal with repairs and maintenance
- [ ] Prices in your area are very high relative to rent
</Checklist>
</Card>

## My recommendation

<Card title="What I'd do in your shoes">
Based on your £60k income and £1,800 rent, here's the honest take:

<Callout tone="success" title="If you can save a deposit and stay put — buy">
Your rent is high relative to your income (36% — above the 30% guideline). Buying a similar flat could cost you about the same monthly, but you'd be **building equity** instead of paying a landlord. If you can save a 10% deposit (£30k) and plan to stay 5+ years, buying is likely the better financial move.

However — if you don't have the deposit yet, or you're not sure about staying put, **keep renting for now** and save aggressively. Don't rush into buying just because it feels like "the right thing."
</Callout>

## One more thing — the "what if" you stay flexible

<Card title="The middle path">
You don't have to pick one forever. Many people **buy a flat, live in it for 5–7 years, then rent it out** when they move. That turns your home into an investment. But that's a decision for later — first, focus on whether buying makes sense *now*.

<Details title="A quick note on shared ownership" open="false">
If you can't afford a full deposit, **shared ownership** (buying 25–75% of a flat and renting the rest) is an option in many areas. It lowers your deposit requirement but comes with extra fees and less control. It's worth considering but not always the best deal.
</Details>
</Card>
