# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system has eight planets, split into two groups: the rocky **inner planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the gas/ice **outer planets** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Here's your guide to each one.

<Card title="The Inner Planets — Rocky Worlds">
<Columns>
<PlanetCard name="Mercury" type="Rocky" distance="57.9M km" diameter="4,879 km" day="59 Earth days" year="88 Earth days" moons="0">
The smallest planet and closest to the Sun. Its surface is heavily cratered, like our Moon, with extreme temperature swings.
</PlanetCard>
<PlanetCard name="Venus" type="Rocky" distance="108.2M km" diameter="12,104 km" day="243 Earth days" year="225 Earth days" moons="0">
The hottest planet due to a runaway greenhouse effect. It spins backwards and has a thick, toxic atmosphere of CO₂.
</PlanetCard>
<PlanetCard name="Earth" type="Rocky" distance="149.6M km" diameter="12,742 km" day="24 hours" year="365.25 days" moons="1">
Our home — the only known world with life. Liquid water covers 71% of its surface, and its atmosphere protects us.
</PlanetCard>
<PlanetCard name="Mars" type="Rocky" distance="227.9M km" diameter="6,779 km" day="24.6 hours" year="687 days" moons="2">
The Red Planet, colored by iron oxide dust. It has the tallest volcano (Olympus Mons) and a canyon system that spans the US.
</PlanetCard>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="The Outer Planets — Gas and Ice Giants">
<Columns>
<PlanetCard name="Jupiter" type="Gas giant" distance="778.5M km" diameter="139,820 km" day="9.9 hours" year="11.9 years" moons="95">
The largest planet — over 1,300 Earths could fit inside. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that's raged for centuries.
</PlanetCard>
<PlanetCard name="Saturn" type="Gas giant" distance="1.43B km" diameter="116,460 km" day="10.7 hours" year="29.5 years" moons="146">
Famous for its stunning ring system made of ice and rock. It's so light it would float in a giant bathtub.
</PlanetCard>
<PlanetCard name="Uranus" type="Ice giant" distance="2.87B km" diameter="50,724 km" day="17.2 hours" year="84 years" moons="28">
This blue-green world rotates on its side, likely from an ancient collision. It's the coldest planet in the solar system.
</PlanetCard>
<PlanetCard name="Neptune" type="Ice giant" distance="4.50B km" diameter="49,244 km" day="16.1 hours" year="165 years" moons="16">
The most distant planet, with supersonic winds reaching 2,100 km/h — the fastest in the solar system. Discovered by math first.
</PlanetCard>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Fun Facts to Remember">
- **Venus** spins backwards — the Sun rises in the west there.
- **Jupiter's** Great Red Spot has been observed for over 350 years.
- **Saturn's** rings are about 280,000 km wide but only ~10 meters thick on average.
- **Neptune** was the first planet found using mathematics before telescopes confirmed it.
- **Pluto** was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006, leaving us with eight planets.
</Card>
