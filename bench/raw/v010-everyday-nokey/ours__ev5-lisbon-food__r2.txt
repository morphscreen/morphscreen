# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon is a food lover's paradise — think fresh seafood, flaky pastries, and hearty comfort food, all at prices that won't break the bank. Here's your essential eating guide.

<Card title="The Must-Try List">
<Checklist title="Your Lisbon food checklist" name="lisbon-food">
- [ ] Pastel de nata (custard tart) — try at Manteigaria or Pastéis de Belém
- [ ] Bacalhau à brás (shredded cod with potatoes and eggs)
- [ ] Sardinhas assadas (grilled sardines) — best in June
- [ ] Bifana (pork sandwich) — head to O Trevo
- [ ] Ginjinha (cherry liqueur) — a shot at A Ginjinha
- [ ] Cataplana de marisco (seafood stew)
- [ ] Pão de deus (coconut bun) — grab at any pastelaria
- [ ] Ameijoas à bulhão pato (clams in garlic-coriander sauce)
</Checklist>

## The Iconic Pastel de Nata

<Card title="Pastel de Nata — The Custard Tart">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Average price" value="€1.30" note="per tart" icon="currency-eur" />
<Stat label="Best rated" value="4.7★" note="Manteigaria" icon="star" />
</Columns>

You can't leave Lisbon without trying one (or five). The perfect one has a shatteringly crisp, caramelized top and a warm, creamy custard center.

**Where to go:**
- **Manteigaria** (Chiado & Time Out Market) — the modern favorite, always fresh out of the oven. <Badge tone="success">Top pick</Badge>
- **Pastéis de Belém** (Belém) — the original since 1837, with a secret recipe. Expect a queue, but it moves fast.
- **Casa Pasteis de Nata** (Príncipe Real) — a great lesser-known spot with a lovely terrace.

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Ask for yours *polvilhado com canela* (dusted with cinnamon). Eat it warm, standing at the counter — that's how locals do it.</Callout>
</Card>

## Seafood & Main Dishes

<Card title="Where to Eat the Classics">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Bacalhau à Brás">
The national dish: shredded salt cod, thin fries, onions, and scrambled eggs, topped with olives.

**Best at:** **Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau** (Alfama) — they do a gourmet version in a pastry shell. Or try **O Velho Eurico** (Graça) for a modern twist.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Cataplana de Marisco">
A bubbling copper pot of clams, prawns, and fish in a garlic-tomato broth. Perfect for sharing.

**Best at:** **Cervejaria Ramiro** (Intendente) — famous for seafood. Go early or be prepared to wait. <Badge tone="warning">Busy</Badge>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Sardinhas Assadas">
Grilled sardines with peppers and boiled potatoes. A summer staple, especially during the June festivals.

**Best at:** **Zé da Mouraria** (Mouraria) — a no-frills spot that's been grilling them for decades.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bifana">
A simple but addictive sandwich: thin slices of marinated pork in a crusty roll, with mustard.

**Best at:** **O Trevo** (Saldanha) — the classic. Or **As Bifanas do Afonso** (Rossio) for a slightly fancier version.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

</Map>

**Top neighborhoods for food:**
- **Alfama** — the oldest district; tiny tascas (taverns) serving petiscos (Portuguese tapas). Try **Canto do Azulejo**.
- **Graça** — hip and local, with great miradouros (viewpoints) and casual eateries like **O Velho Eurico**.
- **Cais do Sodré** — home to the **Time Out Market**, a food hall with stalls from Lisbon's best chefs. Great for a quick, varied meal.

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Time Out Market — worth it?">Yes, for the variety. You can try 5 different dishes from 5 top restaurants in one sitting. It's touristy but the quality is genuinely high. Go at 12:30 or 14:30 to avoid the rush.</Callout>
</Card>

## Sweet Tooth & Drinks

<Card title="Desserts & Ginjinha">
<Columns>
<Card title="Pão de Deus">
A soft, fluffy bun topped with shredded coconut. Perfect with a coffee (a *meia de leite*).

**Best at:** any pastelaria, but **Confeitaria Nacional** (Baixa) is a beautiful historic option.
</Card>
<Card title="Ginjinha">
A sour cherry liqueur served in a shot glass — often with a piece of fruit at the bottom. Sweet, tart, and dangerously easy to drink.

**Best at:** **A Ginjinha** (Rossio) — a tiny stand that's been there since 1840. Drink it at the counter like a local.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Budget & Quick Bites

<Card title="Eat Well on a Budget">
| Spot | What to get | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Manteigaria** | Pastel de nata + coffee | €3 |
| **O Trevo** | Bifana | €4 |
| **Zé da Mouraria** | Sardines with sides | €12 |
| **Time Out Market** | 3 different stalls | €15 |
| **Casa Nepalesa** (Anjos) | Momos (dumplings) | €8 |

<Callout tone="success" title="Money-saving tip">Lunch menus (*prato do dia*) at local tascas cost €8–12 and include soup, main, drink, and coffee. Look for handwritten boards outside restaurants between 12:00–15:00.</Callout>
</Card>
