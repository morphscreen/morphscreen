# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making a true Neapolitan pizza at home is about mastering three things: a soft, airy dough, a simple San Marzano tomato sauce, and intense heat. While a wood-fired oven reaches 900°F (480°C), you can get surprisingly close with a home oven and a few tricks.

<Card title="The Dough" subtitle="The heart of Neapolitan pizza">
<Callout tone="info" title="00 Flour is key">Use Italian "00" flour (like Caputo Pizzeria). It's finely milled with higher protein for strength, giving you that soft, chewy texture. All-purpose flour will work but won't be as authentic.</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done" subtitle="5 minutes">Combine 500g 00 flour, 325g water (60-65% hydration), 10g fine sea salt, and 2g fresh yeast (or 1g dry). Mix until shaggy, then knead for 8-10 minutes until smooth and elastic.</Step>
<Step title="Bulk Ferment" status="done" subtitle="2 hours at room temp">Cover and let rise at room temperature until doubled. This starts the fermentation and develops flavor.</Step>
<Step title="Divide & Cold Ferment" status="current" subtitle="8-24 hours in fridge">Divide into 3 equal balls (about 275g each). Place in an oiled container, cover, and refrigerate for 8-24 hours. Longer = more complex flavor.</Step>
<Step title="Temper" status="todo" subtitle="2-3 hours before baking">Remove dough from fridge 2-3 hours before baking. Let come to room temperature. The dough should be relaxed and easy to stretch.</Step>
<Step title="Shape" status="todo" subtitle="1 minute per pizza">Flatten with fingertips, then stretch from the center outward, leaving a thicker rim (cornicione). Aim for thin center, puffy edge. Don't use a rolling pin!</Step>
</Steps>

<Details title="Tips for perfect dough">
- Use cold water (50°F/10°C) to keep dough temperature down during mixing
- Weigh ingredients for consistency
- If dough tears, let it rest 10 minutes before stretching again
- The dough should feel like a soft earlobe — silky and pliable
</Details>
</Card>

<Card title="The Sauce & Toppings" subtitle="Simple and authentic">
<Columns>
<Card title="San Marzano Sauce">
- 1 can (400g) **San Marzano tomatoes** (DOP)
- Pinch of sea salt
- 2-3 fresh basil leaves
- 1 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
- Crush tomatoes by hand; don't cook — the oven does that
</Card>
<Card title="Mozzarella">
- Use **fresh mozzarella di bufala** for authenticity
- Pat dry and tear into small pieces
- Add after sauce, not before
- Too much cheese = soggy center; use about 100g per pizza
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="success" title="Authentic Margherita">The classic: San Marzano sauce, fresh mozzarella, fresh basil, a drizzle of olive oil, and a pinch of salt. That's it. No extra toppings — respect the simplicity.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Baking: The Heat Challenge" subtitle="Getting close to 900°F at home">
<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat oven" status="done" subtitle="1 hour">Place a **pizza steel** (or stone) on the top rack. Preheat oven to **550°F (290°C)** for at least 1 hour. The steel stores heat like a wood-fired oven floor.</Step>
<Step title="Broiler boost" status="current" subtitle="10 minutes before">Switch oven to **broil** (high) for 10 minutes to superheat the steel above 600°F. Watch closely — some ovens get hotter.</Step>
<Step title="Assemble quickly" status="todo" subtitle="30 seconds">Sauce, cheese, basil, oil. Work fast so the dough doesn't stick or cool the steel.</Step>
<Step title="Launch & bake" status="todo" subtitle="6-8 minutes">Slide pizza onto steel using a **pizza peel** with semolina flour. Bake 3-4 minutes, then rotate 180° for even browning. The cornicione should puff and char slightly (leopard spotting).</Step>
<Step title="Finish under broiler" status="todo" subtitle="1-2 minutes">For extra char on top, switch to broil for the last 60-90 seconds. Watch it — it can burn fast.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="No pizza steel?">A pizza stone works but won't get as hot. Alternatively, use a cast-iron skillet upside down as a makeshift steel. Avoid baking sheets — they're too thin to store enough heat.</Callout>
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat" status="done" subtitle="60-70 min">Preheat oven with steel at 550°F, then broil for 10 min.</Step>
<Step title="Stretch dough" status="todo" subtitle="1 min">Shape into 10-12" round, thicker at edges.</Step>
<Step title="Top" status="todo" subtitle="30 sec">Spread sauce, add mozzarella, basil, salt, oil.</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="todo" subtitle="6-8 min">Launch onto steel; rotate halfway; broil at end.</Step>
<Step title="Serve" status="todo" subtitle="1 min">Rest 60 seconds, slice, eat immediately.</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Common Problems & Fixes">
<Compare title="Troubleshooting">
| Issue | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy center | Too much sauce/cheese, low heat | Use less sauce, pat mozzarella dry, bake hotter |
| Dough tears | Over-hydrated or over-stretched | Use lower hydration (60%), rest dough longer |
| Burnt crust, raw center | Oven too hot, dough too thick | Reduce temp slightly, stretch thinner |
| No leopard spots | Not enough heat | Use broiler boost, preheat longer |
| Tough, bready crust | Over-kneaded or too much flour | Knead less, use wet hands, don't add extra flour |
</Compare>
</Card>
