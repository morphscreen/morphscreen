# Which houseplants are hard to kill?

Some plants thrive on neglect. If you're prone to forgetting to water, have low light, or are new to plant parenting, these are the most forgiving houseplants.

<Card title="Snake Plant (Dracaena trifasciata)">
The **snake plant** is nearly indestructible. Its thick, sword-like leaves store water, so it can go a month without a drink. It tolerates low light, bright light, and everything in between. Overwatering is the main way to kill it — so when in doubt, skip the watering.

<KeyValue title="Care at a glance">
| Factor | Value |
|---|---|
| Light | Low to bright indirect |
| Water | Every 2–4 weeks, let soil dry completely |
| Humidity | Any |
| Temperature | 15–30°C |
| Growth | Slow to moderate |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="success" title="Pro tip">Snake plants are also one of the few plants that release oxygen at night, making them great for bedrooms.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)">
The ZZ plant looks like it belongs in a glossy magazine but asks for almost nothing. Its underground rhizomes store water, and its waxy leaves resist dust and pests. It thrives in fluorescent office light and can handle being forgotten for weeks.

<Callout tone="info" title="Note on light">ZZ plants can survive in surprisingly dark spots — even a windowless bathroom — though they grow faster with some indirect light.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)">
Pothos is the plant that forgives everything. Miss a watering and it droops dramatically — but perks back up within hours of a drink. It grows in water or soil, in low or bright light, and you can propagate endless new plants from cuttings.

<Card title="How to keep any plant alive" subtitle="Three simple rules">
<Steps>
<Step title="Water less than you think" status="done">Most houseplants die from overwatering, not underwatering. Stick a finger 2 cm into the soil — if it's dry, water; if damp, wait.</Step>
<Step title="Match light to the plant" status="done">Read the label. A snake plant tolerates low light, but a cactus will stretch and weaken without bright sun.</Step>
<Step title="Use well-draining pots" status="done">Ensure pots have drainage holes. Soggy roots rot; airy soil keeps them happy.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common mistakes to avoid">
<Columns>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Overwatering">Yellow, mushy leaves usually mean too much water. Let the soil dry out between waterings.</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Sudden moves">Plants dislike big changes. Move them gradually to a new spot to avoid shock.</Callout>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Signs your plant is healthy vs struggling">
<Compare title="Plant health check">
| Sign | Healthy | Struggling |
|---|---|---|
| Leaves | Firm, upright, glossy | Droopy, yellow, or brown tips |
| Soil | Dry on top between waterings | Constantly wet or bone dry |
| Growth | New leaves or vines appear | No change for months |
| Roots | White and firm | Brown, mushy, or smelly |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="Ready to start?">
<Callout tone="success" title="Beginner starter kit">Start with a **snake plant** or **pothos** — both are cheap, widely available, and forgiving. Place them near a window but out of direct afternoon sun, and water only when the soil feels dry.</Callout>
