# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

Vancouver is a hiker's paradise, with world-class trails just a short drive from downtown. Here are the top day hikes, from easy coastal strolls to challenging alpine summits.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you go">
Check trail conditions and weather at [BC Parks](https://bcparks.ca) or [Vancouver Trails](https://vancouvertrails.com). Many popular trails fill up early—arrive before 8 AM on weekends. Bring bear spray, layers, and plenty of water.
</Callout>

<Card title="Stawamus Chief (The Chief)">
<Badge tone="danger">Hard</Badge> <Badge tone="info">5-7 hrs</Badge> <Badge tone="neutral">11 km round trip</Badge>

The iconic granite monolith in Squamish, 45 minutes north of Vancouver. Three peaks offer spectacular views over Howe Sound. The trail involves chain-assisted steep sections and ladders—not for those afraid of heights.

- **Trailhead**: Stawamus Chief Provincial Park, Squamish
- **Best time**: May to October
- **Highlights**: Panoramic views of Howe Sound, Shannon Falls, and the surrounding cliffs
- **Tip**: Do all three peaks for the full experience; Peak 2 is the most scenic

<Card title="Garibaldi Lake">
<Badge tone="warning">Moderate</Badge> <Badge tone="info">5-6 hrs</Badge> <Badge tone="neutral">18 km round trip</Badge>

A turquoise alpine lake set against volcanic peaks, just 1.5 hours north of Vancouver. The steady switchback climb rewards you with one of BC's most photographed views.

- **Trailhead**: Rubble Creek trailhead, Garibaldi Provincial Park
- **Best time**: July to October (ice-free)
- **Highlights**: The impossibly blue lake, Barrier viewpoint, and Black Tusk views
- **Tip**: Continue 2 km to the lake's north shore for quieter spots; bring a filter to refill water

<Card title="Quarry Rock (Deep Cove)">
<Badge tone="success">Easy</Badge> <Badge tone="info">1.5-2 hrs</Badge> <Badge tone="neutral">3.8 km round trip</Badge>

A gentle forest walk ending on a granite outcrop with sweeping views of Deep Cove and Indian Arm. Perfect for families or a quick morning escape.

- **Trailhead**: Panorama Drive, Deep Cove (North Vancouver)
- **Best time**: Year-round (muddy after rain)
- **Highlights**: Panoramic views of Indian Arm, the Baden-Powell Trail, and Deep Cove's village
- **Tip**: Reward yourself with a famous Honey's Doughnuts after—just down the hill

<Card title="Lynn Canyon Loop">
<Badge tone="success">Easy</Badge> <Badge tone="info">1-2 hrs</Badge> <Badge tone="neutral">5 km loop</Badge>

A family-friendly loop through temperate rainforest, highlighted by the famous suspension bridge (free, unlike Capilano!). Combine several trails for a longer outing.

- **Trailhead**: Lynn Canyon Park, North Vancouver
- **Best time**: Year-round
- **Highlights**: Suspension bridge over Lynn Creek, Twin Falls, and the 30-foot pool
- **Tip**: Visit the Lynn Canyon Ecology Centre for interactive exhibits on the rainforest

<Card title="Panorama Ridge">
<Badge tone="danger">Hard</Badge> <Badge tone="info">8-10 hrs</Badge> <Badge tone="neutral">30 km round trip</Badge>

The ultimate reward for fit hikers: a 360-degree view of Garibaldi Lake, Black Tusk, and the surrounding glaciers. A long day, but one of the most spectacular in the province.

- **Trailhead**: Rubble Creek trailhead (same as Garibaldi Lake)
- **Best time**: July to October (snow-free)
- **Highlights**: Unmatched views of Garibaldi Lake's turquoise waters, Black Tusk, and Mount Price
- **Tip**: Start before 6 AM; carry a headlamp and extra food—this is a full-day commitment

<Card title="Joffre Lakes">
<Badge tone="warning">Moderate</Badge> <Badge tone="info">3-4 hrs</Badge> <Badge tone="neutral">10 km round trip</Badge>

Three glacier-fed lakes, each more stunning than the last, with views of Mount Matier's glacier. A bit farther (2.5 hrs from Vancouver) but worth the drive.

- **Trailhead**: Joffre Lakes Provincial Park, Pemberton
- **Best time**: July to October (winter requires avalanche gear)
- **Highlights**: Middle Lake's turquoise color, the glacier at Upper Lake, and camping spots
- **Note**: Requires a free day-use pass in summer (book at [BC Parks](https://bcparks.ca))

<Card title="Grouse Grind">
<Badge tone="danger">Hard</Badge> <Badge tone="info">1.5-2 hrs</Badge> <Badge tone="neutral">2.9 km one-way</Badge>

Not for the faint-hearted: 2,830 steps straight up the mountain. Known as "Mother Nature's Stairmaster," it's a workout with a reward—the view from Grouse Mountain's summit.

- **Trailhead**: Grouse Mountain base, North Vancouver
- **Best time**: May to October (closed when icy)
- **Highlights**: Sweat, determination, and the summit view over Vancouver
- **Tip**: Take the free gondola down; the trail is one-way up only. Arrive early to avoid crowds

<Card title="Getting There & Practical Tips">
<Columns>
<div>
**Transport**
- **By car**: Most trailheads are 30 min–2.5 hrs from downtown. Parking fills by 8 AM on weekends.
- **By transit**: Grouse Grind (bus #236), Lynn Canyon (#228), and Quarry Rock (#211/212) are accessible by bus.
- **By rideshare**: Popular for Grouse and Lynn Canyon; less reliable for farther trails.
</div>
<div>
**What to bring**
- Sturdy hiking shoes with grip
- Layers (temperature drops with elevation)
- 2-3 L of water per person
- Snacks and lunch
- Bear spray (for Garibaldi, Joffre, Chief)
- Sunscreen and hat
- First-aid kit
</div>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Safety first">
These trails are in bear country. Make noise, travel in groups, and carry bear spray (know how to use it). Check [AdventureSmart](https://adventuresmart.ca) for trip planning and emergency info. Cell coverage is spotty—tell someone your plan.
</Callout>
</Card>
