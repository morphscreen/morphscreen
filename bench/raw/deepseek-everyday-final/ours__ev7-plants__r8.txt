# Which houseplants are hard to kill?

Low-maintenance plants that forgive missed waterings, low light, and occasional neglect.

<Card title="Top 5 unkillable houseplants">

<Columns>
<Card title="1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)">
- Tolerates weeks without water
- Thrives in low light or bright sun
- Purifies air (NASA study)
- Grows slowly, rarely needs repotting
- Pet warning: toxic if eaten
</Card>

<Card title="2. Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)">
- Droops when thirsty, perks up after watering
- Grows in water or soil
- Handles low light, but variegation needs more light
- Easy to propagate from cuttings
- Pet warning: toxic if eaten
</Card>

<Card title="3. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)">
- Stores water in thick rhizomes
- Survives months of drought
- Shiny, waxy leaves resist dust
- Slow grower, ideal for beginners
- Pet warning: toxic if eaten
</Card>

<Card title="4. Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)">
- Sends out baby "spiderettes" to share
- Tolerates irregular watering
- Prefers bright, indirect light
- Safe for pets
- Grows fast, great for hanging baskets
</Card>

<Card title="5. Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum)">
- Dramatic droop when thirsty, recovers in hours
- Likes low to medium light
- White blooms several times a year
- Pet warning: toxic if eaten
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Overwatering kills more than neglect">
Most "unkillable" plants die from too much love. Let the soil dry out between waterings — stick a finger 2 inches deep; if it's dry, water. If it's damp, wait.
</Callout>

<Card title="Signs you're doing it right (or wrong)">
<Columns>
<Card title="Healthy plant">
- Firm, upright leaves
- New growth monthly
- Soil dries between waterings
- No brown tips
</Card>
<Card title="Warning signs">
- Yellow, mushy leaves → overwatered
- Brown, crispy edges → underwatered
- Pale leaves → too much direct sun
- Drooping → check soil moisture
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Pro tip">
Buy a small moisture meter (under $10). It removes all guesswork — stick it in the soil and water only when it reads "dry". That's the closest thing to a plant-proof guarantee.
</Callout>

<Card title="If you want to try something slightly harder">
<Columns>
<Card title="Next level up">
- Rubber plant (Ficus elastica)

<Card title="Avoid if you're forgetful">
- Ferns (need constant moisture)

- Orchids (specific watering schedule)
</Card>
