Renting vs. buying is one of the biggest financial decisions you'll make. Let's break it down for your situation.

<Card title="The short answer" subtitle="It depends on your goals and timeline">
<Callout tone="info" title="Key question">How long do you plan to stay in this flat or area? If it's under 3–5 years, renting is often smarter. If you're settling down for 5+ years, buying usually wins.</Callout>

**Renting** gives you flexibility, no maintenance costs, and lets you invest your deposit elsewhere. **Buying** builds equity, protects you from rent rises, and gives you stability — but comes with big upfront costs and responsibilities.
</Card>

<Card title="Compare: rent vs. buy">
<Compare title="Rent vs. buy for a £300,000 flat">
| Factor | Renting | Buying |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | £1,800 rent | ~£1,350 mortgage + £200 bills/maintenance |
| Upfront cost | £1,800 deposit | ~£15,000–£20,000 deposit + fees |
| Equity built | None | ~£400–£500/month |
| Flexibility | Move anytime | Selling costs ~2–3% of price |
| Maintenance | Landlord's problem | Your problem (£1–2k/year) |
| Rent/mortgage rises | Likely 3–5%/year | Fixed for 2–5 years |
| Long-term wealth | Depends on investments | Property appreciation + paid-off mortgage |
</Compare>
<p><small>Example: £300k flat, 10% deposit, 4.5% 2-year fixed mortgage. Your actual figures will vary.</small></p>

<Card title="When renting makes sense for you">
<Checklist title="Signs you should keep renting" name="rent-signs">
- [ ] You might move cities or jobs within 3 years
- [ ] You don't have a 10%+ deposit saved yet
- [ ] You value being able to leave at short notice
- [ ] You'd rather invest your savings in stocks/index funds
- [ ] You're not sure about the area long-term
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="When buying makes sense for you">
<Checklist title="Signs you should buy" name="buy-signs">
- [ ] You plan to stay put for 5+ years
- [ ] You have £25,000–£35,000 saved for deposit + fees
- [ ] You want to fix your housing costs
- [ ] You're tired of rent increases (yours has likely gone up £100–200/year)
- [ ] You want to build equity instead of paying a landlord's mortgage
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="The 5% rule" subtitle="A quick way to compare">
<Callout tone="success" title="Rule of thumb">If you can rent a similar flat for less than 5% of its purchase price per year, renting is financially smarter — assuming you invest the difference.</Callout>

For a £300,000 flat:
- **5% rule threshold**: £15,000/year = £1,250/month
- **Your rent**: £1,800/month = 7.2% of £300k
- **Verdict**: At that ratio, buying looks more attractive *if* you're staying put.
