## The inner rocky planets

<Card title="Mercury — the swift messenger">
<Columns>
<div>
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no atmosphere, so temperatures swing wildly between scorching days and freezing nights. A year on Mercury lasts just 88 Earth days, but a single day there (sunrise to sunrise) lasts 176 Earth days — twice as long as its year!

- **Diameter:** 4,879 km
- **Day length:** 176 Earth days
- **Year length:** 88 Earth days
- **Moons:** 0
- **Notable feature:** Heavily cratered, like our Moon
</div>

<Card title="Venus — the veiled twin">
<Columns>
<div>
Venus is often called Earth's "twin" because it's nearly the same size, but it couldn't be more different. A thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide traps heat, making Venus the hottest planet — hot enough to melt lead. It spins backwards, so the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. Its surface is hidden beneath swirling clouds of sulfuric acid.

- **Diameter:** 12,104 km
- **Day length:** 243 Earth days (retrograde)
- **Year length:** 225 Earth days
- **Moons:** 0
- **Notable feature:** Brightest natural object in our night sky after the Moon
</div>
<Gallery>
![Venus in ultraviolet from the Akatsuki spacecraft](find)
</Gallery>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Earth — the blue marble">
<Columns>
<div>
Our home is the only known world with liquid water on its surface and life. About 71% of Earth is covered by oceans, and its atmosphere of nitrogen and oxygen protects us from harmful radiation. Earth's magnetic field and the Moon's stabilizing pull make it a uniquely stable place for life to flourish.

- **Diameter:** 12,742 km
- **Day length:** 24 hours
- **Year length:** 365.25 days
- **Moons:** 1 (the Moon)
- **Notable feature:** Only known planet with life
</div>
<Gallery>
![Earth from the Apollo 17 mission, the famous Blue Marble photo](find)
</Gallery>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Mars — the red planet">
<Columns>
<div>
Mars is a cold desert world with a thin atmosphere, rusty red soil, and the largest volcano in the solar system — Olympus Mons, about 2.5 times the height of Everest. It has polar ice caps and evidence of ancient rivers and lakes, making it a prime target in the search for past life.

- **Diameter:** 6,779 km
- **Day length:** 24.6 hours
- **Year length:** 687 Earth days
- **Moons:** 2 (Phobos & Deimos)
- **Notable feature:** Olympus Mons, tallest volcano known
</div>

## The outer gas giants

<Card title="Jupiter — the king of planets">
<Columns>
<div>
Jupiter is more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. Jupiter has the most moons of any planet — at least 95 — including Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system, bigger than Mercury.

- **Diameter:** 139,820 km
- **Day length:** 9.9 hours (fastest rotation)
- **Year length:** 11.9 Earth years
- **Moons:** 95+
- **Notable feature:** Great Red Spot, a storm on the surface
</div>
<Gallery>
![Jupiter in true color from the Juno spacecraft](find)
</Gallery>
</Columns>

<Card title="Saturn — the jewel of the solar system">
<Columns>
<div>
Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system, made of billions of ice and rock particles. It's the least dense planet — it would float in water if you had a big enough bathtub. Saturn's moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane, making it one of the most intriguing places to study.

- **Diameter:** 116,460 km
- **Day length:** 10.7 hours
- **Year length:** 29.5 Earth years
- **Moons:** 145+
- **Notable feature:** Extensive ring system
</div>
<Gallery>
![Saturn in true color from the Cassini spacecraft](find)
</Gallery>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Uranus — the sideways world">
<Columns>
<div>
Uranus is tilted on its side at nearly 98 degrees, likely from a massive ancient collision. This means it essentially rolls around the Sun, with each pole getting 42 years of sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness. It's an ice giant, made mostly of water, methane, and ammonia ices, giving it a pale cyan color.

- **Diameter:** 50,724 km
- **Day length:** 17.2 hours (retrograde)
- **Year length:** 84 Earth years
- **Moons:** 27
- **Notable feature:** Extreme axial tilt
</div>
<Gallery>
![Uranus in true color from the Voyager 2 spacecraft](find)
</Gallery>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune — the windy blue giant">
<Columns>
<div>
Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun and a deep, vivid blue. It has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching over 2,000 km/h — faster than the speed of sound on Earth. It was the first planet discovered through mathematical prediction rather than direct observation, in 1846.

- **Diameter:** 49,244 km
- **Day length:** 16.1 hours
- **Year length:** 165 Earth years
- **Moons:** 14
- **Notable feature:** Supersonic winds
</div>

<Callout tone="info" title="What about Pluto?">
Pluto was reclassified as a **dwarf planet** in 2006 by the International Astronomical Union. It now shares that category with other worlds like Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake. Dwarf planets orbit the Sun but haven't "cleared their neighborhood" of other objects.
</Callout>

## Why it matters

<Card title="What makes Earth special?">
Earth sits in the **habitable zone** — the "Goldilocks" region where water can exist as a liquid. This, combined with our protective atmosphere, magnetic field, and the Moon's stabilizing influence, created the conditions for life to arise and thrive. Studying other planets helps us understand our own place in the universe and search for life beyond.
