# How to make a proper Neapolitan pizza at home

A true Neapolitan pizza has a soft, puffy, leopard-spotted crust, a bright tomato sauce, and fresh mozzarella — all baked in under 90 seconds at scorching heat. Here's how to get as close as possible in a home kitchen.

<Card title="The three pillars">
<Columns>
<div>
**1. The dough** — 00 flour, water, salt, and yeast. No oil, no sugar. High hydration (60–65%) for a light, airy crust.
</div>
<div>
**2. The heat** — 430°C (800°F) in a wood-fired oven. At home, use a pizza steel or stone in a maximum-heat oven, or a dedicated outdoor pizza oven.
</div>
<div>
**3. The toppings** — San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte mozzarella, fresh basil, and extra-virgin olive oil. Nothing more.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

## The dough recipe

<Card title="Ingredients (for 4 pizzas, ~250g each)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Caputo 00 flour (or other Italian 00) | 500g |
| Water (room temperature) | 300–325g |
| Fine sea salt | 10g |
| Fresh yeast (or 1g dry yeast) | 2g |
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done">
Dissolve the yeast in the water. Add the flour and mix until no dry bits remain. Cover and rest 20 minutes (autolyse).
</Step>
<Step title="Add salt" status="done">
Add the salt and knead 10–15 minutes by hand, or 5–8 minutes in a stand mixer, until smooth and elastic. The dough should pass the windowpane test.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done">
Cover and let rise at room temperature (20–24°C) for 8–12 hours, or until tripled in volume. For a more complex flavor, refrigerate 24–48 hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Shape balls" status="done">
Gently divide into 4 pieces of ~250g. Shape each into a tight ball, seam-side down. Place on a floured tray, cover, and proof 2–4 hours at room temperature.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch" status="done">
Flour your hands and a work surface. Press the ball flat, then stretch from the center outward, leaving a thicker rim (cornicione). Don't use a rolling pin — it knocks out the gas.
</Step>
<Step title="Top & bake" status="done">
Top lightly, then launch into the oven. Bake until the crust is puffed and charred in spots — about 60–90 seconds at 430°C, or 4–6 minutes in a home oven at max.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The oven: your biggest challenge

<Callout tone="warning" title="Home ovens top out at ~250°C">
That's half the heat of a real pizzaiolo's oven. You can still make excellent pizza, but it won't be identical — the crust will be less puffy and the char less pronounced.
</Callout>

<Card title="Oven options compared">
<Compare title="How to bake">
| Feature | Home oven + steel | Outdoor pizza oven | Cast iron skillet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max temperature | 250–290°C | 400–500°C | 250°C |
| Bake time | 5–8 min | 60–90 sec | 6–8 min |
| Crust texture | Good, slightly less puffy | Closest to authentic | Good, but not charred |
| Cost | $50–150 | $200–800+ | $30–50 |
| Ease | Easy, fits any kitchen | Needs outdoor space | Very easy |
| Verdict | Best for most people | For the obsessed | Great entry point |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="For a home oven">
**Use a pizza steel** (not a stone) — it transfers heat faster. Place it on the highest rack, at least 15cm from the top heating element. Preheat at maximum for at least 45 minutes, then switch to broil for the final 5 minutes to get the top scorching hot.
</Card>

## The sauce and toppings

<Card title="The classic Margherita">
| Component | What to use | How to prep |
|---|---|---|
| Sauce | San Marzano tomatoes (DOP) | Crush by hand, add a pinch of salt. **Do not cook it.** |
| Cheese | Fior di latte mozzarella (or buffalo for extra richness) | Tear into 2–3cm pieces, drain well, pat dry. |
| Basil | Fresh leaves | Add after baking, or just before, so it doesn't burn. |
| Oil | Extra-virgin olive oil | A drizzle over the top before baking. |
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Less is more">
Neapolitan pizza is wet and delicate. Use **about 80–90g of sauce and 100–120g of mozzarella** per pizza — too much will make the center soggy.
</Callout>

## Step-by-step assembly

<Steps>
<Step title="Stretch the dough" status="done">
On a floured surface, stretch to a ~30cm round, thicker at the rim. The center should be thin enough to see light through.
</Step>
<Step title="Add sauce" status="done">
Spoon the crushed tomatoes into the center and spread in a spiral outward, leaving a 2–3cm border.
</Step>
<Step title="Add cheese" status="done">
Distribute the mozzarella pieces evenly. Don't overcrowd — it should melt into pools, not a solid blanket.
</Step>
<Step title="Drizzle oil" status="done">
A light drizzle of olive oil over the top. This helps browning and flavor.
</Step>
<Step title="Launch" status="done">
Use a floured pizza peel. Give the pizza a quick shake to make sure it's not stuck, then slide it onto the steel in one swift motion.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake & rotate" status="done">
Bake 2–3 minutes, then rotate 180° for even browning. In a home oven, expect 5–8 minutes total. The crust should be puffed, charred in spots, and the cheese bubbling.
</Step>
<Step title="Finish" status="done">
Remove, add fresh basil leaves, a final drizzle of oil, and a pinch of flaky sea salt. Serve immediately — Neapolitan pizza waits for no one.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Common mistakes to avoid

<Callout tone="danger" title="Don't do these">
- **Over-flouring the dough** — makes the crust tough and floury.
- **Using cold dough** — let it come to room temperature before stretching.
- **Overloading toppings** — a soggy center is the #1 home mistake.
- **Baking on a cold stone** — always preheat at least 45 minutes.
- **Rolling with a pin** — deflates the air bubbles that make the crust light.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

<Card title="What went wrong?">
| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Crust is pale and soft | Oven not hot enough | Preheat longer, use broiler at the end |
| Center is soggy | Too much sauce or wet mozzarella | Drain cheese, use less sauce |
| Crust is tough | Over-kneaded or over-floured | Knead less, use less flour when stretching |
| Dough tears when stretching | Not enough gluten development | Knead longer, rest the dough |
| Bottom burns before top cooks | Steel too close to heat source | Lower the rack slightly |
</Card>

## The verdict

<Card title="Is it worth it?">
Making true Neapolitan pizza at home is a **project**, not a weeknight dinner. The dough needs 24+ hours, the oven needs serious preheating, and the technique takes practice. But when it works — that puffy, charred, chewy-soft crust — it's worth every minute.

**Start simple**: use this recipe, a pizza steel, and a home oven. Once you've mastered that, consider an outdoor pizza oven for the full 90-second experience.
</Card>
