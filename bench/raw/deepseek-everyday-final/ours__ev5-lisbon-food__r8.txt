# What to Eat in Lisbon — and Where

Lisbon is a city of small plates, fresh seafood, and sweet pastries. Here's a practical guide to the dishes you shouldn't miss and the places to find them.

<Card title="Pastel de Nata — the Custard Tart">
The iconic Portuguese egg tart: crisp, flaky pastry with a caramelised, creamy custard. Best eaten warm, dusted with cinnamon.

<Gallery>
![Pastel de nata with cinnamon on a small plate](find)
![Bite into a pastel de nata showing the custard centre](find)
</Gallery>

**Where to go:**
- **Pastéis de Belém** — the original since 1837, in the Belém district. Expect queues, but they move fast. Rua de Belém 84–92.
- **Manteigaria** — modern favourite with several branches (Chiado, Time Out Market). Watch them being made.
- **Fábrica da Nata** — good quality, central locations, less crowded than Belém.

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Ask for it "com canela" (with cinnamon) — it's the traditional way.</Callout>

</Card>

<Card title="Bifana — the Pork Sandwich">
A humble but glorious sandwich: thinly sliced pork in a garlicky, peppery marinade, served on a crusty roll. Often with a dab of mustard.

<Gallery>
![Bifana pork sandwich on a paper plate](find)
![Grilled bifanas on a street stall](find)
</Gallery>

**Where to go:**
- **Casa Bifanas** — right by Rossio station, a classic since 1907. Order one with mustard and a beer.
- **O Trevo** — a tiny spot in Saldanha, beloved by locals for its spicy version.
- **A Provinciana** — in Mouraria, a neighbourhood institution.

</Card>

<Card title="Seafood — from the Atlantic">
Lisbon sits on the Atlantic, so seafood is king. The highlight is **cataplana**, a seafood stew (clams, prawns, fish) cooked in a copper pot, served sizzling.

<Gallery>
![Cataplana seafood stew in a copper pot](find)
![Grilled sardines on a plate with salad](find)
![Fresh clams in garlic butter](find)
</Gallery>

**Where to go:**
- **Cervejaria Ramiro** — the most famous seafood house. Go for the tiger prawns, clams à Bulhão Pato, and the garlic bread. Expect a queue, but it's worth it.
- **Pinoquio** — a traditional spot near Restauradores, great for cataplana and grilled fish.
- **Casa de Pasto** — a modern take on classic Portuguese cooking, excellent seafood rice (arroz de marisco).

<Callout tone="success" title="Seasonal tip">In June, during the Festas de Santo António, grilled sardines (sardinhas assadas) are everywhere — don't miss them.</Callout>

</Card>

<Card title="Petiscos — Portuguese Tapas">
Share small plates like you would in Spain, but with a Portuguese twist. Think fried chouriço, octopus salad, and croquetes.

<Gallery>
![Fried chouriço slices on a plate](find)
![Octopus salad with olives and parsley](find)
</Gallery>

**Where to go:**
- **O Velho Eurico** — tiny, no-frills, incredible petiscos in Graça. Go early or be prepared to wait.
- **Casa Independente** — a lively spot in Intendente with a creative menu and great cocktails.
- **Taberna da Rua das Flores** — a small, beloved taberna in Chiado; the menu changes daily.

</Card>

<Card title="Ginjinha — the Cherry Liqueur">
A sweet, moreish liqueur made from sour cherries. Served in a small shot glass, sometimes with a cherry in the bottom.

<Gallery>
![Ginjinha shot glasses on a wooden bar](find)
![Ginjinha in a chocolate cup](find)
</Gallery>

**Where to go:**
- **A Ginjinha** — the original, right off Rossio Square. Stand at the counter like everyone else.
- **Ginjinha Sem Rival** — in Bairro Alto, a classic rival, often less crowded.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Watch out">It's sweet and goes down easily — pace yourself!</Callout>

</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat — by Area">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Baixa / Chiado">
- **Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau** — bacalhau (cod) cakes with a glass of port.
- **Confeitaria Nacional** — historic pastry shop, great for breakfast.
- **Time Out Market** — a food hall with stalls from top chefs. Good for variety, busy at peak hours.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Alfama">
- **Casa de Pasto** — modern Portuguese, excellent seafood rice.
- **A Travessa do Fado** — fado house with dinner shows — book ahead.
- **Beco do Jasmim** — cosy spot for petiscos, off the tourist trail.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Príncipe Real">
- **A Cevicheria** — famous for ceviche and octopus dishes, stylish.
- **O Prego da Peixaria** — modern prego (steak sandwich) with creative toppings.
- **Mercado de Príncipe Real** — small market with organic produce and snacks.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Graça / Mouraria">
- **O Velho Eurico** — the petiscos gem, tiny and wonderful.
- **A Provinciana** — bifanas and classic Portuguese fare.
- **Tasca do Chico** — a hole-in-the-wall with live fado, great for drinks and petiscos.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="A Day of Eating — Suggested Route">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Pastel de nata & coffee" status="done">
Start at **Manteigaria** in Chiado. Grab a nata and a *meia de leite* (milky coffee).
</Step>
<Step title="Mid-morning: Ginjinha" status="done">
Walk to **A Ginjinha** by Rossio. One shot to wake up the taste buds.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Seafood" status="done">
Head to **Cervejaria Ramiro** for clams, prawns, and garlic bread. Go early (12:15) to beat the queue.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Bifana" status="done">
Stroll to **Casa Bifanas** for a late-afternoon snack. Mustard on top.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Petiscos & dinner" status="done">
Finish in **Graça** at **O Velho Eurico**. Order a spread of petiscos and share. Walk back downhill through the miradouros for the view.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Quick Tips">
<Columns>
<div>
<Badge tone="success">Good to know</Badge>

- **Book ahead** for dinner at popular spots, especially Friday and Saturday.
- **Cash** is handy for tiny bars and tascas; cards work in most restaurants.
- **Portions** are generous — sharing is normal for petiscos.
</div>
<div>
<Badge tone="info">Try also</Badge>

- **Bacalhau à Brás** — shredded cod with potatoes and eggs.
- **Caldo verde** — kale and potato soup with chouriço.
- **Pudim flan** — Portuguese crème caramel for dessert.
</div>
