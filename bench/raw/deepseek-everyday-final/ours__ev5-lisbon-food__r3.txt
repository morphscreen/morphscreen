# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon's food scene blends Atlantic seafood, Moorish spices, and hearty countryside cooking. Here's your essential eating guide, from morning pastries to late-night snacks.

<Card title="The Must-Try Dishes & Where to Get Them">

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Pastéis de Nata">
The iconic custard tart with a caramelized, flaky crust.

**Best spots:**
- **Pastéis de Belém** – The original bakery since 1837, in Belém. Go early to avoid queues.
- **Manteigaria** – Modern rival with a buttery, crisp version, near Time Out Market.
- **Casa Pastéis de Belém** – A local favorite in the Alfama district.

**Tip:** Eat them warm, dusted with cinnamon and powdered sugar.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Bacalhau (Salted Cod)">
Portugal's "faithful friend" – said to have 1,000 recipes.

**Best spots:**
- **Casa de Pasto** – Classic *bacalhau à brás* (shredded cod with potatoes, eggs, and olives).
- **O Velho Eurico** – Modern takes in a cozy, tiled setting in Graça.
- **A Travessa** – Elegant *bacalhau com natas* (baked with cream) in a former convent.

**Try:** *Bacalhau à lagareiro* – roasted with olive oil and garlic, often served with potatoes.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Sardines & Grilled Fish">
Lisbon's love affair with the sea, best in summer.

**Best spots:**
- **Zé da Mouraria** – Tiny, unpretentious spot serving charcoal-grilled sardines in the old Mouraria quarter.
- **Cervejaria Ramiro** – Legendary for shellfish and garlic shrimp (*pregos*), a bit touristy but worth it.
- **O Pescador** – Family-run, with fresh catch daily in Campo de Ourique.

**Season:** June is *Festas de Lisboa* – grilled sardines on every corner.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Bifana (Pork Sandwich)">
A simple, glorious sandwich: marinated pork in a crusty roll.

**Best spots:**
- **Casa das Bifanas** – The classic, near Rossio station, open since 1945.
- **O Trevo** – Another institution, with a slightly spicier sauce.
- **Bifanas do Afonso** – Modern contender in the Mercado de Campo de Ourique.

**Tip:** Add a drop of hot piri-piri sauce and pair with a cold beer.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Cataplana (Seafood Stew)">
A copper-pot stew of clams, prawns, and fish in a rich tomato-saffron broth.

**Best spots:**
- **Uma** – Small, no-frills place in the Baixa, serving only cataplana for two.
- **Ponto Final** – Dramatic riverside setting in Almada (ferry ride away), with a superb cataplana.
- **Casa do Alentejo** – Moorish courtyard, serving a heartier Alentejo-style version.

**Tip:** Soak up the broth with crusty bread.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Ginjinha (Cherry Liqueur)">
Not a dish, but essential: a sweet cherry liqueur, often served with a sour cherry in the glass.

**Best spots:**
- **A Ginjinha** – The original hole-in-the-wall near Rossio since 1840.
- **Ginjinha Sem Rival** – A quieter rival in the Alfama, with a small terrace.

**Tip:** Try it *com elas* (with cherries) for the full experience.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="A Food-Lover's Walking Route" subtitle="Start in the morning, end at night">

<Steps>
<Step title="9:00 – Pastéis de Nata & Galão" status="done">
Start at **Manteigaria** (Rua do Loreto) for a warm pastel and a milky *galão* (coffee with milk).
</Step>
<Step title="10:30 – Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market)" status="done">
Explore the food hall in the historic market. Try **Café de São Bento**'s *prego* (steak sandwich) or **Miguel Castro e Silva**'s seafood.
</Step>
<Step title="13:00 – Bacalhau in the Alfama" status="done">
Walk to **O Velho Eurico** for a modern *bacalhau à brás*, or **Casa de Pasto** for a traditional version.
</Step>
<Step title="15:00 – Ginjinha Break" status="done">
Stop at **A Ginjinha** near Rossio for a quick cherry liqueur pick-me-up.
</Step>
<Step title="19:00 – Bifana & Beer" status="done">
Head to **Casa das Bifanas** for a pre-dinner bifana, then a cold *super bock*.
</Step>
<Step title="21:00 – Seafood Dinner" status="done">
Finish at **Cervejaria Ramiro** for garlic shrimp and shellfish. Go early or be prepared to wait.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Practical Tips">
<Callout tone="info" title="Eat like a local">
- **Lunch** is the main meal (12:30–15:00). Many places offer a *prato do dia* for under €10.
- **Dinner** starts late – 20:00 or later. Book ahead for popular spots.
- **Tap water** is fine to drink. Ask for *água da torneira* to save money.
- **Tipping** is not expected, but rounding up or leaving 5–10% is appreciated.
- **Time Out Market** is touristy but great for a first taste – go on a weekday to avoid crowds.
</Callout>
</Card>
