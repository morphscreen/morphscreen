# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making a true Neapolitan pizza at home is about mastering three things: a high-hydration dough that ferments slowly, a blazing-hot oven, and top-quality ingredients. It's a craft, but absolutely achievable in a home kitchen.

<Callout tone="info" title="The 3 pillars of Neapolitan pizza">
1. **Dough**: 00 flour, water, salt, and yeast — nothing else. Hydration around 60-65%.
2. **Oven**: Needs to reach 430-500°C (800-900°F) for that leopard-spotted, puffy crust.
3. **Ingredients**: San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte mozzarella, fresh basil, extra-virgin olive oil.
</Callout>

## The Dough

This is the heart of it. The dough needs a long, slow fermentation to develop flavor and digestibility.

<Card title="The recipe" subtitle="Makes 4 pizzas (250g each)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Caputo 00 flour (or similar) | 500g |
| Water (room temp) | 320g (64% hydration) |
| Fine sea salt | 10g |
| Fresh yeast (or 1g dry) | 2g |

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done" subtitle="10 minutes">Dissolve yeast in water. Add flour and mix until shaggy. Rest 20 minutes (autolyse). Add salt and knead until smooth and elastic — it should pass the windowpane test.</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done" subtitle="8-24 hours">Cover and let rise at room temperature (18-22°C) for 8-12 hours, or up to 24 hours in a cooler spot. The dough should roughly double.</Step>
<Step title="Divide & ball" status="done" subtitle="1 hour">Gently divide into 4 pieces (about 250g each). Shape each into a tight ball. Place on a floured tray, cover, and rest 1-2 hours at room temperature.</Step>
<Step title="Stretch" status="current" subtitle="30 seconds per pizza">Flour your hands. Press the ball flat, then stretch from the center outward, leaving a thicker rim (cornicione). Aim for 10-12 inches (25-30cm) diameter.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't use a rolling pin">Rolling pins crush the air bubbles that create the puffy crust. Always stretch by hand — the dough should be soft and relaxed enough to stretch easily.</Callout>

## The Oven

This is the biggest challenge at home. A home oven reaches about 250°C (480°F), far below the 430°C+ needed for true Neapolitan pizza.

<Card title="Getting your oven hot enough">
<Columns>
<Card title="Option 1: Pizza steel + broiler">
- Place a thick (6mm+) steel or stone on the top rack
- Preheat oven at max temperature for 1 hour
- Switch to broiler for 10 minutes just before baking
- Bake for 4-6 minutes, rotating once
- **Result**: Good, but not quite Neapolitan
</Card>
<Card title="Option 2: Dedicated pizza oven">
- Ooni, Gozney, Roccbox, or similar
- Reaches 450-500°C in 20-30 minutes
- Bake for 60-90 seconds
- **Result**: True Neapolitan, leopard spots and all
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The 90-second bake">In a proper pizza oven at 450°C+, the pizza cooks in 60-90 seconds. The bottom should be leopard-spotted, the crust puffy and slightly charred, and the cheese just melted — never browned.</Callout>

## The Toppings

Neapolitan pizza is minimalist. The quality of each ingredient matters more than the quantity.

<Card title="Classic Margherita">
| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| San Marzano tomatoes | 100g | Crushed by hand, drained slightly |
| Fior di latte mozzarella | 80g | Torn into pieces, not shredded |
| Fresh basil | 4-5 leaves | Added after baking or just before |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 1 tbsp | Drizzled over the top |
| Fine sea salt | A pinch | On the tomatoes |

<Callout tone="neutral" title="San Marzano vs. regular">True San Marzano tomatoes are grown in the volcanic soil near Mount Vesuvius. They're sweeter and less acidic. If you can't find them, any good quality whole peeled Italian plum tomatoes will work.</Callout>
</Card>

## The Technique

<Card title="Assembling and baking">
<Steps>
<Step title="Sauce first" status="done" subtitle="30 seconds">Spread tomato sauce in a spiral, leaving a 2-3cm border. Don't over-sauce — the crust should stay crisp.</Step>
<Step title="Cheese second" status="done" subtitle="30 seconds">Distribute torn mozzarella pieces evenly. Don't overload — Neapolitan pizza is about balance.</Step>
<Step title="Launch" status="current" subtitle="10 seconds">Use a floured peel. Shake gently to ensure it's not stuck, then launch with a quick forward-back motion. In a home oven, bake on the top rack.</Step>
<Step title="Watch & rotate" status="todo" subtitle="60-90 seconds">In a pizza oven, rotate every 20-30 seconds for even cooking. In a home oven, rotate once at the halfway point.</Step>
<Step title="Finish" status="todo" subtitle="10 seconds">Remove, add fresh basil, drizzle with olive oil, and let rest 1 minute before slicing.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Common mistakes">
- **Over-hydrated dough**: Too sticky, won't stretch properly. Stick to 60-65% hydration.
- **Cold dough**: Never stretch a cold ball — it will tear. Always bring to room temperature.
- **Too much sauce**: Makes the center soggy. Less is more.
- **Slicing too soon**: Let it rest so the cheese sets. Use a serrated knife or pizza cutter.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

<Details title="Crust is too dense or bread-like">
Your dough is likely over-proofed or under-hydrated. Check your fermentation time — in a warm kitchen, 8 hours may be too long. Also ensure your flour has enough protein (00 flour from brands like Caputo is ideal).
</Details>

<Details title="Crust burns but center is raw">
Your oven is too hot or the pizza is too close to the heat source. In a pizza oven, lower the flame or raise the pizza. In a home oven, move the rack down slightly.
</Details>

<Details title="Dough is too sticky to stretch">
Your hydration is too high for your flour, or the dough hasn't rested enough. Add a little flour to your hands and work surface, but don't knead extra flour into the dough itself. Let it rest 30 more minutes.
</Details>

<Details title="Pizza sticks to the peel">
Use semolina flour or coarse cornmeal on the peel, not regular flour which absorbs moisture. Also, don't assemble the pizza too far in advance — launch within 1-2 minutes of assembly.
</Details>

## Practice Makes Perfect

The first few attempts won't be perfect — that's normal. Keep a notebook, track your hydration, fermentation time, and oven temperature. Adjust one variable at a time.

<Callout tone="success" title="The journey">The difference between a good pizza and a great one is often just 24 hours of extra fermentation, or 20°C more oven temperature. Keep refining, and you'll get there.</Callout>
