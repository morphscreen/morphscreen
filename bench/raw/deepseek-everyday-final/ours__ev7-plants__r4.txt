# Houseplants That Are Hard to Kill

Looking for greenery that survives neglect, low light, and busy schedules? These are the most forgiving houseplants—perfect for beginners or anyone without a green thumb.

<Callout tone="info" title="The golden rule">
Even "unkillable" plants need *some* care: water when the soil is dry, avoid direct afternoon sun, and don't leave them sitting in standing water.
</Callout>

<Card title="The Top 7 Nearly Indestructible Plants">
<Columns>
<div>
#### 1. Snake Plant (*Sansevieria*)
- Tolerates weeks without water
- Thrives in low light or bright light
- Purifies air
</div>
<div>
#### 2. Pothos (*Epipremnum aureum*)
- Grows in almost any light
- Droops when thirsty, bounces back quickly
- Easy to propagate in water
</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>
#### 3. ZZ Plant (*Zamioculcas zamiifolia*)
- Stores water in thick rhizomes
- Survives low light and irregular watering
- Shiny, waxy leaves
</div>
<div>
#### 4. Spider Plant (*Chlorophytum comosum*)
- Produces baby plantlets
- Tolerates occasional drought
- Great in hanging baskets
</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>
#### 5. Aloe Vera
- Needs very little water
- Sun-loving but forgiving
- Useful gel for minor burns
</div>
<div>
#### 6. Cast Iron Plant (*Aspidistra elatior*)
- Lives up to its name
- Handles dark corners, dust, and neglect
- Slow-growing but nearly immortal
</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>
#### 7. Peace Lily (*Spathiphyllum*)
- Dramatic droop when thirsty—then perks up in hours
- Blooms white flowers in low light
- Tolerates missed waterings
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="How to Keep Them Alive (Even When You Forget)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Choose the right pot" status="done">Always use a pot with drainage holes. Add a saucer to catch excess water.</Step>
<Step title="Water only when dry" status="done">Stick your finger an inch into the soil. If it's dry, water. If it's damp, wait.</Step>
<Step title="Pick a spot with indirect light" status="done">Most of these plants prefer bright, indirect light but tolerate lower light. Avoid scorching south-facing windows.</Step>
<Step title="Feed sparingly" status="done">Fertilize once a month in spring and summer, skip in winter. More harm comes from overfeeding than underfeeding.</Step>
<Step title="Dust the leaves" status="done">Wipe leaves with a damp cloth every few weeks so they can photosynthesize.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes That Kill Even Tough Plants">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Overwatering is the #1 killer">
Most "unkillable" plants die from too much love. Root rot from soggy soil is the most common cause of death.
</Callout>

| Mistake | Why it's fatal | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Watering on a fixed schedule | Soil dries at different rates | Check soil moisture first |
| No drainage hole | Roots sit in water | Repot into a pot with holes |
| Direct hot sun | Leaves scorch and burn | Move to bright indirect light |
| Cold drafts | Tropical plants hate cold air | Keep away from AC vents and windows in winter |
| Giant pot | Excess soil holds water | Size up only 1–2 inches at a time |
</Card>

<Card title="Signs Your Plant Is Telling You Something">
<Details title="Drooping leaves" open="false">
Usually means thirsty—water and it should perk up within hours. If the soil is already wet, it's overwatered instead.
</Details>
<Details title="Yellow leaves">
Most often overwatering. Let the soil dry out fully before the next watering. Cut off yellowed leaves.
</Details>
<Details title="Brown crispy tips">
Underwatering, low humidity, or excess fertilizer. Trim the brown tips and adjust.
</Details>
<Details title="Leggy, stretched growth">
Not enough light. Move closer to a window, but not into direct sun.
</Details>
<Details title="Pale, washed-out leaves">
Too much direct sun. Move to a brighter but indirect spot.
</Details>
</Card>
