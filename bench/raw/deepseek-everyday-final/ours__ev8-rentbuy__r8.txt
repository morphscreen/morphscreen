# Should I rent or buy a flat?

Deciding between renting and buying depends on your finances, lifestyle, and how long you plan to stay put. Let's break it down for your situation.

<Card title="What you can likely afford to buy">
With a £60k salary and no other debts, most lenders will offer around **4.5× your income** — roughly **£270,000** — with a 10–20% deposit. Here's a realistic picture:

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Property price | £270,000 |
| Deposit (10%) | £27,000 |
| Mortgage (90%) | £243,000 |
| Monthly mortgage (~4.5% over 30 yrs) | ~£1,230 |
| Service charge / ground rent (if leasehold) | £150–£300 |
| Buildings insurance | ~£15 |
| **Total monthly cost** | **~£1,400–£1,550** |

That's **£250–£400 less** than your current rent — but you'll also have **one-off costs** like stamp duty, legal fees, and surveys.

<Callout tone="info" title="Stamp duty for first-time buyers">
If this is your first home and it costs under £425,000, you pay **no stamp duty** on the first £425,000. So at £270k, that's £0.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The big question: how long will you stay?">
<Steps>
<Step title="Under 3 years" status="done" subtitle="Renting usually wins">
Buying costs ~3–5% of the price in fees and taxes upfront, and you won't build enough equity to cover those in a short time. Renting keeps you flexible.
</Step>
<Step title="3–5 years" status="todo" subtitle="It's close">
If prices rise modestly (2–4%/yr), buying can break even or slightly beat renting. But if they drop, you could lose out.
</Step>
<Step title="5+ years" status="todo" subtitle="Buying usually wins">
Over longer horizons, owning typically builds wealth through equity and price growth, and your monthly cost stays roughly fixed (with a fixed-rate mortgage) while rents rise.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Other things to consider">
<Columns>
<Card title="Renting keeps you flexible">
- Move for a new job or relationship easily
- No repair bills — a broken boiler is your landlord's problem
- No risk if house prices fall
- You can invest the deposit elsewhere
</Card>
<Card title="Buying gives you control">
- No more rent increases or eviction notices
- Decorate, renovate, get a pet
- Build equity — part of your payment is forced savings
- Potential price growth over time
</Card>
</Card>

<Card title="A quick rule of thumb">
A common guideline is the **price-to-rent ratio**:

<Math tex="\text{Price-to-rent} = \frac{\text{Property price}}{\text{Annual rent}}" label="Price-to-rent ratio" />

If the ratio is **under 15**, buying is usually better financially.  
If it's **over 20**, renting is often smarter.

For you: £270,000 ÷ (£1,800 × 12) = **12.5** — that leans toward buying, **if** you're ready to stay put.

</Card>

<Card title="What to do next">
1. **Check your deposit savings** — do you have £30k+ ready?
2. **Get a mortgage agreement in principle** — free and no obligation
3. **Estimate your monthly budget** with a mortgage calculator
4. **Think about your 5-year plan** — job stability, relationship, family plans
5. **Consider the hidden costs of owning** — repairs, service charges, insurance

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't forget">
Renting is not "throwing money away" — it buys you flexibility and certainty. Buying is not always "the best investment" — it's a leveraged bet on property prices. The right answer depends on your life plans, not just the maths.
</Callout>
</Card>
