# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making an authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is a rewarding challenge. The key is understanding that it's defined by three things: a high-hydration dough with a long fermentation, a blistered and leopard-spotted crust, and a simple topping of San Marzano tomatoes and fresh mozzarella.

<Card title="The Neapolitan Rules" subtitle="What makes it authentic">
<KeyValue title="The Spec">
| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Dough | Type 00 flour, 55–65% hydration, 24–48h fermentation |
| Oven temperature | 430–480°C (800–900°F) — wood-fired ideally |
| Bake time | 60–90 seconds |
| Crust | Puffy, charred edges (leopard spots), soft and chewy |
| Toppings | San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte mozzarella, fresh basil, EVOO |
| Size | 28–35 cm (11–14 in) diameter |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The biggest hurdle">
Home ovens max out at ~250°C (480°F). To get close to Neapolitan results, you'll need a pizza steel or stone and the **broiler on high** for the last minute. It's not perfect, but it's very good.
</Callout>

<Card title="The Dough" subtitle="The heart of it all">
This makes 4 pizzas. Start 24–48 hours before you plan to bake.

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix the dough" status="done">
In a bowl, combine **500g tipo 00 flour** (or bread flour), **325g cold water** (65% hydration), **10g fine sea salt**, and **2g fresh yeast** (or 1g dry yeast). Mix until shaggy, then knead for 10–15 minutes until smooth and elastic.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done">
Cover and let it rise at room temperature for 8–12 hours, until doubled. Then divide into 4 balls of ~210g each.
</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment" status="done">
Place the balls on a floured tray, cover tightly, and refrigerate for 24–48 hours. This develops flavor and digestibility.
</Step>
<Step title="Bring to room temp" status="done">
Take the dough out 2–3 hours before baking. They should be soft, pillowy, and easy to stretch.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="The Toppings" subtitle="Simple, but specific">
<Columns>
<Card title="Sauce">
- **1 can (400g) San Marzano tomatoes** (DOP if possible)
- Crush by hand, add a pinch of salt
- Do **not** cook it — it cooks in the oven
- Spread thinly, leaving a 2–3 cm border
</Card>
<Card title="Cheese">
- **125g fior di latte mozzarella** per pizza
- Tear into small pieces and drain well
- Place on the sauce after it's spread
- Add 2–3 fresh basil leaves
- Finish with a drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Shaping and Baking" subtitle="The technique">
<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat your steel" status="done">
Place a pizza steel or stone on the **top rack** of your oven. Preheat at maximum (250°C / 480°F) for at least 45 minutes. Switch to **broil** for the last 10 minutes.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch by hand" status="done">
Flour your hands. Press the dough ball flat, then stretch from the center outward, leaving the rim thicker. Don't use a rolling pin — it knocks out the air.
</Step>
<Step title="Top quickly" status="done">
Sauce, cheese, basil, oil. Work fast — the dough shouldn't sit wet for long.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="done">
Launch the pizza onto the steel. Bake for 2–3 minutes, then switch to broil for 1–2 minutes until the crust is charred and the cheese bubbles. Rotate once if needed.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Watch the crust">
The signature leopard spots come from the dough blistering against intense heat. If your crust is pale, your oven isn't hot enough — try the broiler earlier or a thicker steel.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes" subtitle="And how to avoid them">
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Dough too wet to handle | Use less water (60% hydration) or flour your hands more |
| Soggy center | Drain the mozzarella well, use less sauce |
| Crust too thick | Stretch the edges thinner, but keep them puffy |
| Pizza sticks to peel | Use semolina flour on the peel, not just flour |
| Oven not hot enough | Preheat longer, use a steel (not a stone), try the broiler |
</Card>

<Card title="Tools You'll Need">
| Tool | Why |
|---|---|
| Pizza steel (6mm+ thick) | Retains enough heat for charring |
| Wood or metal peel | To launch and retrieve the pizza |
| Digital scale | Accuracy matters in dough making |
| Kitchen thermometer | Check your oven's real temperature |
| Tipo 00 flour | Finer grind, higher gluten for stretch |
</Card>
