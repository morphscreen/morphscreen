# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

Vancouver is surrounded by mountains, forests, and ocean — some of the world's best hiking is within an hour of downtown. Here are the top day hikes, from easy coastal strolls to challenging summit pushes.

<Tab title="Stawamus Chief">
## Stawamus Chief — the classic summit

The granite monolith rising over Squamish, 45 minutes north of Vancouver, offers three peaks with panoramic views of Howe Sound and the Tantalus Range. **Peak 1 is the most popular** and the shortest; Peaks 2 and 3 require more scrambling.

<Gallery>
![Stawamus Chief granite face seen from Squamish below](find)
![View of Howe Sound from the Chief's first peak](find)
![Hikers on the chain section near the summit of the Chief](find)
</Gallery>

<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Trailhead | Stawamus Chief Provincial Park, Squamish |
|---|---|
| Distance | 11 km round trip (Peak 1) |
| Elevation gain | 700 m |
| Time | 5–6 hours |
| Difficulty | Hard |
| Permits | None |
| Facilities | Washrooms, picnic area at base |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Not for the faint of heart">The trail includes steep staircases, exposed sections, and a short chain-assisted scramble near the top. Bring gloves for the chains and avoid the summit in wet weather.</Callout>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Garibaldi Lake">
## Garibaldi Lake — turquoise alpine water

The jewel of Garibaldi Provincial Park, this hike rewards you with a stunning glacier-fed lake in a volcanic basin. The trail climbs steadily through old-growth forest before opening up to the lake's impossibly blue water.

<Gallery>
![Garibaldi Lake's turquoise water with snow-capped peaks behind](find)
![The trail through old-growth cedar forest up to Garibaldi Lake](find)
![Panorama Ridge overlooking Garibaldi Lake from above](find)
</Gallery>

<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Trailhead | Rubble Creek Trailhead, 1 hr north of Vancouver |
|---|---|
| Distance | 18 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | 820 m |
| Time | 6–7 hours |
| Difficulty | Moderate–Hard |
| Permits | Day use free; camping requires a reservation |
| Facilities | Outhouses at the lake, tent platforms |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="info" title="Go further">For the full experience, continue to **Panorama Ridge** (an extra 6 km and 300 m gain) for the iconic view over the lake and Black Tusk. Or extend to **Taylor Meadows** for wildflower meadows in July.</Callout>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Quarry Rock">
## Quarry Rock — quick reward

The most popular hike in Deep Cove, this short but steep trail ends at a rocky viewpoint overlooking Indian Arm. Perfect for a half-day outing or a first hike with visitors.

<Gallery>
![Quarry Rock viewpoint overlooking Indian Arm and Deep Cove](find)
![The forested trail leading to Quarry Rock](find)
![Kayaks in Deep Cove harbour seen from the trail](find)
</Gallery>

<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Trailhead | Panorama Drive, Deep Cove (North Vancouver) |
|---|---|
| Distance | 3 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | 100 m |
| Time | 1.5–2 hours |
| Difficulty | Easy–Moderate |
| Permits | None |
| Facilities | Washrooms and cafés in Deep Cove |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Go early or avoid weekends">This trail draws huge crowds — the parking lot fills by 8 am on sunny weekends. Take the bus from downtown Vancouver or arrive before 7:30 am.</Callout>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Lynn Canyon">
## Lynn Canyon — suspension bridge and waterfalls

A lush rainforest canyon with a free suspension bridge (unlike the pricey Capilano), swimming holes, and easy trails along a rushing river. The Baden-Powell Trail passes through here for longer adventures.

<Gallery>
![Lynn Canyon suspension bridge over the turquoise river below](find)
![Twin Falls in Lynn Canyon during spring runoff](find)
![The mossy rainforest trail along Lynn Creek](find)
</Gallery>

<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Trailhead | Lynn Canyon Park, North Vancouver |
|---|---|
| Distance | 4 km round trip (loop) |
| Elevation gain | 100 m |
| Time | 1.5–2 hours |
| Difficulty | Easy |
| Permits | None (bridge is free) |
| Facilities | Café, washrooms, ecology centre |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="success" title="Family friendly">This is the best option with kids — the trails are wide, the bridge is thrilling, and there's a swimming pond at **30 Foot Pool** in summer.</Callout>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Bowen Lookout">
## Bowen Lookout — sunset views without the crowds

A quieter alternative on Cypress Mountain that still delivers big views. The trail climbs through forest before emerging at a rocky ledge overlooking Howe Sound and Bowen Island.

<Gallery>
![Bowen Lookout viewpoint over Howe Sound at sunset](find)
![The forested switchbacks on the Cypress Bowl trail](find)
![Bowen Island seen from the lookout across the sound](find)
</Gallery>

<KeyValue title="Trail details">
| Trailhead | Cypress Bowl ski area, West Vancouver |
|---|---|
| Distance | 10 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | 350 m |
| Time | 3–4 hours |
| Difficulty | Moderate |
| Permits | None |
| Facilities | Washrooms at the ski lodge |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="info" title="Best in the evening">The lookout faces west, making it one of the few Vancouver hikes where you can watch the sunset. Bring a headlamp for the walk back.</Callout>
</Tab>

<Card title="Practical tips">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check trail conditions" status="done">Check **BC Parks** and **Vancouver Trails** for closures, snow, and wildlife warnings before you go.</Step>
<Step title="Pack the essentials" status="done">Water (at least 1 L per person), snacks, layers, a rain shell, and a first-aid kit. Vancouver weather changes fast.</Step>
<Step title="Arrive early" status="done">Popular trailheads fill by 8–9 am on weekends. Take transit where possible — Deep Cove and Lynn Canyon are bus-accessible.</Step>
<Step title="Leave no trace" status="done">Pack out everything, stay on trails, and keep dogs on leash where required.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
