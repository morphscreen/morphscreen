# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is about mastering a few key elements: high-hydration dough, a blazing-hot oven, and quality ingredients. Here's the complete guide.

<Card title="Ingredients" subtitle="Sourcing matters">
<Columns>
<Card title="Dough (for 4 pizzas)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Tipo 00 flour (or bread flour) | 500g |
| Water (room temp) | 325g |
| Fine sea salt | 10g |
| Fresh yeast (or 1g dry yeast) | 2g |
</Card>
<Card title="Toppings (per pizza)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| San Marzano tomatoes | 80g |
| Fresh mozzarella (fior di latte) | 100g |
| Fresh basil | 3-4 leaves |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | drizzle |
| Fine salt | pinch |
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Make the dough" status="done" subtitle="12-48 hours before baking">
Mix flour and water, rest 20 minutes. Add salt and yeast, knead 10-15 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough should be soft and slightly sticky.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done" subtitle="Room temperature, 2-4 hours">
Cover and let rise until doubled. Then divide into 4 balls (about 200g each).
</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment" status="done" subtitle="Refrigerator, 24-48 hours">
Place balls in an oiled container, cover, refrigerate. This develops flavor and makes the dough more extensible.
</Step>
<Step title="Preheat your oven" status="current" subtitle="The critical step">
Neapolitan pizza needs extreme heat. Options, best to worst:
</Step>
<Step title="Shape and top" status="todo" subtitle="Quick hands">
Remove dough 1 hour before baking. Stretch by hand from the center, leaving a thicker rim. Top with crushed tomatoes, then mozzarella, basil, and oil.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake and serve" status="todo" subtitle="60-90 seconds">
Bake until leopard-spotted and puffed. Drizzle with oil, serve immediately.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Getting the Heat Right" subtitle="Without a wood-fired oven">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Home ovens max out at ~250°C">
You can't reach 430°C in a standard oven, but you can get close with these methods:
</Callout>

<Columns>
<Card title="Option 1: Oven + Pizza Steel">
- Place a pizza steel (or stone) on the highest rack
- Preheat at max temperature for 45-60 minutes
- Switch to broiler for 5-10 minutes just before baking
- Bake 4-6 minutes, rotating once
</Card>
<Card title="Option 2: Dedicated Pizza Oven">
- Outdoor gas or wood-fired ovens (e.g., Ooni, Roccbox) reach 400-500°C
- Bake in 60-90 seconds
- Best results without building a brick oven
</Card>
<Card title="Option 3: Cast Iron Skillet">
- Preheat skillet on high heat
- Finish under the broiler
- Good for a thicker, pan-style crust
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="The Technique" subtitle="Shaping and baking">
<Steps>
<Step title="Bring dough to room temp" status="done">
Take dough out 1 hour before baking. Keep it covered.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch, don't roll" status="done">
Press from the center outward, leaving the rim untouched. The dough should be thin in the middle, ~2-3mm, with a 1-2cm crust.
</Step>
<Step title="Top quickly" status="done">
Add tomatoes first, then cheese, then basil. Work fast so the dough doesn't stick.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="done">
Slide onto the hot steel/skillet. In a home oven, bake 4-6 minutes; in a pizza oven, 60-90 seconds. Look for leopard spotting and charred bubbles.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
| Mistake | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cold dough | Hard to stretch, tears | Rest 1 hour at room temp |
| Too much sauce | Soggy center | 2-3 tbsp max, spread thin |
| Wet mozzarella | Watery pizza | Pat dry before topping |
| Over-kneading | Tough crust | Stop when smooth and elastic |
| Rushing the rise | Bland flavor | Minimum 24h cold ferment |
</Card>

<Card title="The Right Equipment">
<Columns>
<Card title="Essential">
- Pizza steel or stone (1/4" steel is best)
- Wooden peel for launching
- Digital scale (precision matters)

</Card>
<Card title="Nice to have">

- Dough scraper
- Pizza cutter (not essential, Neapolitans eat with knife and fork)
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="info" title="The Verace Pizza Napoletana standard">
True Neapolitan pizza has a soft, elastic crust with a puffed, leopard-spotted rim. The center is thin and slightly wet, so it's traditionally eaten with a knife and fork. It's never cut into slices.
</Callout>

<Card title="Serving Suggestions">
- Serve immediately on a preheated plate
- Add a drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil before serving
- Pair with a light, crisp Italian beer or a chilled white wine
- Eat with a knife and fork, starting from the center
</Card>
