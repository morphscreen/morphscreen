# Rent or buy? A worked comparison for your situation

<Callout tone="info" title="The short answer">
At £60k a year with £1,800/month rent, buying is usually the better long-term financial move **if** you can afford the deposit and plan to stay put for 5+ years. But it's close — and it depends heavily on where you live, what you'd buy, and how long you'll stay.
</Callout>

## The detailed breakdown

<Columns>
<Card title="Cost of renting">
- £1,800/month × 12 = **£21,600/year**
- With 3% annual rent increases: ~**£114,600 over 5 years**
- You get nothing back — no equity, no asset
- Freedom to move with a month's notice
- No maintenance, no surprise bills
</Card>

<Card title="Cost of buying (typical £295k flat)">
- Deposit: **£25,000–£30,000**
- Mortgage on £265k at 4.5% (25 yrs): ~**£1,470/month**
- Service charge + ground rent: **£150–£300/month**
- Insurance, repairs, boiler cover: **~£100/month**
- Total monthly: **~£1,720–£1,870** — similar to rent
- But ~**£900/month** goes to paying down the mortgage (equity)
</Card>
</Columns>

## The 3 questions that decide it

<Tabs>
<Tab title="How long will you stay?">
<Callout tone="warning" title="The 5-year rule">
Buying costs £5,000–£10,000 upfront (stamp duty, solicitor, survey, moving). You need **at least 5 years** in the property to recoup those costs and beat renting.

- **Under 3 years** → rent
- **3–5 years** → borderline, depends on price growth
- **5+ years** → buy, almost certainly
</Callout>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Can you get the deposit?">
<Stat label="Typical 10% deposit" value="£29,500" note="for a £295k flat" icon="bank" />
<Stat label="Help to Buy / shared ownership" value="Yes" note="if first-time buyer" icon="home" tone="success" />

You'll need **£25k–£40k** saved. If you can save £1,000/month on top of rent, that's 2–3 years of saving. If you have family help or a LISA, it's faster.
</Tab>

In London, your £295k budget gets a studio or small 1-bed in Zone 4–5. Elsewhere, it buys a 2-bed with parking. Check local price-to-rent ratios: if buying costs less than 15× annual rent, buy. If more than 20×, rent and invest the difference.

## A decision framework

<Steps>
<Step title="Save the deposit" status="done" subtitle="You likely have £10k–£20k already">
If not, aim for 10% of your target price. A LISA gives you a free £1,000/year toward it.
</Step>
<Step title="Check you can afford the monthly cost" status="current" subtitle="Mortgage + service charge + bills ≤ your current rent + £200">
Lenders typically allow 4–4.5× income. At £60k, that's **£240k–£270k** mortgage — plus your deposit.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide: stay put or buy" status="todo" subtitle="If you'll stay 5+ years and can afford it — buy">
Run the numbers for your specific area. If prices are flat or falling, renting and investing the difference can win.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The verdict

<Card title="My recommendation for you">
<Callout tone="success" title="Buy — if you can answer yes to all three">
1. **You'll stay 5+ years** (job stable, no plans to move cities)  
2. **You have or can save a £25k+ deposit** within 2 years  
3. **Your target area has flats within £295k** that you'd genuinely live in

If any of those is "no", keep renting but **invest the gap** — put the difference between rent and a mortgage into an ISA. That way you're still building wealth.
