# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A balanced itinerary mixing iconic sights, local neighborhoods, great food, and a little romance.

<Card title="Trip at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Essentials">
| | |
|---|---|
| Best time to go | March–May or October–November |
| Suggested budget (per person) | ¥80,000–¥120,000 (≈ $550–$800) excluding flights |
| Getting around | Suica/PASMO card (IC) + Tokyo Metro & JR lines |
| Where to stay | Shinjuku, Shibuya, or Ginza for convenience |
| Language | Japanese; English signage is common in central areas |
| Currency | Japanese Yen (¥), cash still widely used |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Day-by-day plan">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Day 1 – Arrival & Shinjuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Arrive & settle in">
Check in to your hotel in Shinjuku or nearby. Drop bags, grab a coffee, and get your IC card at the airport.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Shinjuku Gyoen Garden">
A peaceful stroll through this large park — perfect to shake off jet lag. Entry ¥500 per person.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho">
Dinner in Golden Gai's tiny bars or Omoide Yokocho's alley of yakitori stalls. Try a *toro* skewer and a highball.
</Step>
</Steps>
<Callout tone="info" title="Jet lag tip">Stay awake until at least 9 PM local time to reset your body clock faster.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 2 – Shibuya & Harajuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Meiji Shrine">
Enter through the torii gate and walk the forested path to this serene Shinto shrine. Free entry.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday" status="done" subtitle="Harajuku & Takeshita Street">
Explore quirky fashion, crepes, and vintage shops. For a calmer vibe, walk down Omotesando's tree-lined avenue.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Shibuya Crossing & Hachiko">
See the famous scramble crossing from the Starbucks or Shibuya Sky viewpoint (book ahead). Meet Hachiko statue nearby.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Dinner in Shibuya">
Try *izakaya* hopping in Nonbei Yokocho or a *shabu-shabu* dinner. End with a night view from Miyashita Park rooftop.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 3 – Asakusa & Sumida River">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Senso-ji Temple">
Arrive early (before 9 AM) to beat crowds. Walk through Kaminarimon gate, the Nakamise shopping street, and the main hall.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning" status="done" subtitle="Nakamise Street snacks">
Try *ningyo-yaki* (small filled cakes) and *senbei* (rice crackers) from the stalls.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Sumida River cruise">
Take a water bus from Asakusa to Hamarikyu Gardens (about 35 min). Stroll the garden and have matcha at the teahouse.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Tokyo Skytree or Rooftop bar">
Optional: ascend the Skytree for sunset views, or pick a riverside bar in Asakusa for a calmer evening.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 4 – Day trip: Nikko or Kamakura">
<Steps>
<Step title="Choose one" status="done" subtitle="Nikko (nature + temples)">
Take the Tobu train (~2h). Visit Toshogu Shrine, Kegon Falls, and Lake Chuzenji. A full-day excursion.
</Step>
<Step title="Or" status="done" subtitle="Kamakura (coast + temples)">
A 1-hour ride from Tokyo. See the Great Buddha, Hase-dera, and walk the Daibutsu hiking trail to Kencho-ji. End at Yuigahama beach.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Return to Tokyo">
Come back in the evening and have a casual dinner near your hotel — ramen or *katsu curry* are easy wins.
</Step>
</Steps>
<Callout tone="neutral" title="Which to pick?">Nikko is more dramatic and further; Kamakura is closer and easier. Both are doable in one day.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 5 – Ginza & Departure">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Tsukiji Outer Market">
Sample fresh sushi, tamago (egg omelette), and grilled seafood at the market stalls. Open from ~5 AM, best before 10 AM.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday" status="done" subtitle="Ginza shopping & art">
Browse flagship stores, visit the Ginza Six rooftop garden, or catch an exhibition at the Artizon Museum.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Last-minute souvenirs">
Pick up stationery, tea, or cosmetics at Don Quijote or Loft. Head to the airport (allow 1.5–2h for the trip).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="Shinjuku – Best for nightlife & convenience">
- **Pros:** Major transport hub, endless dining, Golden Gai nearby
- **Cons:** Can be overwhelming, rooms often small
- **Budget:** ¥15,000–¥30,000/night

- **Cons:** Busy streets, pricier hotels
- **Budget:** ¥18,000–¥35,000/night
</Card>
<Card title="Ginza – Upscale & calm">
- **Pros:** Quiet evenings, luxury hotels, near Tsukiji
- **Cons:** Less nightlife, fewer budget options
- **Budget:** ¥25,000–¥50,000/night
</Card>
</Columns>

<Card title="Practical tips">
<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
- Buy a **Suica or PASMO** card at the airport or any station (¥500 deposit, refundable).
- The **Tokyo Metro 24/48/72-hour ticket** is great value if you stay central.
- Taxis are expensive; use trains and walking.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Etiquette">
- Remove shoes when entering temples, traditional restaurants, or homes.
- Don't eat while walking; stand near the stall instead.
- Tipping is not customary — it can cause confusion.
- Keep your voice low on trains.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="success" title="Money">
- Cash is still king in smaller shops and markets; carry ¥10,000–¥20,000.
- Credit cards work in most hotels, chain restaurants, and department stores.
- ATMs at 7-Eleven and Japan Post accept foreign cards.
</Callout>
</Card>
