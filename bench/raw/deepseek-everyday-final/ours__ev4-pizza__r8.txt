# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making an authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is achievable with the right technique, ingredients, and a bit of patience. The key is a high-hydration dough, a blazing-hot oven, and top-quality toppings.

<Callout tone="info" title="The Neapolitan Standard">
True Neapolitan pizza (VPN) is governed by strict rules: it must be cooked in a wood-fired oven at 485°C (905°F) for 60-90 seconds, use only 00 flour, San Marzano tomatoes, and fresh mozzarella. At home, we adapt the heat but keep the spirit.
</Callout>

## The Essential Ingredients

<Card title="What You'll Need">
<Columns>
<Card title="For the Dough (2 pizzas)">
- 500g (17.6 oz) "00" flour (Caputo Pizzeria is ideal)
- 325ml (11 oz) warm water (65% hydration)
- 10g (0.35 oz) fine sea salt
- 2g (0.07 oz) fresh yeast (or 0.5g dry yeast)
</Card>
<Card title="For the Toppings">
- 200g (7 oz) San Marzano tomatoes (whole, crushed by hand)
- 250g (8.8 oz) fresh mozzarella (fior di latte), torn
- Fresh basil leaves
- Extra-virgin olive oil
- Fine sea salt
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## The Method

<Steps>
<Step title="Make the dough" status="done" subtitle="Day before: 15 minutes">
In a large bowl, dissolve the yeast in the warm water. Add the flour and mix until shaggy. Add salt and knead by hand for 10-15 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough should be soft and slightly sticky.
</Step>
<Step title="First rise" status="done" subtitle="18-24 hours in the fridge">
Cover the bowl with plastic wrap and refrigerate for 18-24 hours. This slow fermentation develops flavor and makes the dough easier to stretch.
</Step>
<Step title="Shape the balls" status="done" subtitle="2 hours before baking">
Turn the dough onto a floured surface, divide into 2 pieces (about 420g each), and shape each into a tight ball. Place on a floured tray, cover, and let rise at room temperature for 2 hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Preheat your oven" status="current" subtitle="At least 1 hour before baking">
You need maximum heat. A pizza steel or cast-iron skillet in a home oven at 260°C (500°F) for 1 hour works, but a **portable pizza oven** (like an Ooni or Roccbox) that reaches 400-500°C (750-930°F) is far better. Place the pizza steel on the highest rack.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch, top, and bake" status="todo">
Flatten a ball with your fingertips, then stretch from the center outward, keeping the edges thicker for the crust. The dough should be thin in the middle. Top with crushed tomatoes, a pinch of salt, torn mozzarella, and a drizzle of oil. Launch onto the hot steel and bake for 90 seconds at 500°C, or 4-5 minutes at 260°C. Rotate once for even browning.
</Step>
<Step title="Finish and serve" status="todo">
Remove when the crust is puffed with leopard spots and the cheese is molten. Add fresh basil, a final drizzle of olive oil, and serve immediately — no cutting with a knife, just fold and eat.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Common Mistakes">
- **Too much flour when stretching** — makes the crust tough. Use a little semolina instead.
- **Over-topping** — less is more. A Neapolitan pizza should be saucy in the center, not loaded.
- **Cold dough** — always let balls come to room temperature before stretching, or they'll spring back.
- **Not hot enough** — if your oven only reaches 220°C, you'll get a bread-like crust, not a Neapolitan one.
</Callout>

If you're just starting, a **cast-iron skillet** is the cheapest way to get a decent crust. Preheat it on the stovetop until smoking, then transfer to the oven for the final bake. It won't be perfect, but it's a great first step.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

<Details title="My crust is pale and doughy">
Your oven wasn't hot enough. Wait longer for the steel to preheat (at least 45 minutes), and consider using the broiler for the last minute to brown the top.
</Details>

<Details title="The dough tears when I stretch it">
The dough is either too cold or not developed enough. Let it warm for another hour, and be gentler — use your fingertips, not a rolling pin, and let gravity help stretch it.
</Details>

<Details title="The center is soggy">
You used too much sauce or cheese. Use only 2-3 tablespoons of crushed tomatoes per pizza, and drain the mozzarella well before tearing.
</Details>

<Details title="I don't have 00 flour">
Bread flour (12-13% protein) works, but use slightly less water (60% hydration). All-purpose flour will make a flatter, less chewy crust.
</Details>
