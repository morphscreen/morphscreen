# Rent or buy a flat?

*Your situation: £60k salary, £1,800/month rent*

<Callout tone="info" title="The short answer">
At your income and rent level, buying is likely to be financially better in the long run **if** you can afford the deposit and plan to stay put for at least 5–7 years. But it depends on where you live, how much deposit you have, and how much you value flexibility.
</Callout>

<Card title="The 5-year rule">
<Steps>
<Step title="Under 2 years" status="todo">Buying almost never wins — transaction costs eat any gains.</Step>
<Step title="2–5 years" status="todo">It's a close call. You might break even, but renting is safer if you might move.</Step>
<Step title="5–7 years" status="current">This is the tipping point. Buying usually starts to beat renting.</Step>
<Step title="7+ years" status="todo">Buying is strongly favoured in most UK markets.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="What about the deposit?">

<Callout tone="danger" title="The biggest blocker">
With a £30,000 deposit (10% of £300k), you're at the edge of what's possible. If you have less than £25k saved, buying is likely off the table for now.
</Callout>

| Your deposit | Max property | Monthly mortgage (5%) | vs £1,800 rent |
|---|---|---|---|
| £15,000 (5%) | £285,000 | £1,660 | −£140 |
| £30,000 (10%) | £300,000 | £1,580 | −£220 |
| £45,000 (15%) | £315,000 | £1,500 | −£300 |
| £60,000 (20%) | £330,000 | £1,420 | −£380 |

<Callout tone="success" title="Deposit boosters to consider">
- **Lifetime ISA**: Save up to £4,000/yr, get a 25% government bonus (up to £1,000/yr)
- **Help to Buy / shared ownership** in some areas
- **First-time buyer stamp duty relief**: No stamp duty up to £425k
- **Gifted deposit** from family
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="A practical decision framework">

<Checklist title="Ask yourself these questions" name="buy-vs-rent">
- [ ] Do I plan to stay in the same city for 5+ years?
- [ ] Do I have at least £30k saved for a deposit and costs?
- [ ] Is my job stable (or will it be easy to find another locally)?
- [ ] Am I comfortable with £1,500–£1,600/month housing costs?
- [ ] Do I want the freedom to decorate, own pets, and not deal with landlords?
- [ ] Could I afford the mortgage if interest rates rise to 7%?
- [ ] Am I prepared to handle repairs (boiler breakdowns, leaks, etc.)?
</Checklist>

<Callout tone="success" title="If you answered 'yes' to 5 or more">
Buying is likely the right move for you. Start saving aggressively and look at properties in your price range.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="If you answered 'no' to 3 or more">
Renting is probably wiser right now. Focus on building your deposit and career, and revisit buying in 1–2 years.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Next steps if you lean towards buying">

<Step title="Save the deposit" status="current">Aim for 10–15% of the property price plus £5k for fees.</Step>

<Card title="Final verdict">

<Callout tone="info" title="My recommendation for you">
Given your £60k income and £1,800 rent, **renting is costing you 36% of your gross income** — that's high. If you can afford a 10% deposit and plan to stay put for 5+ years, **buying a flat up to £300k would save you money from month one** and build wealth over time.

If you're in London where £300k buys very little, **renting and investing the difference** is a legitimate strategy — just make sure you're actually investing that £220/month, not spending it.

Either way, your rent is too high relative to your income. Something should change: either buy, negotiate your rent, or move somewhere cheaper.
</Callout>
