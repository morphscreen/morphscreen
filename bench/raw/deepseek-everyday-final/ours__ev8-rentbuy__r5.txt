## The short answer

There's no single "right" choice — it depends on how long you'll stay, what you can afford as a deposit, and how much you value flexibility. Here's a framework to decide.

<Card title="Renting vs buying — your key trade-offs">
<Compare title="Rent vs buy">
| Factor | Rent | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | £1,800 fixed | Mortgage + bills + maintenance (often £1,500–£2,300) |
| Upfront cost | Deposit (usually 1 month) | Deposit (5–20% + fees) |
| Flexibility | Move with 1–2 months' notice | Selling takes months; costs ~2–5% of value |
| Building wealth | No — money goes to landlord | Yes — you build equity over time |
| Maintenance | Landlord's problem | Yours (budget ~1% of value/year) |
| Stability | Rent can rise; you can be asked to leave | Fixed mortgage; you decide |
</Compare>
</Card>

## The 5-year rule

<Callout tone="success" title="Rule of thumb">
If you're likely to stay in the same place **5+ years**, buying usually wins financially. If you might move sooner, renting often makes more sense — because buying and selling costs eat your gains.
</Callout>

<Card title="When buying beats renting">
**You're likely to win if:**
- You plan to stay 5+ years
- You have a stable job and income
- You want to put down roots (decorate, renovate, pets)
- Property prices in your area are rising

**Renting wins if:**
- You might move for work or a partner
- You value the freedom to leave quickly
- You don't have a deposit saved yet
- You'd rather invest your savings elsewhere (stocks, etc.)
</Card>

## What to do next

<Steps>
<Step title="Check your savings" status="current">
Do you have at least £25,000–£30,000 for a deposit plus fees? If not, that's your first goal.
</Step>
<Step title="Estimate your timeline" status="todo">
Are you likely to stay in your current city/area for 5+ years? Check job stability and personal plans.
</Step>
<Step title="Get a mortgage in principle" status="todo">
This is free and doesn't commit you — it shows what you could borrow and helps you shop realistically.
</Step>
<Step title="Compare properties" status="todo">
Look at flats in your area. Compare the mortgage cost of similar places to your £1,800 rent — including service charges.
</Step>
</Steps>

## A final note

<Callout tone="info" title="The emotional side matters too">
Money isn't everything. Owning gives you security and freedom to make a place yours. Renting gives you flexibility and less stress. Both are legitimate — the right answer is the one that fits your life, not just your spreadsheet.
</Callout>
