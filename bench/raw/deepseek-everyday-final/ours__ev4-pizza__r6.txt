# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

<Callout tone="info" title="The gold standard">
A true Neapolitan pizza (pizza napoletana) has a puffy, leopard-spotted crust, a soft and elastic interior, and a simple topping of San Marzano tomatoes, fresh mozzarella, basil, and olive oil. It's cooked in a blazing-hot wood-fired oven for 60–90 seconds — but you can get surprisingly close at home.
</Callout>

<Card title="The essentials at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Dough hydration | 60–65% |
| Flour | Tipo 00 (Caputo Pizzeria or similar) |
| Yeast | Fresh or active dry, very small amount |
| Fermentation | 8–24 hours (cold ferment recommended) |
| Oven temperature | 430–500°C (800–900°F) — or as hot as your oven goes |
| Cook time | 60–90 seconds |
| Topping | San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte mozzarella, fresh basil, EVOO |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

## The dough — the heart of the pizza

<Card title="Ingredients for 4 pizzas (each ~250g)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Tipo 00 flour | 500 g |
| Water (room temp) | 300–325 g (60–65% hydration) |
| Fresh yeast (or 1g active dry) | 2–3 g (a pea-sized piece) |
| Fine sea salt | 10 g |

**Method:**

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done" subtitle="5 minutes">
Dissolve the yeast in the water, then add to the flour. Mix until shaggy, then add salt and knead until the dough is smooth and elastic — about 10 minutes by hand or 5 in a stand mixer.
</Step>
<Step title="First rise" status="done" subtitle="2 hours at room temp">
Cover the dough and let it rise until doubled. Then divide into 4 balls of ~250g each.
</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment" status="current" subtitle="8–24 hours in the fridge">
Place the balls in an oiled container, cover tightly, and refrigerate. This develops flavor and makes the dough easier to stretch. Remove 1–2 hours before baking.
</Step>
<Step title="Shape" status="todo" subtitle="The tricky part">
Flatten the ball with your fingers, then stretch from the center outward. Use your knuckles to gently stretch the edges, leaving a slightly thicker rim (the cornicione). Don't use a rolling pin — it knocks out the air.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Common mistakes">
- **Too much yeast** — a tiny amount is all you need for a slow, flavorful rise.
- **Over-flouring the work surface** — it makes the dough slide off the peel.
- **Stretching with a rolling pin** — you'll get a cracker, not a puffy Neapolitan crust.
</Callout>

## The sauce and toppings — simple and authentic

<Card title="Sauce & toppings">
**Sauce (for 4 pizzas):**

- 1 can (400g) **San Marzano tomatoes** (or good-quality whole peeled tomatoes)
- Pinch of fine sea salt
- Optional: a few fresh basil leaves, 1 tsp extra-virgin olive oil

Crush the tomatoes by hand or with a potato masher — do not blend. The sauce should be chunky and uncooked.

**Toppings per pizza:**

- 80–100 g **fior di latte mozzarella** (or buffalo mozzarella, well-drained and torn)
- 5–6 fresh basil leaves
- A drizzle of **extra-virgin olive oil**

<Callout tone="success" title="The order matters">
Sauce first, then mozzarella, then basil and oil. Don't overload — a Neapolitan pizza is about the crust, not the toppings.
</Callout>
</Card>

## Cooking — getting as close to 900°F as you can

<Card title="Oven options ranked">
<Compare title="How to cook it">
| Method | Max temp | Cook time | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wood-fired outdoor oven (Ooni, Gozney) | 500°C (930°F) | 60–90 sec | Closest to the real thing |
| Pizza steel in home oven | 260°C (500°F) | 4–6 min | Very good, slightly thicker crust |
| Cast iron skillet + broiler | 260°C (500°F) | 3–4 min | Good for a single pizza |
| Baking stone | 230°C (450°F) | 6–8 min | Acceptable, but not Neapolitan |
</Compare>

<Callout tone="info" title="For a home oven">
Preheat your oven with a **pizza steel** on the top rack at maximum temperature for at least 45 minutes. Switch to **broil** for the last 5 minutes. Slide the pizza onto the steel, bake for 2–3 minutes, then finish under the broiler for 1–2 minutes until the crust is charred in spots.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Baking tips">
- Use **semolina flour** (not regular flour) on your peel — it acts like ball bearings.
- Shake the peel gently before launching to make sure the pizza slides.
- Rotate the pizza once halfway through for even browning.
- Look for **leopard spotting** — dark charred blisters on the crust, not an even brown.
</Card>

<Card title="What a proper Neapolitan pizza looks like">
| Feature | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Crust (cornicione) | Puffy, 1–2 cm tall, leopard-spotted, slightly chewy |
| Center | Thin, soft, slightly saucy — folds easily |
| Sauce | Bright, tangy, not watery |
| Cheese | Melted but not browned, creamy |
| Overall | Eaten with a knife and fork or folded — never sliced into wedges |
</Card>

## Recipe card

<Card title="Full recipe summary">
<Steps>
<Step title="Make the dough" status="done" subtitle="Day before">
Mix, knead, bulk rise 2 hours, divide, cold ferment 8–24 hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Prep toppings" status="done" subtitle="30 min before baking">
Crush tomatoes, tear mozzarella, pick basil. Remove dough from fridge.
</Step>
<Step title="Preheat oven" status="done" subtitle="45 min before baking">
Pizza steel on top rack, max heat, then broil.
</Step>
<Step title="Shape and top" status="done" subtitle="Per pizza">
Stretch by hand, sauce, cheese, basil, oil.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="done" subtitle="2–6 min depending on oven">
Launch, rotate, look for leopard spots, serve immediately.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The secret">
The difference between good and great Neapolitan pizza is **fermentation time** and **oven temperature**. A 24-hour cold ferment gives you flavor and digestibility; a screaming-hot oven gives you that puffy, charred crust. Master those two, and you're 90% of the way there.
</Callout>
