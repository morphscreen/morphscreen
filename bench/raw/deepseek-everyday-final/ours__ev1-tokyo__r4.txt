# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A balanced itinerary mixing iconic sights, hidden gems, great food, and romantic moments. Designed for first-time visitors who want to experience both the classic and the contemporary.

<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
Pick up a **Suica or Pasmo IC card** at the airport — you'll tap it on trains, buses, and even at convenience stores. The Tokyo Metro and JR lines are efficient; Google Maps works well for routing.
</Callout>

## Day 1 — Arrival & Shinjuku Nights

<Steps>
<Step title="Land & settle in" status="done">Check into your hotel in Shinjuku, Shibuya, or Ginza (central is best). Drop bags and grab a quick lunch at a department store food hall.</Step>
<Step title="Golden Gai" status="done">Wander the tiny alleyways of Golden Gai — over 200 micro-bars squeezed into six narrow lanes. Great for a pre-dinner drink.</Step>
<Step title="Omoide Yokochō" status="done">Dinner at this atmospheric "Memory Lane" — yakitori skewers and beer under glowing lanterns.</Step>
<Step title="Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building" status="done">Free observation deck with panoramic night views of the city lights.</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Where to stay">
| Area | Vibe | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Shinjuku | Neon chaos, endless dining | Night owls, first-timers |
| Shibuya | Trendy, youthful energy | Shopping, cafes |
| Ginza | Upscale, calm | Luxury, easy airport access |
| Asakusa | Traditional, riverside | Culture lovers |
</Card>

## Day 2 — Imperial Palace, Asakusa & Sumida River

<Steps>
<Step title="Imperial Palace East Gardens" status="done">Start with a peaceful walk through the former castle grounds. Free entry; the moat and stone walls are stunning in the morning light.</Step>
<Step title="Senso-ji Temple" status="done">Tokyo's oldest temple in Asakusa. Walk through the thunder gate (Kaminarimon) and the shopping street Nakamise-dori leading to the main hall.</Step>
<Step title="Sumida River cruise" status="done">Take a short boat ride down the Sumida River — a different view of the city skyline and the Tokyo Skytree.</Step>
<Step title="Tokyo Skytree at sunset" status="done">Ride up to the observation deck for golden-hour views. Book tickets online in advance to skip lines.</Step>
</Steps>

## Day 3 — Shibuya, Harajuku & Meiji Shrine

<Steps>
<Step title="Meiji Shrine" status="done">A serene forest in the middle of the city. Walk the gravel path through towering trees to the main shrine.</Step>
<Step title="Takeshita Street" status="done">The heart of Harajuku's kawaii culture — crepes, quirky fashion, and photo booths.</Step>
<Step title="Omotesando" status="done">A tree-lined boulevard with flagship stores in stunning architecture. Great for window shopping.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Crossing" status="done">Watch the famous scramble crossing from the Starbucks or Shibuya Sky observation deck. At night it's pure energy.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner in Shibuya" status="done">Choose from a tiny ramen bar, an izakaya, or a sushi conveyor belt — all within walking distance.</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Shibuya Sky vs. Shibuya Crossing">
| | Shibuya Sky | Street Level |
|---|---|---|
| View | 360° rooftop panorama | Right in the action |
| Cost | ~¥2,000 | Free |
| Best time | Sunset | Anytime |
| Crowds | Book ahead | Always busy |
</Card>

## Day 4 — Day Trip: Hakone or Nikko

<Columns>
<Card title="Option A: Hakone (hot springs & Mt. Fuji)">
- ~90 min by train from Shinjuku
- **Hakone Open-Air Museum** — sculptures with mountain views
- **Lake Ashi cruise** with Fuji views on clear days
- **Owakudani** volcanic valley with black eggs
- **Onsen (hot spring)** soak — book a private bath for two
</Card>
<Card title="Option B: Nikko (temples & nature)">
- ~2 hours by train from Asakusa
- **Toshogu Shrine** — lavishly decorated UNESCO site
- **Kegon Falls** — 97m waterfall
- **Lake Chuzenji** — serene mountain lake
- Less crowded than Hakone; more walking
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Check the weather">
Mt. Fuji visibility is best in winter and early spring. If it's cloudy, Hakone loses much of its charm — Nikko is a better bet in any weather.
</Callout>

## Day 5 — TeamLab, Ginza & Departure

<Steps>
<Step title="teamLab Planets" status="done">A digital art museum where you walk through water and mirror rooms. Book tickets for the first entry slot (9:00 or 10:00) to avoid crowds. Allow 90 minutes.</Step>
<Step title="Ginza stroll" status="done">Tokyo's most elegant district — browse the flagship stores, including the 12-floor Uniqlo and the historic Kabukiza Theatre.</Step>
<Step title="Last lunch" status="done">Splurge on a sushi omakase or a kaiseki lunch in Ginza. Many top restaurants offer reasonable midday sets.</Step>
<Step title="Head to the airport" status="done">Narita Express (N'EX) or Keikyu Line to Haneda — allow 60–90 minutes depending on your airport.</Step>
</Steps>

## Practical tips

<Card title="Essentials">
| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| Suica/Pasmo | Get at airport; refundable deposit |
| Pocket Wi-Fi or eSIM | Essential for maps and translations |
| Cash | Many small shops and restaurants are cash-only |
| Shoes | You'll walk 15,000+ steps/day — wear comfortable ones |
| Reservations | Book teamLab, Skytree, and day-trip trains in advance |
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="One more thing">
For a special evening, book a **dinner cruise on the Sumida River** or a **private onsen with a city view** — both are memorable for couples.
</Callout>
