# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system contains eight planets orbiting the Sun, each unique in size, composition, and character. They fall into two main groups: the four rocky **inner planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the four **outer giants** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune).

## The Inner Rocky Planets

<Card title="Mercury">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="4,879 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="57.9M km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="59 Earth days" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="88 Earth days" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
The smallest planet, barely larger than Earth's Moon. Its surface is heavily cratered and scorched by the Sun — temperatures swing from -173°C at night to 427°C by day. It has no atmosphere to speak of and no moons.
</Card>

<Card title="Venus">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="12,104 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="108.2M km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="243 Earth days" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="225 Earth days" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
The hottest planet, wrapped in a thick CO₂ atmosphere with clouds of sulfuric acid. A runaway greenhouse effect keeps the surface at about 465°C — hot enough to melt lead. It spins backwards, so the Sun rises in the west.
</Card>

<Card title="Earth">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="12,742 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="149.6M km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="24 hours" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="365.25 days" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
Our home — the only known world with liquid water oceans and life. Its magnetic field and ozone layer shield it from solar radiation, and its single large moon stabilises the axis, giving us seasons.
</Card>

<Card title="Mars">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="6,779 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="227.9M km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="24.6 hours" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="687 Earth days" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
The Red Planet, coloured by iron oxide dust. It hosts the tallest volcano in the solar system (Olympus Mons, 22 km high) and a canyon system (Valles Marineris) that would stretch across the USA. Polar ice caps and dried riverbeds hint at a wetter past.
</Card>

## The Outer Giant Planets

<Card title="Jupiter">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="139,820 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="778.5M km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="9.9 hours" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="11.9 Earth years" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
The giant of the solar system — more than twice as massive as all other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. With 95 known moons, it's a mini solar system in its own right.
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="116,460 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="1.43B km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="10.7 hours" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="29.5 Earth years" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
Famous for its spectacular ring system made of ice and rock particles. Saturn is so light it would float in water. Its moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and liquid methane lakes — one of the most Earth-like places in the solar system.
</Card>

<Card title="Uranus">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="50,724 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="2.87B km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="17.2 hours" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="84 Earth years" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
An ice giant tipped on its side — its axis is tilted 98°, so it rolls around the Sun. Methane in its atmosphere gives it a pale cyan colour. It has faint rings and 28 known moons, and is the coldest planet, with temperatures near -224°C.
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Diameter" value="49,244 km" icon="ruler" />
<Stat label="Distance from Sun" value="4.50B km" icon="map-pin" />
<Stat label="Day length" value="16.1 hours" icon="clock" />
<Stat label="Year length" value="165 Earth years" icon="calendar" />
</Columns>
The most distant planet, discovered by mathematics before it was seen through a telescope. It has the fastest winds in the solar system, reaching 2,100 km/h. Its moon Triton orbits backwards and may be a captured Kuiper Belt object.
</Card>

## A Quick Tour

<Steps>
<Step title="Mercury" status="done" subtitle="Closest to the Sun">A scorched, cratered world with no atmosphere, whipping around the Sun in just 88 days.</Step>
<Step title="Venus" status="done" subtitle="Hottest planet">A toxic, cloudy pressure cooker with surface temperatures hot enough to melt lead.</Step>
<Step title="Earth" status="done" subtitle="Our home">The only known world with liquid water, a breathable atmosphere, and life.</Step>
<Step title="Mars" status="done" subtitle="The Red Planet">A cold desert world with polar caps, volcanoes, and evidence of ancient rivers.</Step>
<Step title="Jupiter" status="done" subtitle="The giant">A banded gas giant with the Great Red Spot and dozens of moons.</Step>
<Step title="Saturn" status="done" subtitle="Ringed beauty">A light gas giant with magnificent rings and the moon Titan.</Step>
<Step title="Uranus" status="done" subtitle="Tipped over">A pale cyan ice giant rolling on its side, the coldest planet.</Step>
<Step title="Neptune" status="done" subtitle="Farthest out">A deep blue world with supersonic winds, discovered by calculation.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Beyond the planets">
Pluto, once considered the ninth planet, is now classified as a **dwarf planet** along with Eris, Haumea, Makemake, and Ceres. These worlds orbit the Sun but haven't cleared their neighbourhoods of debris.
</Callout>

<Card title="Did you know?">
- **Venus spins backwards** — the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east.
- **Jupiter's Great Red Spot** has been shrinking but is still about 1.3 times Earth's width.
- **A day on Venus** is longer than its year.
- **Saturn's rings** are about 280,000 km wide but only about 10 metres thick in places.
- **Neptune was found by math** — astronomers predicted its position before anyone saw it.
</Card>
