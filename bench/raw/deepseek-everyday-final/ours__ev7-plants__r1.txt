# Which houseplants are hard to kill?

Some houseplants are nearly indestructible. They tolerate low light, missed waterings, and general neglect—perfect for beginners or busy people.

<Card title="Top 6 nearly unkillable houseplants">
<Columns>
<Card title="1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)">
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Once every 2–4 weeks; let soil dry completely
- **Why tough:** Stores water in thick leaves; survives weeks without water
- **Bonus:** Purifies air, releases oxygen at night
</Card>

<Card title="2. Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)">
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 1–2 weeks; droops when thirsty, perks back up after watering
- **Why tough:** Grows in almost any light; forgiving if you forget
- **Bonus:** Trailing vines look great on shelves
</Card>

<Card title="3. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas)">
- **Light:** Very low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 3–4 weeks; likes to dry out fully
- **Why tough:** Rhizomes store water; survives months of neglect
- **Bonus:** Glossy leaves add a modern look
</Card>

<Card title="4. Spider Plant (Chlorophytum)">
- **Light:** Bright indirect, tolerates some low light
- **Water:** Weekly; brown tips if too dry, but recovers
- **Why tough:** Grows fast, produces baby plants
- **Bonus:** Safe for pets
</Card>

<Card title="5. Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra)">
- **Light:** Very low to medium
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; drought tolerant
- **Why tough:** The name says it all—survives dark corners and neglect
- **Bonus:** One of the slowest-growing, most resilient plants
</Card>

<Card title="6. Jade Plant (Crassula ovata)">
- **Light:** Bright, some direct sun
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; soak and dry
- **Why tough:** Succulent leaves store water; thrives on neglect
- **Bonus:** Can live for decades and become a small tree
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="The golden rule">
Most of these plants die from **too much water**, not too little. When in doubt, wait another week. Always use a pot with drainage holes.
</Callout>

<Card title="Beginner tips">
- **Start with one plant** — Snake plant or Pothos are the easiest
- **Check soil before watering** — stick a finger 2 cm deep; if dry, water
- **Use well-draining soil** — mix cactus soil with regular potting mix
- **Don't repot too often** — these plants like being snug
- **Watch for signs** — yellow leaves = overwatered; wrinkled leaves = thirsty

Even if you forget about them for a month, these six will forgive you. They're the perfect first houseplants.
