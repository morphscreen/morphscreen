# Should I rent or buy a flat?

The short answer: **it depends on your goals, timeline, and where you live**. With a £60k salary and £1,800/month rent, you're in a strong position — but buying isn't automatically better. Let's break it down.

## The core trade-off

<Card title="Renting vs buying">
<Compare title="Renting vs buying">
| Factor | Renting | Buying |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Deposit usually 1–2 months' rent | Deposit typically 10–20% of price |
| Monthly cost | £1,800 (fixed for term) | Mortgage + insurance + maintenance |
| Flexibility | Move with 1–2 months' notice | Selling takes months, costs thousands |
| Building equity | No | Yes, but slowly at first |
| Maintenance | Landlord pays | You pay (budget 1% of value/year) |
| Price risk | None | Prices can fall |
| Long-term wealth | Limited | Historically strong |
</Compare>
</Card>

## What you can afford to buy

<Card title="Affordability check">
With a £60k salary, most lenders offer **4–4.5× your income**:

| Purchase price | Deposit (10%) | Mortgage | Est. monthly payment* |
|---|---|---|---|
| £250,000 | £25,000 | £225,000 | £1,320 |
| £300,000 | £30,000 | £270,000 | £1,580 |
| £350,000 | £35,000 | £270,000 (max) | £1,580 + £80k deposit |

*Assumes 5% fixed rate over 25 years. You'd also pay **stamp duty** (0% up to £250k, 5% above that up to £925k) and legal fees (~£2–3k).

<Callout tone="info" title="The hidden costs of buying">
Beyond the deposit, budget **£5,000–£15,000** for stamp duty, surveys, legal fees, and moving costs. Then add **~1% of the property value per year** for maintenance (£2,500–£3,500 on a £300k flat).
</Callout>
</Card>

## What matters most: your situation

<Card title="Questions to ask yourself">
<Checklist title="Your personal checklist" name="rent-vs-buy">

## The verdict

<Card title="Bottom line">
<Columns>
<Card title="Rent if…">
- You might move within 3–4 years
- You don't have a 10% deposit yet
- You value flexibility over building equity
- You'd rather invest your savings elsewhere (stocks, etc.)
</Card>
<Card title="Buy if…">
- You're settled for 5+ years
- You have £30k+ saved for a deposit
- You want stability and to build wealth long-term
- You're buying somewhere you'd be happy to live for a decade
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="neutral" title="A middle path">
Consider **renting while saving aggressively** for a bigger deposit. A larger deposit means a smaller mortgage, lower monthly payments, and better rates — which can tip the scales firmly in favour of buying when you're ready.
</Callout>
</Card>

## Next steps if you're leaning toward buying

<Steps>
<Step title="Save a deposit" status="done" subtitle="You'll need at least 10% — ideally 15–20%">
Start with £25–30k for a £250–300k flat. Use a Lifetime ISA (£4k/year, government adds 25%) if you're a first-time buyer.
</Step>
<Step title="Check your credit score" status="todo">
A good score (700+) gets you the best mortgage rates. Check for free with ClearScore or Experian.
</Step>
<Step title="Get an Agreement in Principle" status="todo">
A lender will confirm how much they'll lend you. This takes 15 minutes online and shows sellers you're serious.
</Step>
<Step title="Find a good mortgage broker" status="todo">
A whole-of-market broker can find rates you won't see on comparison sites. Expect to pay £300–£500 or get it free from some lenders.
</Step>
<Step title="Start flat-hunting" status="todo">
Look at areas where £250–300k buys a 1–2 bed flat. Factor in commute costs, council tax, and service charges (for leaseholds).
</Step>
</Steps>
