# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A balanced itinerary covering iconic sights, hidden gems, great food, and romantic moments — all connected by Tokyo's excellent transit system.

<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
Pick up a **Suica or Pasmo IC card** at the airport (¥500 refundable deposit). It works on all trains, subways, buses, and most convenience stores. The **Tokyo Metro 24/48/72-hour pass** (¥800/¥1,200/¥1,500) is great value if you'll ride the metro a lot.
</Callout>

<Card title="Day 1: Arrival & Shinjuku" subtitle="Neon nights and your first taste of Tokyo">
<Steps>
<Step title="Arrive at airport" status="done" subtitle="Narita or Haneda">
Take the **Narita Express (N'EX)** or **Keisei Skyliner** into the city (60–90 min). From Haneda, the **Keikyu Line** is fastest. Buy your IC cards here.
</Step>

<Step title="Check in & rest" status="done" subtitle="Hotel in Shinjuku or Shibuya">
Drop your bags, freshen up. If you landed early, take a short nap — you'll need energy for the evening.
</Step>

<Step title="Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho" status="current" subtitle="Tiny bars and yakitori alleys">
Start at **Omoide Yokocho** ("Memory Lane") for grilled skewers at a counter seat, then wander into **Golden Gai** — over 200 tiny bars packed into six alleys. Try a *shochu* highball or sake at a two-seat bar.
</Step>

<Step title="Dinner: Izakaya hopping" status="todo" subtitle="Shinjuku's food scene">
Hop between izakayas: try *monjayaki* (savory pancake), *takoyaki*, and fresh sashimi. End with *ramen* at **Fuunji** or **Ichiran** for a late-night bowl.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

---

<Card title="Day 2: Old Tokyo" subtitle="Asakusa, Sumida River, Akihabara & Ueno">
<Steps>
<Step title="Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise-dori" status="done" subtitle="Tokyo's oldest temple">
Arrive early (before 9 AM) to beat the crowds. Walk through the **Kaminarimon Gate**, down **Nakamise-dori** shopping street (try *ningyo-yaki* — little filled cakes), and up to the temple. Pull an **omikuji** (fortune) — if you get a bad one, tie it up and leave it behind.
</Step>

<Step title="Sumida River cruise" status="done" subtitle="To Hinode Pier">
Take a river bus from **Asakusa Pier** to **Hinode Pier** (35 min, ~¥1,200). Sit on the right side for views of the **Skytree** and **Rainbow Bridge**. From Hinode, ride the Yurikamome line to **Odaiba** for lunch.
</Step>

<Step title="Akihabara" status="current" subtitle="Electric Town">
Spend the afternoon in **Akihabara**: browse multi-floor electronics shops, anime stores, and retro game arcades. Visit **@home Cafe** or a *maid cafe* for a quirky experience. Don't miss the **Yodobashi Camera** megastore.
</Step>

<Step title="Ueno Park & Ameyoko" status="todo" subtitle="Museums & street food">
Walk or ride to **Ueno Park** — if you're lucky it'll be cherry blossom season. Visit the **Tokyo National Museum** (closed Mondays). End at **Ameyoko Market**, a lively street with food stalls and bargains.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

---

<Card title="Day 3: Imperial & Art" subtitle="Palace grounds, Ginza, digital art">
<Steps>
<Step title="Imperial Palace East Gardens" status="done" subtitle="Free, open 9 AM–4 PM">
Walk the **East Gardens** and see the **Nijubashi Bridge**. Join the free English tour of the inner grounds (9:50 or 13:50, ID required). The gardens are serene — a green heart in the city.
</Step>

<Step title="Ginza" status="done" subtitle="Luxury shopping & architecture">
Stroll **Chuo-dori** (closed to traffic on weekends). Visit **Itoya** stationery store (11 floors of paper art), **Uniqlo Ginza** (flagship), and the **Kabukiza Theatre** — even just for a photo. Lunch at a *sushi-ya* counter: try **Sushi Iwa** or **Kyubey** (mid-range).
</Step>

<Step title="TeamLab Planets" status="current" subtitle="Immersive digital art — book ahead">
Located at **Toyosu**, take the Yurikamome line. Walk barefoot through water and mirror rooms — the **Garden Area** is stunning at dusk. Book tickets 2–4 weeks ahead (¥3,200). Allow 1.5–2 hours.
</Step>

<Step title="Tokyo Tower at night" status="todo" subtitle="Iconic views">
Take the Hibiya Line to **Kamiyacho** and walk to **Tokyo Tower**. The **Main Deck** (¥1,200) is enough — go 30 min before sunset to see both day and night views. For free views, head to the **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building** in Shinjuku instead.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

---

<Card title="Day 4: Shibuya & Harajuku" subtitle="Youth culture, fashion, and the world's busiest crossing">
<Steps>
<Step title="Meiji Shrine" status="done" subtitle="A forest in the city">
Enter through the massive **torii gate** and walk the forested path (15 min). Watch a **Shinto wedding** if you're lucky. Write a wish on an *ema* plaque. Free, open sunrise–sunset.
</Step>

<Step title="Harajuku & Takeshita Street" status="done" subtitle="Kawaii culture">
Walk down **Takeshita Street** for crepes, quirky shops, and cosplay photo ops. Browse **Kiddy Land** (5 floors of toys). For vintage, explore **Cat Street** and **Ura-Harajuku** backstreets.
</Step>

<Step title="Shibuya Crossing & Hachiko" status="current" subtitle="The scramble">
Watch the crossing from the **Shibuya Sky** observation deck (book ahead, ¥2,000) or the **Starbucks** on the second floor of QFRONT. See the **Hachiko statue** — the faithful dog. Shop at **Shibuya 109** and **MEGA Don Quijote**.
</Step>

<Step title="Dinner in Shimokitazawa" status="todo" subtitle="Vintage & indie vibe">
Take the Odakyu Line (10 min) to **Shimokitazawa** ("Shimokita") — a bohemian neighborhood of vintage shops, record stores, and tiny eateries. Try **Thai food at Chao-Bang**, or *okonomiyaki* at a local spot. Finish with craft beer at **DevilCraft**.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

---

<Card title="Day 5: Choose your adventure" subtitle="Day trip or stay in the city">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Option A: Nikko day trip">
<Steps>
<Step title="Early train to Nikko" status="done" subtitle="2h from Tokyo">
Take the **Tobu Limited Express** (¥2,860 round trip, reserve ahead) or **JR Nikko Line**. Leave by 7 AM.
</Step>

<Step title="Toshogu Shrine" status="done" subtitle="UNESCO World Heritage">
The lavish mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu. See the **"See No Evil" monkeys** and the **Sleeping Cat**. Allow 2 hours.
</Step>

<Step title="Lake Chuzenji & Kegon Falls" status="current" subtitle="Mountains & waterfalls">
Take the bus (40 min) to **Lake Chuzenji** — a caldera lake at 1,269 m. Walk to **Kegon Falls** (97 m drop). Lunch on *yuba* (tofu skin) — a Nikko specialty.
</Step>

<Step title="Return & onsen" status="todo" subtitle="Soak away the day">
Back in Tokyo by evening, visit **Oedo Onsen Monogatari** in Odaiba (¥1,200) for a relaxing soak before your farewell dinner.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Option B: Relax in Tokyo">
<Steps>
<Step title="Sleep in & brunch" status="done" subtitle="Slow morning">
Try a **kissaten** (old-school coffee shop) in Jimbocho or a trendy brunch at **Sarabeth's** in Shinjuku.
</Step>

<Step title="Odaiba" status="done" subtitle="Seaside & shopping">
Cross the **Rainbow Bridge** on the Yurikamome line. Visit **TeamLab Borderless** (if you didn't do Planets), **DiverCity** with its giant Gundam, and **Aqua City** mall. Walk the **Odaiba Seaside Park**.
</Step>

<Step title="Onsen & spa" status="current" subtitle="Unwind Japanese-style">
Spend the afternoon at **Oedo Onsen Monogatari** (tattoo-friendly, ¥1,200). Wear a *yukata*, soak in the baths, and try the foot bath café.
</Step>

<Step title="Farewell dinner in Roppongi" status="todo" subtitle="Tokyo's nightlife district">
Take the Oedo Line to **Roppongi**. Dinner at **Gonpachi** (the "Kill Bill" restaurant) for izakaya-style, or **Robataya** for robatayaki (grilled over charcoal). End with a night view from **Roppongi Hills Mori Tower** (¥1,800).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

### Must-eat dishes
- **Sushi** — Tsukiji outer market or conveyor-belt at **Uobei** (¥100/plate)

<KeyValue title="Essentials">
| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Currency | Japanese Yen (¥). Cards accepted in most places, but keep cash for small shops, temples, and street food |
| Tipping | Not expected — ever. Service charge is included |
| Language | English limited outside tourist areas. Learn: *arigatou* (thank you), *sumimasen* (excuse me), *gochisousama* (that was delicious) |
| Wi-Fi | Free at 7-Eleven, Starbucks, and many stations. Consider renting a pocket Wi-Fi (~¥1,000/day) |
| Etiquette | No eating while walking. Quiet on trains. Remove shoes in temples and some restaurants |
| Emergency | Police: 110, Ambulance/Fire: 119 |
</KeyValue>

<Card title="Before you go">
<Details title="Tipping & payment">
No tipping in Japan — it's considered rude. Pay at the register (many places don't accept tips). Cards are widely accepted in Tokyo, but always carry ¥5,000–10,000 cash for small shops, temple stalls, and vending machines.
</Details>

<Details title="Onsen etiquette">
Wash your body thoroughly before entering the bath. Enter naked (no swimsuits). Tie up long hair. Don't dunk your towel in the water. If you have tattoos, check the policy — many onsens now accept them, but some don't.
</Details>

<Details title="Train etiquette">
Don't talk on the phone. Keep conversations quiet. Give up seats to elderly, pregnant, or disabled passengers. Eat only on long-distance trains (shinkansen), not commuter lines. Queue at the marked spots on the platform.
</Details>

<Details title="Weather & seasons">
- **March–April**: Cherry blossoms, mild (10–18°C)
- **May–June**: Warm, start of rainy season
- **July–August**: Hot & humid (28–35°C), festivals
- **September–October**: Typhoon season, then pleasant
- **November–December**: Cool, autumn colors
- **January–February**: Cold (2–10°C), possible snow
</Details>
</Card>
