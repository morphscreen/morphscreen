<Card title="Full Itinerary">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Day 1 — Shinjuku & Shibuya">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Shinjuku Gyoen Garden" status="done">
A peaceful start — mix of Japanese, French & English gardens. Perfect for a picnic breakfast.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Meiji Shrine" status="done">
Forest in the city. Walk the gravel paths, see the giant torii gate, and try a wooden ema wish plaque.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Harajuku (Takeshita St)" status="done">
Crepes, crêpes, and more crepes. Try the famous *Takeshita Street* for street food and quirky fashion.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shibuya Crossing" status="done">
The world's busiest intersection. Watch from Starbucks (2F) for the best view, then explore Shibuya's shops and alleys.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Omoide Yokochō (Memory Lane)" status="done">
Tiny yakitori stalls under the train tracks. Cozy, smoky, and delicious — a classic Tokyo night.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 2 — Old Tokyo: Asakusa & Ueno">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Senso-ji Temple" status="done">
Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the Kaminarimon gate, browse Nakamise-dori's souvenir stalls, and get your fortune (omikuji).
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Ueno Park & Museums" status="done">
Huge park with museums. Pick one: Tokyo National Museum (history) or the Ueno Zoo (pandas).
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ameyoko Market" status="done">
Street market under the train tracks — seafood, fruit, and cheap eats. Try the fresh sushi bowls.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Akihabara" status="done">
Electric Town! Arcades, anime shops, and maid cafés. Even if you're not into anime, the neon is unreal.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Akihabara izakaya hopping" status="done">
End the day with a casual *tachinomi* (standing bar) or an izakaya with all-you-can-drink sets.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 3 — Water & Futuristic Tokyo">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Tsukiji Outer Market" status="done">
The inner market has moved, but the outer market is still amazing for breakfast sushi, tamago (egg) skewers, and fresh wasabi.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Hamarikyu Gardens" status="done">
A beautiful tidal garden with a tea house in the middle. Watch the skyscrapers from a traditional setting.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: TeamLab Planets" status="done">
Immersive digital art — you walk through water and light. Book tickets in advance (¥3,200 each).
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Odaiba" status="done">
Rainbow Bridge views, the giant Gundam statue, and a relaxed seaside promenade. Great for sunset photos.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 4 — Skyline & Ginza">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Tokyo Skytree" status="done">
At 634m, the world's tallest tower. Go early to skip queues. The view stretches to Mt. Fuji on clear days.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Kappabashi (Kitchen Town)" status="done">
Restaurant-supply street — perfect for souvenir *sample food* (looks real, tastes like plastic!). Buy a fake ramen magnet.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Ginza" status="done">
Tokyo's luxury district. Even window shopping is stunning. Don't miss the Itoya stationery store (8 floors!).
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Ginza rooftop bars" status="done">
Sip cocktails with a skyline view. *Bar High Five* is legendary for its craft cocktails (¥2,500–3,000).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Day 5 — Artsy & Relaxed">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Shimokitazawa" status="done">
Vintage shops, indie cafés, and a bohemian vibe. Perfect for unique souvenirs and thrift finds.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Kichijoji" status="done">
Take the train to this charming neighborhood. Try a *katsu curry* at a local spot.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Inokashira Park" status="done">
Rent a swan boat, stroll the paths, and visit the small zoo. A calm end to a busy trip.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Shibuya (final night)" status="done">
Return to Shibuya for a farewell dinner. Try *okonomiyaki* (savory pancake) or *monjayaki* — cook it yourself!
</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="Practical Tips">
<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
Get a **Suica or Pasmo IC card** at the airport — tap on/off trains, buses, and convenience stores. Download **Google Maps** or **Japan Travel by Navitime** for real-time routes.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Cash is king">
Many smaller shops, temples, and street stalls only take cash. Withdraw ¥10,000–20,000 at 7-Eleven ATMs (they work with foreign cards).
</Callout>
<Callout tone="success" title="Book ahead">
TeamLab Planets, Skytree, and popular restaurants (especially dinner) — book 2–4 weeks in advance, especially on weekends.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="neutral" title="Etiquette quickies">
- No eating while walking (stand near the stall).
- Bow slightly when thanking.
- Shoes off in temples, ryokan, and some restaurants.
- Tipping is not practiced — service is already included.
</Callout>
