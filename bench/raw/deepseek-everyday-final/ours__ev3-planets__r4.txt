# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system is home to eight planets, each unique in its composition, atmosphere, and orbit. They are divided into two main groups: the **inner rocky planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the **outer gas and ice giants** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune).

<Card title="The inner rocky planets" subtitle="Small, dense, and close to the Sun">
<Columns>
<Card title="Mercury">
<Badge tone="neutral">Smallest planet</Badge>
- Closest to the Sun (57.9 million km)
- Daytime temperatures reach 430°C, night drops to -180°C
- No atmosphere to speak of; heavily cratered like our Moon
- A year lasts just 88 Earth days
</Card>
<Card title="Venus">
<Badge tone="warning">Hottest planet</Badge>
- Similar size to Earth but with a thick CO₂ atmosphere
- Surface temperature averages 465°C — hot enough to melt lead
- Spins backwards and a day is longer than its year
- Covered in sulfuric acid clouds
</Card>
<Card title="Earth">
<Badge tone="success">Our home</Badge>
- The only known world with life
- 71% covered by liquid water oceans
- One Moon, a stable climate, and a protective magnetic field
- Average distance from the Sun: 149.6 million km
</Card>
<Card title="Mars">
<Badge tone="info">The Red Planet</Badge>
- Iron oxide (rust) gives it its colour
- Has the largest volcano (Olympus Mons) and canyon (Valles Marineris)
- Thin CO₂ atmosphere; polar ice caps of water and CO₂
- Home to the most robotic explorers of any planet
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="The outer gas and ice giants" subtitle="Huge, cold, and mostly hydrogen">
<Columns>
<Card title="Jupiter">
<Badge tone="warning">Largest planet</Badge>
- 11 times Earth's diameter; could fit 1,300 Earths inside
- Great Red Spot: a storm larger than Earth, raging for centuries
- At least 95 moons, including the four big Galilean moons
- A day lasts under 10 hours — the fastest spin of any planet
</Card>
<Card title="Saturn">
<Badge tone="info">The ringed jewel</Badge>
- Famous for its dazzling ring system of ice and rock
- Less dense than water — it would float in a giant bathtub
- 146 known moons; Titan has a thick atmosphere and liquid methane lakes
- A year lasts 29.4 Earth years
</Card>
<Card title="Uranus">
<Badge tone="neutral">The sideways planet</Badge>
- Rotates on its side, likely from an ancient giant impact
- An ice giant of water, methane, and ammonia
- Methane gives it a pale cyan colour
- Faint, dark rings discovered in 1977
</Card>
<Card title="Neptune">
<Badge tone="info">The windiest world</Badge>
- Winds reach 2,100 km/h — the fastest in the solar system
- Deep blue due to methane in its atmosphere
- Discovered by mathematics in 1846 before being seen
- Its moon Triton orbits backwards and has geysers of nitrogen
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Pluto's demotion">
Pluto was reclassified as a **dwarf planet** in 2006 by the International Astronomical Union because it hasn't cleared its orbital neighbourhood of other objects. The solar system also contains many other dwarf planets, including Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake.
</Callout>

<Card title="How far apart are they?">
The planets are separated by vast distances. If the Sun were the size of a basketball, Earth would be a peppercorn 30 metres away, and Neptune would be a marble nearly a kilometre away.
</Card>
