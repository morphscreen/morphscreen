# What to Eat in Lisbon: A Food Lover's Guide

Lisbon is a city that lives for food — from golden *pastéis de nata* to fresh seafood grilled over charcoal. Here's where to eat the classics, plus a few modern twists.

<Card title="The Essential Bites">
<Columns>
<div>
<h4>Pastéis de Nata</h4>
<p>Custard tarts with a caramelized, flaky crust. Best eaten warm with a sprinkle of cinnamon.</p>
<Badge tone="success">Must-try</Badge>
</div>
<div>
<h4>Bacalhau (Salted Cod)</h4>
<p>They say there are 1,001 recipes. Try it *à brás* (shredded with potatoes, eggs, and olives) or grilled.</p>
</div>
<div>
<h4>Bifana</h4>
<p>A humble but mighty pork sandwich, marinated in garlic and wine, served on a crusty roll.</p>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat the Classics">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Pastéis de Nata">
<p>The undisputed king is <strong>Pastéis de Belém</strong> — the original recipe from 1837, baked in the same shop since then. Go early or late to avoid the queue.</p>
<p>For a neighborhood favorite, try <strong>Manteigaria</strong> (Chiado) — they're crispier and less sweet, and you can watch them being made.</p>

</Tab>
<Tab title="Bacalhau">
<p><strong>Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau</strong> (Chiado) serves a modern take: a croquette filled with creamy cod, deep-fried in a breadcrumb shell. Pair it with a glass of *vinho verde*.</p>
<p>For a traditional plate, head to <strong>Varina</strong> (Alfama) — a no-frills tasca where the *bacalhau à brás* is generous and homey.</p>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bifana">
<p>The classic is <strong>O Trevo</strong> (Rossio) — a tiny counter where they've served the same recipe since 1956. Order it *com pimenta* (with hot sauce) if you dare.</p>
<p>For a gourmet version, <strong>Casa Bica</strong> (Bica) does a slow-cooked pork bifana with crispy onions and aioli.</p>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Seafood">
<p>Lisbon is all about the ocean. <strong>Cervejaria Ramiro</strong> is a legend for garlic shrimp, clams, and giant tiger prawns — order the *pão com chouriço* to mop up the sauce.</p>
<p>For a more local vibe, <strong>Zé da Mouraria</strong> serves grilled sardines and *carapaus* (small mackerel) with boiled potatoes and olive oil.</p>
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="A Food Tour in One Day">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Pastel & Coffee" status="done" subtitle="Pasteis de Belém or Manteigaria">
Start with a warm pastel de nata and a *bica* (espresso) at a counter. The Belém bakery is worth the tram ride.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Tasca Classics" status="done" subtitle="Varina or Zé da Mouraria">
Sit down for bacalhau or grilled sardines with a *imperial* (draft beer). Watch the locals — lunch is a serious affair.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Bifana Break" status="done" subtitle="O Trevo or Casa Bica">
A bifana is the perfect 4pm pick-me-up. Grab one standing at the counter, then walk it off in the Alfama hills.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Seafood Feast" status="done" subtitle="Cervejaria Ramiro">
End with garlic shrimp and clams, crusty bread for the sauce, and a cold *vinho verde*. Go hungry — it's generous.
</Step>
<Step title="Nightcap: Ginjinha" status="done" subtitle="A Ginjinha (Rossio)">
A tiny glass of sour cherry liqueur, often served in a chocolate cup. Sweet, strong, and very Lisbon.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Practical Tips">
<Columns>
<div>
<h4>When to Go</h4>
<p>Lunch is 12:30–15:00, dinner starts at 19:30. Many spots close between. Go early for popular places like Ramiro — no reservations.</p>
</div>
<div>
<h4>How to Order</h4>
<p>At counters, just point and say *"um pastel"* or *"uma bifana"*. At tascas, ask for the *prato do dia* (daily plate) — it's the freshest and often cheapest.</p>
</div>
<div>
<h4>Budget</h4>
<p>Pastel: €1.50 · Bifana: €3.50 · Bacalhau plate: €12–18 · Seafood feast: €25–40 per person. Cash is still king at small counters.</p>
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Vegetarian?">
Lisbon has you covered too. Try <strong>O Asiático</strong> (Príncipe Real) for creative plant-based tapas, or <strong>Jardim das Cerejas</strong> for a cozy vegan tasca with *bacalhau* made from jackfruit.
</Callout>
