# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A balanced itinerary mixing iconic sights, hidden gems, great food, and romantic moments — designed for a couple who wants to experience both the classic and the unexpected.

<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
Pick up a **Suica or Pasmo IC card** at the airport — it works on all trains, subways, and most convenience stores. The Tokyo Metro and JR lines are your best friends; Google Maps with transit directions will get you everywhere.
</Callout>

<Card title="Day 1 — Arrival & Shinjuku Nightlife" subtitle="Get your bearings in the neon heart of Tokyo">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Arrive & check in" status="todo">
Land at Narita or Haneda. Take the Narita Express or Skyliner into the city. Check into your hotel in **Shinjuku** — a great base for first-timers.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shinjuku Gyoen Garden" status="todo">
A peaceful escape from the chaos. This sprawling park has Japanese, French, and English garden sections. In spring, it's one of the best cherry-blossom spots in the city. *Entry: ¥500 per person.*
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho" status="todo">
For dinner, dive into **Omoide Yokocho** ("Memory Lane") — tiny alleyways packed with smokey yakitori stalls. Later, explore **Golden Gai**, a maze of 200+ micro-bars. Find a bar with room for two, order a highball, and soak in the atmosphere.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 2 — Classic Tokyo: Asakusa, Ueno & Akihabara" subtitle="Old Tokyo meets otaku culture">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise-dori" status="todo">
Start early at **Senso-ji**, Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the iconic **Kaminarimon Gate**, then browse **Nakamise-dori** — a shopping street with traditional snacks like *ningyo-yaki* (sweet filled cakes).
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Tempura in Asakusa" status="todo">
Try a sit-down tempura lunch at a local spot like **Daikokuya** — a beloved institution serving crispy, golden tempura over rice.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Ueno Park & Akihabara" status="todo">
Stroll through **Ueno Park**, home to museums and a lovely pond. Then hop on the train to **Akihabara** — the electric town. Explore anime shops, arcades, and themed cafés. Even if you're not into anime, the energy is infectious.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Dinner in Kanda" status="todo">
Head a few stops south to **Kanda** for dinner at a cozy *izakaya* (Japanese pub). Try *otsumami* (small plates) and a local craft beer.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 3 — Day Trip: Hakone & Mt. Fuji Views" subtitle="Onsen, lake cruises, and the iconic mountain">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Check the weather">
Mt. Fuji is notoriously shy — it's only visible on clear days. Check the forecast the night before; if it's cloudy, swap this day with Day 4 (Shibuya/Harajuku) and go later.
</Callout>
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Romancecar to Hakone" status="todo">
Take the **Odakyu Romancecar** from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto (~85 min). Buy the **Hakone Free Pass** (¥6,100) which covers the round-trip train plus all local transport: cable car, ropeway, and boat.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Hakone Ropeway & Owakudani" status="todo">
Ride the **Hakone Ropeway** up to **Owakudani**, a volcanic valley with steaming vents. Try the famous *kuro-tamago* — eggs boiled in the sulfurous hot springs that turn black. Legend says they add 7 years to your life.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Lake Ashi cruise" status="todo">
Take the ropeway down to **Lake Ashi** and board a pirate-ship-style cruise across the lake. On clear days, you'll get stunning views of Mt. Fuji reflecting on the water.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Onsen & kaiseki dinner" status="todo">
Check into a ryokan (traditional inn) in Hakone for the night. Soak in the private **onsen** (hot spring), then enjoy a multi-course **kaiseki** dinner in your room. This is the splurge night of the trip — worth every yen.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 4 — Shibuya, Harajuku & a Sunset View" subtitle="Youth culture, shopping, and Tokyo's best skyline">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Meiji Shrine" status="todo">
Start at **Meiji Jingu**, a serene Shinto shrine tucked in a forest right in the city. The massive torii gates and quiet gravel paths feel a world away from the chaos outside.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Harajuku & Takeshita Street" status="todo">
Walk down to **Harajuku** and dive into **Takeshita Street** — a riot of colorful fashion, crepe stands, and quirky shops. For a quieter vibe, explore the backstreets of **Cat Street** for vintage boutiques and indie cafés.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shibuya Crossing & shopping" status="todo">
Walk to **Shibuya** and experience the famous **scramble crossing** — the busiest pedestrian intersection in the world. Take a photo from the Starbucks on the second floor of the QFRONT building. Then shop at **Shibuya 109** or explore the trendy **Miyashita Park** complex.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Sunset at Shibuya Sky" status="todo">
End the day at **Shibuya Sky** (rooftop observation deck, ¥2,000). Time it for sunset — you'll see the city light up in every direction, with Mt. Fuji on the horizon on clear days. Book tickets online in advance to skip the line.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 5 — Tsukiji, Ginza & Departure" subtitle="Fresh sushi, high-end streets, and last-minute souvenirs">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Tsukiji Outer Market" status="todo">
Head to the **Tsukiji Outer Market** for a final culinary adventure. The inner wholesale market has moved to Toyosu, but the outer market still has dozens of stalls selling fresh sushi, tamagoyaki (rolled omelette), and grilled seafood. Try the uni (sea urchin) if you're feeling adventurous.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Ginza stroll" status="todo">
Walk to **Ginza**, Tokyo's luxury shopping district. Even if you're not buying designer goods, the architecture is stunning — check out the futuristic **Kabukiza Theatre** and window-shop along Chuo-dori.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Last-minute shopping & airport" status="todo">
Pick up souvenirs — Japanese snacks, stationery, or a bottle of sake — at **Don Quijote** in Ginza. Then grab your bags from the hotel and head to the airport. The **Narita Express** and **Keikyu Line** both connect directly from major stations.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to stay" subtitle="Two great bases for a couple">
<Columns>
<Card title="Shinjuku — for first-timers">
- **Pros:** Endless dining, nightlife, and transit connections; close to Day 1 & 3 activities
- **Cons:** Can feel overwhelming; some areas are seedy at night
- **Recommendation:** Park Hyatt Tokyo (luxury, *Lost in Translation* vibes) or Hotel Gracery (mid-range, has a Godzilla head on the roof)
</Card>
<Card title="Ginza — for a quieter, upscale stay">
- **Pros:** Elegant, central, close to Tsukiji; great for the last day
- **Cons:** Fewer late-night options; pricier for the same room size
- **Recommendation:** Mitsui Garden Hotel Ginza Premier (mid-range, great skyline views) or Hoshinoya Tokyo (ryokan-style luxury)
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Money-saving tips">
- **Eat at convenience stores** (7-Eleven, FamilyMart) for breakfast — the onigiri, egg sandwiches, and coffee are surprisingly good and cost under ¥500.
- **Lunch is the deal:** Many high-end restaurants offer set lunches at half the dinner price.
- **Buy a Suica card** and use it everywhere — no cash headaches.
- **Skip taxis** — the subway is faster and way cheaper.
- **Book Shibuya Sky and Hakone ryokan in advance** — they sell out, especially on weekends.
</Card>
