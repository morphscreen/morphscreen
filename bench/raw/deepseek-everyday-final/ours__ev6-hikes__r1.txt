# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

From rugged coastal cliffs to alpine meadows, the mountains around Vancouver offer some of the most spectacular day hiking in the world. Here are the top picks, organized by difficulty and experience.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you go">
Always check trail conditions and weather forecasts. Many trails require a [BC Parks day pass](https://bcparks.ca) during peak season (June–September). Carry bear spray, layers, and plenty of water.
</Callout>

## The Top Hikes

<Card title="1. Stawamus Chief – The Classic Summit">
The Chief towers over Squamish, 70 km north of Vancouver. Its three granite peaks offer jaw-dropping views of Howe Sound. The **Second Peak** is the most popular, with chains and ladders on the final section.

**Highlights:** Panoramic views, rock climbing routes visible from the trail, and the famous "Chief's" sheer face.

**Trail details:** Start at the Stawamus Chief Provincial Park trailhead. The route is steep and relentless, with 2,500+ stairs and rock steps. The summit of Second Peak is the payoff—a 360° view of the sound, surrounding mountains, and the town below.

**Tips:** Go early (7 AM) to beat crowds. Bring gloves for the chains—they get cold and slippery. The First Peak is a shorter, quieter option if you want fewer people.

<Card title="2. Garibaldi Lake – Turquoise Alpine Gem">
This is the quintessential BC alpine hike. The lake's impossibly blue water (glacial silt) sits beneath the massive Black Tusk volcano and the Garibaldi massif. It's a long, steady climb, but the reward is worth every switchback.

**Trail details:** The trail starts at the Rubble Creek trailhead (90 min from Vancouver). The first 6 km are a relentless grind up 900 m, then it levels out through meadows to the lake. The emerald water and the panorama of mountains will make you forget the climb.

**Highlights:** The lake itself, the Barrier (a massive lava dam), and views of Black Tusk and Mount Price. In July, wildflowers carpet the meadows.

**Tips:** Pack a picnic and spend time at the lake—don't rush back. The hike is doable in a day, but camping at the lake is a popular option for a sunrise view.

<Card title="3. Dog Mountain – Easy Summit Views">
For a short, family-friendly hike with a huge payoff, Dog Mountain on Mount Seymour delivers. It's the perfect introduction to Vancouver's North Shore mountains.

**Trail details:** Start at the Mount Seymour ski area parking lot (45 min from downtown). The trail is mostly gentle, through serene forest, with a few rocky sections near the summit. The final push is a short scramble over boulders.

**Highlights:** Unobstructed views of downtown Vancouver, the harbour, and the Gulf Islands. On a clear day, you can see Mount Baker in Washington State.

**Tips:** The trail is well-marked but can be muddy—wear sturdy shoes. Sunset hikes are magical here, but bring a headlamp for the return.

<Card title="4. Joffre Lakes – Three Shades of Blue">
Three glacier-fed lakes, each more stunning than the last. This trail in Joffre Lakes Provincial Park (2.5 hrs from Vancouver) is famous for its unreal colours—from milky turquoise to deep sapphire.

**Trail details:** The trail is well-built with boardwalks and stairs. Middle Lake is the first payoff at 2.5 km, then Upper Lake at 5.5 km. The final lake sits beneath the Matier Glacier, which calves into the water.

**Highlights:** The glacier, the three lakes, and the chance to see ice chunks floating in the water. The scenery is so surreal it looks photoshopped.

**Tips:** You need a [day-use pass](https://bcparks.ca) from mid-June to September. The trail is popular for a reason—go on a weekday if possible. The upper lake is the most photogenic; save time for it.

<Card title="5. Howe Sound Crest Trail – For the Adventurous">
This is the ultimate North Shore traverse. It's not for beginners—it's a full-day, 30 km epic with 1,500 m of elevation gain and loss, connecting Cypress Mountain to Porteau Cove.

**Trail details:** Most people hike it northbound from Cypress Bowl to Porteau Cove, descending to the highway for a shuttle pickup. The trail is rugged, with exposed ridges, steep drops, and sections requiring route-finding.

**Highlights:** The best ridge walking in the region. Views of Howe Sound, the Tantalus Range, and the Lions (two iconic peaks) are constant. The alpine meadows in July and August are spectacular.

**Tips:** This is a serious undertaking—only attempt in good weather with proper gear. Bring a map (GPS backup), plenty of food, and a headlamp. Arrange a pickup at Porteau Cove or take the bus back.

## Essential Gear

<Card title="Day hike checklist">
- **Footwear:** Sturdy hiking boots or trail runners with good grip
- **Layers:** Merino base, fleece, waterproof shell (weather changes fast)
- **Navigation:** Map, compass, GPS or phone with offline maps
- **Sun protection:** Hat, sunglasses, sunscreen (even on cloudy days)
- **Food & water:** 2–3 L water per person, high-energy snacks
- **Safety:** Bear spray, first-aid kit, headlamp, whistle
- **Extras:** Trekking poles (saves knees on descents), camera
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Wildlife awareness">
Black bears and cougars are active on all these trails. Make noise, travel in groups, keep dogs on leash, and know how to use bear spray (practice the draw before you go).
</Callout>

## Getting There

Most trailheads are accessible by car from downtown Vancouver. Public transit reaches some (like Cypress and Seymour in summer), but a car is easiest. Carpooling is recommended—parking fills early on weekends.
