<Card title="Day 1 — Shinjuku: Arrival & Neon Nights">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Arrive & check in" status="done">
Land at Haneda (HND) or Narita (NRT), take the Narita Express or limousine bus to Shinjuku. Check into your hotel, drop bags, and grab a quick *konbini* breakfast.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Explore Shinjuku Gyoen" status="done">
A peaceful 58-hectare garden with Japanese, French, and English landscapes. Perfect for a stroll and picnic. Entry ¥500.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho" status="done">
Tiny lantern-lit alleys packed with micro-bars and yakitori stalls. Try a *highball* and grilled skewers. Go early (6pm) to avoid queues.
</Step>
<Step title="Night: Observation decks" status="done">
Head to the free Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatory (until 10:30pm) for a sweeping night view — Mount Fuji on clear days.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 2 — Shibuya & Harajuku: Pop Culture & Fashion">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Meiji Shrine" status="done">
A serene Shinto shrine in a forested grove. Watch traditional weddings on weekends. Free entry.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Harajuku Takeshita Street" status="done">
Kawaii culture hub — crepes, quirky boutiques, and vintage shops. Try a *crépe à la Harajuku* for lunch.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shibuya Crossing & Hachiko" status="done">
Experience the world's busiest intersection. Visit the Hachiko statue, then shop at Shibuya 109 or Loft.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Dinner in Shibuya" status="done">
Book a *izakaya* or *omakase* sushi counter. Try *Uobei Shibuya* for conveyor-belt sushi on a budget.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 3 — Asakusa & Ueno: Old Tokyo">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Senso-ji Temple" status="done">
Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the Kaminarimon Gate and Nakamise-dori shopping street. Arrive before 9am to avoid crowds.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Ueno Park & Museums" status="done">
Visit the Tokyo National Museum (¥1,000) or Ueno Zoo (¥600). Grab a *soba* lunch nearby.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Yanaka Ginza" status="done">
A charming *shitamachi* (old-town) shopping street with traditional snacks and crafts. Try *menchi-katsu* (fried meat croquette).
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Sumida River cruise" status="done">
Take a water bus from Asakusa to Hinode Pier at sunset — great views of Tokyo Skytree. Book online in advance.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 4 — Akihabara & Ginza: Tech & Luxury">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Akihabara Electric Town" status="done">
Explore multi-story electronics stores, anime shops, and arcades. Visit *Yodobashi Camera* for gadgets and *Mandarake* for collectibles.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Lunch at a maid café (optional)" status="done">
A quirky Tokyo experience. Try *@home café* — book ahead for weekends. Otherwise, grab ramen at *Ichiran Akihabara*.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Ginza shopping" status="done">
Walk the upscale Chuo-dori boulevard (pedestrianised on weekends). Visit *Ginza Six* for design stores and the *Kabukiza Theatre* for a kabuki show (from ¥4,000).
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Fine dining" status="done">
Splurge on a *kaiseki* (multi-course) dinner or *teppanyaki* at a Ginza restaurant. Book 1–2 weeks ahead.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 5 — Odaiba & Departure">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: teamLab Planets" status="done">
An immersive digital art museum (book timed entry online, ¥3,200). Walk through water and mirror rooms — bring shorts; you'll be barefoot.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Odaiba seaside" status="done">
Relax at Odaiba Beach, see the mini Statue of Liberty, and shop at DiverCity Tokyo Plaza (the giant Gundam statue is outside).
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Last-minute shopping" status="done">
Pick up souvenirs at the Tokyo Teleport area or head back to Shinjuku for *Don Quijote* (duty-free, tax-free with passport).
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Airport transfer" status="done">
Take the Yurikamome line to Shimbashi, then the Narita Express or Keikyu line to Haneda. Allow 90 minutes to the airport.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Practical Tips for Couples">
<Columns>
<Card title="Getting Around">
- **Suica or Pasmo IC card** — tap on/off trains and buses, also pay at convenience stores
- **Tokyo Metro 24/48/72-hour pass** — ¥800/¥1,200/¥1,500 for unlimited rides on metro lines
- **JR Yamanote Line** — loops through Shinjuku, Shibuya, Akihabara, Ueno, and Tokyo stations
- **Taxis** — expensive, but useful late at night (flag a black cab or use the GO app)
</Card>
<Card title="Where to Stay">
- **Shinjuku** — best for nightlife and transit (recommended for first-timers)
- **Shibuya** — trendier, close to shopping and cafes
- **Asakusa** — cheaper, old-town charm, quieter at night
- **Ginza** — upscale, great for luxury shopping, near Tsukiji
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Money-saving tips">
- Eat at *konbini* (7-Eleven, FamilyMart) for breakfast — ¥500–¥800 each
- Lunch sets are cheaper than dinner (¥1,000–¥1,500)
- Many shrines and parks are free
- Book teamLab and any shows online in advance for discounts

<Card title="Essential Japanese Phrases">
| Phrase | Meaning | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| *Sumimasen* | Excuse me / sorry | Getting attention, apologising |
| *Arigatou gozaimasu* | Thank you | After any service |
| *Eigo o hanasemasu ka?* | Do you speak English? | At restaurants, stations |
| *Kore o kudasai* | This one, please | Pointing at menus or items |
| *Oishii* | Delicious | Complimenting food |
