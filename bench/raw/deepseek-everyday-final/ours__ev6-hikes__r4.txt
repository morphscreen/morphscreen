## 1. Quarry Rock (Deep Cove)

<Card title="Quarry Rock">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="3.8 km" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="100 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="1.5–2 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Easy" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Quarry Rock viewpoint overlooking Indian Arm fjord](find)
![Baden-Powell Trail through mossy forest](find)
</Gallery>

The quintessential first hike near Vancouver. This short forest trail climbs gently through towering cedars and firs before opening onto a granite outcrop with sweeping views of **Indian Arm** — a deep fjord dotted with sailboats and framed by steep green slopes. Perfect for families, trail runners, or anyone wanting a rewarding view without breaking a sweat.

**Getting there:** 30 minutes from downtown. Park at Deep Cove and follow the Baden-Powell Trail signs.

<Callout tone="info" title="Go early">The parking lot at Deep Cove fills by 8 am on weekends. Take the #211 bus from downtown if you don't want to drive.</Callout>

</Card>

## 2. Lynn Canyon Park & Suspension Bridge

<Card title="Lynn Canyon Park">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="2–5 km" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="50–150 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="1–3 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Easy–Moderate" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Lynn Canyon Suspension Bridge over the gorge](find)
![Twin Falls cascading into Lynn Creek](find)
</Gallery>

A free alternative to the Capilano Suspension Bridge, Lynn Canyon offers a **50-metre swinging bridge** over a rushing gorge, then a network of trails through temperate rainforest. Don't miss **Twin Falls** and the **30 Foot Pool** — a deep emerald swimming hole that's irresistible on hot summer days.

**Getting there:** 30 minutes from downtown. Free parking at Lynn Canyon Park.

<KeyValue title="Highlights">
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Suspension bridge | Free, 50 m long |
| Swimming | 30 Foot Pool, lifeguarded in summer |
| Ecology centre | Free, learn about the rainforest |
| Trail options | 2 km loop up to 5 km network |
</KeyValue>

</Card>

## 3. Grouse Grind

<Card title="Grouse Grind">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="2.9 km" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="853 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="1.5–2 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Hard" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Grouse Grind wooden staircase trail](find)
![View of Vancouver from Grouse Mountain summit](find)
</Gallery>

Known locally as **"Mother Nature's Stairmaster,"** the Grind is a relentless 2,830-step climb up the face of Grouse Mountain. It's not about distance — it's about vertical. The reward is a panoramic view of Vancouver, the Fraser River delta, and on clear days, all the way to Mount Baker in Washington state.

**The catch:** you need to ride the **Skyride gondola down** (around $20 CAD), or you'll face a brutal 3-hour descent.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Fitness required">This is a cardio workout, not a nature walk. Allow 1.5–2 hours up. The trail is closed in wet or icy conditions — check the Grouse Mountain website first.</Callout>

**Getting there:** 45 minutes from downtown. Take the #236 bus from Lonsdale Quay.

</Card>

## 4. Stawamus Chief (The Chief)

<Card title="Stawamus Chief">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="11 km (all peaks)" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="550–700 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="4–6 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Moderate–Hard" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Granite face of the Stawamus Chief from Shannon Falls](find)
![View from the Second Peak summit over Howe Sound](find)
</Gallery>

A massive granite monolith rising 700 metres above **Howe Sound** in Squamish, the Chief offers three distinct peaks, each with its own panorama. The trail is a mix of forest switchbacks, rocky steps, and — on the final approach — **chain-assisted scrambles** up sheer rock faces. The views over the sound, the islands, and the coastal mountains are simply world-class.

**Getting there:** 1 hour from Vancouver, just off the Sea-to-Sky Highway.

<KeyValue title="Peak comparison">
| Peak | Distance (return) | Time | Vibe |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Peak | 5 km | 2–3 hrs | Most popular, chain section |
| Second Peak | 7 km | 3–4 hrs | Best views, longer |
| Third Peak | 11 km | 4–6 hrs | Quietest, most remote |
</KeyValue>

</Card>

## 5. Garibaldi Lake

<Card title="Garibaldi Lake">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="18 km (return)" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="900 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="6–8 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Moderate–Hard" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Turquoise Garibaldi Lake with Black Tusk behind](find)
![Switchbacks through alpine meadows below the lake](find)
</Gallery>

One of the most spectacular alpine lakes in the province, Garibaldi's glacier-fed waters glow an impossible shade of **electric turquoise**. The trail climbs steadily through old-growth forest and subalpine meadows before bursting out at the lake's edge, with the jagged **Black Tusk** looming behind. In late July and August, the meadows are carpeted with wildflowers.

**Getting there:** 1.5 hours from Vancouver on the Sea-to-Sky Highway. Parking at the Rubble Creek lot fills by 7 am on weekends — arrive early or take the shuttle from Squamish.

<Callout tone="success" title="Best time to go">Late summer (August–September) after the snow melts. The lake is cold but swimmable — and utterly unforgettable.</Callout>

</Card>

## 6. Joffre Lakes Provincial Park

<Card title="Joffre Lakes">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="10 km (return)" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="500 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="4–5 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Moderate" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Lower Joffre Lake with turquoise water and glacier](find)
![Middle Joffre Lake viewed from the trail](find)
</Gallery>

Three stacked lakes, each more stunning than the last, fed by the **Matier Glacier** that hangs above the highest one. The trail is well-graded and accessible, making it one of the most popular day hikes in BC. The colour of the water — a milky glacial blue — has to be seen to be believed.

**Getting there:** 2.5 hours from Vancouver along the Duffey Lake Road past Pemberton.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Day-use pass required">Since 2021, Joffre Lakes requires a free day-use pass from 6 am–6 pm. Book it online up to 2 days ahead — they sell out fast.</Callout>

</Card>

## 7. Mount Seymour (First Pump Peak)

<Card title="Mount Seymour">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="8 km (return)" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="460 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="3–4 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Moderate" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Mount Seymour trail with views of Vancouver below](find)
![First Pump Peak ridge with Howe Sound in the distance](find)
</Gallery>

The closest alpine summit to Vancouver, Mount Seymour offers **big views with minimal effort**. From the ski-area parking lot, a well-marked trail climbs through subalpine forest and open meadows to **First Pump Peak** (1,450 m), where you can see Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands, and the entire city skyline spread out below. On clear days, even Mount Rainier appears on the southern horizon.

**Getting there:** 45 minutes from downtown. The road to the parking lot is steep and winding — allow extra time.

<Details title="Trail variations">
- **First Pump Peak** (8 km return, 3–4 hrs): the classic view
- **Second Pump Peak** (10 km, 4–5 hrs): a little higher, a little quieter
- **Tim Jones Peak** (11 km, 5–6 hrs): the most rewarding, least crowded
- **Mount Seymour Summit** (12 km, 6–7 hrs): the true summit, requires some scrambling
</Details>

</Card>

## 8. Golden Ears Summit

<Card title="Golden Ears Summit">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Distance" value="16 km (return)" />
<Stat label="Elevation gain" value="1,500 m" />
<Stat label="Time" value="8–10 hrs" />
<Stat label="Difficulty" value="Hard" />
</Columns>

<Gallery>
![Golden Ears summit ridge with mountain views](find)
![Alder Flats meadow below the summit](find)
</Gallery>

The most challenging hike on this list, Golden Ears rewards those willing to work with a **true wilderness summit experience**. The trail climbs relentlessly through mossy rainforest to **Alder Flats** (a popular camp spot), then switchbacks steeply above treeline to the rocky summit ridge. From the top, the view spans the Fraser Valley, the Coast Mountains, and the entire Vancouver skyline.

**Getting there:** 1.5 hours from Vancouver. Enter through Golden Ears Provincial Park (parking fee applies).

<Callout tone="danger" title="Only for experienced hikers">This is a serious undertaking — snow can persist into July, and the exposed summit ridge is dangerous in poor weather. Start before 7 am, carry the ten essentials, and turn back if conditions worsen.</Callout>

</Card>

## Essential Tips

<Card title="Before you go">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check the weather" status="done">Mountain weather changes fast. Check the forecast for the specific elevation you're hiking, not just Vancouver.</Step>
<Step title="Pack the ten essentials" status="done">Navigation, headlamp, sun protection, first aid, knife, fire, shelter, extra food, extra water, extra layers.</Step>
<Step title="Start early" status="done">Trailheads fill by 7–8 am on weekends. Early starts also mean better weather and fewer crowds.</Step>
<Step title="Tell someone your plan" status="done">Share your route and expected return time with a friend or family member.</Step>
<Step title="Leave no trace" status="done">Pack out everything, stay on trails, and keep wildlife wild.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Essential gear">
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Hiking boots | Trails are rocky and rooty; ankle support helps |
| Layers | Temperatures drop 5–10°C with elevation |
| 2 L water | Most hikes have no water sources |
| Bear spray | Black bears and cougars are present in all these areas |
| Headlamp | Even "day" hikes can run late |
| Map (offline) | Cell service is unreliable in the mountains |
</Card>
