# What to Eat in Lisbon, Portugal

Lisbon is a food lover's paradise — think fresh seafood, creamy pastries, smoky grilled sardines, and bold flavors influenced by centuries of maritime history. Here's your essential eating guide, from iconic dishes to the best spots to try them.

<Card title="The Must-Try Dishes">
<Columns>
<div>
### Pastéis de Nata
The iconic custard tart with a crispy, caramelized top and flaky pastry. Best enjoyed warm with a sprinkle of cinnamon.

**Where:** *Pastéis de Belém* (the original since 1837) or *Manteigaria* in Chiado for a modern take.
</div>
<div>
### Bacalhau (Salted Cod)
Portugal's "faithful friend" — said to have 1,000 recipes. Try *bacalhau à brás* (shredded cod with potatoes, eggs, and olives) or *bacalhau com natas* (baked with cream).

**Where:** *Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau* near the Sé, or *Solar dos Presuntos* for a refined version.
</div>
<div>
### Sardinhas Assadas (Grilled Sardines)
A summer staple, especially during the June festivals. Whole sardines grilled over charcoal, served with boiled potatoes and salad.

**Where:** *Casa de Pasto* in Alfama, or any *tasca* (traditional tavern) during festival season.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="More Lisbon Classics">
<Columns>
<div>
### Bifana
A humble but glorious sandwich: marinated pork in a crusty roll, often with a kick of mustard or piri-piri.

**Where:** *Casa Bifanas* near Rossio Square — a local institution.
</div>
<div>
### Arroz de Marisco
A rich, saffron-hued seafood rice stew loaded with shrimp, clams, and crab. Comfort food at its finest.

**Where:** *Cervejaria Ramiro* (famous for seafood) or *Uma Marisqueira* in Saldanha.
</div>
<div>
### Ginjinha
Not a dish, but essential: a sour cherry liqueur served in a shot glass, sometimes with a piece of fruit at the bottom.

**Where:** *A Ginjinha* in Rossio — tiny, standing-room-only, and perfect.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat by Neighborhood">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Alfama (Old Town)">
The historic heart — narrow alleys, fado music, and rustic taverns.

- **Casa de Pasto**: Grilled sardines and petiscos (small plates) in a cozy, tiled room.
- **Chapitô à Mesa**: Elevated Portuguese with a stunning view over the Tagus.
- **Cantinho do Aziz**: A surprising gem — Mozambican-Portuguese fusion with bold curries.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Chiado & Bairro Alto">
Trendy, artsy, and buzzing with life from morning to late night.

- **Manteigaria**: Arguably the best pastéis de nata in the city center.
- **Belcanto**: Two Michelin stars — José Avillez's modern Portuguese tasting menu (book ahead).
- **A Brasileira**: A historic café for coffee and people-watching since 1905.
- **O Trevo**: Late-night bifanas and beer, a local favorite.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Cais do Sodré & Santos">
Once a red-light district, now a hip foodie hub.

- **Time Out Market**: A food hall with 30+ stalls from top chefs — perfect for grazing.
- **Cervejaria Ramiro**: Legendary for garlic shrimp, tiger prawns, and steak sandwiches.
- **Sea Me**: A hybrid fish market and restaurant — pick your catch, they'll cook it.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Belém">
A bit out of the center, but worth the trip for the pastry pilgrimage.

- **Pastéis de Belém**: The original recipe, baked fresh all day. Go early to avoid the queue.
- **Encore**: A modern bistro with a creative take on Portuguese classics.
- **Churrasqueira do Ramiro**: Casual grilled chicken and piri-piri sauce.
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="Quick Bites & Street Food">
<Columns>
<div>
**Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market)**  
Open daily, 10:00–23:00. Try the *francesinha* (a sandwich with ham, sausage, steak, and melted cheese in a tomato-beer sauce) from Café de São Bento, or the octopus salad from Marlene Vieira.

</div>
<div>
**Pastelaria Versailles**  
An elegant 1920s pastry shop in Estrela. Perfect for a mid-morning *galão* (milky coffee) and a *pão de deus* (coconut-topped bun).

</div>
<div>
**A Ginjinha**  
The classic spot for a *ginjinha* shot — just €1 per glass, served with a cherry. Stand at the counter like a local.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Tips for Eating Like a Local">
<Callout tone="info" title="Know before you go">
- **Lunch** is the main meal, typically 13:00–15:00. Many restaurants offer a *prato do dia* (daily special) for €8–12.
- **Dinner** starts late — 20:00 at the earliest, often later.
- **Tipping** is not expected, but rounding up or leaving 5–10% for great service is appreciated.
- **Reservations** are essential for top spots like Belcanto and Ramiro, especially on weekends.
- **Portions** are generous — share a starter and save room for dessert.
</Callout>

<Card title="A Sample Foodie Day in Lisbon">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done">
Start at **Manteigaria** in Chiado for a warm pastel de nata and a *meia de leite* (milky coffee). Take it to go and wander the streets.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday" status="done">
Head to **Time Out Market** for a grazing lunch — try the fried croquettes, a seafood rice bowl, and a glass of *vinho verde*.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done">
Walk off lunch with a *ginjinha* at **A Ginjinha**, then explore the Alfama district's viewpoints.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done">
Book a table at **Cervejaria Ramiro** for garlic shrimp, clams, and a steak sandwich — a true Lisbon feast.
</Step>
<Step title="Night" status="done">
End with a nightcap at **Park Bar** (on top of a parking garage) for city views and a *porto tónico* (white port with tonic).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Final Word">
Lisbon rewards the curious eater. Don't over-plan — let the smell of grilled sardines guide you down a side street, follow the locals to a busy *tasca*, and always say yes to dessert. *Bom apetite!*
