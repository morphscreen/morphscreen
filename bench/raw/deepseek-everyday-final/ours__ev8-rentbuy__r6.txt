# Rent or buy? A personal look at your numbers

<Callout tone="info" title="The short answer">
At £60k a year with £1,800/month rent, buying *can* make sense — but it depends heavily on your deposit, how long you'll stay, and where you're looking. Here's how to think it through.
</Callout>

<Card title="The real cost of renting vs buying">
Renting £1,800/month adds up fast — but buying has hidden costs too. Compare the **total cost over 5 years** in a typical scenario.

<Columns>
<Card title="Renting — 5 years">
- Rent paid: **£108,000**
- No equity built
- No maintenance costs
- Flexibility to move
- **Net cost: £108,000** (plus inflation-linked rent rises)
</Card>

<Card title="Buying — 5 years (typical)">
- Mortgage payments (interest-only portion): ~£45,000
- Deposit: £30,000 (locked in)
- Stamp duty: £0 (first-time buyer under £425k)
- Solicitor, survey, fees: ~£3,000
- Maintenance (~1% of value/yr): ~£13,500
- **Net cost: ~£61,500** — but you own a share of the flat
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="success" title="The key insight">
If you stay **5+ years**, buying usually wins financially — even after costs — because you're building equity instead of paying someone else's mortgage. If you might move in under 3 years, renting is often safer.
</Callout>

<Card title="The decision framework">
Work through these steps to decide what's right for you.

<Steps>
<Step title="How long will you stay?" status="current">
If under 3 years → renting wins. If 5+ years → buying usually wins.
</Step>
<Step title="Do you have a deposit?" status="todo">
10% of the flat price = £27k–£35k for most 1–2 bed flats. Plus £3–5k for fees.
</Step>
<Step title="Is your job stable?" status="todo">
Lenders want 3+ months of payslips. A probation period or contract role can delay a mortgage.
</Step>
<Step title="Can you handle the risk?" status="todo">
Boiler breaks, roof leaks, service charges — as an owner, that's on you. Budget 1% of the flat's value per year.
</Step>
<Step title="What's the market like where you live?" status="todo">
In London, £270k buys a studio or tiny 1-bed. Outside London, it could buy a 2-bed with a garden. Location changes everything.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Your next steps">
<Checklist title="If you're leaning toward buying" name="buying-checklist">
- [ ] Save a 10% deposit (£27k–£35k)
- [ ] Get a mortgage agreement in principle
- [ ] Research areas: check sold prices on Rightmove/Zoopla
- [ ] Factor in service charges and ground rent for flats
- [ ] Get a survey before committing
- [ ] Compare 2-year vs 5-year fixed rates
</Checklist>

<Checklist title="If you're leaning toward renting" name="renting-checklist">
- [ ] Negotiate your rent — £1,800 is high for many areas
- [ ] Invest the difference (if you save £300+/month vs buying)
- [ ] Consider a longer tenancy for stability
- [ ] Build your deposit anyway — future flexibility
</Checklist>

---

<Card title="Bottom line">
<Callout tone="success" title="My recommendation">
If you have **£30k+ saved** and plan to stay **5 years or more**, buying is likely the better financial choice — especially outside London. If you're unsure about the future, renting isn't "wasting money" — it's buying flexibility. Either way, **£1,800/month is a lot** at your salary, so look at whether you can reduce that cost first.
</Callout>
