# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon's food scene blends fresh Atlantic seafood, hearty rustic stews, and sweet eggy pastries — all best enjoyed at casual tascas, historic bakeries, and lively markets.

<Card title="The 5 Must-Try Dishes">
<Columns>
<Card title="1. Pastel de Nata">
The iconic custard tart with a caramelized, flaky crust. Best eaten warm with a sprinkle of cinnamon.
</Card>
<Card title="2. Bacalhau à Brás">
Shredded salted cod mixed with matchstick potatoes, onions, and scrambled eggs, topped with black olives.
</Card>
<Card title="3. Bifana">
A simple yet addictive sandwich of thin pork slices in a spicy garlic-paprika marinade, served on a crusty roll.
</Card>
<Card title="4. Caldeirada">
A rich fish stew with tomatoes, peppers, and potatoes, loaded with whatever the day's catch brings.
</Card>
<Card title="5. Sardinhas Assadas">
Grilled sardines — a summer staple, often eaten at street festivals with bread and salad.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat by Neighborhood">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Alfama & Castelo">
- **Casa de Pasto** – rustic tascas serving caldeirada and grilled fish.
- **Cervejaria Ramiro** (just outside, in Intendente) – famous for giant tiger prawns and garlicky clams.
- **Pastelaria Santo António** – a local spot for pastéis de nata with a view of the cathedral.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Baixa & Chiado">
- **Café A Brasileira** – historic coffeehouse (est. 1905) for a bica (espresso) and people-watching.
- **Manteigaria** – one of the best pastel de nata shops, with a visible baking counter.
- **Time Out Market** – a gourmet food hall with stalls from top Lisbon chefs.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Príncipe Real & Bairro Alto">
- **A Cevicheria** – creative Peruvian-Portuguese fusion, famous for its octopus ceviche.
- **Ramiro do Poço** (different from Cervejaria Ramiro) – a tiny, no-frills spot for grilled sardines.
- **Taberna da Rua das Flores** – a modern tasca with a daily-changing menu of small plates.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Belém">
- **Pastéis de Belém** – the original bakery (since 1837), serving warm tarts with a secret recipe.
- **A Margem** – a riverside restaurant with fresh seafood and views of the Tagus.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="A Foodie Day in Lisbon">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Pastéis & Coffee" status="done" subtitle="Start at Pastéis de Belém or Manteigaria">
Grab a warm pastel de nata and a bica. If in Belém, walk along the river after.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Market Lunch" status="done" subtitle="Time Out Market or Mercado da Ribeira">
Try a bifana from a stall, plus a glass of vinho verde. Share a plate of amêijoas à Bulhão Pato (clams in garlic-coriander sauce).
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Ginjinha Break" status="done" subtitle="A tiny shot of sour cherry liqueur">
Head to A Ginjinha in Rossio or Ginjinha Sem Rival in Bairro Alto — often served in a chocolate cup.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Seafood Dinner" status="done" subtitle="Cervejaria Ramiro or a local tasca">
Order gambas à la guilho (garlic shrimp) and a whole grilled sea bream. Finish with a prego (steak sandwich).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Drinks to Pair">
- **Ginjinha** – sour cherry liqueur, served chilled.
- **Vinho Verde** – light, slightly sparkling white wine.
- **Super Bock** – the classic Portuguese lager.
- **Medronho** – a strong firewater made from arbutus berries (for the brave).

<Callout tone="info" title="Local tip">
Don't ask for "pasteis de nata" at Pastéis de Belém — locals just order "pasteis" and everyone knows. Also, expect to eat late: lunch at 1–2pm, dinner at 8–10pm.
</Callout>
</Card>
