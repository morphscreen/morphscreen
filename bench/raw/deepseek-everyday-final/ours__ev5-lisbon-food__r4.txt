# What to Eat in Lisbon & Where to Find It

Lisbon's food scene blends fresh Atlantic seafood, hearty rustic stews, and sweet pastries — often served in tiled, bustling *tascas* (small family-run taverns). Here's the essential hit list.

<Card title="The Can't-Miss Classics">
<Columns>
<div>

### Pasteis de Nata
The iconic custard tart with a caramelized, flaky crust. Best eaten warm, dusted with cinnamon.

**Where:** [Manteigaria](https://maps.google.com/?q=Manteigaria+Lisbon) (Chiado & Time Out Market) or the legendary [Pastéis de Belém](https://maps.google.com/?q=Pasteis+de+Belem+Lisbon).

</div>
<div>

### Bacalhau à Brás
Shredded salt cod with shoestring potatoes, onions, and scrambled eggs, topped with olives. Portugal's beloved "national dish" — said to have 1,000 recipes.

**Where:** [Casa Portuguesa do Pastel de Bacalhau](https://maps.google.com/?q=Casa+Portuguesa+do+Pastel+de+Bacalhau) near the cathedral.

</div>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<div>

### Bifana
A spicy pulled-pork sandwich marinated in garlic and paprika. Street-food perfection for under €3.

**Where:** [Casa Bifanas](https://maps.google.com/?q=Casa+Bifanas+Lisbon) (Rossio) — a no-frills classic since 1925.

</div>
<div>

### Grilled Sardines
In season (May–October), whole sardines grilled over charcoal, served with boiled potatoes and salad. A summer ritual.

**Where:** [Zé da Mouraria](https://maps.google.com/?q=Ze+da+Mouraria+Lisbon) — a tiny, legendary spot in the Mouraria quarter.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Tab title="Time Out Market">

This food hall in the Mercado da Ribeira gathers 24+ of Lisbon's best chefs under one roof. Great for grazing without reservations.

- **Manteigaria** — pasteis de nata
- **Café de São Bento** — steak with cream sauce
- **Sea Me** — fresh oysters and seafood rice

<Callout tone="info" title="Tip">Go early (before 12:30) or late (after 8pm) to avoid the crowds.</Callout>

<Card title="Quick Reference">
<Columns>
<div>

### Sweet Tooth
- **Pastéis de nata** — cinnamon-dusted custard tarts
- **Ginjinha** — sour cherry liqueur, often served in a chocolate cup
- **Bolo de arroz** — light rice muffins

</div>
<div>

### Seafood Lovers
- **Arroz de marisco** — rich seafood rice (like a soupy paella)
- **Amêijoas à Bulhão Pato** — clams in garlic-cilantro sauce
- **Polvo à lagareiro** — octopus with olive oil and potatoes

</div>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="success" title="Golden Rule">Dinner starts late in Lisbon — restaurants fill up around 9–10pm. Book ahead for popular spots, and don't be shy about sharing petiscos (Portuguese tapas) to taste more.</Callout>
</Card>
