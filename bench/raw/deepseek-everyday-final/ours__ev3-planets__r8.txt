# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system is home to eight planets, each with its own unique character — from scorching rocky worlds to icy giants. Here's a guide to every one of them.

## The Inner Rocky Planets

<Card title="Mercury" subtitle="The Swiftest Planet">
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no atmosphere, so temperatures swing wildly between extreme heat and cold. A year on Mercury lasts just 88 Earth days.

<KeyValue title="Mercury at a glance">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 57.9 million km |
| Diameter | 4,879 km |
| Day length | 59 Earth days |
| Year length | 88 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Venus" subtitle="Earth's Twin">
Venus is nearly the same size as Earth but completely different. Thick clouds of sulfuric acid trap heat in a runaway greenhouse effect, making it the hottest planet — about 465°C. It also spins backwards, so the Sun rises in the west.

<KeyValue title="Venus at a glance">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 108.2 million km |
| Diameter | 12,104 km |
| Day length | 243 Earth days |
| Year length | 225 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Earth" subtitle="Our Home">
Earth is the only known planet with liquid water on its surface and life as we know it. Its atmosphere, magnetic field, and distance from the Sun all work together to make it habitable. About 71% of the surface is covered by oceans.

<KeyValue title="Earth at a glance">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 149.6 million km |
| Diameter | 12,742 km |
| Day length | 24 hours |
| Year length | 365.25 days |
| Moons | 1 |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Mars" subtitle="The Red Planet">
Mars gets its rusty color from iron oxide on its surface. It has the tallest volcano in the solar system (Olympus Mons) and a canyon system that would stretch across the US. Scientists have found evidence of ancient rivers and lakes, making it a prime target in the search for past life.

<KeyValue title="Mars at a glance">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 227.9 million km |
| Diameter | 6,779 km |
| Day length | 24.6 hours |
| Year length | 687 Earth days |
| Moons | 2 (Phobos, Deimos) |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

## The Outer Gas Giants

<Card title="Jupiter" subtitle="The King of Planets">
Jupiter is the largest planet — more than twice as massive as all the others combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. Jupiter has at least 95 moons, including Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system.

<KeyValue title="Jupiter at a glance">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 778.5 million km |
| Diameter | 139,820 km |
| Day length | 9.9 hours |
| Year length | 11.9 Earth years |
| Moons | 95+ |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn" subtitle="The Ringed Jewel">
Saturn's magnificent rings, made of ice and rock, make it the most recognizable planet. It's also the least dense — it would float in a giant bathtub. Saturn has 146 confirmed moons, including Titan, which has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane.

<KeyValue title="Saturn at a glance">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 1.43 billion km |
| Diameter | 116,460 km |
| Day length | 10.7 hours |
| Year length | 29.4 Earth years |
| Moons | 146 |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

## The Ice Giants

<Card title="Uranus" subtitle="The Tilted World">
Uranus is unique because it rotates on its side, likely from a massive ancient collision. This gives it extreme seasons — each pole gets 42 years of sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness. It's an ice giant, made mostly of water, methane, and ammonia ices.

<KeyValue title="Uranus at a glance">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 2.87 billion km |
| Diameter | 50,724 km |
| Day length | 17.2 hours |
| Year length | 84 Earth years |
| Moons | 27 |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune" subtitle="The Windy World">
Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun — dark, cold, and mysterious. It has the fastest winds in the solar system, reaching over 2,000 km/h. It was the first planet found by mathematical prediction rather than observation, in 1846.

<KeyValue title="Neptune at a glance">

|---|---|
| Distance from Sun | 4.50 billion km |
| Diameter | 49,244 km |
| Day length | 16.1 hours |
| Year length | 165 Earth years |
| Moons | 14 |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="What about Pluto?">
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 because it hasn't cleared its orbit of other debris. The solar system also has other dwarf planets, like Ceres, Eris, and Haumea.
</Callout>
