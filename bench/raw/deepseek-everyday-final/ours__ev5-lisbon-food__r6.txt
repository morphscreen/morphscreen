# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon's food scene is a love letter to the sea, the sun, and centuries of global trade. You'll find crispy cod fritters, custard tarts with shattering pastry, and grilled sardines that taste like the Atlantic. Here's your essential eating guide.

<Card title="The Icons: Must-Try Dishes">
<Columns>
<Card title="Pastéis de Nata">
The legendary custard tart with a caramelized, blistered top and flaky, crispy pastry. Best eaten warm, dusted with cinnamon.
</Card>
<Card title="Bacalhau à Brás">
Shredded salt cod, matchstick potatoes, and onions bound with scrambled eggs, topped with olives. Portugal's beloved cod dish, done right.
</Card>
<Card title="Sardinhas Assadas">
Grilled sardines, usually served at summer festivals. Simple: salt, fire, and a squeeze of lemon. Eat with your hands.
</Card>
<Card title="Bifana">
A humble but addictive sandwich: thin slices of marinated pork in a crusty roll, with spicy mustard. The ultimate street food.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat: Neighborhood by Neighborhood">

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Alfama & Castelo">
The oldest quarter, with tiny tascas and fado houses. Perfect for traditional, home-style cooking.

- **Casa de Pasto** – A tiny, family-run spot for *bacalhau à brás* and grilled meats. No menu, just ask what's fresh.
- **Cervejaria Ramiro** – A Lisbon institution (technically in nearby Intendente) famous for giant garlic shrimp, crab, and *prego* sandwiches. Expect a queue; it's worth it.
- **A Tasca do Chico** – More for fado than food, but the petiscos (small plates) are perfect with a glass of wine.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Baixa & Chiado">
The elegant downtown, packed with historic cafés and modern bakeries.

- **Pastéis de Belém** – The original custard tart bakery since 1837. Go early to avoid lines, and eat them warm with cinnamon and powdered sugar.
- **Manteigaria** – A modern rival making arguably the best *pastéis de nata* in town. Watch them being made behind glass.
- **Café A Brasileira** – A historic coffee house where poet Fernando Pessoa once sat. Order a *bica* (espresso) and a *pastel de nata*.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bairro Alto & Príncipe Real">
Nightlife and trendy eateries, but also some of the city's best traditional spots.

- **Casa da Índia** – A beloved tasca serving huge portions of *bacalhau* and grilled meats. Book ahead; it's always packed.
- **A Cevicheria** – Peruvian-Portuguese fusion, famous for its ceviche and playful desserts. A modern classic.
- **Taberna da Rua das Flores** – A tiny, no-reservations spot with a daily-changing menu of seasonal petiscos. Arrive early or be prepared to wait.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Cascais & the Coast">
A short train ride away, but worth it for fresh seafood by the sea.

- **Mar do Inferno** – A cliffside restaurant in Cascais with spectacular views and grilled fish. The name means "Sea of Hell" for the rough waters below.
- **Adega do Ribatejo** – In Carcavelos, a rustic spot known for its *sardinhas assadas* and grilled *dourada* (sea bream).
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="A Quick Bite: Street Food & Markets">
- **Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market)** – A food hall with stalls from Lisbon's best chefs. Great for trying many things at once, though it's touristy. Go for lunch, not dinner.
- **Bifanas at As Bifanas do Afonso** – A tiny counter in Baixa serving the city's most famous pork sandwich, dripping with spicy jus.
- **Gelados de Lisboa** – A local ice cream chain with creative flavors like *pastel de nata* and *figo com queijo* (fig with cheese).

<Card title="The Sweet Endings">
No trip is complete without dessert. Beyond the *pastel de nata*, try:

- **Bolo de Bolacha** – A no-bake biscuit cake with coffee and buttercream. Find it at any *pastelaria*.
- **Toucinho do Céu** – A dense almond-and-egg-yolk cake with a syrupy glaze, from convent recipes.
- **Ginjinha** – Not a dessert, but the cherry liqueur served in a chocolate cup is the perfect digestif. Try it at **A Ginjinha** in Rossio.

<Card title="Practical Tips">
- **When to eat:** Lunch is 12:30–14:30, dinner 20:00–22:30. Many places close between lunch and dinner.
- **Reservations:** For popular spots like Casa da Índia or A Cevicheria, book a day ahead.
- **Prices:** A *pastel de nata* costs about €1.50; a full meal with wine runs €20–35 per person.
- **Water:** Tap water is safe and free; ask for "água da torneira" to avoid paying for bottled.
- **Tipping:** Round up or leave 5–10% for good service; not expected but appreciated.
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Vegetarian?">
Lisbon is more veg-friendly than ever. Try **Ao 26** (vegan fine dining), **Culto** (creative plant-based), or the *francesinha* at **A Providência** – a veggie version of the famous sandwich. And don't skip the *salada de polvo* (octopus salad) if you're pescatarian.
</Callout>
