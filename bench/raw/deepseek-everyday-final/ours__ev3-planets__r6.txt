# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system is home to eight planets, each with its own unique characteristics. Here’s an overview of each, from the scorching inner worlds to the icy giants at the edge.

## The Inner Rocky Planets

<Card title="Mercury" subtitle="The Swiftest Planet">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 4,879 km |
| Distance from Sun | 57.9 million km |
| Day length | 59 Earth days |
| Year length | 88 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has virtually no atmosphere, so temperatures swing wildly between -180°C at night and 430°C during the day. Despite its proximity to the Sun, it is not the hottest planet—that title goes to Venus.

![Mercury's cratered surface as seen by NASA's MESSENGER probe](find)
</Card>

<Card title="Venus" subtitle="Earth's 'Twin'">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 12,104 km |
| Distance from Sun | 108.2 million km |
| Day length | 243 Earth days (retrograde) |
| Year length | 225 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>

Venus is nearly the same size as Earth, but it's a hostile world. Its thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide traps heat, making it the hottest planet at a scorching 465°C. It spins backwards—the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. Its surface is hidden by clouds of sulfuric acid.

![Venus's thick yellowish clouds in ultraviolet light from the Akatsuki orbiter](find)
</Card>

<Card title="Earth" subtitle="Our Home">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 12,742 km |
| Distance from Sun | 149.6 million km |
| Day length | 24 hours |
| Year length | 365.25 days |
| Moons | 1 |
</KeyValue>

Earth is the only known world with liquid water on its surface and life. Its atmosphere is 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, and its magnetic field shields us from solar radiation. About 71% of the surface is covered by oceans.

![Earth as a blue marble seen from Apollo 17](find)
</Card>

<Card title="Mars" subtitle="The Red Planet">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 6,779 km |
| Distance from Sun | 227.9 million km |
| Day length | 24.6 hours |
| Year length | 687 Earth days |
| Moons | 2 (Phobos and Deimos) |
</KeyValue>

Mars is a cold desert world with a thin atmosphere of mostly carbon dioxide. It has the tallest volcano in the solar system—Olympus Mons, 21.9 km high—and a canyon system, Valles Marineris, that would stretch across the United States. Evidence suggests it once had rivers and lakes.

![Mars's rusty surface with polar ice cap from the Hubble Space Telescope](find)
</Card>

## The Outer Gas Giants

<Card title="Jupiter" subtitle="King of the Planets">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 139,820 km |
| Distance from Sun | 778.5 million km |
| Day length | 9.9 hours |
| Year length | 11.9 Earth years |
| Moons | 95 (including Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto) |
</KeyValue>

Jupiter is the largest planet, more than twice as massive as all the others combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. Jupiter's magnetic field is 20,000 times stronger than Earth's, and its moon Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system.

![Jupiter's bands and Great Red Spot from the Juno spacecraft](find)
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn" subtitle="The Jewel of the Solar System">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 116,460 km |
| Distance from Sun | 1.43 billion km |
| Day length | 10.7 hours |
| Year length | 29.5 Earth years |
| Moons | 146 (including Titan and Enceladus) |
</KeyValue>

Saturn is famous for its stunning ring system made of ice and rock particles. It's the least dense planet—it would float in water. Its moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane, while Enceladus shoots geysers of water from its icy crust.

![Saturn with its rings in natural color from the Cassini spacecraft](find)
</Card>

<Card title="Uranus" subtitle="The Sideways Planet">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 50,724 km |
| Distance from Sun | 2.87 billion km |
| Day length | 17.2 hours (retrograde) |
| Year length | 84 Earth years |
| Moons | 28 |
</KeyValue>

Uranus is an ice giant, tilted on its side at 98 degrees—likely from a massive ancient collision. This gives it extreme seasons: each pole gets 42 years of sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness. Its atmosphere contains methane, which gives it a pale cyan color.

![Uranus's pale blue-green disk as seen from the Voyager 2 spacecraft](find)
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune" subtitle="The Windiest World">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 49,244 km |
| Distance from Sun | 4.5 billion km |
| Day length | 16.1 hours |
| Year length | 165 Earth years |
| Moons | 16 (including Triton) |
</KeyValue>

Neptune is the farthest planet, a deep blue ice giant with the fastest winds in the solar system—up to 2,100 km/h. It was the first planet found by mathematical prediction rather than observation. Its moon Triton orbits backwards and is likely a captured Kuiper Belt object.

![Neptune's deep blue atmosphere with the Great Dark Spot from Voyager 2](find)
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="What about Pluto?">
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. It orbits in the Kuiper Belt and has five moons, but it hasn't cleared its orbital neighborhood of other objects—one of the criteria for full planet status.
</Callout>
