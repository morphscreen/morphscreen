# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system is home to eight planets, each with its own unique character. They are generally divided into two groups: the four **inner rocky planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars) and the four **outer gas and ice giants** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune).

## The Inner Rocky Planets

These four planets are small, dense, and made mostly of rock and metal. They have solid surfaces and few or no moons.

<Card title="Inner planets">
<Columns>
<Card title="Mercury">
- **Diameter:** 4,879 km (smallest)
- **Day length:** 59 Earth days
- **Year length:** 88 Earth days
- **Moons:** 0
- **Fun fact:** Despite being closest to the Sun, it's not the hottest — that's Venus.
</Card>

<Card title="Venus">
- **Diameter:** 12,104 km
- **Day length:** 243 Earth days (longer than its year!)
- **Year length:** 225 Earth days
- **Moons:** 0
- **Fun fact:** A runaway greenhouse effect makes it the hottest planet, with a thick CO₂ atmosphere.
</Card>

<Card title="Earth">
- **Diameter:** 12,742 km
- **Day length:** 24 hours
- **Year length:** 365.25 days
- **Moons:** 1
- **Fun fact:** The only known place in the universe with life, and the only planet with liquid water on its surface.
</Card>

<Card title="Mars">
- **Diameter:** 6,779 km
- **Day length:** 24.6 hours
- **Year length:** 687 Earth days
- **Moons:** 2 (Phobos and Deimos)
- **Fun fact:** Home to the tallest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons (about 22 km high).
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## The Outer Giant Planets

These four planets are huge, have many moons, and are mostly gas or ice. They all have ring systems, though Saturn's are the most spectacular.

<Card title="Outer planets">
<Columns>
<Card title="Jupiter">
- **Diameter:** 139,820 km (largest)
- **Day length:** 9.9 hours (fastest spin)
- **Year length:** 11.9 Earth years
- **Moons:** 95 (including Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system)
- **Fun fact:** The Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries.
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn">
- **Diameter:** 116,460 km
- **Day length:** 10.7 hours
- **Year length:** 29.5 Earth years
- **Moons:** 146 (most of any planet)
- **Fun fact:** Its rings are made of billions of ice chunks, some as small as dust, others as big as houses.
</Card>

<Card title="Uranus">
- **Diameter:** 50,724 km
- **Day length:** 17.2 hours
- **Year length:** 84 Earth years
- **Moons:** 27
- **Fun fact:** It rotates on its side, likely knocked over by an ancient collision, so its poles point almost at the Sun.
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune">
- **Diameter:** 49,244 km
- **Day length:** 16.1 hours
- **Year length:** 165 Earth years
- **Moons:** 14
- **Fun fact:** It has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching over 2,000 km/h.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Details title="What about Pluto?" open="false">
Pluto was considered the ninth planet from 1930 until 2006, when the International Astronomical Union reclassified it as a **dwarf planet**. It didn't meet the new definition because it hasn't cleared its neighbourhood of other objects in the Kuiper Belt. Other dwarf planets include Eris, Haumea, Makemake, and Ceres.
</Details>

<Callout tone="info" title="Why are the planets different?">
The key differences come from where they formed. Close to the young Sun, it was too hot for gases like hydrogen and helium to condense, so only rocky materials could clump together. Farther out, cooler temperatures allowed huge amounts of gas and ice to accumulate, building the giant planets.
</Callout>
