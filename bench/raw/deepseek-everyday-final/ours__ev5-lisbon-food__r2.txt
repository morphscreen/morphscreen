# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon's food scene is a delicious mix of Atlantic seafood, hearty stews, and sweet pastries — all best enjoyed at casual tascas, historic bakeries, and lively markets.

<Card title="The Must-Try Dishes">
<Columns>
<div>

### Petiscos (Starters & Small Plates)

- **Amêijoas à Bulhão Pato** – clams in garlic, olive oil, and cilantro
- **Chouriço assado** – flame-grilled smoked sausage at your table
- **Peixinhos da horta** – crispy battered green beans

### Seafood

- **Bacalhau** – salt cod, said to have 1,000 recipes; try *à Brás* (shredded with potatoes, eggs, onions)
- **Sardinhas assadas** – grilled sardines, especially during June's Santo António festival
- **Arroz de marisco** – rich seafood rice, a Portuguese paella cousin

</div>
<div>

### Mains

- **Bifana** – spicy pulled-pork sandwich on crusty bread
- **Francesinha** – a towering sandwich with ham, sausage, steak, melted cheese, and beer sauce (more Porto, but Lisbon does it well)
- **Cozido à portuguesa** – a hearty boiled meat and vegetable stew

### Sweets

- **Pastel de nata** – the iconic custard tart with a caramelized top and flaky crust
- **Arroz doce** – creamy cinnamon rice pudding
- **Ginjinha** – sour cherry liqueur, often served in a chocolate cup

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

**The old-town heart** – narrow lanes, fado music, and family-run taverns.

- **Manteigaria** – arguably the best pastel de nata in town; watch them being made.
- **Casa de Pasto** – modern petiscos with a twist, in a cozy setting.
- **A Baiuca** – a tiny tasca serving rustic Alentejo dishes; book ahead.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Baixa & Chiado">
<Gallery>
![Francesinha sandwich cut open showing layers](find)
![Seafood rice in a clay pot](find)

**The downtown core** – grand plazas, shopping streets, and classic cafés.

- **A Brasileira** – a historic café, great for coffee and people-watching.
- **Cervejaria Trindade** – a beautiful beer hall in a former monastery, serving seafood and steaks.
- **O Prego da Peixaria** – gourmet *prego* (steak sandwich) spot with a modern vibe.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Cais do Sodré & Time Out Market">
<Gallery>
![Time Out Market food hall with stalls](find)
![Bifana sandwich with mustard](find)

**The riverside hub** – a lively market, trendy bars, and late-night eats.

- **Time Out Market** – a curated food hall with 30+ stalls from top chefs. Try *Croquete do Mar* for a seafood croquette.
- **Ponto Final** – a rustic dockside restaurant across the river, famous for sunset views.
- **A Cevicheria** – Peruvian-Portuguese fusion; the octopus ceviche is legendary.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Príncipe Real & Bairro Alto">

**The bohemian quarter** – boutique shops, garden cafés, and buzzing nightlife.

- **A Picanha** – a tiny spot for a perfect *bifana* and cold beer.
- **Ginjinha Espinheira** – a historic hole-in-the-wall for the cherry liqueur.
- **Casa Independente** – a mansion-turned-cultural-space with a leafy terrace.

<Card title="A Foodie Day in Lisbon">
<Steps>
<Step title="Breakfast" status="done" subtitle="9:00">
Grab a *pastel de nata* and a *galão* (milky coffee) at **Manteigaria** in Chiado.
</Step>
<Step title="Mid-morning snack" status="done" subtitle="11:00">
Wander to the **Mercado da Ribeira** (Time Out Market) for a *bifana* from a stall, or just browse.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch" status="done" subtitle="13:00">
Head to Alfama for grilled sardines or *bacalhau à Brás* at a family tasca like **Casa de Pasto**.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon treat" status="done" subtitle="16:00">
Take a tram to **Belém** and queue at **Pastéis de Belém** – the original custard tart bakery since 1837.
</Step>
<Step title="Aperitif" status="done" subtitle="18:30">
Stop at a *ginjinha* bar in Bairro Alto for a cherry liqueur in a chocolate cup.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner" status="done" subtitle="20:30">
Cross the river to **Ponto Final** for *arroz de marisco* at sunset, or stay in town for a *francesinha* at **Cervejaria Trindade**.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Practical Tips">
<Callout tone="info" title="Cash vs. Card">
Most places accept cards, but small tascas and market stalls often prefer cash. Carry €20–€30.
</Callout>

- **Reservations** – Popular spots like *A Baiuca* and *Ponto Final* book out days ahead; reserve online or by phone.
- **Timing** – Lunch is 12:30–15:00, dinner starts at 19:30–20:00. Many kitchens close between.
- **Tipping** – Not obligatory; rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good service is appreciated.
- **Portions** – *Petiscos* are meant for sharing; order 2–3 per person alongside a main.
- **Markets** – The Time Out Market gets crowded at peak hours; go early (12:00) or late (15:00).
</Card>

<Card title="Top 5 Experiences Not to Miss">
<Columns>
<div>

1. **Pastel de nata at Manteigaria** – watch the tarts being baked while you wait.
2. **Sardines in June** – during the Festas de Lisboa, streets fill with grills.
3. **A fado dinner** – traditional music with a meal in Alfama (book a casa de fado).
4. **Time Out Market crawl** – sample a dozen dishes from one counter each.
5. **Ginjinha shot** – a locals' ritual, best at dusk.

</div>
<div>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Avoid Tourist Traps">
Restaurants with photos of food in English outside, or staff aggressively beckoning on Rua das Portas de Santo Antão, are usually overpriced. Walk a block or two away for the real thing.
</Callout>

</div>
