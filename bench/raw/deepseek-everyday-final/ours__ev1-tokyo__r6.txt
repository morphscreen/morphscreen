A balanced itinerary mixing iconic sights, hidden gems, great food, and romantic moments.

<Card title="Day-by-Day Plan">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Day 1 — Arrival & Shinjuku">

<Steps>
<Step title="Arrive at Narita/Haneda" status="done" subtitle="Morning">Take the Narita Express or Skyliner into the city (about 1 hour). Check into your hotel in Shinjuku — a great base for nightlife and transport.</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ramen in Omoide Yokochō" status="done" subtitle="12:30 PM">Wander the narrow alleys of "Memory Lane" for a bowl of rich tonkotsu ramen at a tiny counter shop.</Step>
<Step title="Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building" status="done" subtitle="2:00 PM">Free observation deck with panoramic views of Mt. Fuji on clear days.</Step>
<Step title="Shinjuku Gyoen Garden" status="done" subtitle="4:00 PM">A peaceful stroll through Japanese, French, and English landscape gardens.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner & Golden Gai" status="done" subtitle="7:00 PM">Izakaya dinner, then explore the 200+ tiny bars in Golden Gai — each seating just a handful. Perfect for a cozy nightcap.</Step>
</Steps>

</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 2 — Shibuya & Harajuku">

<Steps>
<Step title="Meiji Shrine" status="current" subtitle="9:00 AM">A serene Shinto shrine tucked in a forest — a world away from the city buzz.</Step>
<Step title="Harajuku & Takeshita Street" subtitle="11:00 AM">Crepes, quirky fashion, and colorful street style. Pop into the backstreets for vintage shops and cat cafés.</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Uobei Sushi" subtitle="1:00 PM">Conveyor-belt sushi with high-tech ordering — fun and affordable.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Crossing & Hachikō Statue" subtitle="3:00 PM">The world's busiest intersection. Watch the organized chaos from the Starbucks above, then snap the loyal dog statue.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Sky" subtitle="5:30 PM">Sunset views from the rooftop observatory — magical as the city lights up.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Izakaya in Shibuya" subtitle="8:00 PM">Try *otsumami* (Japanese tapas) at a local favorite like Uoshin Noguchi.</Step>
</Steps>

</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 3 — Asakusa & Sumida River">

<Steps>
<Step title="Sensō-ji Temple" status="current" subtitle="9:00 AM">Tokyo's oldest temple. Walk through the Kaminarimon Gate and Nakamise-dori shopping street.</Step>
<Step title="Kimono Rental & Photo Walk" subtitle="11:00 AM">Rent kimonos for a couple of hours and stroll the historic streets — a memorable keepsake.</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Tempura at Daikokuya" subtitle="1:30 PM">A legendary tendon (tempura rice bowl) shop, famous for its crispy shrimp.</Step>
<Step title="Sumida River Cruise" subtitle="3:30 PM">Take a water bus to Hinode Pier — a relaxing way to see the skyline from the water.</Step>
<Step title="TeamLab Planets" subtitle="5:00 PM">Immersive digital art museum where you walk through water and gardens. Book tickets in advance.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Monjayaki in Tsukishima" subtitle="8:00 PM">Cook your own savory pancake on a griddle — a fun, interactive Tokyo specialty.</Step>
</Steps>

</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 4 — Day Trip to Hakone or Nikko">

<Columns>
<Card title="Option A: Hakone (Mount Fuji views)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Hakone Ropeway" status="current" subtitle="Morning">Cable car over volcanic valleys with stunning Fuji views (weather permitting).</Step>
<Step title="Lake Ashi Cruise" subtitle="Afternoon">Pirate-ship boat ride with the mountain as a backdrop.</Step>
<Step title="Onsen (Hot Spring)" subtitle="Evening">Soak in a ryokan's private bath — a quintessential Japanese experience.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
<Card title="Option B: Nikko (Temples & Nature)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Tōshō-gū Shrine" status="current" subtitle="Morning">A lavishly decorated UNESCO World Heritage site.</Step>
<Step title="Kegon Falls" subtitle="Afternoon">One of Japan's most spectacular waterfalls (97m).</Step>
<Step title="Lake Chūzenji" subtitle="Late afternoon">A peaceful lakeside stroll before the train back.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
</Columns>

<Callout tone="info" title="Travel time">Hakone: ~2h each way (Odakyu Romancecar). Nikko: ~2h each way (Tobu Limited Express). Both are doable as a day trip, though an overnight in Hakone's ryokan is a splurge worth considering.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Day 5 — Ginza, Akihabara & Farewell">

<Steps>
<Step title="Tsukiji Outer Market" status="current" subtitle="9:00 AM">Fresh seafood, tamagoyaki, and street snacks for a final food crawl.</Step>
<Step title="Ginza Shopping" subtitle="11:00 AM">High-end department stores (Mitsukoshi, Ginza Six) and the iconic Wako clock tower.</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Sushi at Tsukiji" subtitle="1:00 PM">One last omakase — splurge on the morning's catch.</Step>
<Step title="Akihabara Electric Town" subtitle="3:00 PM">Anime, manga, retro game shops, and themed cafés. Even if you're not into otaku culture, it's a spectacle.</Step>
<Step title="Rooftop Sunset at Asakusa" subtitle="6:00 PM">Head back to Asakusa for a final view of Tokyo Skytree from the Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center's free terrace.</Step>
<Step title="Farewell Dinner" subtitle="8:00 PM">A kaiseki (multi-course) meal at a quiet restaurant, toasting to an unforgettable trip.</Step>
</Steps>

</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Stay">
<Columns>
<Card title="Shinjuku" subtitle="Best for first-timers">
- Endless dining, nightlife, and transport links
- Hotel Gracery (Godzilla head!) or Park Hyatt for views
- Budget: $150–250/night
</Card>
<Card title="Ginza" subtitle="Best for luxury & quiet">
- Upscale, central, with easy access to Tsukiji
- Four Seasons or cheaper business hotels nearby
- Budget: $200–350/night
</Card>
<Card title="Asakusa" subtitle="Best for culture & charm">
- Traditional vibe, near Sensō-ji and the river
- Ryokan-style stays like Onsen Ryokan Asakusa
- Budget: $100–200/night
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Tab title="Cafés & Sweets">
- **% Arabica** (Shibuya) — minimalist latte art, ¥500
- **Hidemi Sugino** (Ginza) — Michelin-starred pastries
- **Toro Coffee** (Asakusa) — pour-over in a wooden machiya
- **Flipper's** (Shinjuku) — the fluffiest pancakes in town
</Tab>
<Tab title="Budget Tips">
- **Convenience stores** (7-Eleven, FamilyMart) — great breakfasts under ¥500
- **Department store basements** (Depachika) — free samples and gourmet takeaway
- **Lunch sets** are often half the dinner price at the same restaurant
- **Drink vending machines** — water is ¥100, coffee ¥120
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Practical Tips">
<Columns>
<Card title="Transport">
- **Suica/Pasmo card** — tap on/off trains, buses, and convenience stores (refundable deposit)
- **JR Yamanote Line** circles the main hubs (Shinjuku, Shibuya, Tokyo, Ueno)
- **Tokyo Metro** has two networks — Google Maps works great
- **Taxis** are expensive; only for late nights or luggage
</Card>
<Card title="Money & Language">
- **Cash is king** — many smaller shops and restaurants don't take cards
- **Japan Rail Pass** only pays off if you also travel to Kyoto/Osaka
- **English** is limited; use Google Translate's camera for menus
- **Tipping is not customary** — service is included
</Card>
<Card title="Etiquette">
- **Quiet on trains** — no phone calls, keep conversations low
- **No eating while walking** — eat at the shop or a designated spot
- **Shoes off** in temples, ryokans, and some restaurants
- **Bow** slightly when greeting — a nod suffices for foreigners
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>
