# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system is home to eight planets, each with its own unique character. They fall into two groups: the four rocky **inner planets** and the four gas and ice giants of the **outer solar system**.

## The Inner Rocky Planets

<Card title="Mercury">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 4,879 km |
| Distance from Sun | 57.9 million km |
| Day length | 59 Earth days |
| Year length | 88 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>

The smallest planet and closest to the Sun, Mercury is a cratered world of extremes. Its surface temperature swings from 430°C in daylight to −180°C at night. With no atmosphere to speak of, it looks like our Moon but is far denser, with a huge iron core.
</Card>

<Card title="Venus">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 12,104 km |
| Distance from Sun | 108.2 million km |
| Day length | 243 Earth days (retrograde) |
| Year length | 225 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>

Venus is the hottest planet, wrapped in a thick carbon-dioxide atmosphere that traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect, raising surface temperatures to 465°C. It spins backwards and so slowly that its day is longer than its year. Beneath the clouds lies a volcanic landscape with mountains and vast plains.
</Card>

<Card title="Earth">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 12,742 km |
| Distance from Sun | 149.6 million km |
| Day length | 24 hours |
| Year length | 365.25 days |
| Moons | 1 |
</KeyValue>

Our home world is the only known planet with liquid water on its surface and life. A protective magnetic field and a nitrogen–oxygen atmosphere make it unique. Its single large Moon stabilizes the tilt of its axis, giving us steady seasons.
</Card>

<Card title="Mars">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 6,779 km |
| Distance from Sun | 227.9 million km |
| Day length | 24.6 hours |
| Year length | 687 Earth days |
| Moons | 2 (Phobos and Deimos) |
</KeyValue>

The Red Planet gets its colour from iron oxide — rust — in its soil. Mars hosts the tallest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons (21 km high), and a canyon system, Valles Marineris, that would stretch across the United States. Dry riverbeds and polar ice caps hint at a wetter past.
</Card>

## The Outer Giant Planets

<Card title="Jupiter">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 139,820 km |
| Distance from Sun | 778.5 million km |
| Day length | 9.9 hours |
| Year length | 11.9 Earth years |
| Moons | 95 |
</KeyValue>

Jupiter is the largest planet — more than twice as massive as all the others combined. Its swirling bands of ammonia clouds and the Great Red Spot, a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries, make it instantly recognizable. Its many moons include Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system.
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 116,460 km |
| Distance from Sun | 1.43 billion km |
| Day length | 10.7 hours |
| Year length | 29.5 Earth years |
| Moons | 146 |
</KeyValue>

Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system, made of countless chunks of ice and rock ranging from dust grains to house-sized boulders. It is the least dense planet — it would float in water. Its moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane.
</Card>

<Card title="Uranus">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 50,724 km |
| Distance from Sun | 2.87 billion km |
| Day length | 17.2 hours (retrograde) |
| Year length | 84 Earth years |
| Moons | 27 |
</KeyValue>

Uranus is an ice giant, tilted on its side at 98 degrees, likely knocked over by an ancient collision. This gives it extreme seasons, with each pole spending 42 years in sunlight and then 42 years in darkness. Methane in its atmosphere gives it a pale cyan hue.
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune">
<KeyValue title="Fast facts">
| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 49,244 km |
| Distance from Sun | 4.50 billion km |
| Day length | 16.1 hours |
| Year length | 165 Earth years |
| Moons | 14 |
</KeyValue>

The most distant planet, Neptune is a deep blue ice giant with the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching 2,100 km/h. It was discovered in 1846 through mathematical prediction rather than direct observation. Its moon Triton orbits backwards and likely is a captured Kuiper Belt object.
</Card>

## A Quick Tour

<Steps>
<Step title="Mercury" status="done" subtitle="0–88 days">The closest planet to the Sun, a cratered, airless world with wild temperature swings.</Step>
<Step title="Venus" status="done" subtitle="88–225 days">A scorching, cloud-shrouded world with a runaway greenhouse effect.</Step>
<Step title="Earth" status="done" subtitle="225–365 days">Our home: the only known world with life, oceans and a breathable atmosphere.</Step>
<Step title="Mars" status="done" subtitle="1–2 years">A cold desert with volcanoes, canyons and signs of ancient water.</Step>
<Step title="Jupiter" status="done" subtitle="2–12 years">The giant of the solar system, with storms bigger than Earth.</Step>
<Step title="Saturn" status="done" subtitle="12–29 years">The ringed beauty, a low-density gas giant with dozens of moons.</Step>
<Step title="Uranus" status="done" subtitle="29–84 years">An ice giant tilted on its side, with extreme 42-year seasons.</Step>
<Step title="Neptune" status="done" subtitle="84–165 years">The farthest planet, dark, cold and whipped by supersonic winds.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Pluto and beyond">
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 — it shares its orbit with other Kuiper Belt objects and hasn't cleared its neighbourhood. The solar system holds five recognized dwarf planets, including Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake.
</Callout>
