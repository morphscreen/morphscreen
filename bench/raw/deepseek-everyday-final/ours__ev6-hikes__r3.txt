Greater Vancouver is blessed with world-class hiking right at its doorstep. Here are the top day hikes, from easy coastal strolls to challenging mountain summits.

## 1. Stawamus Chief (The Chief)

The most iconic hike in the region, this granite monolith towers over Squamish, about 1 hour north of Vancouver. Three peaks offer stunning views of Howe Sound and the surrounding mountains.

<Card title="The Chief at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Location | Squamish, 60 km north of Vancouver |
| Distance | 11 km round trip (all three peaks) |
| Elevation gain | 700 m |
| Time | 5–6 hours |
| Difficulty | Hard – steep, rocky, some scrambling |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Gallery>
![The Stawamus Chief peak from the base, with Howe Sound behind](find)
![View from the top of The Chief looking over Howe Sound](find)
![The steep granite face of The Chief with climbers visible](find)
</Gallery>

The Chief has three peaks, each worth the climb. **Peak 1** is the most popular, with a chain-assisted section near the top. **Peak 2** requires more scrambling but is quieter. **Peak 3** is the least crowded and offers the most solitude.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Weekend tip">
Arrive before 8:00 am on weekends – the parking lot fills up fast. The lower lots at the Shannon Falls trailhead overflow by mid-morning.
</Callout>

## 2. Garibaldi Lake

This turquoise alpine lake is the crown jewel of Garibaldi Provincial Park. The hike climbs steadily through old-growth forest before bursting out at the lake's edge, where the colour will stop you in your tracks.

<Card title="Garibaldi Lake at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Location | Garibaldi Provincial Park, 90 km north of Vancouver |
| Distance | 18 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | 810 m |
| Time | 6–7 hours |
| Difficulty | Moderate – long but steady |
| Dogs | Not allowed in the park |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Gallery>
![Garibaldi Lake's brilliant turquoise water with snow-capped peaks behind](find)
![The trail through old-growth forest on the way to Garibaldi Lake](find)
![Panorama of the lake from the Barrier viewpoint](find)
</Gallery>

The trail starts at the Rubble Creek parking lot and climbs steadily for about 6 km. Take a break at the **Barrier viewpoint** – the glacial lake below is stunning. The final stretch levels out to reveal the lake in all its glory.

<Callout tone="info" title="Extend your trip">
If you have energy left, continue to **Panorama Ridge** (another 6 km each way) for the most spectacular view in the park. Or visit **Battleship Islands** – you can paddle or swim across the lake in summer.
</Callout>

## 3. Lynn Canyon Park Loop

An easy, family-friendly hike through a lush temperate rainforest, complete with a suspension bridge (free, unlike the nearby Capilano) and swimming holes.

<Card title="Lynn Canyon at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Location | North Vancouver, 20 min from downtown |
| Distance | 5 km loop |
| Elevation gain | 100 m |
| Time | 1–2 hours |
| Difficulty | Easy – well-maintained trails |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Gallery>
![The Lynn Canyon suspension bridge over the river, surrounded by green forest](find)
![The turquoise pool at Twin Falls in Lynn Canyon](find)
![A wooden boardwalk through mossy old-growth forest in Lynn Canyon](find)
</Gallery>

The main loop takes you across the 50-metre suspension bridge, down to the river, past **Twin Falls**, and back through the forest. In summer, the 30-foot pool at Twin Falls is a favourite swimming spot.

<Callout tone="success" title="Free and close by">
Unlike the famous Capilano Suspension Bridge (which costs $60+), Lynn Canyon's bridge is **completely free**. Combine it with a visit to the Lynn Canyon Ecology Centre for a full day out.
</Callout>

## 4. Quarry Rock (Deep Cove)

A short but rewarding climb to a granite outcrop overlooking the beautiful Deep Cove inlet. Perfect for a half-day adventure with a post-hike treat at the famous Honey Doughnuts & Goodies.

<Card title="Quarry Rock at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Location | Deep Cove, North Vancouver, 30 min from downtown |
| Distance | 6 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | 200 m |
| Time | 2–3 hours |
| Difficulty | Moderate – some steep sections |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Gallery>
![The view from Quarry Rock over Deep Cove and Indian Arm](find)
![The forested trail leading up to Quarry Rock](find)
![Kayaks and paddleboards in Deep Cove, seen from above](find)
</Gallery>

The trail is mostly forested with some stone steps and roots. The payoff is the sweeping view of Indian Arm, with the village of Deep Cove far below.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Parking nightmare">
The Deep Cove lot fills by 9:00 am on sunny weekends. Park at **Panorama Park** and walk 10 minutes, or take the #212 bus from Phibbs Exchange. After your hike, reward yourself with a honey doughnut – they're legendary.
</Callout>

## 5. Panorama Ridge

The ultimate full-day hike for experienced hikers. This ridge offers a 360-degree view of Garibaldi Lake, Black Tusk, and the entire Tantalus Range.

<Card title="Panorama Ridge at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Location | Garibaldi Provincial Park, 90 km north of Vancouver |
| Distance | 30 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | 1,500 m |
| Time | 10–12 hours |
| Difficulty | Hard – long, steep, alpine terrain |
| Dogs | Not allowed in the park |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Gallery>
![The 360-degree view from Panorama Ridge over Garibaldi Lake and Black Tusk](find)
![Hikers on the ridge with the Tantalus Range in the distance](find)
![Wildflowers in the alpine meadows on the approach to Panorama Ridge](find)
</Gallery>

This is the same trailhead as Garibaldi Lake, but you continue past the lake and climb through alpine meadows to the ridge. The final 2 km are steep and can have snow well into July.

<Callout tone="danger" title="Not for beginners">
This is a **full-day commitment** with significant elevation gain. Check the weather forecast carefully, bring layers, and carry a headlamp in case you're slower than planned. Snow can linger on the upper sections until August.
</Callout>

## 6. Dog Mountain (Mount Seymour)

A quick but rewarding hike in Mount Seymour Provincial Park, offering one of the best views of the city skyline and the Fraser Valley.

<Card title="Dog Mountain at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Location | Mount Seymour Provincial Park, North Vancouver |
| Distance | 5 km round trip |
| Elevation gain | 350 m |
| Time | 2–3 hours |
| Difficulty | Moderate – some roots and mud |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Gallery>
![The view from Dog Mountain over Vancouver and the Fraser River](find)
![The rocky summit of Dog Mountain with the city below](find)
![The forested trail through Mount Seymour Provincial Park](find)
</Gallery>

The trail starts from the Mount Seymour ski area parking lot. It's mostly forested with some rocky sections, ending at a granite outcrop that feels like a secret viewpoint.

<Callout tone="info" title="Best at sunset">
This is the **best sunset hike** in the area. The city lights come on as you descend, and the view of the North Shore mountains glowing pink is unforgettable. Bring a headlamp for the return.
</Callout>

## 7. Lighthouse Park Loop

A gentle coastal hike through old-growth forest to a historic lighthouse, with views of the Strait of Georgia and the Gulf Islands.

<Card title="Lighthouse Park at a glance">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Location | West Vancouver, 30 min from downtown |
| Distance | 7 km loop |
| Elevation gain | 150 m |
| Time | 2–3 hours |
| Difficulty | Easy – mostly flat, some roots |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Gallery>
![The Point Atkinson Lighthouse surrounded by granite and forest](find)
![The coastal trail with views of the Strait of Georgia](find)

The park has several interconnected trails. The **Valley of the Giants** trail takes you past massive 500-year-old Douglas firs. The lighthouse itself is a working navigation aid, with a small museum open on weekends.

## Essential Tips

<Card title="Before you go">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check the weather" status="done" subtitle="Mountain weather changes fast">
Always check the forecast for the specific mountain, not just Vancouver. Rain in the city can mean snow on the trails.
</Step>
<Step title="Pack the 10 essentials" status="done" subtitle="Even for short hikes">
Navigation, headlamp, sun protection, first aid, knife, fire starter, extra layers, extra food, extra water, emergency shelter.
</Step>
<Step title="Start early" status="done" subtitle="Beat the crowds and afternoon storms">
Most trailheads fill by 8:00 am on weekends. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer.
</Step>
<Step title="Leave no trace" status="done" subtitle="Pack out everything">
Pack out everything, stay on trails, and use the washrooms at the trailhead – there are none on trail.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Getting there">
<KeyValue title="Transport options">
| | |
|---|---|
| Car | Best option – most trailheads have parking (arrive early!) |
| Transit | Lynn Canyon and Quarry Rock are bus-accessible from downtown |
| Rideshare | Works for popular spots, but cell service is spotty on trails |
| Tour companies | Several offer guided day trips to The Chief and Garibaldi Lake |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Season matters">
- **May–June**: Trails can be muddy; snow lingers at elevation
- **July–September**: Best conditions; alpine wildflowers peak in August
- **October–November**: Fall colours, but shorter days and rain
- **December–April**: Most high trails have snow – stick to Lynn Canyon and Lighthouse Park, or bring snowshoes for Dog Mountain
</Callout>
