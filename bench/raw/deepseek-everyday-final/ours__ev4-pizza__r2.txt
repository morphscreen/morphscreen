# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is about mastering a few key elements: a high-hydration dough, a scorching-hot oven, and quality ingredients. It's a craft, but with the right technique, you can get remarkably close to the puffy, leopard-spotted pies of Naples.

## The Dough: The Heart of the Pizza

The dough is where Neapolitan pizza is won or lost. You need "00" flour, water, salt, and yeast — nothing else.

<Card title="Ingredients for 4 Pizzas (250g each)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| "00" flour (Caputo Pizzeria is ideal) | 1000g |
| Water (room temperature) | 600-650g |
| Fine sea salt | 20-30g |
| Fresh yeast or active dry yeast | 2g fresh or 1g dry |
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix and Autolyse" status="done">
Mix the flour and water until just combined, no further. Cover and let rest for 20-30 minutes. This "autolyse" lets the flour absorb water and starts gluten development.
</Step>

<Step title="Add Salt and Yeast" status="done">
Dissolve the salt into the dough with a pinch of water, then add the yeast. Knead — by hand or with a mixer — for 10-15 minutes until the dough is smooth and elastic.
</Step>

<Step title="Bulk Ferment" status="done">
Cover the dough and let it rise at room temperature (65-70°F) for 8-12 hours. It should roughly double in volume.
</Step>

<Step title="Ball the Dough" status="done">
Gently divide the dough into 4 equal pieces (about 250g each). Shape each into a tight ball, then rest them in a covered container for another 4-6 hours at room temperature.
</Step>

<Step title="Stretch, Don't Roll" status="current">
Flatten a ball with your fingertips from the center outward, leaving a slightly thicker rim (the cornicione). Stretch by hand — never use a rolling pin, which destroys the air bubbles.
</Step>
</Steps>

> **Pro tip:** A 24-hour cold ferment in the fridge after balling develops more flavor. Take the dough out 2-3 hours before baking to warm up.

## The Oven: Getting as Hot as Possible

<Card title="Oven Options for Home Bakers">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Wood-Fired Oven">
The gold standard. If you have an outdoor wood-fired oven that reaches 800-900°F, you're in business. Bake for 60-90 seconds, turning frequently.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Ooni / Roccbox / Gozney">
These countertop pizza ovens are designed for this. They hit 900°F in 20 minutes using gas or wood pellets. They're a serious investment ($300-600) but deliver authentic results.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Home Oven + Steel">
A home oven tops out around 500-550°F, but a 1/4-inch thick baking steel preheated for an hour gets close. You'll need 4-6 minutes per pizza, and the crust will be crispier and less puffy — still delicious, but not strictly Neapolitan.
</Tab>

<Tab title="Grill">
A charcoal grill with a pizza stone can reach 600-700°F. It's a good middle ground, but managing heat is trickier.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

## The Toppings: Simple and Authentic

Neapolitan pizza is minimalist. The classic is the **Margherita**: San Marzano tomatoes, fresh mozzarella (fior di latte), fresh basil, and extra-virgin olive oil.

<Card title="The Classic Margherita">
| Ingredient | What to Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tomatoes | San Marzano DOP, crushed by hand | Sweet, low-acid, and authentic |
| Cheese | Fior di latte (fresh mozzarella), torn into pieces | Creamy, milky, melts beautifully |
| Basil | Fresh leaves, added after baking | Its aroma is delicate and shouldn't be cooked |
| Olive oil | Extra-virgin, drizzled before baking | Adds richness and helps char |
| Salt | A pinch of flaky sea salt | Brightens everything |
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="DOP vs. Regular">
If you can find San Marzano tomatoes with the DOP certification, use them. They're grown in the volcanic soil near Mount Vesuvius and have a distinctive sweet-tart flavor. If not, any good-quality whole peeled Italian tomatoes will work.
</Callout>

## The Technique: Assembly and Baking

<Steps>
<Step title="Prep the Sauce" status="done">
Crush the tomatoes by hand in a bowl. Add a pinch of salt and a drizzle of olive oil. Don't cook it — the oven does that.
</Step>

<Step title="Stretch the Dough" status="done">
On a floured surface, press the dough ball flat, then stretch from the center outward. Keep the rim thicker. Aim for a 10-12 inch disc.
</Step>

<Step title="Top It" status="done">
Spread a thin layer of sauce — about 2-3 tablespoons — leaving the rim clean. Add torn mozzarella pieces, then a few basil leaves and a drizzle of oil.
</Step>

<Step title="Launch and Bake" status="current">
If using a pizza oven, use a wooden peel to launch the pizza onto the hot stone. Bake for 60-90 seconds, rotating every 15-20 seconds for even charring. In a home oven, bake on the steel for 4-6 minutes, then finish under the broiler for 30 seconds to get some leopard spotting.
</Step>

<Step title="Finish and Serve" status="todo">
When the crust is puffy with charred spots and the cheese is bubbling, pull it out. Add fresh basil, a final drizzle of oil, and a pinch of flaky salt. Slice and eat immediately — Neapolitan pizza waits for no one.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't Do This">
- **Overworking the dough:** Handle it gently to keep the gas bubbles intact.
- **Too much sauce:** A thin layer is key; too much makes the center soggy.
- **Cold dough:** Always let the dough come to room temperature before stretching.
- **Wet mozzarella:** Pat the mozzarella dry with paper towels before topping, or your pizza will be watery.
- **Rolling with a pin:** It flattens the air pockets — stretch by hand.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

<Details title="Why is my crust not puffy?">
Your dough likely didn't ferment long enough, or your oven isn't hot enough. The puff comes from the rapid expansion of gas in the dough at high heat. Try a longer room-temperature ferment and hotter oven.
</Details>

<Details title="Why is the center soggy?">
Either too much sauce, wet mozzarella, or the pizza wasn't baked long enough. Use less sauce, dry the mozzarella, and make sure your oven is screaming hot.
</Details>

<Details title="Why does my dough tear when stretching?">
The dough is either too cold, too dry, or not well-developed. Let it warm up longer, add a bit more water next time, and be gentler when stretching.
</Details>

<Details title="Can I use regular all-purpose flour?">
You can, but you'll get a chewier, less tender crust. "00" flour is milled finer and has a lower protein content, which gives that soft, airy crumb. Bread flour is a decent substitute.
</Details>

Making Neapolitan pizza at home is a project, not a quick meal. But the payoff — a blistered, chewy, puffy crust with a bright, simple topping — is one of the most rewarding things you can pull out of an oven. Start with the dough a day ahead, get your oven as hot as it will go, and practice your stretch. Your first few might not be perfect, but by the third or fourth, you'll be hooked.
