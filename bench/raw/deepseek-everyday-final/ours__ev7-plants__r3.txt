# Houseplants that are hard to kill

Some plants practically thrive on neglect. If you're new to plant care or tend to forget watering, these resilient species are your best bet — most tolerate low light, irregular watering, and average indoor conditions.

<Card title="The top 5 nearly indestructible houseplants">
<Columns>
<div>

### 1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; let soil dry completely
- **Why it survives:** Stores water in thick leaves; tolerates drought, low light, and even fluorescent office lighting
- **Bonus:** One of the best air-purifying plants

</div>
<div>

### 2. Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** When top inch of soil is dry
- **Why it survives:** Grows in almost any light; trailing vines are forgiving if you forget to water
- **Bonus:** Easy to propagate — just cut a stem and place in water

</div>
</Columns>
<Columns>
<div>

### 3. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; very drought-tolerant
- **Why it survives:** Rhizomes store water; survives weeks without attention and low light
- **Bonus:** Glossy leaves look polished with minimal care

</div>
<div>

### 4. Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)
- **Light:** Bright indirect, tolerates low
- **Water:** Weekly; let soil dry slightly between waterings
- **Why it survives:** Hardy roots; produces baby plantlets you can share
- **Bonus:** Great for hanging baskets

</div>
</Columns>
<Columns>
<div>

### 5. Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior)
- **Light:** Very low to medium
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks
- **Why it survives:** Earned its name — tolerates neglect, temperature swings, and almost no light
- **Bonus:** Slow-growing but long-lived; a classic Victorian favorite

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snake Plant | Low–bright | Every 2–3 wks | Slow | Toxic |
| Pothos | Low–bright | Weekly | Fast | Toxic |
| ZZ Plant | Low–bright | Every 2–3 wks | Slow | Toxic |
| Spider Plant | Bright–low | Weekly | Fast | Safe |
| Cast Iron Plant | Very low–medium | Every 2–3 wks | Slow | Safe |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Pet owners">Snake plant, pothos, and ZZ plant are toxic to cats and dogs if ingested. If you have curious pets, choose spider plant or cast iron plant instead.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="How to keep them alive (even if you forget)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Choose the right spot" status="done" subtitle="Most of these tolerate low light, but avoid direct afternoon sun — it can scorch leaves.">Place near a north- or east-facing window, or a few feet from a south/west window. Snake and ZZ plants even do fine in windowless rooms.</Step>
<Step title="Water less than you think" status="done" subtitle="Overwatering is the #1 killer of houseplants.">Stick a finger into the soil. If it's dry 1–2 inches down, water thoroughly until it drains out the bottom. If still damp, wait. When in doubt, skip a week.</Step>
<Step title="Use well-draining soil" status="done" subtitle="Regular potting mix with perlite or sand works well.">A pot with drainage holes is essential — standing water causes root rot, the most common cause of death for these tough plants.</Step>
<Step title="Feed occasionally" status="done" subtitle="Once a month in spring and summer is plenty.">Use a balanced liquid houseplant fertilizer diluted to half strength. Skip fertilizing in winter when growth slows.</Step>
<Step title="Repot only when needed" status="done" subtitle="These plants like being slightly root-bound.">Repot every 1–2 years into a pot one size larger, ideally in spring. Otherwise, just refresh the top inch of soil annually.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Signs your plant is struggling">
<Columns>
<div>

**Underwatering** (usually not fatal)
- Wilting or drooping leaves
- Brown, crispy leaf tips
- Soil pulling away from pot edges

**Fix:** Water thoroughly, and it usually bounces back within a day or two.

</div>
<div>

**Overwatering** (the real danger)
- Yellowing, mushy leaves
- Soft, brown roots
- Mold on soil surface
- Foul smell from pot

**Fix:** Stop watering, let soil dry fully. If roots are rotting, repot in fresh dry soil and trim damaged roots.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to start?">
<Columns>
<div>

<Callout tone="success" title="Best first plant">**Pothos** is the most forgiving — it tells you when it's thirsty (leaves droop) and recovers quickly after a drink. It also grows fast, giving you visible reward for your care.

</div>
<div>

<Callout tone="info" title="Lowest maintenance">**Snake plant** can go a month without water in low light and still look fine. If you travel often or are prone to forgetting, this is your safest bet.

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Quick shopping checklist">
<Checklist title="Before you buy" name="plant-checklist">
- [ ] Pot with drainage holes
- [ ] Well-draining potting mix (cactus/succulent mix works for snake, ZZ, jade, aloe)
- [ ] Watering can with narrow spout (or just a cup)
- [ ] A saucer or tray to catch excess water
- [ ] Choose a spot with indirect light before bringing the plant home
</Checklist>
