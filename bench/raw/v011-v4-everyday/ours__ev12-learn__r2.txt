# How Photosynthesis Works

<Card title="Photosynthesis at a Glance">
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose (food) and oxygen. It's how most life on Earth gets its energy and oxygen.

## The Two Main Stages

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Light-Dependent Reactions">

**Where:** Thylakoid membranes inside chloroplasts  
**When:** Only in sunlight

1. **Light hits chlorophyll** — the green pigment in plants
2. **Water splits** — releasing oxygen (the O₂ we breathe)
3. **Energy is stored** — in molecules called ATP and NADPH

These energy molecules are like charged batteries, ready to power the next stage.

<Callout tone="info" title="Key point">
This stage needs light directly — that's why it's "light-dependent."
</Callout>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent)">

**Where:** Stroma (fluid) inside chloroplasts  
**When:** Can happen day or night (uses stored energy)

1. **CO₂ is captured** from the air
2. **Energy from ATP/NADPH** is used to build glucose
3. **Glucose** is formed — the plant's food

<Callout tone="info" title="Key point">
This stage doesn't need light directly, but it needs the energy made in the first stage.
</Callout>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Step by Step

<Steps>
<Step title="Sunlight hits the leaf" status="done" subtitle="Energy capture">
Chlorophyll absorbs photons (light particles). This excites electrons, starting the energy conversion.
</Step>

<Step title="Water splits" status="done" subtitle="Oxygen released">
The plant splits water molecules (H₂O) into hydrogen, electrons, and oxygen. Oxygen is released as a byproduct.
</Step>

<Step title="Energy carriers form" status="done" subtitle="ATP & NADPH">
The excited electrons power the creation of ATP and NADPH — the plant's energy currency.
</Step>

<Step title="Carbon dioxide is fixed" status="done" subtitle="Calvin cycle begins">
CO₂ from the air enters the leaf through tiny pores called stomata and is attached to a molecule called RuBP.
</Step>

<Step title="Glucose is built" status="done" subtitle="Food production">
Using ATP and NADPH energy, the plant converts the fixed carbon into glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆).
</Step>
</Steps>

## Where It Happens

<Card title="The Leaf — Photosynthesis Factory">

<KeyValue title="Leaf Parts">
| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Stomata | Tiny pores that let CO₂ in and O₂ out |
| Mesophyll cells | Where most chloroplasts are found |
| Chloroplasts | The organelles where photosynthesis happens |
| Thylakoids | Stacked membranes inside chloroplasts (light stage) |
| Stroma | Fluid inside chloroplasts (Calvin cycle) |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="success" title="Why leaves are green">
Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light best, but reflects green light — that's why leaves look green to us.
</Callout>
</Card>

## Quick Facts to Remember

<Card title="Key Takeaways">
<Checklist title="Photosynthesis essentials" name="photosynthesis-check">
- [ ] Needs sunlight, water, and CO₂
- [ ] Produces glucose and oxygen
- [ ] Happens in chloroplasts (mostly in leaves)
- [ ] Has two stages: light-dependent and Calvin cycle
- [ ] Chlorophyll makes plants green
- [ ] Powers almost all life on Earth
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Misconceptions">
| Myth | Truth |
|---|---|
| Plants get food from soil | Plants make their own food through photosynthesis |
| Photosynthesis happens only in leaves | It can also happen in green stems and some other plant parts |
| Plants only photosynthesize during the day | The light stage needs day, but the Calvin cycle can continue at night |
| Plants breathe in CO₂ and breathe out O₂ | They do both, but the net effect is CO₂ in, O₂ out during photosynthesis |
</Card>
