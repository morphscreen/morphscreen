<Card title="What buying would look like">

<Callout tone="info" title="Key assumptions">
- 25-year repayment mortgage
- Service charge: £200/month (typical for flats)
- Building insurance: £20/month
- No major repairs in year 1
</Callout>

<Details title="How the mortgage calculation works" open=false>
For a £243,000 loan (after £27k deposit) at **5% interest** over 25 years:
- Monthly repayment: ~£1,280
- Total paid over 25 years: ~£384,000
- Of which interest: ~£141,000

At **4% interest**:
- Monthly: ~£1,150
- Total paid: ~£345,000
- Interest: ~£102,000
</Details>
</Card>

<Card title="Next steps for you">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check your savings" status="current" subtitle="Do you have the deposit?">
For a £270,000 flat you need **£27,000 deposit** plus **~£3,000** for legal fees, surveys and moving costs. That's **£30,000 total upfront**.
</Step>
<Step title="Get a mortgage in principle" status="todo" subtitle="Free, no commitment">
A broker can tell you exactly how much you can borrow and at what rate. This takes 24 hours and costs nothing.
</Step>
<Step title="Compare flats in your area" status="todo" subtitle="What does £270k buy?">
Look at 3-5 flats in your price range. Check service charges and lease length (aim for 90+ years).
</Step>
<Step title="Run the numbers on your specific flat" status="todo" subtitle="Use the calculator below">
Once you find a flat, plug in the actual price, service charge and mortgage rate.

<Card title="Verdict">
<Callout tone="info" title="For you specifically">
At £1,800/month rent on a £60k salary, you're spending **50% of your take-home** on housing — that's high. Buying could cut that to ~35-40%.

**Buying makes sense if:**
- You have £30k saved
- You'll stay 5+ years
- You find a flat with reasonable service charges

**Stick with renting if:**
- You might move in under 3 years
- You don't have the deposit yet
- You value flexibility above all
</Callout>
