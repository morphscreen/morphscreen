<Card title="How to Choose the Right Fit">
<Steps>
<Step title="Know your arch type" status="current" subtitle="Wet test">
Check your footprint: if you see most of your foot, you have low arches (overpronation). A curved print with little contact means high arches (underpronation). A normal print means neutral.
</Step>
<Step title="Try them on in the evening" status="todo" subtitle="Feet swell during the day">
Leave a thumb's width (about 1 cm) between your longest toe and the shoe's end. Wear the socks you'll run in.
</Step>
<Step title="Take a test jog" status="todo" subtitle="On a treadmill or sidewalk">
Your heel should not slip. The shoe should bend where your foot bends. No hot spots or pinching.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Pro Tips for Road Running Shoes">
<Callout tone="info" title="Replace every 300–500 miles">
Road running shoes lose cushioning over time. If your knees or shins start hurting, it's probably time for a new pair.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="success" title="Buy from a store with a return policy">
Many specialty running stores let you return worn shoes within 30 days. Use that to test them on a real run.
</Callout>
</Card>
