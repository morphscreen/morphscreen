# What to eat in Lisbon (and where to find it)

Lisbon's food is all about fresh seafood, rich stews, and sweet pastries. Here's what you shouldn't miss and the best spots for each.

<Card title="The must-try dishes">
<Columns>
<Card title="Pastéis de nata">
The iconic custard tart with a crispy, flaky crust. Eat it warm, dusted with cinnamon and powdered sugar.

**Where:** **Manteigaria** (Time Out Market or Chiado) — they make them all day, so they're always fresh.
<Button ask="Tell me more about Manteigaria">More on Manteigaria</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Bacalhau à Brás">
Shredded salted cod with onions, thin fried potatoes, and scrambled eggs, topped with black olives and parsley. One of hundreds of cod dishes — the Portuguese say there's a recipe for every day of the year.

**Where:** **Tasca da Esquina** in Campo de Ourique.
<Button ask="Tell me more about Tasca da Esquina">More on Tasca da Esquina</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Caldo Verde">
A simple but soul-warming soup of shredded kale, potatoes, and chouriço sausage. A starter you'll see everywhere.

**Where:** **Cervejaria Trindade** — a beautiful old brewery in Chiado.
<Button ask="Tell me more about Cervejaria Trindade">More on Cervejaria Trindade</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato">
Clams cooked in a garlic and coriander sauce with a splash of white wine. Light, fragrant, and perfect with crusty bread.

**Where:** **Ramiro** (A Cervejaria) — Lisbon's most famous seafood house.
<Button ask="Tell me more about Ramiro">More on Ramiro</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Bifana">
A simple sandwich of thin, marinated pork slices on a soft bread roll, often with mustard or hot piri-piri sauce. Street food at its best.

**Where:** **Conga** in Alfama.
<Button ask="Tell me more about Conga">More on Conga</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Polvo à Lagareiro">
Octopus roasted with olive oil, garlic, and potatoes until tender and slightly charred. A Sunday lunch favourite.

**Where:** **O Velho Eurico** in Graça.
<Button ask="Tell me more about O Velho Eurico">More on O Velho Eurico</Button>
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Tips for eating in Lisbon">
- **Time Out Market** (Mercado da Ribeira) is great for a quick overview — many stalls under one roof, but it's touristy and pricey.
- **Tascas** (small family-run taverns) are where locals eat. Look for a handwritten menu and a full house.
- **Reservations** are essential at Ramiro and O Velho Eurico, especially on weekends.
- **Tipping** is not expected — rounding up the bill is fine.
- **Lunch** is the main meal (13:00–15:00). Many restaurants have a *prato do dia* (dish of the day) for €8–12.
</Card>
