# What to Eat in Lisbon & Where to Find It

Lisbon's food scene blends fresh seafood, bold flavours, and comforting classics. Here's your guide to the must-try dishes and the best spots to eat them.

<Card title="The 7 Must-Try Dishes">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Pastéis de Nata">
<Badge tone="success">Iconic</Badge>

The legendary custard tart: crispy, flaky pastry shell filled with a creamy, lightly caramelised egg custard, dusted with cinnamon and icing sugar.

**Where to try it:** Pastéis de Belém (the original bakery, since 1837) or Manteigaria (modern favourite in Chiado).

<Button ask="Tell me more about pastéis de nata in Lisbon">More on pastéis de nata</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bacalhau à Brás">
<Badge tone="info">Classic</Badge>

Shredded salted cod (bacalhau) sautéed with finely chopped onions, matchstick fries, and scrambled eggs, topped with black olives and fresh parsley. Portugal's national ingredient done right.

**Where to try it:** O Frade or A Tasca do João.

<Button ask="Where can I find the best bacalhau à Brás?">Find bacalhau à Brás spots</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Sardinhas Assadas">
<Badge tone="info">Seasonal</Badge>

Grilled fresh sardines, simply seasoned with sea salt, served with boiled potatoes and a salad. Best eaten during the June festivities (Festas de Lisboa), but available year-round.

**Where to try it:** Zé da Mouraria or A Casa do Sardinha.

<Button ask="Where to eat grilled sardines in Lisbon">Show sardine spots</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bifana">
<Badge tone="neutral">Street food</Badge>

A simple yet addictive sandwich: thinly sliced pork loin marinated in garlic and white wine, simmered in its juices, served on a crusty bread roll. Some add mustard or hot piri-piri sauce.

**Where to try it:** O Trevo (Alfama) or Casa das Bifanas (Rossio).

<Button ask="Tell me about the best bifanas in Lisbon">More on bifanas</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Caldo Verde">
<Badge tone="neutral">Comfort food</Badge>

Portugal's most famous soup: a silky purée of potatoes, onions, and shredded kale, finished with a drizzle of olive oil and a slice of chouriço sausage. Perfect on a cool evening.

**Where to try it:** O Velho Eurico or A Cevicheria (though known for ceviche, their caldo verde is excellent).

<Button ask="Where can I get caldo verde in Lisbon?">Find caldo verde</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Arroz de Marisco">
<Badge tone="success">Seafood feast</Badge>

A rich, brothy seafood rice packed with clams, prawns, crab, and sometimes lobster, cooked with tomatoes, garlic, coriander, and a touch of piri-piri. Served in a large pot for sharing.

**Where to try it:** Cervejaria Ramiro (iconic seafood house) or Marisqueira Uma.

<Button ask="Tell me more about arroz de marisco in Lisbon">More on seafood rice</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Ginjinha">
<Badge tone="warning">Drink</Badge>

A sweet sour cherry liqueur, served in a small chocolate cup or a shot glass. Often drunk as a digestif. Some bars serve it *com elas* (with the cherries soaked in the glass).

**Where to try it:** A Ginjinha (Largo de São Domingos) or Ginjinha Sem Rival.

</Tab>

<Columns>
<Card title="When to Eat">
- **Breakfast** (8–10am): Pastel de nata + *galão* (milky coffee)
- **Lunch** (12:30–2:30pm): *Prato do dia* (daily special) €8-12
- **Petiscos** (5–7pm): Small plates with wine or beer
- **Dinner** (8–10pm): Main meal, often seafood
- **Late night** (after 10pm): Ginjinha or a bifana

<Button ask="Tell me more about eating times in Lisbon">More on meal times</Button>
</Card>
<Card title="Money-Saving Hacks">
- **Prato do dia** at lunch is always cheaper than dinner
- **Petiscos** (small plates) let you try 3-4 dishes for the price of one main
- **Tascas** (small family-run eateries) are cheaper than restaurants
- **Time Out Market** has stalls from top chefs at lower prices
- **Avoid** restaurants with people outside trying to lure you in (tourist traps)

<Button ask="Give me more budget tips for eating in Lisbon">More budget tips</Button>
</Card>
<Card title="What to Drink">
- **Vinho Verde** – Light, slightly sparkling white wine (perfect with seafood)
- **Super Bock** or **Sagres** – National beers
- **Ginjinha** – Cherry liqueur (after dinner)
- **Porto** – Ruby or Tawny (with dessert)
- **Medronho** – Strong firewater (for the brave)
