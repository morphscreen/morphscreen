<Card title="Common Mistakes (That These Plants Forgive)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Overwatering" status="done" subtitle="#1 killer of most plants">
These plants prefer dry soil. Wait until the top 2 inches are dry before watering. Snake Plants and ZZ Plants can go a month without water in winter.
</Step>
<Step title="Too little light" status="done" subtitle="But these plants don't mind">
All five tolerate low light, though Pothos and Spider Plants grow faster with some indirect sun. Avoid direct afternoon sun — it scorches leaves.
</Step>
<Step title="Wrong pot" status="done" subtitle="Drainage is key">
Always use a pot with drainage holes. These plants rot quickly if roots sit in water. Terracotta pots help wick away excess moisture.
</Step>
<Step title="Neglecting pests" status="done" subtitle="Rare but worth checking">
Wipe leaves every few months. If you see tiny webs or sticky spots, a quick spray with soapy water fixes most issues.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="How to Rescue a Dying Plant">
<Callout tone="success" title="Don't give up yet">
Most "dead" houseplants can bounce back. Here's what to check first.
</Callout>

<Checklist title="Rescue checklist" name="rescue">
- [ ] Check for root rot: mushy, brown roots? Trim them and repot in dry soil
- [ ] Move to brighter (but indirect) light
- [ ] Stop watering for 2 weeks
- [ ] Remove dead or yellow leaves — they drain energy
- [ ] Check under leaves for pests (tiny bugs, webbing)
- [ ] Wipe dust off leaves with a damp cloth
- [ ] Wait 4 weeks before fertilizing again
</Checklist>
