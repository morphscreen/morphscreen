<Card title="Day 1: Arrival & Shinjuku Neon" subtitle="Sunday, October 26">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check in" subtitle="Hotel in Shinjuku area" status="current">
Drop bags at your hotel. Shinjuku is central and well-connected.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ramen at Omoide Yokocho" subtitle="~¥1,000/person">
Tiny alley with smoky ramen and yakitori stalls. Try the pork bone tonkotsu.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shinjuku Gyoen Garden" subtitle="¥500 entry">
Peaceful Japanese garden with a greenhouse. Great for a stroll before dark.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building" subtitle="Free observation deck">
Head up at sunset for a 360° view of Tokyo. No queue if you go 30 min before sunset.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Izakaya in Golden Gai" subtitle="~¥3,000/person">
Narrow lanes of tiny bars and eateries. Pick one with a friendly vibe.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Day 2: Shibuya & Harajuku" subtitle="Monday, October 27">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Meiji Jingu Shrine" subtitle="Free, opens at sunrise">
Walk through the forested approach to this serene Shinto shrine.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Harajuku street food" subtitle="~¥800/person">
Crepes, taiyaki (fish-shaped cake), and rainbow cotton candy on Takeshita Street.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shibuya Scramble & Hachiko Statue" subtitle="Free">
Cross the famous intersection, then see the loyal dog statue.
</Step>
<Step title="Late afternoon: Shibuya Sky" subtitle="¥2,000/person">
Rooftop observation deck with glass corners. Book online to skip queues.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Shibuya Center-gai" subtitle="~¥2,500/person">
Explore the buzzing food street. Try conveyor belt sushi or okonomiyaki.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Day 3: Asakusa & Sumida River" subtitle="Tuesday, October 28">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise-dori" subtitle="Free">
Tokyo's oldest temple. The approach street is lined with souvenir stalls.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Tempura near the temple" subtitle="~¥1,500/person">
Crispy shrimp and vegetable tempura on rice. Look for a long queue — it's a good sign.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Sumida River Cruise" subtitle="¥1,000/person">
Boat to Hamarikyu Gardens or Odaiba. Great skyline views from the water.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Tokyo Skytree" subtitle="¥2,100/person">
Tallest tower in Japan. Book sunset slot for city lights coming on.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Monja in Tsukishima" subtitle="~¥3,000/person">
Try monjayaki, a savory Tokyo pancake you cook at your table.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Day 4: Ueno & Akihabara" subtitle="Wednesday, October 29">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Ueno Park & Museums" subtitle="Park free, museums ~¥600">
Choose from Tokyo National Museum (samurai armor, ukiyo-e) or the zoo.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ameya-Yokocho Market" subtitle="~¥1,000/person">
Bustling market street under train tracks. Try fresh seafood or yakitori.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Akihabara Electric Town" subtitle="Free to explore">
Anime, manga, electronics, and arcades. Visit a multi-floor gacha machine shop.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Maid Cafe or Retro Arcade" subtitle="~¥2,000/person">
A quirky Tokyo experience. Or try the 8-floor Taito Game Station.
</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Yakiniku (BBQ) in Akihabara" subtitle="~¥3,000/person">
Grill your own premium beef at the table. All-you-can-eat options available.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Day 5: Ginza & Departure" subtitle="Thursday, October 30">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Tsukiji Outer Market" subtitle="~¥1,500/person">
Fresh sushi breakfast, grilled scallops, tamago (egg) on a stick. Arrive by 9am.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Ginza Luxury Stroll" subtitle="Free">
Window-shop flagship stores, see the Kabuki-za theatre, and visit Uniqlo's 12-floor flagship.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ginza high-end sushi" subtitle="~¥5,000/person">
Splurge on a sushi omakase set. Reservations recommended.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: TeamLab Borderless (if time)" subtitle="¥3,800/person">
Immersive digital art museum in Azabudai Hills. Book tickets in advance.
</Step>
<Step title="Head to airport" subtitle="Narita/Haneda">
Allow 1–1.5 hours by train. Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner from central Tokyo.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Essential Tips">
<Columns>
<Card title="Transport">
- **Suica/Pasmo card** — tap on/off trains and buses, also pay at convenience stores
- Buy at airport or any JR station. ¥500 deposit refundable.
- **Japan Rail Pass** not worth it for Tokyo-only trips
</Card>
<Card title="Money">
- **Cash is king** — many small shops and restaurants don't take cards
- Withdraw at 7-Eleven or Lawson ATMs (low fees)
- Budget ~¥10,000–15,000 per person per day for food, transport, entry fees
</Card>
<Card title="Etiquette">
- No talking on phones on trains
- No tipping — it's not expected
- Bow instead of handshake
- Take off shoes when entering homes and some restaurants
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Questions">
<Details title="Is the itinerary too packed?">
You can swap days or skip activities. Tokyo is best enjoyed at a relaxed pace. Consider dropping one activity per day to leave room for discovery.
</Details>
<Details title="What if we have dietary restrictions?">
Tokyo is very accommodating. Look for:
- **Vegetarian:** Buddhist temples serve shojin ryori (vegan). Many ramen shops have veggie broth.
- **Gluten-free:** Sushi (vinegared rice), sashimi, and tamago are safe. Avoid soy sauce or bring tamari.
- **Halal:** Look for halal-certified restaurants in Shinjuku and Asakusa.
</Details>
<Details title="How's the weather in late October?">
Mild and pleasant. Expect 15–20°C (59–68°F). Light jacket or sweater in the evening. Rain is possible — pack a small umbrella.
</Details>
