# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is about three things: the right dough, the right heat, and the right toppings. Let's break it down step by step.

<Card title="What You'll Need">
<Columns>
<div>
### Equipment
- Pizza steel or baking stone (1/2" thick minimum)
- Pizza peel (wooden or metal)
- Kitchen scale (digital, grams)
- Large mixing bowl
- Dough scraper
- Infrared thermometer (optional but helpful)

### Ingredients for 4 pizzas (250g each)
- **500g** "00" flour (Caputo Pizzeria or similar)
- **325g** water (65% hydration)
- **10g** fine sea salt
- **2g** active dry yeast (or 0.6g fresh yeast)
</div>
<div>
### Toppings per pizza
- **70-80g** San Marzano tomatoes (crushed by hand)
- **100-125g** fresh mozzarella di bufala (torn into pieces)
- **6-8** fresh basil leaves
- **1 tbsp** extra virgin olive oil
- Fine sea salt to taste
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Step-by-Step Process

<Steps>
<Step title="Make the dough" status="current" subtitle="Day 1, evening — 15 min active">
1. Dissolve yeast in water (room temp, 68-72°F / 20-22°C)
2. Mix flour and salt in a bowl
3. Pour water into flour, mix with a fork until shaggy
4. Knead on a clean surface for 10-15 minutes until smooth and elastic
5. Ball up, place in oiled bowl, cover with plastic wrap
6. Let rise at room temp for 8-12 hours (overnight)

**Dough temperature target:** 75-78°F / 24-26°C after mixing
</Step>
<Step title="Ball the dough" subtitle="Next morning — 5 min">
1. Turn dough onto floured surface
2. Divide into 4 equal pieces (about 250g each)
3. Shape each into a tight ball
4. Place on floured tray, cover loosely with plastic
5. Let rest 2-4 hours at room temp until doubled
</Step>
<Step title="Prepare the oven" subtitle="30 min before baking">
**For home ovens (max temp ~550°F / 290°C):**
- Place pizza steel/stone on upper rack (6-8" from broiler)
- Preheat at max temp for 45-60 minutes
- Switch to broil for 10 minutes before baking

**For outdoor pizza ovens (Ooni, Roccbox, etc.):**
- Preheat to 750-900°F / 400-480°C
- Use the infrared thermometer to check the stone temp
</Step>
<Step title="Shape the pizza" subtitle="2 min per pizza">
1. Generously flour your work surface
2. Press ball flat with fingertips, leaving a 1/2" rim
3. Stretch by draping over knuckles, rotating — **no rolling pin**
4. Target: 10-12" diameter, slightly thicker rim (cornicione)
5. Place on floured peel, shake to check it slides
</Step>
<Step title="Top and bake" subtitle="1-2 min per pizza">
1. Spread crushed tomatoes in a thin layer (not edge to edge)
2. Add torn mozzarella pieces
3. Sprinkle salt, add basil, drizzle olive oil
4. Launch onto hot stone with a quick jerk
5. **Home oven:** Bake 4-6 minutes, then broil 1-2 min until spots appear
6. **Pizza oven:** Bake 60-90 seconds, rotating halfway
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="The Key to Leoparding">
<Callout tone="info" title="The secret is heat">
Neapolitan pizza's characteristic "leoparding" — those dark, slightly charred spots — comes from **very high heat** (800°F+ / 430°C+). In a home oven, you're limited to about 550°F. To compensate:
- Use the **broiler** at the end to add top heat
- Let the steel/stone preheat for a **full hour**
- Keep pizzas **small** (10-11") so they cook through faster
</Callout>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
| Mistake | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too much flour on the peel | Burns and makes the crust bitter | Use semolina or coarse cornmeal instead |
| Over-topping | Makes the center soggy | Less is more — thin layer of sauce, scattered cheese |
| Cold dough | Doesn't stretch evenly, bubbles poorly | Let dough come to room temp (2-4 hours) |
| Rolling pin | Squeezes out gas, makes dense crust | Hand stretch only |
| Opening the oven too often | Loses heat | Check through the window, not the door |
</Card>

<Card title="Alternative: Same-Day Dough">
If you don't have time for overnight fermentation:

<Steps>
<Step title="Quick dough" subtitle="4 hours total">
1. Use **4g yeast** (double the amount)
2. Use warm water (85°F / 30°C)
3. Knead 15 minutes
4. Bulk rise: 2 hours
5. Ball and rest: 2 hours

The flavor won't be as complex, but the texture will still be good.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Troubleshooting">
<Details title="Dough is too sticky" open=false>
- Don't add more flour during kneading — it should come together
- Wet your hands instead of adding flour
- If it's truly unworkable, you added too much water next time (reduce to 60% hydration)
</Details>
<Details title="Pizza sticks to the peel" open=false>
- Use semolina or coarse cornmeal on the peel, not flour
- Work quickly — the longer it sits, the more it sticks
- Give the peel a shake between each topping addition
- If stuck, use a dough scraper to loosen it
</Details>
<Details title="Center is soggy" open=false>
- Too much sauce or cheese — use less
- Dough was too thick in the center — stretch more evenly
- Stone wasn't hot enough — preheat longer
- Try par-baking the crust for 2 minutes before adding toppings
</Details>
<Details title="Crust is too pale" open=false>
- Not enough heat — use the broiler at the end
- Too much flour on the crust — brush it off before baking
- Oven door opened too often — keep it closed
</Details>
</Card>
