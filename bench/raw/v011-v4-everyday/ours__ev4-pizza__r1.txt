# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is absolutely achievable. The key is getting the dough right and creating high heat. Let's walk through everything you need.

## The Dough Recipe

<Card title="Ingredients for 4 pizzas (280g each)">
<KeyValue title="What you need">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Tipo 00 flour (or bread flour) | 500g |
| Water (room temperature) | 325g (65% hydration) |
| Fine sea salt | 15g |
| Fresh yeast (or 3g active dry) | 3g |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="Step-by-Step Dough">
<Steps>
<Step title="Dissolve yeast" status="current" subtitle="5 minutes">Mix yeast into water until fully dissolved. Use room temperature water (20-22°C).</Step>
<Step title="Mix flour and salt" subtitle="2 minutes">In a large bowl, combine flour and salt. Make a well in the centre.</Step>
<Step title="Combine" subtitle="10 minutes">Pour yeast water into the well. Mix with one hand until it comes together into a shaggy mass.</Step>
<Step title="Knead" subtitle="15-20 minutes">Turn onto a clean surface. Knead until smooth, elastic, and passes the windowpane test. No need to add extra flour — it will come together.</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" subtitle="8-12 hours at room temp">Place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover with plastic wrap. Let rise until doubled.</Step>
<Step title="Ball up" subtitle="30 minutes rest">Divide into 4 equal pieces (about 280g each). Shape each into a tight ball. Place on a floured tray, cover, and rest 30 minutes.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Windowpane Test">
<Callout tone="info" title="How to check your dough">
Take a small piece of dough and gently stretch it between your fingers. If it stretches thin enough to see light through without tearing, it's ready. If it tears, knead 2-3 more minutes and test again.
</Callout>
</Card>

## Shaping the Pizza

<Card title="Stretching Technique">
<Steps>
<Step title="Flour your hands and surface" subtitle="Lightly, not excessively" status="current">Too much flour makes the dough slide off. Use just enough to prevent sticking.</Step>
<Step title="Press into a disc" subtitle="Use fingertips, not palm">Place a dough ball on the floured surface. Press from the centre outward, leaving a 1cm rim (the cornicione).</Step>
<Step title="Stretch from the centre" subtitle="Air in the rim">Lift the disc. Let gravity stretch it as you rotate it between your hands. Gently stretch the centre, not the rim. Aim for 10-12 inches, centre about 3mm thick.</Step>
<Step title="Transfer to peel" subtitle="Quickly">Lay the stretched dough onto a floured pizza peel. Give it a gentle shake to ensure it slides freely.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## The Sauce & Toppings

<Card title="Classic Margherita Toppings">
<Columns>
<Card title="San Marzano Sauce">
- 1 can (400g) San Marzano tomatoes
- Crush by hand
- Add a pinch of salt
- No cooking needed — it goes on raw
</Card>
<Card title="Toppings per pizza">
- 80-100g fresh mozzarella (fior di latte or buffalo)
- Tear into pieces, don't slice
- 6-8 fresh basil leaves
- Drizzle of extra virgin olive oil
- Pinch of salt
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Crucial: Low moisture">
Pat your mozzarella dry with paper towels before using. Wet cheese creates a soggy pizza and prevents proper browning.
</Callout>

## Cooking — The Biggest Challenge

Neapolitan pizza needs **430-480°C (800-900°F)** and cooks in **60-90 seconds**. Here's how to get closest at home:

<Card title="Home Oven Options">
<Compare title="Methods compared">
| Factor | Oven + Baking Steel | Oven + Pizza Stone | Cast Iron Skillet | Outdoor Pizza Oven (Ooni, Roccbox) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max temperature | 260-290°C (500-550°F) | 260-290°C | 260-290°C | 450-500°C (850-930°F) |
| Cook time | 4-6 minutes | 5-8 minutes | 3-4 minutes | 60-90 seconds |
| Crust quality | Good | Fair | Good (focaccia-like) | Excellent |
| Cost | $80-150 | $40-80 | Already own | $300-800 |
| Authenticity | 6/10 | 5/10 | 5/10 | 9/10 |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="Best Home Method: Oven + Baking Steel">
<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat for 45-60 minutes" status="current">Place baking steel on the top rack. Preheat oven to its maximum temperature (usually 260°C/500°F or "broil" setting). The steel needs this long to fully absorb heat.</Step>
<Step title="Switch to broil" subtitle="5 minutes before launching">Turn the oven to high broil. This gets the top heat intense enough to char the rim.</Step>
<Step title="Launch the pizza" subtitle="Work quickly">Slide the pizza from the peel onto the steel. Close the door immediately.</Step>
<Step title="Rotate after 2 minutes" subtitle="Even cooking">After 2 minutes, use tongs to rotate the pizza 180°. Cook another 1-3 minutes until the rim is puffed and spotted with dark brown/black blisters.</Step>
<Step title="Rest 1 minute" subtitle="Then slice">Let it rest on a wire rack for 1 minute before slicing. This prevents a soggy bottom.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Card title="Pizza Troubleshooting">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Soggy centre">
**Cause:** Too much sauce or wet mozzarella.  
**Fix:** Use 2-3 tablespoons of sauce max. Pat cheese dry. Don't overload.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Dough tears when stretching">
**Cause:** Not enough rest time or overworked gluten.  
**Fix:** Let dough balls rest 30+ minutes at room temp before shaping. If still tearing, rest 10 more minutes.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Pale, soft crust">
**Cause:** Oven not hot enough or steel/stone not preheated long enough.  
**Fix:** Preheat at least 45 minutes. Use the broiler for the last 5 minutes.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Pizza sticks to peel">
**Cause:** Too much time on peel or not enough semolina.  
**Fix:** Use coarse semolina or cornmeal on the peel (not flour). Work quickly — launch within 2 minutes of topping.
</Callout>
</Card>
