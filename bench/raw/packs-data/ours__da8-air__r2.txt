<Card title="Outdoor Exercise Advice">
<Callout tone="info" title="Moderate Air Quality — Proceed with Caution">
<strong>General population:</strong> It's generally safe to exercise outdoors. Listen to your body — reduce intensity if you experience coughing, throat irritation, or unusual fatigue.

<strong>Sensitive groups (asthma, heart/lung disease, children, older adults):</strong> Consider limiting prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors. Opt for lower-intensity activities (walking vs. running) or move workouts indoors. Keep rescue inhalers handy if you have asthma.

<strong>Timing tip:</strong> Ozone peaks mid-afternoon. Morning or evening exercise typically has lower ozone levels. PM2.5 can be elevated overnight — check the hourly forecast before early-morning sessions.

<strong>Indoor alternative:</strong> If AQI rises above 100, shift to indoor cardio (treadmill, cycling, rowing) with windows closed and air filtration running.
</Callout>
