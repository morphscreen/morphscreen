<Card title="Ingredients (serves 4)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Spaghetti | 400 g |
| Guanciale (or pancetta) | 150 g |
| Egg yolks | 4 large |
| Whole egg | 1 large |
| Pecorino Romano, finely grated | 80 g |
| Parmigiano Reggiano, finely grated | 40 g |
| Freshly ground black pepper | 1–2 tsp |
| Salt | for pasta water |
</Card>

<Card title="Method">
<Steps>
<Step title="Prep the guanciale" status="done">
Cut the guanciale into ½ cm cubes or short strips. Place in a large cold pan and set over medium‑low heat. Render slowly until the fat is translucent and the meat is crisp, 8–10 minutes. Remove pan from heat.
</Step>
<Step title="Cook the pasta" status="done">
Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add spaghetti and cook 1 minute less than the package says for al dente. Reserve 1 cup of pasta water, then drain.
</Step>
<Step title="Make the sauce" status="done">
In a bowl, whisk together egg yolks, whole egg, most of the Pecorino and Parmigiano, and a generous amount of black pepper. The mixture should be thick.
</Step>
<Step title="Combine" status="current">
Add the hot pasta to the pan with the rendered guanciale and fat. Toss over *very low* heat (or off heat) for 30 seconds. Remove from heat completely. Pour the egg‑cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing vigorously and adding splashes of reserved pasta water until a creamy emulsion forms. The residual heat cooks the eggs without scrambling.
</Step>
<Step title="Serve" status="todo">
Plate immediately. Finish with the remaining cheese and another crack of black pepper.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Key tips">
• Use guanciale for authentic flavor; pancetta works in a pinch.  
• The pan must be off the heat when you add the egg mixture — carryover heat is enough.  
• Pasta water is essential: its starch binds the sauce.  
• Eat immediately; carbonara doesn’t reheat well.
</Callout>
