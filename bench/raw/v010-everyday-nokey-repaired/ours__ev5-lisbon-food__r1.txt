# Eating in Lisbon

Lisbon is a city of small plates, fresh seafood, and custard tarts. Here's where to start.

<Card title="The short list">
| Dish | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pastel de nata | Manteigaria | Crisp puff pastry, warm custard, cinnamon dusting |
| Bifana | O Trevo | Spicy pulled pork in a crusty roll, standing at the counter |
| Sardinhas assadas | A Casa do Bacalhau | Grilled sardines with peppers and boiled potatoes |
| Bacalhau à Brás | Ramiro | Salt cod with shoestring fries, eggs, and olives |
| Ginjinha | A Ginjinha | Sour cherry liqueur in a chocolate cup, a shot at the bar |
</Card>

<Card title="Where to eat by neighborhood">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Alfama">
The oldest quarter, narrow lanes, fado houses. Go for traditional tascas.

- **A Casa do Bacalhau** — cod done every way, with a view over the Tagus
- **Cantinho do Aziz** — Mozambican-Portuguese fusion, tiny and loud
- **Pois, Café** — breakfast with fresh bread and strong coffee

<Tab title="Baixa & Chiado">
The grand center, good for a sit-down meal between sights.

- **Ramiro** — famous for garlic shrimp and giant tiger prawns, expect a queue
- **Manteigaria** — the best pastel de nata, watch them being made
- **Cervejaria Trindade** — beer hall in a former monastery, great bifanas

<Button ask="Show me more Baixa spots">More in Baixa</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Cais do Sodré">
The old docks, now buzzing with bars and casual eats.

- **O Trevo** — the bifana spot, eat it standing up
- **Time Out Market** — dozens of stalls, from octopus to sushi
- **A Ginjinha** — stop for a cherry shot before dinner

<Button ask="Show me more Cais do Sodré spots">More in Cais do Sodré</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Príncipe Real">
Trendy, with gardens and modern takes on classics.

- **A Cevicheria** — Peruvian-Portuguese ceviche, the octopus is a must
- **O Velho Eurico** — seasonal small plates, book ahead
- **Prado** — fine dining with local ingredients, worth the splurge

<Card title="Tips for eating well">
- **Go early** — lunch at 12:30, dinner at 19:30, or you'll queue
- **Share everything** — portions are generous, and the table next to you will be friendly
- **Drink the house wine** — it's cheap, local, and often better than the bottled list
- **Skip tourist-trap fado houses** — the food is bland and the price is double. Ask a local for the real ones

<Callout tone="warning" title="Watch the tourist menus">On Rua Augusta and around Praça do Comércio, "menu turístico" means frozen food and inflated prices. Walk two streets off the main drag and you'll eat better for less.</Callout>
</Card>
