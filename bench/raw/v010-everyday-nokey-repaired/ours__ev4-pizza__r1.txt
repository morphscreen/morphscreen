# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making an authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is absolutely achievable. The key is understanding the **three pillars**: the dough, the sauce, and the heat. Let's break it down.

<Card title="The Three Pillars of Neapolitan Pizza">
<Columns>
<div>
<Badge tone="info">1. Dough</Badge>

A high-hydration dough (60-65% water) made with **00 flour**, water, salt, and a tiny amount of yeast. It needs a long, slow fermentation.
</div>
<div>
<Badge tone="success">2. Sauce</Badge>

**San Marzano tomatoes** (or another high-quality whole peeled tomato), crushed by hand, with just a pinch of salt. Never cooked.
</div>
<div>
<Badge tone="warning">3. Heat</Badge>

The secret weapon. A wood-fired oven hits **900°F (480°C)** and cooks a pizza in 60-90 seconds. At home, you'll need to get as close as possible.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="The Pizza Dough (Makes 4 x 250g balls)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done">Combine 500g 00 flour, 325g water (65% hydration), 10g fine sea salt, and 2g fresh yeast (or 1g dry yeast). Mix until no dry flour remains.</Step>
<Step title="Knead" status="done">Knead for 10-15 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough should pass the windowpane test (stretch a small piece thin without tearing).</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done">Cover and let rest at room temperature for 8-12 hours, or until doubled. For more flavor, use 1g yeast and ferment 18-24 hours.</Step>
<Step title="Ball and cold ferment" status="done">Divide into 4 equal pieces, shape into tight balls, and refrigerate for 24-72 hours. This develops the complex, slightly tangy flavor.</Step>
<Step title="Temper" status="current">Remove from fridge 2-3 hours before baking. The dough should be at room temperature and relaxed when you stretch it.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="The Sauce & Assembly">
<Columns>
<div>
**The Sauce** — 1 can (400g) of San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand. Add a pinch of salt. That's it. No garlic, no oregano, no olive oil in the sauce. The tomatoes should taste bright and sweet.

**The Cheese** — Fresh mozzarella di bufala, torn into pieces, or fior di latte (cow's milk mozzarella) for a slightly drier result. Pat it dry before using.

**The Basil** — A few fresh leaves, added after baking or just before it goes in.
</div>
<div>
**Stretching** — Dust the dough ball in 00 flour, press it flat with your fingers, then stretch from the center outward, leaving a thicker **cornicione** (crust) about 1-2cm wide. Don't use a rolling pin.

**Saucing** — Spread the sauce in a circular motion, leaving the cornicione bare. Add the mozzarella, then a drizzle of **extra virgin olive oil**.

**Baking** — Slide the pizza onto a hot surface (see below) and bake until the cornicione is puffy, leopard-spotted, and charred in spots.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Getting the Heat Right at Home">
<Callout tone="warning" title="The biggest challenge">A home oven tops out around 500°F (260°C) — too low for a true 60-second Neapolitan bake. You need to maximize heat and simulate a blazing floor.</Callout>

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Best: Pizza Steel/Stone + Broiler">
Use a **1/2-inch thick steel or stone** on the top rack. Preheat for at least 1 hour at maximum temperature, then switch to **broil** for 5-10 minutes. The steel should read 600°F+ on an infrared thermometer. Bake for 4-5 minutes, rotating once. This gives a good leopard-spotting.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Great: Outdoor Pizza Oven">
A dedicated pizza oven like an **Ooni or Gozney** reaches 800-900°F in 15-20 minutes. This is the closest you'll get to a wood-fired oven at home. Use gas or wood pellets. Bake for 60-90 seconds, turning frequently.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Good: Cast Iron Skillet">
Preheat a cast iron skillet on the stovetop for 10 minutes on high, then transfer the pizza to the skillet and finish under the broiler. This is a solid fallback if you don't have a steel.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="The Classic Margherita Recipe">
Here's the full recipe for one pizza. Scale as needed.

<KeyValue title="Per Pizza">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Dough ball | 250g |
| San Marzano tomatoes | 80g (about 3 tbsp crushed) |
| Fresh mozzarella | 100g, torn and patted dry |
| Extra virgin olive oil | 1 tsp |
| Fresh basil | 2-3 leaves |
| Fine sea salt | Pinch |
</KeyValue>

<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat" status="done">Heat your steel/stone in the oven at max for 1 hour, then broil for 5-10 minutes.</Step>
<Step title="Stretch" status="done">Flour the dough, press, and stretch into a 10-12 inch round with a thicker rim.</Step>
<Step title="Top" status="done">Spread the crushed tomatoes. Add the mozzarella pieces. Drizzle with olive oil.</Step>
<Step title="Bake" status="done">Slide onto the hot steel. Bake 4-5 minutes, rotating once, until the crust is puffed and charred in spots.</Step>
<Step title="Finish" status="done">Add fresh basil leaves, a final drizzle of olive oil, and a pinch of salt. Slice and eat immediately.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Checklist title="Check your technique" name="pizza-checklist">
- [ ] Using bread flour instead of 00 flour — 00 is finer and gives a softer crumb
- [ ] Over-kneading or under-kneading — aim for a smooth, elastic dough
- [ ] Skipping the cold ferment — this is where the flavor develops
- [ ] Using a rolling pin — it crushes the air bubbles you've worked for
- [ ] Overloading with toppings — less is more; the crust should be the star
- [ ] Opening the oven door too often — let the heat build up
- [ ] Not preheating long enough — steel needs a full hour
</Checklist>
</Card>
