# Should you rent or buy a flat?

This is one of the biggest financial decisions you'll make, so let's break it down properly. With your £60k salary and £1,800 monthly rent, you're in a strong position — but the answer depends on your plans, your savings, and how long you'll stay put.

<Card title="The quick verdict">
<Callout tone="info" title="The 5-year rule">
Buying usually beats renting if you'll stay in the property **at least 5 years**. If you might move sooner, renting is often the smarter financial play — the upfront costs of buying (stamp duty, legal fees, moving costs) are too heavy to recoup in a short time.
</Callout>

<Compare title="Renting vs buying — at a glance">
| Factor | Renting | Buying |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | £2,000–£4,000 (deposit + fees) | £15,000–£40,000 (deposit + costs) |
| Monthly cost | £1,800 (fixed for term) | £1,400–£2,200 (mortgage + upkeep) |
| Maintenance | £0 — landlord's problem | £100–£300/month average |
| Freedom to move | High — notice period only | Low — selling takes months |
| Building wealth | None — money gone | Yes — equity grows |
| Market risk | None | Prices can fall |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="When buying wins — and when it doesn't">

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Buy if…">
<Checklist title="Signs you're ready to buy" name="ready-to-buy">
- [ ] You plan to stay put for **5+ years**
- [ ] You have a **10%+ deposit** saved (ideally 15–20%)
- [ ] Your job is stable and you're not likely to relocate
- [ ] You want to put down roots — decorate, renovate, have pets
- [ ] You're comfortable with maintenance responsibility
- [ ] You can afford the mortgage even if interest rates rise 2%
</Checklist>
</Tab>

<Tab title="Rent if…">
<Checklist title="Signs renting is the better call" name="keep-renting">
- [ ] You might move cities or countries within **3–5 years**
- [ ] You don't yet have a **10% deposit** saved
- [ ] Your income is variable or your job is insecure
- [ ] You value flexibility over building equity
- [ ] You don't want the hassle of repairs and maintenance
- [ ] House prices in your area look overvalued
</Checklist>
</Tab>

<Card title="Next steps">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check your savings" status="current" subtitle="Do you have a 10%+ deposit?">
If not, work out a savings plan. At £280/month saved (the difference between buying and renting), it would take you about **3 years** to build a £10,000 deposit from scratch.
</Step>
<Step title="Get a mortgage agreement in principle" status="todo" subtitle="Free, no commitment">
This shows sellers you're serious and tells you exactly what you can borrow. Most lenders offer this online in minutes.
</Step>
<Step title="Research your area's price-to-rent ratio" status="todo" subtitle="Is buying actually cheaper where you live?">
Compare similar flats' sale prices vs. rental prices in the neighbourhoods you like.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide on your time horizon" status="todo" subtitle="Will you stay 5+ years?">
If yes, buying is likely the better call. If you're unsure, keep renting and save aggressively.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't forget the hidden costs">
Service charges on flats can be £1,000–£4,000/year and can rise sharply. Always ask for the **seller's management pack** before offering, and factor in a £200–£300/month buffer for repairs and unexpected issues.
</Callout>
</Card>
