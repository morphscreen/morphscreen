# Photosynthesis: How Plants Turn Light into Food

Photosynthesis is the process where plants, algae, and some bacteria convert sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose (food) and oxygen. It's the foundation of almost all life on Earth.

<Callout tone="info" title="The Big Picture">
Every breath you take uses oxygen that came from photosynthesis, and nearly everything you eat traces its energy back to this process.
</Callout>

## The Overall Equation

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />

In words: **carbon dioxide + water + sunlight → glucose + oxygen**

<Card title="What goes in, what comes out">
<KeyValue title="Reactants (inputs)">
| Input | Role |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | Source of carbon for glucose |
| Water (H₂O) | Source of electrons and hydrogen |
| Sunlight | Energy to power the reactions |
</KeyValue>

<KeyValue title="Products (outputs)">
| Output | Role |
|---|---|
| Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) | Food/energy for the plant |
| Oxygen (O₂) | Released into the air (a byproduct) |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

## Where It Happens

Photosynthesis occurs in the **chloroplasts** — small green organelles inside plant cells. The green color comes from **chlorophyll**, the pigment that captures light.

<Tree title="Where photosynthesis happens">
- Leaf
  - Mesophyll cell
    - Chloroplast
      - Thylakoid (where light reactions occur)
      - Stroma (where the Calvin cycle occurs)
</Tree>

## The Two Stages

Photosynthesis happens in two main stages, like a factory with two shifts.

<Steps>
<Step title="Stage 1: Light-Dependent Reactions" status="current" subtitle="In the thylakoid membranes">
Sunlight splits water molecules, releasing oxygen, and produces energy-carrying molecules (ATP and NADPH). This stage literally needs light.
</Step>
<Step title="Stage 2: Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent)" status="todo" subtitle="In the stroma">
Using the ATP and NADPH from Stage 1, the plant fixes carbon dioxide into glucose. This stage can run in the dark, as long as the energy from Stage 1 is available.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="Stage 1: Light-Dependent Reactions">
| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Chlorophyll absorbs light energy |
| 2 | Water splits (photolysis): 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂ |
| 3 | Oxygen is released as a byproduct |
| 4 | Energy is stored as ATP and NADPH |
</Card>

<Card title="Stage 2: Calvin Cycle">
| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | CO₂ enters the cycle |
| 2 | Carbon is fixed using ATP and NADPH |
| 3 | Glucose is built from the fixed carbon |
| 4 | The cycle regenerates to accept more CO₂ |
</Card>

## Why Plants Are Green (and Why That Matters)

<Details title="Why green and not black?" open="true">
Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light — the colors that carry the most energy. It **reflects green light**, which is why leaves look green to us. Interestingly, green light is actually the most abundant in sunlight, so some plants have adapted to use a bit of it too, but the main pigments still favor red and blue.
</Details>

## What Limits Photosynthesis?

<Card title="Key factors">
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Light intensity | More light → faster rate (up to a saturation point) |
| CO₂ concentration | More CO₂ → faster rate (up to a limit) |
| Temperature | Optimal around 25–35°C; too hot denatures enzymes |
| Water availability | Water shortage closes stomata, limiting CO₂ intake |
</Card>

## Quick Reference: Photosynthesis at a Glance

| Feature | Light-Dependent | Calvin Cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Thylakoid membrane | Stroma |
| Needs light? | Yes | No (needs ATP/NADPH) |
| Inputs | Water, light | CO₂, ATP, NADPH |
| Outputs | O₂, ATP, NADPH | Glucose |
| Also called | Photolysis | Carbon fixation |

<Callout tone="success" title="Key takeaway">
Photosynthesis is a two-part process: first capture light energy and store it in chemical bonds (ATP/NADPH), then use that energy to build glucose from CO₂. The oxygen we breathe is just a byproduct — but a very important one!
</Callout>
