# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system has eight planets orbiting the Sun, split into two groups: the rocky **inner planets** and the gas/ice giants of the **outer solar system**. Here's a complete tour, from closest to the Sun to farthest.

<Card title="What Makes Each Planet Special">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Mercury">
- **Closest to the Sun**, but not the hottest (Venus is).
- Extreme temperature swings: scorching days, freezing nights.
- Covered in craters, looking like our Moon.
- Has almost no atmosphere.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Venus">
- **Hottest planet** due to a thick CO₂ atmosphere and runaway greenhouse effect.
- Surface pressure 92× Earth's — like being 900 m underwater.
- Spins **backwards** (sunrise in the west).
- Shrouded in sulfuric acid clouds.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Earth">
- The **only known planet with life**.
- 71% covered in liquid water.
- Active plate tectonics and a protective magnetic field.
- One large Moon, stabilizing our tilt.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Mars">
- The **Red Planet** — rusted iron in its soil.
- Has the tallest volcano (Olympus Mons, 21.9 km) and deepest canyon (Valles Marineris).
- Polar ice caps of water and CO₂.
- Prime target for human exploration.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Jupiter">
- **Largest planet** — 2.5× the mass of all others combined.
- The Great Red Spot is a storm bigger than Earth, raging for centuries.
- 95 known moons, including Ganymede (largest moon in the solar system).
- Has faint rings.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Saturn">
- Famous for its **magnificent rings** of ice and rock.
- **Least dense planet** — would float in water.
- 146 moons; Titan has a thick atmosphere and methane lakes.
- Its hexagon-shaped polar storm is a mystery.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Uranus">
- **Rotates on its side** (97.7° tilt), likely from an ancient collision.
- An "ice giant" of water, methane, and ammonia.
- Methane gives it a pale blue-green color.
- Has faint, dark rings.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Neptune">
- **Windiest planet** — supersonic winds up to 2,100 km/h.
- Deep blue due to methane.
- Discovered by math (predicted before seen) in 1846.
- Its moon Triton orbits backwards.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="What about Pluto?">
Pluto was reclassified as a **dwarf planet** in 2006 because it hasn't cleared its orbit of debris. It joins Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake in that category. It's still fascinating — a frozen world with a heart-shaped glacier.
</Callout>
