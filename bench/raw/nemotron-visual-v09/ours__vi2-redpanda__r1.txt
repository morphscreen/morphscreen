<Card title="Where it lives" subtitle="Geographic range & habitat">
**Range:** The red panda (*Ailurus fulgens*) is native to the eastern Himalayas and southwestern China.  
**Countries:** Nepal, India (Sikkim, West Bengal, Arunachal Pradesh), Bhutan, Myanmar, and China (Yunnan, Sichuan, Tibet).  
**Habitat:** Temperate broadleaf and coniferous forests with a dense understory of bamboo, at elevations of 2,200–4,800 m (7,200–15,700 ft). They prefer steep, north‑facing slopes with plenty of tree cover and access to water.  
**Home range:** 1–4 km² per individual; ranges overlap little between adults.
</Card>

<Card title="What it eats" subtitle="Diet & foraging behavior">
**Primary food:** Bamboo (especially *Arundinaria* and *Fargesia* species) – makes up ~85–95 % of the diet.  
**Supplementary foods:** Fruits, berries, acorns, roots, lichens, insects, bird eggs, and small rodents.  
**Feeding habits:**  
- Mostly crepuscular (active at dawn and dusk).  
- Uses a modified wrist bone (“false thumb”) to grasp bamboo stems.  
- Can consume up to 20 % of its body weight in bamboo each day (≈1–2 kg).  
- Selective feeder: prefers young, tender leaves and shoots.
</Card>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Major threats">
- **Habitat loss & fragmentation** from logging, agriculture, and infrastructure development.  
- **Poaching** for fur and the illegal pet trade.  
- **Climate change** shifting bamboo distributions upward in elevation.  
- **Human–wildlife conflict** (livestock grazing, free‑roaming dogs).
</Callout>

<Callout tone="success" title="Conservation actions">
- Protected areas across the range (e.g., Langtang National Park, Singalila National Park).  
- Community‑based forest management and bamboo restoration.  
- Anti‑poaching patrols and stricter wildlife‑trade enforcement.  
- Captive‑breeding programs (e.g., Red Panda Network, zoos worldwide) with reintroduction trials.
</Callout>
