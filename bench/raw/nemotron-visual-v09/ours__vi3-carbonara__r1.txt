# Spaghetti Carbonara

![Spaghetti carbonara in a white bowl with creamy sauce, pancetta, and parsley garnish](find)

<Card title="Time">
<Stat label="Prep" value="10 min" />
<Stat label="Cook" value="15 min" />
<Stat label="Total" value="25 min" />
<Stat label="Serves" value="4" />
</Card>

<Card title="Ingredients for 4">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Spaghetti | 400 g (14 oz) |
| Guanciale or pancetta | 150 g (5 oz), diced |
| Egg yolks | 4 large |
| Whole egg | 1 large |
| Pecorino Romano | 80 g (¾ cup), finely grated |
| Parmigiano Reggiano | 40 g (½ cup), finely grated |
| Black pepper | 1–2 tsp, freshly cracked |
| Salt | For pasta water |
</Card>

<Card title="Steps">
<Steps>
<Step title="Prep the pork" status="done">
Place a large cold skillet over medium heat. Add the diced guanciale (or pancetta) and render slowly until golden and crisp, 6–8 minutes. Remove with a slotted spoon; leave the fat in the pan.
</Step>
<Step title="Cook the pasta" status="done">
Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook spaghetti 1 minute shy of al dente. Reserve 1 cup pasta water, then drain.
</Step>
<Step title="Make the sauce" status="done">
In a bowl, whisk egg yolks, whole egg, both cheeses, and a generous amount of black pepper into a thick paste.
</Step>
<Step title="Combine" status="current">
Return the skillet with rendered fat to **low heat**. Add the drained pasta and a splash of pasta water; toss to coat. Remove from heat completely—let cool 30 seconds.
</Step>
<Step title="Finish" status="todo">
Pour the egg-cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing vigorously and adding more pasta water as needed to create a silky sauce. Fold in the crisp guanciale. Serve immediately with extra cheese and pepper.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Key tips">
• **Off the heat** when adding eggs—residual heat cooks them gently into a custard, not scrambled eggs.  
• **Pasta water** is essential: its starch emulsifies the sauce.  
• **Guanciale** is traditional; pancetta works well too. Avoid smoky bacon.  
• **Pecorino** brings salt and tang; Parm rounds it out. Use both for balance.
</Callout>
