*Ailurus fulgens* — the "firefox" of the Himalayas

<Card title="Where It Lives" subtitle="Native range and habitat">
**Range:** Eastern Himalayas and southwestern China — Nepal, India (Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh), Bhutan, northern Myanmar, and China (Sichuan, Yunnan, Tibet).

**Habitat:** Temperate broadleaf and coniferous forests with dense bamboo understory, at elevations of **2,200–4,800 m (7,200–15,700 ft)**. They prefer steep, north-facing slopes with access to water and old-growth trees for nesting.

<Callout tone="info" title="Key habitat requirement">Continuous canopy cover and abundant bamboo (*Arundinaria*, *Thamnocalamus*, *Phyllostachys* spp.) are essential — habitat fragmentation is a major threat.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="What It Eats" subtitle="Diet and foraging">
**Primary food:** Bamboo leaves and shoots (**85–95%** of diet). They selectively eat the most nutritious parts — tender leaves, young shoots, and leaf buds.

**Supplemental foods:** Fruits (berries, apples), acorns, roots, lichens, insects, bird eggs, and small vertebrates (occasionally).

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Feeding adaptations">
- **Pseudo-thumb** (modified wrist bone) for grasping bamboo stems
- Strong, curved claws for climbing and stripping leaves
- Low metabolic rate — spends ~13 hrs/day foraging to meet energy needs
</Callout>

| Season | Bamboo % | Other foods |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | 70% | Shoots, flowers, insects |
| Summer | 85% | Fruits, berries |
| Autumn | 90% | Acorns, roots |
| Winter | 95% | Mature leaves, lichens |
</Card>

**Major threats:**
- **Habitat loss & fragmentation** — logging, agriculture, infrastructure, livestock grazing
- **Poaching** — for fur (hats, pelts) and pet trade
- **Inbreeding depression** — small, isolated subpopulations
- **Climate change** — bamboo die-offs, upward range shift limited by mountain tops

<Callout tone="success" title="Conservation actions">
- Protected in all range countries (CITES Appendix I)
- Community-based monitoring in Nepal, India, Bhutan
- Habitat corridors & restoration projects (e.g., Red Panda Network, WWF)
- Captive breeding programs (Global Species Management Plan)
</Callout>
</Card>
