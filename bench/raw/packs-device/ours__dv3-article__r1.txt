## Summary

A team at MIT's Research Laboratory of Electronics has unveiled a superconducting quantum processor that sustains quantum coherence for 100 microseconds — ten times longer than the previous record. The advance, published today in *Nature*, uses a novel error-correction architecture and improved materials fabrication to dramatically reduce decoherence from environmental noise.

## Key Points

<Details title="🔬 Technical breakthroughs" open="true">
- **New qubit design**: Fluxonium qubits with enhanced anharmonicity reduce leakage errors
- **Materials innovation**: Tantalum-based superconducting circuits replace aluminum, cutting surface loss
- **Error correction**: Real-time feedback loop detects and corrects bit-flip errors mid-computation
- **Scalable architecture**: Modular tile design allows chipping multiple processors together
</Details>

<Details title="📈 Implications">
- **Drug discovery**: Simulating molecular interactions at scale becomes feasible
- **Cryptography**: Timeline for breaking RSA encryption potentially accelerated
- **Optimization**: Logistics, finance, and materials science problems see near-term gains
- **Investment**: Quantum hardware startups raised $2.3B in Q1 2024 alone
</Details>

<Details title="⚠️ Remaining challenges">
- Gate fidelity still below 99.9% threshold for fault-tolerant computing
- Cryogenic infrastructure costs remain prohibitive for widespread deployment
- Error-correction overhead requires ~1,000 physical qubits per logical qubit
</Details>
