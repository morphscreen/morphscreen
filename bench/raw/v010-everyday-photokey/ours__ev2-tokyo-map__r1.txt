## Day 1 — Neon, crowds & the city’s pulse

<Card title="Day 1: Shinjuku → Shibuya → Harajuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Shinjuku" status="done" subtitle="Start at the station’s west exit">
Walk the **west side** for department stores (Takashimaya, Lumine), then cross to the east for the famous **Memory Lane (Omoide Yokocho)** — tiny yakitori alleys that look frozen in time. Don’t miss the view from the **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building** (free observatory, 45th floor).
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Omoide Yokocho" status="done" subtitle="Yakitori & beer">
Order *tsukune* (chicken meatballs) and *reba* (liver) at one of the 6-seat counters. It’s tight, smoky, and perfect.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shibuya" status="done" subtitle="The scramble crossing & Hachiko">
Watch the **Shibuya Scramble** from the Starbucks in QFRONT, then walk to **Hachiko statue** (meeting point). Browse **Shibuya 109** (fashion) or **Mega Don Quijote** (souvenirs, snacks, cosmetics).
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Harajuku & Omotesando" status="done" subtitle="Takeshita Street → Omotesando">
Walk **Takeshita Street** for crepes and quirky fashion, then stroll the zelkova-lined **Omotesando** avenue (flagship stores). End at **Ura-Hara** backstreets for vintage shopping.
</Step>
<Step title="Night: Golden Gai" status="done" subtitle="Tiny bars, big character">
End in **Shinjuku’s Golden Gai** — over 200 micro-bars in 6 alleys. Pick one with 6 seats, order a highball, and chat with the owner (many speak some English).
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="Money-saver">The Tokyo Metro 24h ticket (¥800) covers Day 1–2 if you stay within central Tokyo. Get it at any metro station.</Callout>
</Card>

## Day 2 — Old Tokyo, temples & otaku culture

<Card title="Day 2: Asakusa → Ueno → Akihabara">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Asakusa" status="done" subtitle="Senso-ji temple & Nakamise-dori">
Arrive early (before 9am) to see **Senso-ji** without crowds. Walk **Nakamise-dori** shopping street for *ningyo-yaki* (little cakes) and *senbei* (rice crackers). Rent a kimono nearby if you want photos.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Ueno" status="done" subtitle="Ameto Yokocho street food">
Take the Ginza Line to Ueno. **Ameyoko** market is a chaotic, fun strip of fresh seafood, spices, and street snacks. Try *taiyaki* (fish-shaped cake with red bean).
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Ueno Park & museums" status="done" subtitle="Culture fix">
Choose one: **Tokyo National Museum** (Japanese art & samurai swords), **Ueno Zoo** (pandas), or just wander the park’s ponds and shrines.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Akihabara" status="done" subtitle="Electric town">
Explore the multi-floor electronics and anime stores. Even if you’re not into anime, the **Mandarake** complex is fascinating. Try a **maid café** if you dare (just for the experience).
</Step>
</Steps>

## Day 3 — Ginza shopping, Tsukiji sushi & Odaiba sunset

<Card title="Day 3: Ginza → Tsukiji → Odaiba">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Tsukiji Outer Market" status="done" subtitle="The best sushi breakfast">
The inner wholesale market has moved to Toyosu, but the **outer market** still has 400+ stalls. Have *tamagoyaki* (grilled egg) and *uni* (sea urchin) at a tiny counter. Go before 10am for the freshest.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Ginza" status="done" subtitle="Luxury & department stores">
Walk to Ginza (10 min). Visit **Ginza Six** (rooftop garden), **Uniqlo flagship** (12 floors), and **Itoya** stationery store (8 floors of paper heaven). For a treat, try **HIGASHIYA** for modern wagashi.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: TeamLab Planets" status="done" subtitle="Immersive art (book ahead)">
Take the Yurikamome line to Toyosu, then walk. **teamLab Planets** (book tickets weeks ahead) is a barefoot, water-filled digital art experience. It’s as good as everyone says.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Odaiba" status="done" subtitle="Sunset & futuristic views">
From teamLab, walk to **Odaiba Seaside Park** for the rainbow bridge sunset. Visit **DiverCity** for the life-size Gundam statue, and grab dinner at the mall’s food court.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Book ahead">teamLab Planets tickets sell out. Book at least 1–2 weeks in advance. Entry is timed (30-min slots).</Callout>
</Card>

## Day 4 — Parks, shrines & hipster vibes

<Card title="Day 4: Meiji Shrine → Yoyogi → Shimokitazawa">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Meiji Shrine" status="done" subtitle="Forest in the city">
Enter through the giant torii gate and walk the forested path (10 min). Write an *ema* (wooden wish plaque) and buy a *goshuin* (temple stamp). Go early — it gets busy.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Yoyogi Park" status="done" subtitle="Picnic & people-watching">
Grab a coffee and *onigiri* from a konbini and sit by the pond. On weekends you’ll see dance crews, jugglers, and cosplayers.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Harajuku backstreets" status="done" subtitle="Hidden ramen & cafes">
Skip the touristy main street. Walk **Cat Street** (parallel to Takeshita) for quieter shops. Try **Afuri** for yuzu ramen, or **The Little BAKERY Tokyo** for pastries.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shimokitazawa" status="done" subtitle="Vintage & indie">
Take the Odakyu line to **Shimokitazawa** (15 min). This is Tokyo’s Brooklyn — vintage clothing (Flamingo, Chicago), indie record stores, and tiny live houses. End with **Floresta** for animal-shaped donuts.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Day 5 — Views, art & a skyline finale

<Card title="Day 5: Roppongi → Tokyo Tower → Shibuya Sky">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Roppongi" status="done" subtitle="Art & architecture">
Visit **Mori Art Museum** (in Roppongi Hills) — the view from the 53rd floor is included. Or choose **21_21 Design Sight** for design exhibitions. Stroll the **Tokyo Midtown** complex for lunch options.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Tokyo Tower" status="done" subtitle="The classic postcard view">
Take the Oedo line to **Tokyo Tower**. Go up to the **Main Deck** (150m) — the view of Mt. Fuji on clear days is unbeatable. The **Foot Town** below has souvenir shops and a One Piece café.
</Step>
<Step title="Late afternoon: Zojo-ji Temple" status="done" subtitle="Tower + temple photo op">
Walk 5 minutes to **Zojo-ji Temple**. The contrast of the pagoda with Tokyo Tower behind it is one of the most iconic shots in the city.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Shibuya Sky" status="done" subtitle="Sunset & night views">
End at **Shibuya Sky** (rooftop observatory, 229m). Time it for **sunset** — you get day, dusk, and neon night all in one visit. Book your slot online in advance.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="Final dinner idea">After Shibuya Sky, walk 5 minutes to **Miyashita Park** — the rooftop has food stalls, a climbing wall, and even a skate park. Grab *yakiniku* or *okonomiyaki* and toast to 5 days well spent.</Callout>
</Card>

## Quick reference: transport & pass

<Card title="Getting around">
<KeyValue title="Tokyo transport basics">
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Best pass | Suica / Pasmo IC card (tap on/off all trains & buses) |
| Where to buy | Any JR East ticket machine (English available) |
| Cost | ¥500 deposit + load ¥2,000–3,000 |
| Refund | Yes, at JR East offices (¥220 fee) |
| Alternative | Tokyo Metro 24/48/72h ticket (¥800/¥1,200/¥1,500) |
| Key lines | JR Yamanote (loop), Ginza Line, Oedo Line |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="info" title="IC card tip">You can also use Suica/Pasmo at most konbini (7-Eleven, FamilyMart), vending machines, and some restaurants. Load it at any station.</Callout>
</Card>

<Checklist title="Before you go" name="tokyo-checklist">
- [ ] Book teamLab Planets tickets (Day 3)
- [ ] Book Shibuya Sky sunset slot (Day 5)
- [ ] Get a pocket Wi-Fi or eSIM (recommended: Japan Wireless, Sakura Mobile)
- [ ] Download Google Maps + offline Tokyo map
- [ ] Bring a power bank (you’ll use your phone a lot)
