# Rent or buy? Let's work it out for your numbers

You earn £60k and pay £1,800 a month in rent. The short answer: **buying usually wins long-term if you can put down a deposit and plan to stay 5+ years** — but it depends heavily on where you live and what you'd buy. Let's break it down.

<Card title="The real question: how long will you stay?">
<Columns>
<Card title="Stay 2–3 years">
- **Buying costs you more** — stamp duty, legal fees, surveys, moving costs eat your deposit
- You'd likely sell before prices recover those costs
- **Renting wins** if you might move for work or family
</Card>

<Card title="Stay 5+ years">
- Equity builds even if prices are flat
- You fix your housing cost against rent inflation
- **Buying usually wins** — you keep the growth, not your landlord
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="What you could afford">
<Plan name="Buying" price="£1,700/mo" period="mortgage (est.)" note="Based on £300k flat, 10% deposit, 4.5% rate, 25-year term" featured="true">
- **Deposit needed:** £30,000
- **Monthly mortgage:** ~£1,300–£1,500 (interest + principal)
- **Plus service charge & insurance:** £150–£250
- **Total monthly:** ~£1,700 (similar to rent)
- **You keep** the principal you pay off
</Plan>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't forget the one-off costs">
- Survey: £300–£800
- Legal fees: £1,000–£2,000
- Moving costs: £500–£1,500
- **Total upfront:** roughly £32,000–£34,000
</Callout>
