# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon is a city of bold flavors: salt cod, grilled sardines, custard tarts, and hearty stews. Here’s a practical guide to the must-tries and the best spots to find them.

<Card title="The Essential Lisbon Food Map" subtitle="Start with these icons, then explore">
<Columns>
<div>

**Starters & Snacks**
- **Pastéis de nata** – custard tarts with crispy, caramelized tops
- **Tremoços** – pickled lupini beans, eaten with beer
- **Chouriço assado** – grilled smoked sausage, often flambéed at the table

**Main Dishes**
- **Bacalhau à brás** – shredded salt cod with potatoes, eggs, and olives
- **Sardinhas assadas** – grilled sardines, best in June
- **Arroz de marisco** – seafood rice, richer than paella
- **Bifana** – pork sandwich with spicy mustard

**Sweets & Drinks**
- **Ginjinha** – sour cherry liqueur, served in a tiny cup
- **Queijada de Sintra** – sweet cheese pastry
- **Vinho verde** – young, slightly sparkling white wine

</div>
<div>

**Where to Go by Neighborhood**
- **Alfama** – old-town taverns with fado music and grilled fish
- **Baixa/Chiado** – classic cafés and pastelarias
- **Cais do Sodré** – trendy food halls and craft beer
- **LX Factory** – converted industrial complex with food trucks and markets

</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Tab title="Quick & Cheap (< €10)">

- **Casa Bifanas** – a 5-minute bifana stop.
- **Confeitaria Nacional** – for pastéis de nata and coffee, since 1829.
- **A Ginjinha** – a €1 shot at the bar.

- **Tasca do Chico** – grilled sardines and fado in Alfama (book ahead).

- **Cervejaria Ramiro** – famous for garlic shrimp and shellfish (go early, always packed).

<Card title="A Day of Eating in Lisbon" subtitle="Follow this route to hit the highlights">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Pastéis & Coffee" status="done" subtitle="Pastéis de Belém, or any pastelaria">
Start with two warm pastéis de nata and a *galão* (milky coffee). If you're at Belém, stroll the monastery gardens after.
</Step>
<Step title="Lunch: Seafood Rice" status="current" subtitle="Pinoquio or Cervejaria Ramiro">
Order arroz de marisco for two, and don't skip the garlic bread. A crisp vinho verde cuts the richness.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Bifana & Ginjinha" status="todo" subtitle="Casa Bifanas, then A Ginjinha">
A quick bifana with piri-piri, then a ginjinha shot at the bar. Walk it off through the Baixa.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Sardines & Fado" status="todo" subtitle="Tasca do Chico, Alfama">
Grilled sardines, a cold beer, and live fado. Book a table for the 8pm session.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Practical Tips" subtitle="Avoid the tourist traps">
<Columns>
<div>

**Do**
- Eat at the counter at pastelarias – it's cheaper and fresher.
- Ask for *pão e azeitonas* (bread and olives) only if you want them – they're not free.
- Carry cash for small taverns and markets.

</div>
<div>

**Don't**
- Don't eat at restaurants with photos on the menu near Praça do Comércio.
- Don't order "pasteis de nata" at a place that only sells them to tourists.
- Don't skip the *tremoços* with a beer – they're a local ritual.

</div>
</Columns>
