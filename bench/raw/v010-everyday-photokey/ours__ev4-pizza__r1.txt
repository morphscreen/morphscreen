# The Art of Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making a true Neapolitan pizza (pizza napoletana) is about respecting a few key principles: the right flour, a long cold fermentation, a blazing-hot oven, and restraint with toppings. Here's your complete guide.

<Card title="What makes it 'Neapolitan'?">
The Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (AVPN) sets the rules: dough made with type 0 or 00 flour, natural yeast or brewer's yeast, San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte mozzarella, fresh basil, and extra-virgin olive oil. The pizza must bake in a wood-fired oven at 430–480°C (800–900°F) for 60–90 seconds. The crust should be soft, elastic, with leopard-spotted charring and a puffy, slightly chewy cornicione (rim).
</Card>

## The Dough: Your Foundation

<Card title="The 24-hour dough (for 4 pizzas, ~250g each)">
This recipe follows the classic hydration and fermentation curve. Start the day before you want to eat.

| Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % |
|---|---|---|
| Caputo "00" Pizzeria flour (or similar strong 00) | 500 g | 100% |
| Water, cold (4°C/39°F) | 325 g | 65% |
| Fine sea salt | 10 g | 2% |
| Fresh yeast (or 1 g dry yeast) | 2 g | 0.4% |

**Steps:**

<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done" subtitle="Day 1, morning">Dissolve yeast in water. Add 70% of flour, mix until shaggy. Rest 20 minutes (autolyse). Add salt and remaining flour. Knead 10–15 minutes until smooth and elastic.</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="done" subtitle="Day 1, afternoon">Cover and rest at room temperature (20–22°C) for 2 hours. Fold the dough once after 1 hour.</Step>
<Step title="Divide and ball" status="done" subtitle="Day 1, evening">Divide into 4 equal pieces (~215 g each). Shape each into a tight ball. Place in a lightly oiled container, cover tightly.</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment" status="done" subtitle="Day 1, night">Refrigerate at 4°C for 18–24 hours. This develops flavor and extensibility.</Step>
<Step title="Temper" status="done" subtitle="Day 2, 2–3 hours before baking">Remove dough balls, place on floured surface, cover with a damp cloth. Let come to room temperature. They should have doubled and feel airy.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Hydration is a range">
65% is a good starting point. If your flour is weaker (lower protein), drop to 60%. If you're experienced, you can push to 70% for an even airier crumb. The dough should feel soft but not sticky after kneading.
</Callout>

## The Oven: The Hardest Part

A home oven reaches ~250°C (480°F) max—far from the 430°C+ needed for true Neapolitan. Here's how to get close:

<Columns>
<Card title="Option A: Ooni / Roccbox (recommended)">
Portable pizza ovens reach 450–500°C in 20 minutes. They run on gas or wood pellets. This is the closest you'll get to a wood-fired oven at home. <Badge tone="success">Best results</Badge>

</Card>

<Card title="Option B: Steel + broiler hack">
Place a 6mm+ baking steel on the top rack. Preheat at max for 1 hour, then switch to broiler for 10 minutes. Launch the pizza, bake 3–4 minutes, then finish under the broiler for char. It works, but the crust won't be as puffy.
</Card>

<Card title="Option C: Cast iron skillet">
Preheat a cast-iron skillet in the oven at max. Stretch the dough, lay it in the hot skillet, top quickly, and bake. The bottom crisps well but the top may need a broiler finish.
</Card>
</Columns>

## Stretching, Topping, and Baking

<Steps>
<Step title="Stretch, don't roll" status="todo" subtitle="The cornicione is sacred">On a floured surface, press the dough ball flat from the center outward, leaving the rim untouched. Then stretch by draping over your fists, rotating. Aim for a 10–12" round with a 1–2 cm puffy rim. Never use a rolling pin—it kills the air.</Step>
<Step title="Top it fast" status="todo" subtitle="Less is more">Spoon a thin layer of crushed San Marzano tomatoes (just 2–3 tablespoons). Add torn mozzarella fior di latte, 2–3 basil leaves, a drizzle of olive oil. Work quickly—the dough should not sit wet.</Step>
<Step title="Launch and bake" status="todo" subtitle="The dance">Use a well-floured peel. Slide the pizza onto the hot stone/steel. In a wood-fired oven: 60–90 seconds, rotating once. In a home oven: 3–4 minutes, then broil if needed. The bottom should be leopard-spotted, the rim puffy with slight char.</Step>
<Step title="Rest 2 minutes" status="todo" subtitle="Patience">Let it rest on a wire rack (not a plate—steam will soften the crust). Then slice, eat, repeat.</Step>
</Steps>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Card title="Troubleshooting">
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy center | Too much sauce or wet mozzarella | Drain mozzarella, pat dry, use less sauce |
| Tough, chewy crust | Over-kneaded or too much flour | Knead less, keep dough well-hydrated |
| No leopard spots | Oven not hot enough | Preheat longer, use broiler finish |
| Dough tears when stretching | Too cold or under-proofed | Let it warm fully, handle gently |
| Cornicione too thin | Pressed the rim | Leave the outer 2 cm untouched |
</Card>

## Your First Attempt: A Checklist

<Checklist title="Before you bake" name="pizza-check">
- [ ] Dough has fermented 18–24 hours in fridge
- [ ] Dough balls at room temperature for 2 hours
- [ ] Oven/steel preheated at max for at least 30 minutes
- [ ] Mozzarella drained and patted dry
- [ ] San Marzano tomatoes crushed and lightly salted
- [ ] Flour on the peel, not too much
- [ ] Pizza launches without sticking (test shake before topping)
- [ ] Timer ready for 60–90 seconds (wood-fired) or 3–4 min (home oven)
</Checklist>
