# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

Tokyo is a city that rewards curiosity—mix iconic sights with quiet neighborhoods, and leave room to wander. Here's a balanced 5-day plan built for a couple, with a mix of culture, food, nature, and nightlife.

<Callout tone="info" title="Getting around">
Get a **Suica or Pasmo IC card** at the airport—tap in and out of trains and buses, and pay at convenience stores. Most trains run from ~5am to midnight. Google Maps works well for routes.
</Callout>

<Card title="Day 1 — Arrival & Shinjuku">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Arrive & settle">Land at Narita or Haneda, pick up IC cards, and take the train to your hotel in Shinjuku. Drop bags, grab a quick lunch at a depachika (department store food hall).</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Shinjuku Gyoen">Walk off jet lag in this sprawling garden—perfect for a first stroll. Then wander the neon streets of Kabukicho (safe by day).</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Omoide Yokocho">Tiny yakitori alleys under train tracks. Order skewers and beer at a counter seat—great for people-watching and a casual dinner.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 2 — Asakusa & Sumida River">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Senso-ji Temple">Arrive early to beat crowds. Walk through the Kaminarimon gate and Nakamise shopping street—try freshly grilled senbei (rice crackers).</Step>
<Step title="Late morning" status="done" subtitle="Sumida River walk">Stroll along the river toward the Sky Tree. Cross the river for a classic view of the temple from the water.</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Tokyo Skytree">Optional: go up for panoramic views. If heights aren't your thing, the shopping complex below has great food and a planetarium.</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="River cruise">Take a Sumida River cruise back toward Asakusa at sunset—city lights reflecting on the water.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 3 — Nature & Art">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Meiji Shrine">A serene forest shrine in the middle of the city. Watch a traditional wedding procession if you're lucky.</Step>
<Step title="Late morning" status="done" subtitle="Harajuku & Omotesando">Wander Takeshita Street for quirky fashion, then walk the tree-lined Omotesando avenue for stylish cafes and shops.</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="teamLab Planets">Immersive digital art you walk through barefoot—water, light, and mirrors. Book tickets in advance; it's a favorite for couples.</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Roppongi night view">Head to the Mori Tower observation deck for a glittering skyline. Have dinner nearby—Roppongi has great izakayas.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 5 — Ueno & Departure">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Ueno Park & Museums">Visit the Tokyo National Museum (samurai armor, ukiyo-e prints) or the quieter Ueno Zoo. Cherry blossoms in spring, lotus ponds in summer.</Step>
<Step title="Lunch" status="done" subtitle="Ameyoko Market">A lively market street under train tracks—street food, fresh fruit, and souvenir shopping.</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Final wander & airport">Pick up last-minute gifts at a depachika (try the melon pan), then take the Skyliner or Narita Express to the airport.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Packing & Tips">
<Details title="What to bring" open="false">
- Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk 10–15k steps/day)
- A small towel for public restrooms (many don't have paper towels)
- A reusable bag for shopping (plastic bags cost extra)
- An umbrella—compact ones are easy to find at convenience stores
- A power adapter if you're from the US (Japan uses two-prong plugs, no ground)
</Details>

<Details title="Etiquette quick tips" open="false">
- Don't eat while walking—stand and finish, or find a spot
- Trains are quiet: no phone calls, keep conversations low
- Bow slightly when greeting or thanking
- Take off shoes when entering homes, some temples, and traditional restaurants
- Tipping is not practiced—good service is standard
</Details>
</Card>
