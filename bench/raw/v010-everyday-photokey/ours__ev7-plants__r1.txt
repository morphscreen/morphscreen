## The Details

<Card title="1. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)">
<KeyValue title="Quick facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Light | Low to bright indirect |
| Water | Every 2-3 weeks, let soil dry completely |
| Pet friendly | No (toxic to cats & dogs) |
| Growth | Slow, upright, 2-4 ft tall |
</KeyValue>

The snake plant is the ultimate survivor. It tolerates weeks of neglect, thrives in dim corners, and even purifies the air. Its thick, succulent leaves store water, so overwatering is the only real way to kill it—and even then, it forgives a lot.

<Callout tone="warning" title="The only way to kill it">Overwatering. Always let the soil dry out completely between waterings. When in doubt, skip a week.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="2. Pothos (Devil's Ivy)">
<KeyValue title="Quick facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Light | Low to bright indirect |
| Water | Every 1-2 weeks, when top inch is dry |
| Pet friendly | No |
| Growth | Trailing, fast, can grow in water |
</KeyValue>

Pothos is practically indestructible. It grows in water or soil, tolerates low light, and even tells you when it's thirsty by drooping—then perks right back up after watering. A great hanging plant or shelf spiller.

<Callout tone="info" title="Bonus">Pothos can grow in a jar of water on a windowsill. No soil, no mess, just add water every few weeks.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="3. ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas)">
<KeyValue title="Quick facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Light | Very low to bright indirect |
| Water | Every 2-3 weeks |
| Pet friendly | No |
| Growth | Slow, glossy, upright |
</KeyValue>

The ZZ plant survives where almost nothing else can—windowless offices, dark corners, and long stretches of neglect. Its rhizomes store water, so it's perfect for chronic underwaterers. It only needs watering about once a month in winter.

<Callout tone="success" title="The set-and-forget plant">Water it once a month, forget about it, and it still looks great.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="4. Spider Plant (Chlorophytum)">
<KeyValue title="Quick facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright indirect, tolerates low |
| Water | Every 1-2 weeks |
| Pet friendly | Yes ✅ |
| Growth | Arching, produces baby plantlets |
</KeyValue>

Spider plants are forgiving, fast-growing, and the only pet-safe option on this list. They produce "spiderettes" (baby plants) that you can propagate endlessly. They're happy in most conditions and just need a drink when the soil feels dry.

<Callout tone="success" title="Great for pet owners">The only truly pet-safe plant on this list. Safe for cats and dogs.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="5. Aloe Vera">
<KeyValue title="Quick facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright indirect to direct sun |
| Water | Every 3-4 weeks, let soil dry fully |
| Pet friendly | No |
| Growth | Slow, succulent, medicinal |
</KeyValue>

Aloe vera is a succulent that stores water in its thick leaves. It needs more light than the others, but it's incredibly drought-tolerant—you can literally forget about it for a month. Bonus: you get fresh gel for burns and cuts.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Needs sun">Aloe needs a bright windowsill. It will stretch and pale in low light, but won't die from neglect.</Callout>
</Card>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

<Card title="How to not kill even these">
<Steps>
<Step title="Don't overwater" status="done" subtitle="The #1 killer">Most plants die from too much water, not too little. Stick a finger in the soil—if it's moist, skip watering.</Step>
<Step title="Drainage matters" status="done">Make sure your pot has holes. Waterlogging rots roots even in tough plants.</Step>
<Step title="Dust the leaves" status="done">Wipe leaves with a damp cloth monthly so they can photosynthesize properly.</Step>
<Step title="Watch for signs" status="done">Yellow leaves = overwatered. Brown crispy tips = underwatered or too much sun.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Your new plant's care schedule">
<Checklist title="Getting started" name="plant-care">
- [ ] Buy a pot with drainage holes
- [ ] Use well-draining potting mix (add perlite)
- [ ] Place in your chosen spot (check light needs)
- [ ] Water thoroughly, then wait for soil to dry
- [ ] Set a monthly reminder on your phone
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Start small">Buy a small plant first (4" pot). They're cheaper, easier to move around, and you can learn your plant's needs before committing to a big one.</Callout>
