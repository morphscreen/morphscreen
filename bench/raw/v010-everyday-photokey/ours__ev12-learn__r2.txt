# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae, and some bacteria use to turn sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into food (glucose) and oxygen. It's the foundation of nearly all life on Earth — it produces the oxygen you breathe and the food you eat.

<Card title="The Big Picture">
<Chart type="pie" title="What goes in, what comes out">
| Substance | Role |
|---|---|
| Sunlight | Energy source |
| Water (H₂O) | Electron source, split for oxygen |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | Carbon source for glucose |
| Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) | Food for the plant |
| Oxygen (O₂) | Waste product, released |
</Chart>

The overall equation:

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />

In words: **carbon dioxide + water + sunlight → glucose + oxygen**
</Card>

<Card title="The Two Stages" subtitle="Photosynthesis happens in two linked parts">
<Steps>
<Step title="Light-dependent reactions" status="current" subtitle="In the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts">
Sunlight splits water molecules, releasing oxygen. The energy captured makes ATP and NADPH — the plant's energy currency.
</Step>
<Step title="Light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle)" status="todo" subtitle="In the stroma of chloroplasts">
Using ATP and NADPH from stage 1, the plant fixes carbon dioxide into glucose. This stage doesn't need light directly.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Inside a Leaf">
<Gallery>

![Chloroplast structure with thylakoids and stroma labelled](find)
</Gallery>

- **Stomata** — tiny pores on the leaf surface that let CO₂ in and O₂ out
- **Chloroplasts** — the organelles where photosynthesis happens
- **Chlorophyll** — the green pigment that absorbs light, mostly red and blue wavelengths (that's why leaves look green — they reflect green light)

<Callout tone="info" title="Why leaves change colour in autumn">
Chlorophyll breaks down in cold weather, revealing other pigments like carotenoids (orange/yellow) that were there all along.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Key Factors That Affect Rate">
<Columns>
<Stat label="Light intensity" value="Higher = faster" note="Up to a saturation point" icon="sun" />
<Stat label="CO₂ concentration" value="More = faster" note="Up to a saturation point" icon="wind" />
<Stat label="Temperature" value="Optimal ~25-35°C" note="Too hot denatures enzymes" icon="thermometer" />
</Columns>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Limiting factor">
At any moment, the **slowest** factor controls the rate. If light is dim, adding more CO₂ won't help — light is the limiting factor.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Try It Yourself" subtitle="A simple experiment to see photosynthesis in action">
<Steps>
<Step title="Set up" status="done" subtitle="You'll need: a fresh green leaf, a glass of water, a sunny windowsill">
Fill a glass with water and place a freshly cut leaf in it, stem down.
</Step>
<Step title="Wait and watch" status="current" subtitle="Leave it for 30-60 minutes">
Look closely at the cut end of the stem and the leaf surface. You should see tiny bubbles forming.
</Step>
<Step title="Identify the bubbles" status="todo" subtitle="What are they?">
Those bubbles are **oxygen** — proof the leaf is photosynthesising! The bubbles are oxygen gas released from splitting water molecules.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="success" title="Why this works">
Light hits the leaf, photosynthesis starts, and oxygen gas is released. The bubbles are visible proof of the process in action.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Misconceptions">
<Details title="Plants get their mass from the soil — false">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Actually...">
Most of a plant's dry mass comes from **carbon dioxide in the air**, not from soil. The carbon in glucose (and everything built from it) comes from CO₂. That's why a giant tree can grow from a tiny seed in nutrient-poor soil — it's mostly air and water.
</Callout>
</Details>
<Details title="Photosynthesis happens only in leaves — false">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Actually...">
Green stems, unripe fruits, and even some roots can photosynthesise if they contain chlorophyll. Cacti photosynthesise in their green stems, not their spines.
</Callout>
</Details>
<Details title="Plants breathe in CO₂ and breathe out O₂ all the time — false">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Actually...">
Plants also **respire** — they use oxygen and release CO₂, just like animals. In daylight, photosynthesis usually outpaces respiration, so the net effect is O₂ out. At night, plants only respire.
</Callout>
</Details>
</Card>

<Card title="Why Photosynthesis Matters to You">
- **Oxygen** — every breath you take contains oxygen produced by photosynthesis
- **Food** — all food chains start with photosynthetic organisms
- **Climate** — plants absorb CO₂, helping regulate the atmosphere
- **Wood, paper, cotton, medicines** — all ultimately come from photosynthesis
