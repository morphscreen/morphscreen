# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae, and some bacteria use to turn sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into food (glucose) and oxygen. It's the foundation of almost all life on Earth.

<Callout tone="info" title="The big picture">
Plants are essentially solar-powered sugar factories. They take two simple ingredients — water and carbon dioxide — and use sunlight as the energy source to build sugar, releasing oxygen as a byproduct.
</Callout>

## The Overall Equation

<Card title="In one line">
<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />
</Card>

That reads as: six carbon dioxide molecules + six water molecules + light energy → one glucose molecule + six oxygen molecules.

<Card title="The ingredients and outputs">
<KeyValue title="What goes in and comes out">
| Role | Substance | Where it comes from / goes |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | From the air, through leaf pores (stomata) |
| Input | Water (H₂O) | From the roots, transported up the stem |
| Input | Sunlight | Absorbed by chlorophyll (green pigment) |
| Output | Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) | Used for energy and growth |
| Output | Oxygen (O₂) | Released into the air — that's the oxygen we breathe |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

## Where It Happens

Photosynthesis occurs inside the **chloroplasts** — tiny green organelles found mainly in leaf cells. The green color comes from **chlorophyll**, the pigment that captures light energy.

<Card title="Leaf anatomy at a glance">
<Tree title="Where photosynthesis happens">
- Leaf > Epidermis (outer layer) > Stomata (pores for gas exchange)
- Leaf > Mesophyll (middle layer) > Chloroplasts > Thylakoids (where the light reaction happens)
- Leaf > Mesophyll > Chloroplasts > Stroma (where the Calvin cycle happens)
- Root > Xylem (water transport) > Leaf
</Tree>
</Card>

## The Two Main Stages

Photosynthesis happens in two linked stages: the **light-dependent reactions** and the **Calvin cycle** (light-independent reactions).

<Steps>
<Step title="Light-dependent reactions" status="current" subtitle="In the thylakoid membranes">
Sunlight hits chlorophyll, energizing electrons. This energy is used to split water molecules (releasing oxygen) and generate two energy carriers: **ATP** and **NADPH**. These reactions need light to happen.
</Step>
<Step title="Calvin cycle" status="todo" subtitle="In the stroma">
Using the ATP and NADPH from the first stage, the plant takes carbon dioxide from the air and "fixes" it into glucose — a multi-step process that doesn't directly need light, which is why it's called light-independent. The cycle regenerates its starting molecule (RuBP) to keep going.
</Step>
</Steps>

## A Simple Analogy

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Think of it like charging a battery">
The light reactions are like **charging a battery** — you plug in (sunlight) and store energy as ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle is like **using that battery** to build something useful (glucose) from raw materials (CO₂).
</Callout>

## Common Misconceptions

<Details title="'Plants breathe in CO₂ and breathe out O₂' — is that right?" open="false">
Not exactly. Plants don't "breathe" the way animals do. They take in CO₂ for photosynthesis and release O₂ as a waste product, but they also perform **cellular respiration** — using oxygen to break down glucose for energy, just like animals. During the day, photosynthesis usually dominates; at night, plants only respire.
</Details>

<Details title="'Photosynthesis only happens in leaves'" open="false">
Leaves are the main site, but any green part of the plant — stems, unripe fruits, even some roots exposed to light — can photosynthesize because they contain chlorophyll.
</Details>

## Quick Reference Card

<Card title="Key terms to remember">
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment that captures light energy |
| Stomata | Pores on leaves for gas exchange (CO₂ in, O₂ out) |
| ATP | The energy currency produced in the light reactions |
| NADPH | An electron carrier produced in the light reactions |
| Calvin cycle | The stage that builds glucose from CO₂ |
| RuBP | The molecule that grabs CO₂ to start the Calvin cycle |
</Card>
