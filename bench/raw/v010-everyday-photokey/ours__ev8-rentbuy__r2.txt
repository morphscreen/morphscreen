# Rent or buy? Let's work through it

With a £60k salary and £1,800 rent, you're in a solid position to consider both. Here's how to think it through.

<Callout tone="info" title="Quick reality check">
Your rent is about 36% of your gross income. Lenders typically allow up to 4.5× salary for a mortgage, so you could borrow around £270,000 — plus any deposit you've saved.
</Callout>

## Your monthly numbers

<Card title="Renting vs buying — typical monthly costs">
<Chart type="bar" x="Option" y="£ per month">

|---|---|
| Rent | 1800 |
| Mortgage (270k, 25yr, 4.5%) | 1500 |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Buying isn't always cheaper">
On paper your mortgage payment is lower than rent, but once you add maintenance (0.5–1% of property value per year), buildings insurance, and service charges for flats, the gap narrows — or disappears.
</Callout>
</Card>

## What matters most for you

<Columns>
<Card title="Renting suits you if…">
- You value flexibility — moving for work or life changes is easy
- You don't have a deposit saved (aim for at least 10% of the price)
- You'd rather invest your savings elsewhere
- You're not sure you'll stay in the same city for 5+ years
- You want predictable costs — no surprise boiler repairs
</Card>

<Card title="Buying suits you if…">
- You're settled and plan to stay put for 5+ years
- You have a deposit ready (10–20% of the price)
- You want to build equity instead of paying someone else's mortgage
- You're comfortable with maintenance responsibilities
- You want the freedom to decorate and renovate
</Card>
</Columns>

## The 5-year rule

<Card title="Break-even timeline">
<Chart type="area" x="Years" y="Cumulative cost (£)">
| Year | Renting total | Buying total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 21600 | 23500 |
| 2 | 43200 | 45500 |
| 3 | 64800 | 67500 |
| 4 | 86400 | 89500 |
| 5 | 108000 | 111500 |
| 6 | 129600 | 128000 |
| 7 | 151200 | 144500 |
| 8 | 172800 | 161000 |
</Chart>

<Callout tone="success" title="The rule of thumb">
If you'll stay in the same home for **5 years or more**, buying usually wins. If you might move sooner, renting is often the smarter financial choice — the upfront costs of buying (stamp duty, solicitors, surveys) take years to recover.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Callout tone="neutral" title="The honest answer">
**There's no universal right choice** — it depends on your timeline, savings, and how much you value flexibility. With your income and rent, you *can* afford to buy a modest flat (£250k–£300k range). But if you're not sure you'll stay put for 5 years, renting isn't throwing money away — it's buying flexibility.
</Callout>
