# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system has **eight planets**, split into two groups: the rocky **terrestrial planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the gas/ice **giants** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Here's a tour, from the Sun outward.

- **Mercury** — The smallest planet, with a heavily cratered surface like our Moon. It has almost no atmosphere, so temperatures swing from −173°C at night to 427°C by day.
- **Venus** — The hottest planet due to a runaway greenhouse effect. Its thick CO₂ atmosphere and sulfuric acid clouds hide a volcanic surface. It spins **backwards**, so the Sun rises in the west.
- **Earth** — The only known world with life. Liquid water covers 71% of the surface, and a protective magnetic field shields us from solar wind.
- **Mars** — The Red Planet, coloured by iron oxide dust. It has the tallest volcano (Olympus Mons, 21.9 km) and a canyon system (Valles Marineris) that would stretch across the USA.

<Callout tone="info" title="Why is Venus hotter than Mercury?">
Even though Mercury is closer to the Sun, Venus's thick CO₂ atmosphere traps heat far more effectively — a greenhouse effect that makes it the hottest planet at a scorching 464°C.
</Callout>
</Card>

- **Jupiter** — A stormy giant with the **Great Red Spot**, a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. It has the most moons of any planet (95 confirmed).
- **Saturn** — Famous for its spectacular rings of ice and rock. It's so light that it would float in a big enough bathtub, and its moon **Titan** has lakes of liquid methane.
- **Uranus** — An ice giant that rolls around the Sun on its side (axial tilt 98°). It appears a featureless pale cyan, but has faint rings and 28 known moons.
- **Neptune** — The windiest planet, with supersonic gusts reaching 2,100 km/h. It was the first planet found by **mathematical prediction** (1846) before being observed through a telescope.

<Details title="What about Pluto?">
Pluto was reclassified as a **dwarf planet** in 2006 by the IAU. It orbits in the Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune, shares its orbit with other icy bodies, and hasn't cleared its neighbourhood — a key requirement for full planet status. It's still a fascinating world with a heart-shaped glacier (Sputnik Planitia).
</Details>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Space is mostly empty">
If you scaled the Sun to a basketball, Earth would be a peppercorn 26 metres away, and Neptune a pea 780 metres away. The planets are tiny dots in an enormous void.
