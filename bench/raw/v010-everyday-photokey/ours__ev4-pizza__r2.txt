# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

Making authentic Neapolitan pizza at home is absolutely achievable. The secret lies in three things: a high-hydration dough with a long fermentation, a very hot oven (ideally 450°C/850°F), and top-quality San Marzano tomatoes and fresh mozzarella. Let's break it down step by step.

<Card title="What you'll need">
<KeyValue title="Essential equipment">
| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Pizza stone or steel | Retains heat for a crisp, leopard-spotted base |
| Pizza peel | To launch and retrieve the pizza safely |
| Digital scale | Accuracy is crucial for dough hydration |
| Oven thermometer | Home ovens are often off by 20-30°C |
| Mixing bowl & dough scraper | For mixing and folding the dough |
</KeyValue>

<KeyValue title="Ingredients (for 4 pizzas)">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| '00' flour (or bread flour) | 500g |
| Water (room temperature) | 325g (65% hydration) |
| Fine sea salt | 10g |
| Fresh yeast (or 1g dry yeast) | 3g |
| San Marzano tomatoes (canned) | 1 × 400g can |
| Fresh mozzarella (fior di latte) | 250g |
| Fresh basil | A handful |
| Extra virgin olive oil | 2 tbsp |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="The dough — the heart of it all">
<Steps>
<Step title="Mix" status="done" subtitle="5 minutes">
Dissolve the yeast in the water. In a large bowl, combine the flour and salt. Add the water-yeast mixture and mix with a dough scraper until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest for 20 minutes.
</Step>
<Step title="Fold and develop" status="done" subtitle="15 minutes over 2 hours">
Perform a series of stretch-and-folds every 30 minutes for 2 hours. The dough should become smooth and elastic. It will be sticky — resist adding flour.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk ferment" status="current" subtitle="8–24 hours">
Cover the bowl and refrigerate for 8–24 hours. Longer fermentation develops flavour and digestibility. If you're short on time, let it rise at room temperature for 4–6 hours.
</Step>
<Step title="Shape and proof" status="todo" subtitle="2–3 hours before baking">
Divide the dough into 4 equal balls (~210g each). Place on a floured tray, cover, and let them proof at room temperature until doubled — about 2–3 hours.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="info" title="Hydration tip">
65% hydration (325g water per 500g flour) is a good starting point. If your flour absorbs more, you can go up to 70%. The dough should be soft and slightly sticky, never stiff.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The sauce — simple is better">
<KeyValue title="Sauce ratio">
| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| San Marzano tomatoes | 400g |
| Salt | ½ tsp |
| Olive oil | 1 tbsp (optional) |
</KeyValue>

Crush the tomatoes by hand or with a fork — never blend them. Add salt and a drizzle of olive oil. That's it. **Do not cook the sauce.** It will cook in the oven in 90 seconds, and you want it fresh and bright.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't over-sauce">
Use only about 2 tablespoons of sauce per pizza. Too much makes the centre soggy.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Shaping and topping">
<Steps>
<Step title="Stretch by hand" status="todo" subtitle="The key skill">
Flour your hands and the dough ball. Press from the centre outward, leaving a thicker rim (the cornicione). Stretch gently, letting gravity help — never use a rolling pin. The centre should be thin enough to see light through.
</Step>
<Step title="Top quickly" status="todo" subtitle="Don't dawdle">
Spoon the sauce in a spiral, leaving the rim clean. Tear the mozzarella into small pieces and scatter. Add a few basil leaves. The pizza goes in the oven within 1–2 minutes of topping.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Baking — the make-or-break step">
<KeyValue title="Oven temperatures">
| Oven type | Temperature | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Wood-fired | 450°C (850°F) | 60–90 seconds |
| Home oven with steel | 300°C (570°F) max | 4–6 minutes |
| Grill/broiler method | Highest setting | 3–4 minutes |
</KeyValue>

**For a home oven:**
1. Place your pizza steel or stone on the highest rack, at least 15cm from the top element.
2. Preheat for at least 45 minutes at maximum temperature.
3. Switch to grill/broiler for the last 10 minutes to create intense top heat.
4. Launch the pizza, then watch it — it's done when the rim is puffed and charred in spots, and the cheese is bubbling.

<Callout tone="success" title="The leopard-spot test">
Proper Neapolitan pizza has a soft, airy, slightly charred rim — not a crispy cracker. The bottom should be golden with charred spots. If it's pale and soft, your oven wasn't hot enough.
</Callout>

<Card title="Common problems and fixes">
<Compare title="Troubleshooting">
| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy centre | Too much sauce or topping | Reduce sauce to 2 tbsp, drain mozzarella well |
| Pale, dense crust | Oven not hot enough | Preheat longer, use a steel, try the broiler method |
| Dough tears when stretching | Not enough gluten development | Do more folds, or rest the dough longer |
| Burnt top, raw bottom | Top heat too intense | Lower the rack slightly, or use less broiler time |
| Crust too thick | Rolled too thick or over-proofed | Stretch thinner, watch proofing time |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="Try these variations next">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Margherita DOP">
The classic: San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte mozzarella, fresh basil, and a drizzle of olive oil after baking. No oregano, no garlic — that's the rule.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Marinara">
Tomato sauce, garlic, oregano, and olive oil. No cheese. This is the oldest Neapolitan pizza and a great vegan option.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Diavola">
Add spicy salami (like 'nduja or soppressata) on top of the mozzarella. The heat cuts through the richness.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>
