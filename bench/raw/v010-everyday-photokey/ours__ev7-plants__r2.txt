# Houseplants That Are (Almost) Impossible to Kill

Great news: you don't need a green thumb to enjoy a lush, living space. These plants forgive missed waterings, low light, and general neglect. Here are the best options, plus how to keep them thriving with minimal effort.

<Card title="The Top 5 Bulletproof Plants">
<Columns>
<div>
<Badge tone="success">#1 Best Overall</Badge>
### Snake Plant (Sansevieria)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks; let soil dry completely
- **Bonus:** Purifies air, tolerates almost anything
</div>
<div>
### Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)
- **Light:** Low to bright indirect
- **Water:** When leaves look slightly droopy
- **Bonus:** Grows fast, easy to propagate
</div>
<div>
### ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia)
- **Light:** Very low to bright indirect
- **Water:** Every 3–4 weeks; drought-tolerant
- **Bonus:** Shiny leaves, survives fluorescent office light
</div>
</Columns>
<Columns>
<div>
### Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)
- **Light:** Bright indirect, some direct OK
- **Water:** Weekly; let top inch dry
- **Bonus:** Produces baby plants (spiderettes)
</div>
<div>
### Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior)
- **Light:** Low to medium
- **Water:** Every 2–3 weeks
- **Bonus:** Truly lives up to its name
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="The #1 killer is overwatering">
When in doubt, **skip a week**. These plants store water in their leaves and roots. If leaves look slightly wrinkled or droopy, give a drink — otherwise, leave them alone.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="How to Not Kill Them (Even Less Effort)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Choose the right pot" status="done" subtitle="Drainage holes are non-negotiable">
Use a pot with drainage holes. If you love a decorative pot without holes, keep the plant in a plain nursery pot and slip it inside — then empty any standing water after watering.
</Step>
<Step title="Water less than you think" status="done" subtitle="Stick a finger in the soil first">
Wait until the top 2–3 cm of soil is dry. For snake and ZZ plants, wait until the soil is fully dry. When you do water, water thoroughly until it runs out the bottom.
</Step>
<Step title="Match light to the plant" status="done" subtitle="Most tolerate low light, but bright indirect is ideal">
Snake, ZZ, and Cast Iron plants handle dim corners. Pothos and Spider plants prefer bright, indirect light but won't die in lower light — they'll just grow slower.
</Step>
<Step title="Ignore them" status="done" subtitle="The best care is often no care">
These plants thrive on neglect. Resist the urge to fuss. If you're unsure, do nothing — they'll let you know when they need help.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Starter Kit for Absolute Beginners">
<Checklist title="What to buy" name="starter-kit">

- [ ] One Pothos (trailing variety for a shelf)
- [ ] A pot with drainage holes for each
- [ ] Well-draining potting mix (cactus mix works)
- [ ] A small watering can with a narrow spout
- [ ] A moisture meter (optional, ~$10, removes all guesswork)
</Checklist>
<Callout tone="success" title="Realistic expectation">
With these two plants, you can go **4+ weeks** without watering and still come back to living, green plants. That's not a green thumb — that's just smart plant choices.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Overwatering kills more plants than anything else">
- Yellow, mushy leaves = too much water
- Soil that stays wet for weeks = root rot
- If in doubt, **don't water**
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Sudden light changes cause stress">
Moving a plant from a dark corner to a sunny window will shock it. Transition over a week or two.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="neutral" title="Ignore the "low-maintenance" label on the tag">
Most tags are written by growers, not plant parents. Trust the list above, not the tag.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Quick Recap — What to Remember">
<Steps>
<Step title="Pick a Snake, ZZ, or Cast Iron plant" status="done" subtitle="The three most forgiving">
These three can survive weeks without water and in almost any light.
</Step>
<Step title="Pot with drainage" status="done" subtitle="Non-negotiable">
No drainage holes = root rot = dead plant.
</Step>
<Step title="Water only when soil is dry" status="done" subtitle="Stick a finger in first">
Most people kill by overwatering, not underwatering.
</Step>
<Step title="Place in any light, but avoid scorching sun" status="done" subtitle="Bright indirect is the sweet spot">
Direct afternoon sun can burn leaves; everything else is fine.
</Step>
<Step title="Enjoy your green, low-effort space" status="current" subtitle="You've got this">
These plants will outlive your neglect — and probably your apartment lease.
</Step>
</Steps>
