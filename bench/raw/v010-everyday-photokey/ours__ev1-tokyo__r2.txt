# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

Tokyo is a city that rewards curiosity — neon alleys, ancient shrines, incredible food, and quiet gardens all layered together. This plan balances iconic sights with local neighborhoods, and keeps walking distances reasonable for two people.

<Card title="At a glance" subtitle="Day-by-day theme">
| Day | Theme | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrival & Shinjuku | Neon, observation deck, izakaya dinner |
| 2 | Old Tokyo | Asakusa, Senso-ji, Sumida River cruise |
| 3 | Nature & Art | Meiji Shrine, Harajuku, Shibuya |
| 4 | Day trip | Hakone or Kamakura (choose below) |
| 5 | Markets & Departure | Tsukiji, Ginza, last-minute shopping |
</Card>

<Card title="Day 1 — Arrival & Shinjuku" subtitle="Hit the ground running">
<Steps>
<Step title="Arrive at Narita or Haneda" status="done">Take the Narita Express or limousine bus into the city. Pick up a Suica card at the airport for trains and convenience stores.</Step>
<Step title="Check in & rest" status="done">Drop bags at your hotel in Shinjuku — central for everything. Grab a quick ramen or convenience store snack to recharge.</Step>
<Step title="Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building" status="current">Free observation deck with a 360° view. Go around sunset for the city lights turning on.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner in Omoide Yokocho" status="todo">Tiny yakitori alley under the train tracks. Order skewers, beer, and soak in the atmosphere.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 2 — Old Tokyo" subtitle="Temples, river, and street food">
<Steps>
<Step title="Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa" status="done">Tokyo's oldest temple. Arrive early (before 9am) to beat the crowds. Walk through the Kaminarimon Gate and Nakamise shopping street.</Step>
<Step title="Sumida River cruise" status="current">Take a boat from Asakusa to Hamarikyu Garden — the gardens are beautiful, and the ride is relaxing.</Step>
<Step title="Hamarikyu Garden & tea house" status="todo">A tidal garden with a teahouse on an island. Matcha and a sweet treat while overlooking the skyscrapers.</Step>
<Step title="Evening in Akihabara" status="todo">Electric town — arcades, anime shops, and a maid café if you're curious. Dinner at a standing sushi bar.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 3 — Nature & Art" subtitle="Shrines, fashion, and crossing the road">
<Steps>
<Step title="Meiji Shrine" status="done">A forest in the city. Walk the gravel path, see the giant torii gate, and make a wish at the main hall.</Step>
<Step title="Harajuku Takeshita Street" status="current">Crepes, quirky fashion, and people-watching. Try a sweet crepe from one of the many stands.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Crossing" status="todo">The famous scramble crossing. Watch from the Starbucks upstairs, then walk through it yourself.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Sky observation deck" status="todo">Rooftop view at sunset — book tickets a few days ahead. Dinner at a local izakaya nearby.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Day 4 — Day trip choice" subtitle="Pick one — both are easy from Tokyo">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Hakone (mountains & onsen)">
- **How to get there:** Odakyu Romancecar from Shinjuku, ~85 min
- **What to do:** Lake Ashi cruise, Hakone Open-Air Museum (sculptures + Picasso collection), ropeway to Owakudani volcano valley
- **Onsen:** Soak in a hot spring with Mount Fuji views (weather permitting)
- **Return:** Evening back in Tokyo or stay overnight at a ryokan
</Tab>
<Tab title="Kamakura (temples & coast)">
- **How to get there:** JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo, ~60 min
- **What to do:** Great Buddha at Kotoku-in, Tsurugaoka Hachimangu Shrine, Hase-dera temple with ocean views
- **Lunch:** Local soba noodles or shirasu (tiny sardines) rice bowl
- **Return:** Back by late afternoon, easy for an early dinner
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Card title="Day 5 — Markets & Departure" subtitle="Last tastes and souvenirs">
<Steps>
<Step title="Toyosu Market (early)" status="done">The wholesale fish market moved here. Go early for sushi breakfast — the tuna auction viewing is at 5am, but the restaurants open around 6am.</Step>
<Step title="Tsukiji Outer Market" status="current">The old market area still has street food stalls — tamagoyaki (egg omelette), fresh seafood skewers, and kitchen knife shops.</Step>
<Step title="Ginza stroll" status="todo">Window shop the luxury boutiques, or visit the Itoya stationery store (9 floors of paper and pens).</Step>
<Step title="Departure" status="todo">Head to the airport — allow 90 min from central Tokyo to Narita, 60 min to Haneda.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Quick tips">
<Callout tone="info" title="Train etiquette">
- Stand on the left side of escalators (right side in Osaka)
- Keep your voice down on trains
- No eating or drinking on local trains
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="October weather">
- Average 15–20°C, bring layers
- Occasional rain — pack a small umbrella or buy one at a convenience store
</Callout>
<Callout tone="success" title="Money-saving trick">
- Many attractions are free (Meiji Shrine, Metropolitan Building)
- Convenience store meals (onigiri, sandwiches) are cheap and delicious
- Look for lunch sets — often half the dinner price
</Callout>
</Card>
