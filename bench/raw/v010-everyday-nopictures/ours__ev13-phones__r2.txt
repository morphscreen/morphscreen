# iPhone 16 vs. Pixel 9: Which should you get?

Both are excellent flagship phones, but they excel in different areas. Here's a clear breakdown to help you decide based on what matters most to you.

<Card title="What you get for your money">
<Columns>
<Product name="iPhone 16" price="$799" rating="4.6" count="12,400" badge="Apple ecosystem">
- A18 chip: blazing performance, great for gaming
- Excellent video recording with 4K Dolby Vision
- 60Hz display (less smooth than Pixel)
- 25W wired charging (slower than many rivals)
- iOS with long-term updates (5+ years)
</Product>
<Product name="Pixel 9" price="$799" rating="4.5" count="3,200" badge="AI features">
- Tensor G4: great AI features, but slower than A18
- 120Hz display: smoother scrolling and animations
- 12 GB RAM for better multitasking
- Bigger battery with faster 27W charging
- Android with 7 years of updates
</Product>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Camera: what really matters">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="iPhone 16">
- **Photo style**: Natural, true-to-life colors
- **Video**: Best-in-class, with 4K Dolby Vision and spatial audio
- **Low light**: Good, but not as bright as Pixel
- **Features**: Photographic Styles, Action Mode, macro on ultrawide
</Tab>
<Tab title="Pixel 9">
- **Photo style**: Punchy, vibrant, often more dramatic HDR
- **Video**: Good, but not as stable or detailed as iPhone
- **Low light**: Excellent Night Sight, best-in-class
- **Features**: Magic Editor, Best Take, Add Me (AI editing tools)
</Tab>
</Tabs>
<Callout tone="info" title="Bottom line">If you shoot lots of video or want reliable point-and-shoot photos, get the iPhone. If you love AI editing tools and night shots, the Pixel wins.</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Software and updates">
<Columns>
<Stat label="iPhone 16" value="5+ years" note="iOS updates, typically 5-6 years" icon="shield-check" />
<Stat label="Pixel 9" value="7 years" note="Android updates + security patches" icon="shield-check" />
</Columns>
<p>Both get long support, but the Pixel edges ahead. If you keep phones for 4+ years, that extra year or two matters.</p>
</Card>

<Card title="Ecosystem and extras">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="iPhone 16">
- **Best if you own**: Mac, iPad, Apple Watch, AirPods
- **Features**: AirDrop, Handoff, iMessage, FaceTime
- **Accessories**: MagSafe, huge case selection
- **Trade-in value**: High resale value
</Tab>
<Tab title="Pixel 9">
- **Best if you live in**: Google's world (Gmail, Photos, Assistant)
- **Features**: Call Screening, Recorder transcription, Circle to Search
- **Accessories**: Qi2 wireless, smaller accessory market
- **Trade-in value**: Lower resale, but good promos
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Which one fits you?">
<Columns>
<Card title="Choose iPhone 16 if...">
- You're already in Apple's ecosystem
- You shoot lots of video
- You want a phone that just works with minimal fuss
- You care about resale value
- You prefer iOS's clean, consistent design
</Card>
<Card title="Choose Pixel 9 if...">
- You love AI features and photography tools
- You want a 120Hz display for smooth scrolling
- You're on Android and want the purest experience
- You want a bigger battery and faster charging
- You plan to keep the phone 5+ years
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Final verdict">
<Callout tone="success" title="My recommendation">
- **Get the iPhone 16** if you value video quality, ecosystem integration, and a seamless experience. It's the safer, more polished choice.
- **Get the Pixel 9** if you want the best camera for stills, AI magic, and a smoother display. It's the more innovative, feature-packed option.
</Callout>
<p>Both are $799 and both are excellent. The real question is: which ecosystem do you live in, and what do you shoot most?</p>
</Card>
