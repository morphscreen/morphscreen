# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

Vancouver is a hiker's paradise — you can go from sea level to alpine ridges in under two hours. Here are the best day hikes, organized by effort and reward.

A turquoise alpine lake framed by Black Tusk and Mount Garibaldi. The switchbacks are steep but the payoff is worth it. Swim in the summer, ice-walk in winter.

Three glacier-fed lakes in a row, each more stunning than the last. The upper lake sits right below a glacier. Go early to beat the crowds.

<Card title="Pick by what you want">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Best views">
<Callout tone="info" title="Top pick">**Stawamus Chief** — nothing beats standing on the granite summit with Howe Sound below. For a less crowded alternative with similar payoff, try **Eagle Bluffs**.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Easiest / family friendly">
<Callout tone="success" title="Top pick">**Dog Mountain** — short, gentle, and rewarding. Also consider **Lighthouse Park** (easy, 6 km) or **Whyte Lake** (easy, 5 km) for flat forest loops.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Most challenging">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Top pick">**Garibaldi Lake** — the relentless switchbacks will test your legs, but the lake is a cathedral. For a true scramble, do the **Chief's Second Peak** via the backside.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Winter / snowshoe">
<Callout tone="info" title="Top pick">**Dog Mountain** — groomed trails and a cozy summit. For more adventure, **Hollyburn Mountain** (Cypress) offers open slopes and views.</Callout>
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Safety & etiquette">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Weather changes fast">
Mountains can go from sunny to stormy in 30 minutes. Check forecasts before you go, and turn back if conditions worsen. Snow lingers on high trails well into June.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Leave no trace">
Pack out everything you bring in. Stay on trails to protect fragile alpine plants. Dogs must be on leash in most parks.
</Callout>
</Card>
