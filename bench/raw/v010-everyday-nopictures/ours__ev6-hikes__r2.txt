# Best Day Hikes Near Vancouver

Vancouver is a hiker's paradise — within an hour of downtown you can be on a mountain ridge, beside a waterfall, or deep in old-growth forest. Here are the top picks, organized by difficulty and vibe.

<Card title="Which one fits your day?" subtitle="Pick by how much time and effort you have">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Quick & easy (half day)">
- **Quarry Rock** — 1.5–2 hrs, minimal elevation, best view-per-effort ratio
- **Lynn Loop** — 1.5–2 hrs, flat and forested, great for kids
- **Whytecliff Park** — 2 hrs, ocean views and tidal pools (not a hike, but a lovely walk)

<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">For Quarry Rock, go early (before 8 am) or on a weekday — the parking lot fills by 9 am on weekends.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Moderate challenge (full day)">
- **Garibaldi Lake** — 18 km round trip, 820 m gain, 6–7 hrs. The lake colour is unreal.
- **Norvan Falls** — 14 km, mostly flat, 4–5 hrs. Good for a training day.
- **Mount Seymour** — 8 km, 600 m gain, 3–4 hrs. Panoramic views of the North Shore.

<Callout tone="success" title="Best value">Garibaldi Lake is the single best day hike in the region — the effort-to-reward ratio is unbeatable.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Hard & rewarding (for fit hikers)">
- **Stawamus Chief** — 5 km, 700 m gain, steep and exposed. First peak is the classic.
- **Black Tusk** — 29 km, 1,700 m gain, 10–12 hrs. The iconic volcanic spire.
- **The Lions** — 16 km, 1,200 m gain, 8–10 hrs. The twin peaks visible from downtown.

<Callout tone="danger" title="Safety first">These are real mountain routes — check the forecast, carry proper gear, and turn around if conditions deteriorate.</Callout>
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="What to pack" subtitle="A checklist for any of these hikes">
<Checklist title="Day hike essentials" name="packing-list">
- [x] Sturdy footwear (trail runners or boots)
- [ ] 2–3 litres of water (more for Garibaldi or Black Tusk)
- [ ] Snacks and a lunch
- [ ] Rain shell (even in summer — mountain weather changes fast)
- [ ] Layers (fleece or puffy)
- [ ] Sunscreen and hat
- [ ] Phone with downloaded offline map (AllTrails or Gaia)
- [ ] Headlamp (for late finishes)
- [ ] First-aid kit (small)
- [ ] Bear spray (for Garibaldi, Black Tusk, or The Lions)

<Callout tone="warning" title="Parking & transit">
- **Quarry Rock** — parking is brutal on weekends; take the #212 bus from Phibbs Exchange
- **Garibaldi Lake** — the Daisy Lake lot fills by 7 am on summer weekends; carpool or take the Parkbus
- **Chief** — the lot is huge but fills by 9 am; the Squamish Connector bus runs from downtown
</Callout>

<Card title="Trail etiquette & safety" subtitle="Leave no trace, keep everyone safe">
<Steps>
<Step title="Tell someone your plan" status="done">Share your route and expected return time with a friend. Cell service is unreliable on most of these trails.</Step>
<Step title="Check the forecast" status="done">Mountain weather changes in minutes. If rain is forecast above 800 m, consider a lower route like Lynn Loop.</Step>
<Step title="Start early" status="done">Aim to be at the trailhead by 7–8 am. This avoids the crowds, the heat, and the afternoon thunderstorm risk.</Step>
<Step title="Pack out everything" status="done">No bins on the trail — bring a bag for your wrappers, peels, and any trash you find.</Step>
<Step title="Yield to uphill hikers" status="done">On narrow trails, step aside for people climbing — they have the right of way.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="One more thing" subtitle="If you only do one hike, make it this one">

Go on a weekday in July or September for the best balance of weather and solitude.</Callout>
