# What to Eat in Lisbon (and Where)

Lisbon is a city of bold flavors — think garlic, olive oil, seafood, and custard. Here’s a practical guide to the must-try dishes and the best spots to find them, from iconic institutions to local favorites.

<Card title="The Iconic Dishes" subtitle="Start with these — they define Lisbon">
<Columns>
<Card title="Pastel de Nata">
The famous custard tart with a caramelized, flaky crust. Best eaten warm, dusted with cinnamon.
</Card>
<Card title="Bacalhau à Brás">
Shredded salted cod with onions, thin fries, and eggs — a creamy, comforting classic. Lisbon claims 1,000 ways to cook cod; this is the most beloved.
</Card>
<Card title="Bifana">
A simple but perfect sandwich: thin pork cutlets in a garlicky, paprika-laced broth, piled into a crusty roll.
</Card>
<Card title="Sardinhas Assadas">
Grilled sardines, a summer staple, often served at street festivals. Eat them with bread and a cold beer.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat by Dish">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Pastel de Nata">
<Columns>
<Card title="Manteigaria" subtitle="Best for a quick, perfect bite">
The custard is warm, the pastry shatters, and the line moves fast. Grab one at the counter with an espresso.
</Card>
<Card title="Pastéis de Belém" subtitle="The original, since 1837">
The recipe is a secret, and the queue is long, but the experience is iconic. Go early or late to avoid the rush.
</Card>
</Columns>
<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Ask for “um pastel e um galão” — a custard tart and a milky coffee. That’s the classic pairing.</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bacalhau à Brás">
<Columns>
<Card title="Cervejaria Ramiro" subtitle="A seafood institution that does cod right">
Famous for shrimp and garlic, but their bacalhau is excellent. Expect a wait; it’s worth it.
</Card>
<Card title="Casa de Pasto" subtitle="Local, no-frills, authentic">
A traditional tasca where the cod is homey and generous. Bring cash and patience — service is unhurried.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bifana">
<Columns>
<Card title="O Trevo" subtitle="The classic bifana joint">
A tiny, tiled shop where the pork is juicy and the bread is crisp. Order two — they’re small.
</Card>
<Card title="Casa Búlgara" subtitle="A modern take on the classic">
They add a touch of mustard and pickles, but the soul is intact. A great stop for lunch.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Sardinhas">
<Columns>
<Card title="A Cevicheria" subtitle="Upscale, but the sardines shine">
A stylish spot that treats sardines with respect — grilled, with a citrus kick. Book ahead.
</Card>
<Card title="Zé da Mouraria" subtitle="Street-side grilling at its best">
A kiosk in the Mouraria neighborhood, where sardines are grilled right in front of you. Pair with a Super Bock beer.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="A Quick Eating Route" subtitle="One day, five stops — walk it off between bites">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning" status="done" subtitle="Manteigaria — Pastel de Nata + espresso">Start in Chiado for the best tart in the city.</Step>
<Step title="Late Morning" status="done" subtitle="Mercado da Ribeira — Time Out Market">Grazing heaven: try the bifana from “O Trevo” stall, or a seafood plate from “Marisqueira Azul”.</Step>
<Step title="Lunch" status="done" subtitle="Cervejaria Ramiro — Bacalhau à Brás">The big meal of the day. Order the cod, a plate of garlic shrimp, and crusty bread to mop up the sauce.</Step>
<Step title="Late Afternoon" status="done" subtitle="Pastéis de Belém — The original tart">Take tram 15E to Belém, see the tower, then queue for the secret-recipe custard tart.</Step>
<Step title="Evening" status="done" subtitle="Zé da Mouraria — Grilled sardines">End the day in the old Moorish quarter, watching the grill smoke rise as the sun sets.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Sweet and Savory Extras" subtitle="Worth seeking out">
<Columns>
<Card title="Ginjinha">
A sour cherry liqueur, served in a tiny cup. Try it at **A Ginjinha** in Rossio — the oldest spot in town.
</Card>
<Card title="Tosta Mista">
The Portuguese grilled ham-and-cheese sandwich. Find one at any pastelaria, but the best are at **Café da Garagem** near São Bento.
</Card>
<Card title="Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato">
Clams in a garlic-cilantro broth. Order them at **Ramiro** or any seafood spot — they’re a starter that steals the show.
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="A Few Rules of Thumb">
<Callout tone="info" title="Go local">
Skip restaurants with laminated menus in multiple languages near major squares. Walk two streets away and you’ll find better food for half the price.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Watch the tourist traps">
On Rua Augusta and in Praça do Comércio, the “traditional” spots are often overpriced and underwhelming. The best food is in the side streets.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="success" title="Eat like a local">
Lunch is the big meal (12:30–14:30), dinner is late (20:00–22:00). Many restaurants close between 15:00 and 19:00 — plan your snacks accordingly.
</Callout>
</Card>
