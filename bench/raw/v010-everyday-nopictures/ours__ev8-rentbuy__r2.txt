## What buying could look like

Let's assume a typical flat in your area at **£300,000** with a **10% deposit** (£30,000), a **30-year mortgage** at **4.5% interest**.

<Card title="Monthly costs: rent vs buy (estimate)">
<Chart type="bar" x="Option" y="£ per month">
| Option | Monthly cost |
|---|---|
| Rent | 1800 |
| Mortgage (interest only) | 1013 |
| Mortgage (repayment) | 1520 |
| Buying total (mortgage + service charge + insurance + maintenance) | 1950 |
</Chart>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="The hidden costs of buying">
Beyond the mortgage, budget for:
- **Service charge** (leasehold flats): £150–£300/month
- **Buildings insurance**: ~£20/month
- **Maintenance fund**: ~£100–£200/month
- **Stamp duty** (one-off): 0% up to £250k, then 5% on the portion above
</Callout>

## The real question: what's your timeline?

<Card title="Decision guide">
<Steps>
<Step title="Staying put for 5+ years?" status="current" subtitle="Buying usually wins">You build equity, and the upfront costs (stamp duty, legal fees, moving) get spread over more years. Even with service charges, you're likely ahead.</Step>
<Step title="Moving within 3 years?" status="todo" subtitle="Keep renting">The costs of buying and selling (fees, stamp duty, stress) will likely swallow any gains. Rent and invest the difference instead.</Step>

## The verdict

<Card title="So, rent or buy?">
<Columns>
<Card title="Rent if...">
- You might move within **3 years**
- You value flexibility and no maintenance headaches
- You prefer to invest the difference in stocks (potentially higher returns)
- You're not ready for the responsibility of homeownership
</Card>
<Card title="Buy if...">
- You plan to stay **5+ years**
- You want to build equity instead of paying someone else's mortgage
- You're comfortable with maintenance and service charges
- You can afford the upfront costs (deposit + fees ≈ £35k–£40k on a £300k flat)
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Bottom line">
With your £1,800 rent, you're paying a **premium for flexibility**. If you're settled for 5+ years, buying a £300k flat with a 10% deposit is likely to leave you **£20k–£40k better off** over that period, even with service charges. If you're not sure, keep renting and save aggressively toward a bigger deposit.
</Callout>
