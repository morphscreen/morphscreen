# What to Eat in Lisbon, and Where

Lisbon is a city of bold, simple flavors — grilled fish, custard tarts, and hearty stews. Here's your practical eating guide, from must-try dishes to the spots that do them best.

| Dish | What it is | Where to try it |
|---|---|---|
| **Pastel de nata** | Custard tart with burnt top, flaky crust | Manteigaria (Chiado) |
| **Bifana** | Pork cutlet sandwich, mustard + spicy piri-piri | O Trevo (Baixa) |
| **Sardinhas assadas** | Grilled sardines, only in season (June–Sept) | Casa de Pasto (Alfama) |
| **Bacalhau à brás** | Shredded cod with eggs, onions, fries | Ramiro (Intendente) |
| **Caldo verde** | Kale soup with chorizo and potato | Any tasca, try A Travessa |
| **Pão de Deus** | Sweet coconut-topped bun | Confeitaria Nacional (Baixa) |
| **Ginjinha** | Sour cherry liqueur, drunk as a shot | A Ginjinha (Rossio) |

<Callout tone="warning" title="Timing is everything">
Lunch runs 12:00–15:00, dinner 19:30–23:00. Most kitchens close between 15:00 and 19:00 — plan a late afternoon pastel de nata instead.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to eat by neighborhood">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Alfama (old town)">
The most atmospheric, most touristy. Go for fado music and grilled fish.

- **Casa de Pasto** — Family-run, tiny, book ahead. The sardines are the reason.
- **Rua das Flores** — A food street with small producers: cheese, charcuterie, wine.

<Button ask="Tell me more about eating in Alfama">Show me Alfama's best spots</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Chiado & Baixa (center)">
Best for pastéis de nata and quick bites between sights.

- **Manteigaria** — The tart here is warm, always. Eat standing at the counter.
- **O Trevo** — A hole-in-the-wall bifana bar. Order two, they're small.
- **Confeitaria Nacional** — Old-school pastry shop, great for Pão de Deus.

<Button ask="What else should I eat in Chiado?">More Chiado picks</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Intendente & Anjos (up-and-coming)">
Where locals actually eat now. Less pretty, more real.

- **Ramiro** — Famous for seafood (the giant shrimp are legendary). Go for lunch, no booking needed.
- **A Travessa** — A tasca doing honest caldo verde and grilled meats.

<Button ask="What's the best food in Intendente?">Show me Intendente's food</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Belém (west)">
The birthplace of the pastel de nata. Worth a trip just for that.

- **Pastéis de Belém** — The original since 1837. The secret recipe is kept in a locked room. Go early to avoid the queue.

<Card title="Your eating plan for one day" subtitle="A realistic route, if you're hungry">
<Steps>
<Step title="9:00 — Breakfast: pastel de nata + coffee" status="done" subtitle="Manteigaria, Chiado">
Order one tart and a *meia de leite* (milky coffee). Eat it warm, standing at the counter.
</Step>
<Step title="12:30 — Lunch: bifana + caldo verde" status="current" subtitle="O Trevo, Baixa">
Two bifanas and a bowl of soup. Cheap, fast, satisfying.
</Step>
<Step title="16:00 — Snack: ginjinha" status="todo" subtitle="A Ginjinha, Rossio">
A shot of cherry liqueur. Some drink it from a chocolate cup. Do that.
</Step>
<Step title="20:00 — Dinner: grilled fish" status="todo" subtitle="Casa de Pasto, Alfama">
Sardines if in season, otherwise the grilled sea bass. Follow with a *pudim flan*.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Tipping">
Not expected. Round up to the nearest euro at most. A 10% tip is generous and only for great service.
</Callout>

<Card title="Quick tips before you go">
<Checklist title="Eating in Lisbon" name="lisbon-eating-checklist">
- [ ] Learn "Obrigado" (thank you) — it opens doors
- [ ] Carry cash — many tascas don't take cards
- [ ] Book dinner on weekends, especially in Alfama
- [ ] Ask for "piri-piri" on the side — it's hot
- [ ] Don't order the "tourist menu" — the daily special (prato do dia) is better value
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="The one thing to remember">
**Eat the pastel de nata warm, with cinnamon, and don't share it.** Everything else is negotiable.
</Callout>
