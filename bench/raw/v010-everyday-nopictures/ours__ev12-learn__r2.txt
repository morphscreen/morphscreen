# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae, and some bacteria turn sunlight into food — and it's the process that feeds nearly all life on Earth. Let me walk you through it step by step.

<Card title="The Big Picture" subtitle="What photosynthesis actually does">
Photosynthesis takes **light energy**, **water**, and **carbon dioxide** and converts them into **glucose** (sugar) and **oxygen**. The overall chemical equation is:

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />

In plain words: **6 carbon dioxide + 6 water + sunlight → 1 glucose + 6 oxygen**
</Card>

<Card title="Where It Happens">
Photosynthesis occurs in the **chloroplasts** — tiny green structures inside plant cells. The green pigment **chlorophyll** inside them absorbs light, which is why leaves look green (they reflect green light instead of absorbing it).

<KeyValue title="Key parts of a chloroplast">
| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Thylakoid | Disc-shaped sacs where the light reactions happen |
| Stroma | The fluid around thylakoids where the Calvin cycle happens |
| Chlorophyll | Pigment that captures light energy |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Card title="The Two Stages" subtitle="Light reactions and the Calvin cycle">
Photosynthesis happens in two main stages. Think of it like a factory: the first stage collects energy, the second uses it to build sugar.

<Steps>
<Step title="Light reactions" status="done" subtitle="In the thylakoid — needs sunlight">
Chlorophyll absorbs light, which splits water molecules (releasing oxygen gas as a byproduct) and generates two energy-carrying molecules: **ATP** and **NADPH**.
</Step>
<Step title="Calvin cycle" status="done" subtitle="In the stroma — doesn't need light directly">
Using the ATP and NADPH from stage 1, the plant "fixes" carbon dioxide from the air into glucose. This is also called the **dark reactions** because they can run without light (though they usually happen during the day).
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Inputs and Outputs">
| Input | Where it comes from | Output | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunlight | The sun | Oxygen | Released into the air |
| Water (H₂O) | Roots absorb it | Glucose | Used for energy and growth |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | Air through leaf pores (stomata) | — | — |
</Card>

<Card title="Common Misconceptions">
<Callout tone="warning" title="Myth: Plants only photosynthesize during the day">
The **light reactions** need sunlight, but the **Calvin cycle** doesn't. However, in practice most plants do both during the day because the Calvin cycle needs the ATP and NADPH produced by the light reactions.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="info" title="Myth: Plants breathe in CO₂ and breathe out O₂ all the time">
Plants also **respire** (like animals) — they use oxygen and release CO₂ around the clock. Photosynthesis only happens in the light and usually outweighs respiration during the day, so net O₂ is released.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Dig Deeper">
<Details title="What about C4 and CAM plants?">
Some plants (corn, sugarcane, cacti) have evolved alternate pathways to handle hot, dry conditions. **C4 plants** concentrate CO₂ efficiently, while **CAM plants** (succulents) open their stomata at night to reduce water loss. Both still use the same Calvin cycle — they just feed it CO₂ differently.
</Details>
<Details title="What happens to the glucose?">
Glucose is used for:
- **Energy** via cellular respiration
- **Building material** to make cellulose (cell walls) and starch (storage)
- **Other molecules** like proteins and fats
</Details>
</Card>
