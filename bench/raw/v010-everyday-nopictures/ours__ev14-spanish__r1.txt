# 10 Basic Spanish Phrases for Your Trip

<Card title="Your Essential Spanish Survival Kit" subtitle="Learn these 10 phrases and you'll get by in most everyday situations">
<Callout tone="info" title="Pro tip">Spanish pronunciation is very consistent — once you learn the sounds, you can read any word aloud. Focus on the bolded syllables below.</Callout>

<Flashcards title="Practice the 10 phrases">
| Front (English) | Back (Spanish) |
|---|---|
| Hello / Good morning | Hola / Buenos días |
| Please | Por favor |
| Thank you | Gracias |
| Yes / No | Sí / No |
| Excuse me / Sorry | Perdón |
| Where is...? | ¿Dónde está...? |
| How much is it? | ¿Cuánto cuesta? |
| I don't understand | No entiendo |
| Do you speak English? | ¿Habla inglés? |
| Goodbye | Adiós |
</Flashcards>
</Card>

<Card title="Phrase Breakdown by Situation">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Greetings & Politeness">
<Steps>
<Step title="Hola" status="done" subtitle="OH-la">Hello — the universal greeting, works any time of day. <Badge tone="success">Use it everywhere</Badge></Step>
<Step title="Buenos días" status="done" subtitle="BWEH-nos DEE-as">Good morning — use until around noon.</Step>
<Step title="Por favor" status="done" subtitle="por fah-VOR">Please — add it to any request. <Badge tone="info">Key word for politeness</Badge></Step>
<Step title="Gracias" status="done" subtitle="GRAH-see-as">Thank you — say it often; locals appreciate it.</Step>
<Step title="Perdón" status="done" subtitle="per-DON">Excuse me / Sorry — for bumping into someone or getting attention.</Step>
<Step title="Adiós" status="done" subtitle="ah-dee-OS">Goodbye — the classic farewell.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Questions You'll Need">
<Steps>
<Step title="Sí / No" status="done" subtitle="see / noh">Yes / No — the most useful words in any language.</Step>
<Step title="¿Dónde está...?" status="done" subtitle="DON-deh es-TAH">Where is...? — finish with "el baño" (the bathroom), "la playa" (the beach), or "la estación" (the station).</Step>
<Step title="¿Cuánto cuesta?" status="done" subtitle="KWAN-toh KWES-tah">How much is it? — point at the item while asking. <Badge tone="warning">Essential for markets and shops</Badge></Step>
<Step title="No entiendo" status="done" subtitle="noh en-tee-EN-doh">I don't understand — say this and people will slow down or rephrase.</Step>
<Step title="¿Habla inglés?" status="done" subtitle="AH-blah een-GLES">Do you speak English? — a lifesaver when you're stuck.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>

<Card title="Pronunciation Cheat Sheet">
<KeyValue title="Key sounds to remember">
| Letter | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| j | Like an English "h" | "hasta luego" (not "lwego") |
| ll | Like "y" | "llamar" sounds like "yah-mar" |
| ñ | "ny" as in "canyon" | "mañana" = "mah-NYAH-nah" |
| r | Rolled once (tap) | "perdón" — a quick tap of the tongue |
| v | Like a soft "b" | "vino" sounds like "bee-no" |
</KeyValue>

<Callout tone="success" title="Stress rule">If a word ends in a vowel, **n**, or **s**, stress the second-to-last syllable. If it ends in any other consonant, stress the last syllable. Words with an accent mark (á, é, í, ó, ú) stress that syllable instead.</Callout>
</Card>
