# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system has eight planets, split into two main groups: the rocky **inner planets** and the gas/ice **outer planets**. Here's a tour from the Sun outward.

## The Inner Planets (Rocky Worlds)

<Card title="Mercury — The Swift Planet">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 4,879 km |
| Day length | 59 Earth days |
| Year length | 88 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. Its surface is covered in craters, much like our Moon. Despite being nearest to the Sun, it's not the hottest — Venus beats it.
</Card>

<Card title="Venus — The Morning Star">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 12,104 km |
| Day length | 243 Earth days (retrograde) |
| Year length | 225 Earth days |
| Moons | 0 |
</KeyValue>
Venus is similar in size to Earth but completely different in character. A thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide traps heat, making it the hottest planet. It also spins backwards compared to most planets.
</Card>

<Card title="Earth — The Blue Marble">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 12,742 km |
| Day length | 24 hours |
| Year length | 365.25 days |
| Moons | 1 |
</KeyValue>
Our home is the only known planet with liquid water on its surface and life. Its atmosphere of nitrogen and oxygen, plus a magnetic field, protects living things from solar radiation.
</Card>

<Card title="Mars — The Red Planet">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 6,779 km |
| Day length | 24.6 hours |
| Year length | 687 Earth days |
| Moons | 2 |
</KeyValue>
Mars is famous for its rusty red color from iron oxide. It has the largest volcano in the solar system (Olympus Mons) and a canyon system (Valles Marineris) that would stretch across the US.
</Card>

## The Outer Planets (Gas and Ice Giants)

<Card title="Jupiter — The King of Planets">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 139,820 km |
| Day length | 9.9 hours |
| Year length | 11.9 Earth years |
| Moons | 95 |
</KeyValue>
Jupiter is more than twice as massive as all other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. It has a faint ring system and dozens of moons, including Ganymede — the largest moon in the solar system.
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn — The Ringed Jewel">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 116,460 km |
| Day length | 10.7 hours |
| Year length | 29.5 Earth years |
| Moons | 146 |
</KeyValue>
Saturn's spectacular rings are made of billions of ice particles, from dust-sized to house-sized. It's the least dense planet — it could float in a big enough bathtub. Its moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane.
</Card>

<Card title="Uranus — The Tilted Ice Giant">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 50,724 km |
| Day length | 17.2 hours (retrograde) |
| Year length | 84 Earth years |
| Moons | 28 |
</KeyValue>
Uranus is tipped on its side, rolling around the Sun like a ball. This gives it extreme seasons — each pole gets 42 years of sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness. It's an ice giant, with water, methane and ammonia ices.
</Card>

<Card title="Neptune — The Windy Blue World">
<KeyValue title="Key facts">
| | |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 49,244 km |
| Day length | 16.1 hours |
| Year length | 164.8 Earth years |
| Moons | 16 |
</KeyValue>
Neptune is the farthest planet, discovered by mathematics before telescopes confirmed it. It has the strongest winds in the solar system, reaching over 2,000 km/h. Its moon Triton orbits backwards and is likely a captured Kuiper Belt object.
</Card>
