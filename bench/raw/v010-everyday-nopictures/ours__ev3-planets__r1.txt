# The Planets of Our Solar System

Our solar system has eight planets, divided into two main groups: the rocky inner planets and the gas/ice giants of the outer solar system. Here's a complete tour, from the Sun outward.

<Card title="The Inner Planets (Rocky Worlds)">
These four planets are small, dense, and made mostly of rock and metal. They have solid surfaces and few or no moons.

| Planet | Distance from Sun | Diameter | Day Length | Year Length | Moons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 57.9 million km | 4,879 km | 59 Earth days | 88 Earth days | 0 |
| Venus | 108.2 million km | 12,104 km | 243 Earth days (retrograde) | 225 Earth days | 0 |
| Earth | 149.6 million km | 12,742 km | 24 hours | 365.25 days | 1 |
| Mars | 227.9 million km | 6,779 km | 24.6 hours | 687 Earth days | 2 |

<Details title="Quick facts about the inner planets">
- **Mercury**: The smallest planet and closest to the Sun. Its surface temperature swings from -173°C at night to 427°C in the day.
- **Venus**: The hottest planet (464°C average) due to a runaway greenhouse effect. It spins backwards compared to most planets.
- **Earth**: The only known planet with life. About 71% of its surface is covered with liquid water.
- **Mars**: Known as the Red Planet due to iron oxide (rust) on its surface. It has the tallest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons (21.9 km high).
</Details>
</Card>

<Card title="The Outer Planets (Gas and Ice Giants)">
These four planets are enormous, have many moons, and most have ring systems. They are divided into gas giants (Jupiter, Saturn) and ice giants (Uranus, Neptune).

| Planet | Distance from Sun | Diameter | Day Length | Year Length | Moons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jupiter | 778.5 million km | 139,820 km | 9.9 hours | 11.9 Earth years | 95 |
| Saturn | 1.43 billion km | 116,460 km | 10.7 hours | 29.4 Earth years | 146 |
| Uranus | 2.87 billion km | 50,724 km | 17.2 hours (retrograde) | 84 Earth years | 28 |
| Neptune | 4.50 billion km | 49,244 km | 16.1 hours | 164.8 Earth years | 16 |

<Details title="Quick facts about the outer planets">
- **Jupiter**: The largest planet—more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries.
- **Saturn**: Famous for its spectacular ring system, made of ice and rock particles. It is the least dense planet—it would float in water.
- **Uranus**: It rotates on its side, likely knocked over by an ancient collision. Its axis is tilted 98°.
- **Neptune**: The windiest planet, with gusts reaching 2,100 km/h. It was the first planet found by mathematical prediction, not observation.
</Details>
</Card>

<Card title="Not a planet?">
In 2006, the International Astronomical Union reclassified **Pluto** as a **dwarf planet**. It didn't meet one of the three criteria for planethood: it hasn't cleared its orbital neighborhood of other debris.

<Callout tone="info" title="The five recognized dwarf planets">
Ceres (in the asteroid belt), Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, and Eris (all in the Kuiper belt).
</Callout>

**The three criteria for a planet:**
1. It orbits the Sun
2. It has enough gravity to be round
3. It has cleared its orbit of other objects — **Pluto fails this**
</Card>
