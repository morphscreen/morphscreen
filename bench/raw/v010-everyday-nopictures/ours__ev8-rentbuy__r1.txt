# Rent or buy? Let's work it out for your situation

The short answer: **it's close**, but buying looks slightly better *if* you plan to stay put for 5+ years and have a deposit ready. Renting wins if you value flexibility or aren't sure where you'll be in a few years.

<Callout tone="info" title="The big question first">
Buying only beats renting once you stay long enough to spread the upfront costs (stamp duty, legal fees, moving) over many years. That's usually 4–6 years in the UK.
</Callout>

<Card title="The real comparison: 5 years ahead">
Assume you save the difference or the deposit grows, and house prices rise 3% a year (UK long-run average).

| After 5 years | Rent | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Total paid out | £108,000 | ~£114,000 |
| What you own | £0 | ~£40,000 equity |
| Home value | — | ~£406,000 |
| **Net position** | **-£108,000** | **~-£74,000** |

<Callout tone="success" title="The verdict">
After 5 years, buying leaves you roughly **£34,000 better off** even with the higher monthly cost — because you've built equity and benefited from price growth.
</Callout>

If you move after just 2 years, renting is usually cheaper because buying costs (stamp duty, legal, estate agent) eat your gains.
</Card>

<Card title="When buying wins vs. renting wins">
<Columns>
<Card title="Buy if you…">
- Plan to stay **5+ years**
- Have a **10%+ deposit** saved
- Want **stability** (no landlord, no rent hikes)
- Can handle **maintenance costs** (~1% of value/year)
</Card>
<Card title="Rent if you…">
- Might **move for work** in under 4 years
- Don't have a deposit yet
- Value **flexibility** over ownership
- Prefer not to deal with **repairs or service charges**
</Card>
