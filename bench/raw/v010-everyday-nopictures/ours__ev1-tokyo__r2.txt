# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

Tokyo is a city that rewards both structure and spontaneity. Here's a balanced itinerary that mixes iconic sights, local neighborhoods, great food, and a couple of relaxed moments — all walkable or a short train ride apart.

<Card title="At a glance">
<KeyValue title="Trip snapshot">
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Duration | 5 days |
| Travelers | 2 |
| Best season | Spring (cherry blossoms) or autumn (mild weather) |
| Home base | Shinjuku or Shibuya (central, well-connected) |
| Daily budget (mid-range) | ¥15,000–¥20,000 per person (food + transport + entry) |
| JR Pass worth it? | Only if you add a day trip (e.g., Nikko or Kamakura) |
</KeyValue>

## Day 1: Arrival + Shinjuku Evening

<Card title="Day 1 — Get your bearings">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check in & drop bags" status="done">Settle into your hotel or Airbnb. Shinjuku is a great base — lively but with quiet side streets.</Step>
<Step title="Late lunch: Ramen in Omoide Yokocho" status="done">Tiny alleyway stalls with smoky yakitori and noodles. Go early (5pm) to avoid lines.</Step>
<Step title="Golden Gai bar crawl" status="done">A maze of 200+ micro-bars. Pick a themed one (jazz, vinyl, movies). Many charge a small cover (~¥500) that includes a drink.</Step>
<Step title="Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building" status="done">Free observation deck with a 360° night view. Go 30 min before sunset.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Jet lag tip">Try to stay awake until 9pm local time. A quick walk around Shinjuku Gyoen (closed Mondays) helps reset your body clock.</Callout>

---

## Day 2: Classic Tokyo — Shibuya, Harajuku, Meiji Shrine

<Card title="Day 2 — The icons">
<Steps>
<Step title="Meiji Shrine (early)" status="current">A forested sanctuary in the middle of the city. Arrive by 9am to beat crowds. Free entry.</Step>
<Step title="Harajuku & Takeshita Street" status="todo">Crepes, quirky fashion, and people-watching. For a quieter stroll, walk down Omotesando's tree-lined avenue.</Step>
<Step title="Shibuya Crossing & Hachiko Statue" status="todo">The world's busiest intersection. Watch from the Starbucks second floor for a great view.</Step>
<Step title="Dinner: Izakaya in Shibuya" status="todo">Try a local chain like *Uoshin* or *Zauo* for fresh seafood and a lively atmosphere.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Day 3: Culture & Gardens — Asakusa, Ueno, Yanaka

<Card title="Day 3 — Old Tokyo">
<Steps>
<Step title="Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise Street" status="current">Tokyo's oldest temple. Arrive before 9am for fewer crowds. Buy a fortune (omikuji) for fun.</Step>
<Step title="Sumida River cruise to Hamarikyu Gardens" status="todo">A 40-min boat ride to a beautiful tidal garden with a teahouse. Great photo spots.</Step>
<Step title="Ueno Park & museums" status="todo">Pick one: Tokyo National Museum (history) or the Ueno Zoo (pandas). Or just stroll the pond.</Step>
<Step title="Yanaka Ginza (sunset)" status="todo">A nostalgic shopping street with old shops, cats, and a laid-back vibe. Great for souvenirs.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Callout tone="success" title="Money-saving tip">The Tokyo Metro 24/48/72-hour pass is great for days 2–4. It pays for itself after ~3 rides/day.</Callout>

## Day 4: Day Trip or Full-Day Neighborhood Hopping

Pick one option — both are excellent:

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Option A: Nikko (nature + temples)">
<Card title="Nikko day trip">
<Steps>
<Step title="Early train (1h50m from Asakusa)">Take the Tobu Limited Express (reserve seats) or JR to Nikko.</Step>
<Step title="Toshogu Shrine" status="current">A UNESCO site with ornate carvings. Allow 2–3 hours.</Step>
<Step title="Kegon Falls & Lake Chuzenji" status="todo">A bus ride up the mountain. The 97m waterfall is stunning.</Step>
<Step title="Return by 7pm">Back in Tokyo for dinner — try *gyoza* in Ueno.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Option B: Yokohama (port city)">
<Card title="Yokohama day trip">
<Steps>
<Step title="Train (25 min from Shibuya)">Take the Tokyu Toyoko Line to Yokohama.</Step>
<Step title="Cup Noodles Museum" status="current">Make your own instant ramen. Fun and interactive.</Step>
<Step title="Chinatown & waterfront" status="todo">One of the world's largest Chinatowns, then walk to the Red Brick Warehouse.</Step>
<Step title="Cosmo World ferris wheel at sunset" status="todo">A great view over the bay.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Day 5: Last Moments — Akihabara, Ginza, or Relax

<Card title="Day 5 — Flexible finale">
<Columns>
<Card title="If you love tech & anime">
- Akihabara: electronics, arcades, maid cafés
- Visit Mandarake for retro games
- Try a *gyukatsu* (beef cutlet) lunch
</Card>
<Card title="If you prefer shopping & tea">
- Ginza: flagship stores, Uniqlo, Itoya stationery
- Afternoon tea at the Imperial Hotel
- Walk to Tsukiji Outer Market for street food
</Card>
<Card title="If you want calm">
- TeamLab Planets (immersive art) — book ahead
- Shinjuku Gyoen for a picnic
- Onsen day at a *sento* (public bath)
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Before you leave">Buy souvenirs early — Narita/Haneda airport shops are limited. Also, pick up a *Suica* or *Pasmo* IC card for trains; it works on buses and vending machines too.</Callout>

## Final Tips

<Card title="Golden rules for Tokyo">
- **Buy a Suica card** at the airport — it works on all trains, buses, and even some shops.
- **Download Google Maps offline** for your area — it works well for transit directions.
- **Book popular spots ahead** (TeamLab, Ghibli Museum, some restaurants) — at least 2–3 days in advance.
- **Don't tip** — it's not expected and can be seen as rude.
- **Walk on the left** on escalators (except Osaka, but you're in Tokyo).
- **Stay near a station** — you'll thank yourself at 11pm after dinner.
</Card>
