## What Actually Kills These Plants

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Callout tone="warning" title="The #1 killer: overwatering">
More houseplants die from too much water than too little. Most of these plants prefer dry soil between waterings. Stick a finger 2-3 cm into the soil — if it's dry, water; if damp, wait.
</Callout>

<Columns>
<Card title="Signs of overwatering">
- Yellow, mushy leaves
- Brown spots with yellow halos
- Mold on soil surface
- Foul smell from pot
- Roots look dark and slimy
</Card>
<Card title="Signs of underwatering">
- Leaves droop or curl
- Leaf tips turn brown and crispy
- Soil pulls away from pot edges
- Leaves feel thin and papery
- Plant looks faded or dull
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## The Simple Care Routine

<Card title="A 5-Minute Monthly Check">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check soil moisture" status="done">
Poke your finger 2-3 cm into the soil of each plant. Only water if dry.
</Step>
<Step title="Dust the leaves" status="done">
Wipe leaves with a damp cloth — dusty leaves photosynthesize less.
</Step>
<Step title="Look for pests" status="done">
Check undersides of leaves for webs, white specks, or sticky residue.
</Step>
<Step title="Rotate the pot" status="done">
Turn each pot a quarter turn so all sides get light and growth stays even.
</Step>
<Step title="Trim dead leaves" status="current">
Snip off yellow or brown leaves at the base with clean scissors.
</Step>
<Step title="Fertilize (spring & summer only)" status="todo">
Feed with a balanced liquid fertilizer diluted to half strength, once a month.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

## Quick Troubleshooting

<Card title="What's Wrong With My Plant?">
<Details title="Leaves turning yellow">
Usually overwatering. Let the soil dry out completely before watering again. If the pot has no drainage hole, repot into one that does.
</Details>
<Details title="Brown crispy leaf tips">
Usually underwatering or dry air. Water more regularly, and consider misting or placing a small humidifier nearby.
</Details>
<Details title="Leaves drooping">
Check the soil. If dry — water now, it'll recover within hours. If wet — you've overwatered; let it dry out completely.
</Details>
<Details title="No new growth">
Could be too little light or it's winter (normal dormancy). Move to a brighter spot and wait for spring.
</Details>
<Details title="Pale, washed-out leaves">
Too much direct sun. Move to a brighter but indirect light spot.
</Details>
</Card>

## Your Starter Checklist

<Checklist title="Before you buy" name="starter-checklist">
- [ ] Choose a pot with a drainage hole
- [ ] Pick soil labeled "indoor" or "potting mix"
- [ ] Place the plant away from heating vents or AC units
- [ ] Check the soil moisture before watering each time
- [ ] Keep it in a spot with at least some natural light
- [ ] Quarantine new plants from existing ones for 2 weeks
</Checklist>
