<Card title="The trade‑offs">
<Columns>
<Card title="Renting ✓" subtitle="Keep your flexibility">
- No repair or maintenance costs
- Easy to move for a new job or city
- No stamp duty or legal fees upfront
- Your deposit stays in your savings
- Rent can rise each year
</Card>
<Card title="Buying ✓" subtitle="Build equity over time">
- Monthly payment may be similar to rent
- You own the asset (it can grow in value)
- No landlord can evict you
- Fixed-rate mortgage = predictable costs
- You pay for repairs and maintenance
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Key questions to ask yourself">
<Checklist title="Before you decide" name="buy-check">
- [ ] Can I afford a deposit plus stamp duty and legal fees (3–6% of the price)?
- [ ] Do I plan to stay in the same city for at least 5 years?
- [ ] Am I comfortable with the risk of house prices falling?
- [ ] Do I have an emergency fund left after buying (3–6 months of costs)?
- [ ] Is my credit score good enough for a competitive mortgage rate?
- [ ] Would I be okay paying for a new boiler or roof if needed?
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Card title="A rough rule of thumb">
<Callout tone="info" title="The 5‑year rule">
If you plan to stay in the same place **less than 5 years**, renting is usually better — the costs of buying and selling (stamp duty, legal fees, estate agent fees) eat up any equity gains. If you'll stay **5+ years**, buying often wins.
</Callout>
