<Card title="How to keep them alive (minimal effort)">
<Steps>
<Step title="Water less than you think" status="current">
Overwatering is the #1 killer. Wait until the top 2 inches of soil are dry before watering. Snake Plants and ZZ Plants can go 3–4 weeks without water in winter.
</Step>
<Step title="Give them indirect light" status="todo">
Most of these tolerate low light, but they'll grow best in bright, indirect light (near a window but not in direct sun). Avoid dark corners with no windows.
</Step>
<Step title="Use the right pot" status="todo">
Always use a pot with drainage holes. If water pools at the bottom, roots will rot. Terracotta pots help wick away extra moisture.
</Step>
<Step title="Dust the leaves" status="todo">
Every few months, wipe the leaves with a damp cloth. Dust blocks light and can hide pests.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common mistakes to avoid">
<Callout tone="danger" title="Don't overwater">
Yellow, mushy leaves = too much water. Let the soil dry out completely between waterings. If you're unsure, wait another week.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="warning" title="Don't put them in a dark closet">
While they tolerate low light, they still need some light. A room with a window (even north-facing) is fine. A windowless bathroom will slowly kill them.
</Callout>
<Callout tone="info" title="Don't use soil that stays wet">
Regular potting mix is fine, but add perlite or sand to improve drainage. Avoid "moisture control" soils that hold water longer.
</Callout>
</Card>
