# How Photosynthesis Works

<Card title="What is Photosynthesis?" subtitle="The process that fuels life on Earth">
Photosynthesis is how plants, algae, and some bacteria convert sunlight into chemical energy. They take in carbon dioxide and water, and with sunlight's help, produce glucose (sugar) and oxygen.

<Stat label="Global oxygen produced" value="70%" note="from photosynthesis" icon="chart-line" />
<Stat label="Energy captured yearly" value="~100 TW" note="by all plants on Earth" icon="chart-line" />
</Card>

## The Big Picture

<Chart type="bar" x="Inputs & Outputs" y="Molecules per reaction">
| Component | Molecules |
|---|---|
| Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) | 6 |
| Water (H₂O) | 6 |
| Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) | 1 |
| Oxygen (O₂) | 6 |
</Chart>

<Card title="The Chemical Equation" subtitle="The recipe for photosynthesis">
<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + light \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Photosynthesis equation" />

**In words:** Six molecules of carbon dioxide + six molecules of water + sunlight energy → one molecule of glucose + six molecules of oxygen
</Card>

## The Two Stages

<Columns>
<Card title="Stage 1: Light-Dependent Reactions" subtitle="The 'light' part">
**Where:** Thylakoid membranes inside chloroplasts

**What happens:**
- Sunlight hits chlorophyll (the green pigment)
- Water molecules split (photolysis)
- Oxygen is released as a byproduct
- Energy carriers (ATP and NADPH) are produced

<Badge tone="info">Requires sunlight directly</Badge>
</Card>

<Card title="Stage 2: Calvin Cycle" subtitle="The 'synthesis' part">
**Where:** Stroma (fluid inside chloroplasts)

**What happens:**
- Carbon dioxide is fixed (captured)
- Uses ATP and NADPH from Stage 1
- Glucose is built from carbon atoms
- No sunlight needed directly

<Badge tone="info">Can happen day or night</Badge>
</Card>
</Columns>

## Step-by-Step Journey

<Steps>
<Step title="Light Absorption" status="done" subtitle="Photon capture">
Chlorophyll molecules in the thylakoid absorb photons (light particles). This excites electrons, boosting them to a higher energy level.
</Step>
<Step title="Water Splitting" status="done" subtitle="Photolysis">
The excited electrons are replaced by splitting water molecules: H₂O → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ + ½O₂. The oxygen diffuses out of the leaf.
</Step>
<Step title="Electron Transport Chain" status="current" subtitle="Energy conversion">
Excited electrons travel through protein complexes, releasing energy that pumps protons (H⁺) across the thylakoid membrane. This creates a proton gradient.
</Step>
<Step title="ATP & NADPH Production" status="todo" subtitle="Energy storage">
Protons flow back through ATP synthase (like a turbine), generating ATP. Meanwhile, NADP⁺ picks up electrons and becomes NADPH.
</Step>
<Step title="Carbon Fixation" status="todo" subtitle="CO₂ capture">
In the Calvin Cycle, an enzyme called RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP), creating an unstable 6-carbon compound that splits into two 3-carbon molecules.
</Step>
<Step title="Glucose Synthesis" status="todo" subtitle="Sugar building">
Using ATP and NADPH from the light reactions, the 3-carbon molecules are rearranged and combined to form glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆).
</Step>
</Steps>

## Key Players

<KeyValue title="Photosynthesis Components">
| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment that absorbs light (mostly red and blue, reflects green) |
| Chloroplast | The organelle where photosynthesis happens |
| Thylakoid | Disk-shaped membrane where light reactions occur |
| Stroma | Fluid where the Calvin Cycle takes place |
| RuBisCO | The enzyme that captures CO₂ (most abundant protein on Earth) |
| Stomata | Pores in leaves that let CO₂ in and O₂ out |
</KeyValue>

## What Affects Photosynthesis Rate?

<Card title="Factors That Change the Rate">
<Chart type="line" x="Factor Intensity" y="Photosynthesis Rate">
| Factor | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light Intensity | 20 | 70 | 95 |
| CO₂ Level | 15 | 60 | 90 |
| Temperature | 10 | 80 | 50 |
</Chart>

<Callout tone="info" title="Limiting Factor Principle">
The factor in shortest supply limits the overall rate. On a cloudy day, light is the limit. In a sealed greenhouse, CO₂ might be.
</Callout>
</Card>

## Why Photosynthesis Matters

<Columns>
<Card title="For the Planet">
- Produces the oxygen we breathe
- Removes CO₂ from the atmosphere
- Forms the base of almost every food chain
- Stores carbon in plant matter and fossil fuels
</Card>
<Card title="For Us">
- Provides food (crops, fruits, vegetables)
- Supplies wood, paper, and fibers
- Creates fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas)
- Powers biofuel production
</Card>
</Columns>

## Key Terms to Remember

<Flashcards title="Photosynthesis Flashcards">
| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Process where plants convert light energy into chemical energy (glucose) |
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs sunlight |
| Stomata | Tiny pores on leaves for gas exchange (CO₂ in, O₂ out) |
| Calvin Cycle | Light-independent reactions that build glucose from CO₂ |
| RuBisCO | Enzyme that fixes carbon dioxide in the Calvin Cycle |
| Photolysis | Splitting of water molecules using light energy |
| ATP | Energy currency molecule produced during light reactions |
| NADPH | Electron carrier molecule produced during light reactions |
</Flashcards>
