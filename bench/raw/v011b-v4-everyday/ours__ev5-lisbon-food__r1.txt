# What to Eat in Lisbon & Where to Find It

<Card title="Lisbon's Must-Try Dishes">
Lisbon's food is simple, fresh, and full of flavour — think seafood, grilled meats, and sweet eggy pastries. Here's what you shouldn't miss.

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Pastéis de Nata">
The iconic Portuguese custard tart. Warm, flaky pastry filled with creamy, lightly caramelised custard, dusted with cinnamon and icing sugar.

**Where:** **Pastéis de Belém** (Rua de Belém 84-92) — the original bakery since 1837. Expect a queue, but it moves fast. Eat them warm, standing at the counter.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bacalhau à Brás">
Shredded salted cod (bacalhau) sautéed with onions, matchstick potatoes, and scrambled eggs, topped with olives and parsley. Every Portuguese cook has their own version.

**Where:** **Tasca da Esquina** (Rua Domingos Sequeira 41) — a modern tasca that does this classic beautifully.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Sardinhas Assadas">
Grilled sardines, simply seasoned with sea salt, served with boiled potatoes and a drizzle of olive oil. Best in June during the Santos Populares festivals, but available year-round.

**Where:** **Cervejaria Ramiro** (Avenida Almirante Reis 1) — Lisbon's most famous seafood house. Order a plate of grilled sardines and a cold beer.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bifana">
A simple but brilliant sandwich: thin slices of marinated pork loin in a crusty bread roll, often with a dab of spicy mustard or piri-piri sauce.

**Where:** **O Trevo** (Rua do Loreto 37) — a tiny, legendary spot in Chiado. Get it "com piri-piri" (with hot sauce).
</Tab>
<Tab title="Caldo Verde">
Portugal's national soup: a silky purée of potato and onion, with shredded kale and slices of chouriço sausage. Humble, comforting, and perfect on a cool evening.

**Where:** **Cervejaria Trindade** (Rua Nova da Trindade 20) — a historic beer hall in a former monastery, serving excellent caldo verde.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Arroz de Marisco">
A rich, soupy rice dish loaded with prawns, clams, mussels, and sometimes crab or lobster. The rice absorbs all the seafood broth — it's a messy, joyful meal.

**Where:** **Marisqueira Uma** (Rua dos Sapateiros 177) — no-frills, cash-only, and absolutely legendary for this dish. They serve one thing: arroz de marisco.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Time Out Market">
If you're short on time, head to the **Time Out Market** (Mercado da Ribeira). It's a curated food hall with stalls from many of Lisbon's best chefs and restaurants — great for sampling several dishes in one place.
</Callout>

<Details title="What to drink">
- **Vinho Verde** — a light, slightly sparkling young wine, perfect with seafood
- **Ginjinha** — sour cherry liqueur, served in a small chocolate cup
- **Super Bock / Sagres** — the two national beers, both crisp lagers
- **Porto** — a glass of tawny or ruby port to finish a meal
</Details>

<Details title="Dining etiquette">
- Lunch is the main meal (12:00–14:00), dinner is lighter and later (20:00–22:00)
- Tipping: 5–10% is appreciated but not expected; rounding up the bill is common
- Many restaurants have a *couvert* (bread, olives, cheese) placed on your table — you pay for what you eat, so you can decline it
- Reservations recommended for dinner at popular spots
</Details>
