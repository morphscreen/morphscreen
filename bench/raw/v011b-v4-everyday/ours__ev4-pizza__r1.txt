# How to Make a Proper Neapolitan Pizza at Home

<Card title="What Makes a Pizza Neapolitan?">
A true Neapolitan pizza (pizza napoletana) follows strict rules from the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (AVPN). The key characteristics are:

- **Dough**: Soft, elastic, and well-hydrated (60-65% hydration)
- **Crust**: Puffy, leopard-spotted, and slightly charred
- **Toppings**: Simple, high-quality ingredients, used sparingly
- **Cooking**: Very high heat (430-480°C / 800-900°F) for 60-90 seconds
- **Size**: Typically 30-35 cm (11-13 inches)

<Details title="Step-by-step dough method" open=true>
1. **Mix flour and water** — combine in a bowl, mix until no dry flour remains. Cover and let rest 20-30 minutes (autolyse).
2. **Add yeast and salt** — dissolve yeast in a splash of the water (from the 320g) and knead it in. Add salt and knead thoroughly.
3. **Knead** — use the slap-and-fold technique: slap dough on the counter, fold it over itself, rotate 90°, repeat for 10-15 minutes until smooth and elastic. The dough should stretch thin without tearing (windowpane test).
4. **Bulk ferment** — place in a lightly oiled bowl, cover, and let rise at room temperature for 8-12 hours until doubled.
5. **Divide and ball** — gently turn dough onto a floured surface, divide into 3 equal pieces (about 275g each). Shape each into a tight ball by pulling the edges to the bottom.
6. **Cold ferment** — place balls in separate oiled containers, cover, and refrigerate for 24-72 hours. Longer = more flavour and digestibility.
7. **Temper** — remove from fridge 2-3 hours before baking. Leave covered at room temperature until the dough relaxes and becomes pliable.
</Details>
</Card>

<Card title="Stretching the Dough">
<Steps>
<Step title="Prepare your surface" status="current">Dust your work surface and dough ball with flour (semolina or "00" flour).</Step>
<Step title="Press into disc" subtitle="Use fingertips">Gently press the ball into a flat disc, starting from the centre and working outward. Leave a 1-2cm rim untouched — that's your cornicione (crust).</Step>
<Step title="Stretch" subtitle="Hands only, no rolling pin">Pick up the disc, drape it over your knuckles, and rotate your hands to let gravity stretch it. Work around the edge, keeping the centre thin and the rim puffy.</Step>
<Step title="Final shape" subtitle="30-35 cm / 11-13 inches">Lay the stretched dough on a floured peel. It should be slightly thinner in the centre (2-3mm) with a thicker rim (1-2cm). If it shrinks back, let it rest 5 minutes and try again.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Common mistakes">
- **Rolling pin** — destroys the air bubbles. Never use one.
- **Tearing** — if the dough tears, you've stretched too aggressively or the gluten wasn't developed enough.
- **Sticky dough** — use more flour on your hands and surface, not in the dough itself.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="Ingredient sourcing">
- **Tomatoes**: Use DOP San Marzano tomatoes from the Campania region. If unavailable, use good-quality whole peeled Italian tomatoes.
- **Mozzarella**: Buffalo mozzarella is traditional, but fior di latte (cow's milk) works well and is less watery. Drain and pat dry before using.
- **Flour**: Caputo "00" Pizzeria flour is the gold standard. King Arthur Bread Flour is a good substitute.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat your oven" status="current">Place pizza steel/stone on the top rack. Preheat at max temperature for 45-60 minutes. Turn on the broiler for the last 5 minutes.</Step>
<Step title="Prepare the peel" subtitle="Prevent sticking">Dust the peel generously with semolina or coarse flour. Give the peel a shake — the pizza should slide freely.</Step>
<Step title="Launch the pizza" subtitle="Quick, confident motion">Open the oven, place the peel edge at the back of the stone, and jerk the peel back sharply. The pizza should slide off cleanly.</Step>
<Step title="Bake and turn" subtitle="Watch closely">After 2-3 minutes, use a peel or tongs to rotate the pizza 180°. The crust should be puffing and developing spots.</Step>
<Step title="Finish and serve" subtitle="1-2 minutes more">Bake until the crust is golden with charred leopard spots and the cheese is bubbling. Remove, add fresh basil, drizzle olive oil, and serve immediately.</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Safety first">
- The steel/stone is **extremely hot** — use heavy oven mitts.
- Keep a clear path from oven to counter.
- Never use a wet peel — steam will stick the pizza.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="Troubleshooting Guide">
<Columns>
<Card title="Crust Problems">
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pale, soft crust | Oven not hot enough | Preheat longer, use broiler |
| Burnt, not puffed | Too close to broiler | Move stone 5cm lower |
| Dense, cracker-like | Overworked or underfermented | Reduce kneading, extend bulk ferment |
| Uneven puffing | Uneven thickness | Stretch more evenly |
</Card>
<Card title="Topping Problems">
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy centre | Too much sauce or wet mozzarella | Use less sauce, drain mozzarella |
| Cheese slides off | Too much cheese or too cold | Use less, let cheese come to room temp |
| Toppings burn | Added too early | Add delicate toppings after 2 minutes |
</Card>
</Columns>

<Details title="Dough troubleshooting">
- **Dough too sticky**: Don't add more flour to the dough. Use wet hands or a dough scraper. Next time, reduce hydration by 10g water.
- **Dough tears when stretching**: The gluten hasn't relaxed. Let the ball rest 30 minutes at room temperature.
- **Dough doesn't rise**: Yeast may be dead (check expiry) or water too hot (over 40°C kills yeast).
- **Dough too stiff**: Increase hydration next batch. Add 10-20g more water.
</Details>
</Card>
