# What to Eat in Lisbon & Where to Find It

Lisbon's food scene blends fresh seafood, hearty stews, and sweet pastries. Here's your guide to the must-try dishes and the best spots to eat them.

<Card title="The Essential Lisbon Dishes">
<Columns>
<div>
### Pastéis de Nata
The iconic custard tart with a flaky, caramelised top. Eat it warm, dusted with cinnamon.

### Bacalhau à Brás
Shredded salted cod with onions, matchstick potatoes, and scrambled eggs, topped with olives.

### Bifana
A simple but perfect pork sandwich: thin slices of marinated pork in a crusty roll, often with mustard or piri-piri.

### Caldo Verde
Portugal's national soup: kale, potatoes, and chouriço sausage in a silky broth.
</div>
<div>
### Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato
Clams steamed in a garlic, coriander, and white wine sauce. A classic starter.

### Sardinhas Assadas
Grilled sardines, especially during June's Santos Populares festivals. Served with bread and salad.

### Polvo à Lagareiro
Octopus roasted with olive oil, garlic, and potatoes until tender.

### Ginjinha
A sour cherry liqueur served in a small chocolate cup. Drink it as a digestif.
</div>
</Columns>
</Card>

<Card title="Where to Eat: Best Spots by Dish">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Pastéis de Nata">
<Columns>
<div>
**Pastéis de Belém** – The original bakery since 1837. Go early to avoid queues.
</div>
<div>
**Manteigaria** – Modern rival with a visible production line. Multiple locations (Chiado, Time Out Market).
</div>
<div>
**Fábrica da Nata** – Great quality, less crowded than Belém. Several outlets.
</div>
</Columns>
<Button ask="Tell me more about Pastéis de Belém">More on Pastéis de Belém</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bacalhau & Seafood">
<Columns>
<div>
**Cervejaria Ramiro** – Lisbon's most famous seafood house. Expect a wait, but the garlic shrimp and clams are legendary.
</div>
<div>
**Zé da Mouraria** – No-frills tasca serving huge plates of bacalhau à Brás and other cod dishes.
</div>
<div>
**Marisqueira Uma** – Known for one thing: an enormous, garlicky seafood platter. Cash only.
</div>
</Columns>
<Button ask="Tell me more about Cervejaria Ramiro">More on Cervejaria Ramiro</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Bifana & Petiscos">
<Columns>
<div>
**O Trevo** – The classic bifana spot in Chiado. Quick, cheap, and delicious.
</div>
<div>
**Casa das Bifanas** – Another iconic spot near Rossio station. Their bifana comes with a spicy piri-piri sauce.
</div>
<div>
**Tascardoso** – A tiny, family-run place for petiscos (small plates). Try the fried choco (cuttlefish) and the house wine.
</div>
</Columns>
<Button ask="Tell me more about O Trevo">More on O Trevo</Button>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Traditional & Fado">
<Columns>
<div>
**Tasca do Chico** – A tiny, no-reservation tasca with live fado. Go for the atmosphere and a glass of vinho verde.
</div>
<div>
**Cantinho do Avillez** – José Avillez's more casual spot. Excellent bacalhau and a modern take on classics.
</div>
<div>
**A Cevicheria** – For a twist, try Peruvian-Portuguese ceviche. Not traditional, but a fantastic meal.
</div>
</Columns>

For popular spots like Ramiro, Cantinho do Avillez, and any fado house, book a day or two ahead. Smaller tascas (O Trevo, Tasca do Chico) are walk-in only.
