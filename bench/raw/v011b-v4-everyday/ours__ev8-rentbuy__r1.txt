<Callout tone="warning" title="Rent is high">
You're spending half your take-home pay on rent — that's above the recommended 30%. This makes buying more attractive, but only if you can afford the upfront costs.
</Callout>

<Card title="The big trade-offs">
<Columns>
<Card title="Renting ✅">
- No deposit or stamp duty needed
- No repair costs (landlord handles them)
- Easy to move for a new job
- Your £1,800/month goes to someone else's mortgage
- Rent rises over time
- No equity built
</Card>
<Card title="Buying ✅">
- Build equity with every payment
- Fixed mortgage = stable costs for 2–5 years
- Freedom to decorate and renovate
- Big upfront costs (deposit, fees)
- You pay for all repairs
- Harder to move quickly
</Card>
</Columns>
</Card>

## Let's see if you can afford to buy

<Card title="What you'd need for a typical flat">
Assume a £250,000 flat (a realistic starter flat in many UK cities).

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| 10% deposit | £25,000 |
| Stamp duty (first-time buyer) | £0 (on first £425k) |
| Solicitor & surveys | ~£2,000 |
| Moving costs | ~£1,000 |
| **Total upfront** | **~£28,000** |

<Callout tone="info" title="Assumptions">
Mortgage assumes 10% deposit, 25-year term, 4.5% interest rate. Bills & repairs include council tax, insurance, service charge, maintenance fund.
</Callout>

<Tabs>
<Tab title="I want to stay put for 5+ years">
Buying wins. The upfront costs get spread over many years, and you build equity. Even if prices dip, you'll likely come out ahead vs renting.
