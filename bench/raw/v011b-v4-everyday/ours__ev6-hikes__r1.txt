## Stawamus Chief — Squamish

<Badge tone="success">Best views</Badge> <Badge tone="warning">Moderate–Strenuous</Badge>

<KeyValue title="Trail facts">
| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance | 11 km return |
| Elevation gain | 540 m |
| Time | 3–5 hours |
| Season | April–October |
| Difficulty | Moderate to strenuous |
| Dogs | Not allowed on the chain sections |
</KeyValue>

A granite monolith rising above Squamish, the Chief offers three peaks (First, Second, Third) with panoramic views of Howe Sound, the Tantalus Range, and the Squamish Valley. The trail includes steep sections with chains to help you scramble up rock faces.

**Highlights:**
- First Peak has the best viewpoint for the least effort
- Second Peak is the highest (610 m)
- Third Peak is the quietest and most technical
- Go early to avoid crowds, especially on weekends

## Lynn Peak — North Vancouver

<Badge tone="info">Moderate</Badge>

<KeyValue title="Trail facts">
| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance | 9 km return |
| Elevation gain | 510 m |
| Time | 3–4 hours |
| Season | Year-round (snow in winter) |
| Difficulty | Moderate |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>

A forested climb through old-growth Douglas fir and hemlock, Lynn Peak rewards you with views of Vancouver, the Fraser River, and Mount Baker on clear days. The trail starts from the Lynn Headwaters Regional Park.

**Highlights:**
- Shaded trail, good for hot days
- Connects to Norvan Falls for a longer hike
- Less crowded than the Chief
- Great for trail running too

<Tab title="Garibaldi Lake">

## Garibaldi Lake — Whistler Area

<Badge tone="success">Best alpine lake</Badge> <Badge tone="warning">Strenuous</Badge>

<KeyValue title="Trail facts">
| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance | 18 km return |
| Elevation gain | 820 m |
| Time | 5–7 hours |
| Season | July–October (snow-free) |
| Difficulty | Strenuous |
| Dogs | Not allowed |
</KeyValue>

One of BC's most iconic hikes, Garibaldi Lake is a turquoise gem set against the backdrop of the Tantalus Range and Mount Garibaldi. The trail climbs steadily through forest before opening to the lake's stunning shoreline.

**Highlights:**
- The colour of the water is unforgettable
- Panorama Ridge viewpoint (add 2 km each way) is worth the extra effort
- Camping available at the lake (reservations required)
- Bring bug spray in summer

<Tab title="Dog Mountain">

## Dog Mountain — Mount Seymour

<Badge tone="info">Easy–Moderate</Badge>

<KeyValue title="Trail facts">
| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance | 5 km return |
| Elevation gain | 300 m |
| Time | 2–3 hours |
| Season | May–November (snow in winter) |
| Difficulty | Easy to moderate |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>

A short but rewarding hike to a rocky summit with sweeping views of Vancouver, the Fraser Valley, and the Gulf Islands. The trail is family-friendly and one of the most accessible alpine hikes near the city.

**Highlights:**
- Great for beginners and families
- Views of Mount Baker on clear days
- Wildflowers in late spring
- Snowshoe trail in winter

<Tab title="Eagle Bluffs">

## Eagle Bluffs — Cypress Mountain

<Badge tone="info">Moderate</Badge>

<KeyValue title="Trail facts">
| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Distance | 8 km return |
| Elevation gain | 460 m |
| Time | 3–4 hours |
| Season | June–October |
| Difficulty | Moderate |
| Dogs | Allowed on leash |
</KeyValue>

Starting from the Cypress Mountain ski area, this trail climbs through forest and subalpine meadows to a rocky bluff overlooking Howe Sound and the Sunshine Coast. The views are spectacular, especially at sunset.

**Highlights:**
- Connects to the Howe Sound Crest Trail for longer adventures
- Great sunset hike
- Wild blueberries in late summer
- Less crowded than the Chief

<Card title="Essential hiking tips">
<Steps>
<Step title="Check conditions" status="current" subtitle="Before you go">
Check BC Parks and local trail reports for closures, snow, and trail conditions. Many alpine trails are snow-covered into July.
</Step>
<Step title="Pack the 10 essentials" status="todo" subtitle="What to bring">
Map, headlamp, first aid kit, extra food & water, extra layers, rain gear, fire starter, knife, sun protection, emergency shelter.
</Step>
<Step title="Start early" status="todo" subtitle="Beat the crowds">
Aim to be on the trail by 7–8 AM on weekends. Parking lots fill by 9 AM at popular trailheads.
</Step>
<Step title="Leave no trace" status="todo" subtitle="Respect the wilderness">
Pack out all garbage, stay on trails, and keep dogs on leash where required.
</Step>
</Steps>
