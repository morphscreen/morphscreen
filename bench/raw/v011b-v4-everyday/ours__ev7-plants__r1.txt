# Houseplants That Are Hard to Kill

Looking for plants that can survive neglect, low light, and inconsistent watering? These are the most forgiving houseplants.

<Card title="Top 10 Unkillable Houseplants">
<Columns>
<div>

### Snake Plant (Sansevieria)
- Thrives on neglect
- Tolerates low light
- Water every 2-3 weeks
- Also called "Mother-in-Law's Tongue"

### Pothos (Epipremnum aureum)
- Grows in almost any light
- Droops when thirsty (easy reminder!)
- Water when soil is dry
- Propagates easily from cuttings

### ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamifolia)
- Survives months without water
- Tolerates fluorescent light
- Glossy, waxy leaves
- Very pest-resistant

### Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum)
- Adapts to most conditions
- Produces baby "spiderettes"
- Water when soil dries out
- Pet-friendly

### Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum)
- Dramatic droop when thirsty
- Tolerates low light
- White blooms appear regularly
- Water weekly on average

</div>
<div>

### Aloe Vera
- Succulent stores water in leaves
- Needs bright indirect light
- Water every 2-3 weeks
- Gel soothes burns

### Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra)
- Lives up to its name
- Survives in dark corners
- Water sparingly
- Slow-growing but tough

### Chinese Evergreen (Aglaonema)
- Tolerates very low light
- Colorful leaf varieties
- Water when top soil is dry
- Thrives in offices

### Philodendron
- Heart-shaped leaves
- Vining or upright types
- Water when dry
- Very forgiving

### Jade Plant (Crassula ovata)
- Succulent with thick stems
- Needs bright light
- Water every 2-4 weeks
- Can live for decades

</div>
</Columns>

<Card title="Golden Rules for Keeping Any Plant Alive">
<Steps>
<Step title="Don't overwater" status="done">
Most houseplants die from too much water, not too little. Stick your finger 2 inches into the soil — only water if it feels dry.
</Step>
<Step title="Match light to the plant" status="done">
Low light doesn't mean no light. Snake plants and ZZ plants tolerate dark corners; succulents need bright windows.
</Step>
<Step title="Use the right pot" status="done">
Always use a pot with drainage holes. Standing water causes root rot, the #1 killer.
</Step>
<Step title="Ignore them a little" status="current">
Many tough plants actually thrive on neglect. Don't fuss — let them be.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="Common Mistakes to Avoid">
<Details title="How do I know if I'm overwatering?" open=false>
Yellow, mushy leaves, and soil that stays wet for over a week. Let the plant dry out completely before watering again. If the roots smell bad, repot with fresh soil.
</Details>
<Details title="Why are my leaves turning brown?" open=false>
Crispy brown edges usually mean too little water or dry air. Soft brown spots mean overwatering. Trim off dead parts with clean scissors.
</Details>
<Details title="Should I use fertilizer?" open=false>
Wait at least 6 months after buying. Then use a balanced houseplant fertilizer at half strength, only during spring and summer.
</Details>
</Card>
