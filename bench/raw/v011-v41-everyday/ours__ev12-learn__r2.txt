# How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae, and some bacteria turn **light, water, and carbon dioxide** into **sugar and oxygen**. It's the reverse of breathing: instead of burning sugar with oxygen, it builds sugar using light.

<Callout tone="info" title="The one-line version">
Light + water + CO₂ → sugar + oxygen. Plants are solar-powered sugar factories.
</Callout>

<Card title="The overall equation" subtitle="What goes in, what comes out">
<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light} \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Balanced equation for photosynthesis" />

Six carbon dioxide molecules and six water molecules, powered by light, become one glucose molecule and six oxygen molecules. The oxygen you breathe is a **byproduct** — the plant's real goal is the glucose.
</Card>

<Card title="Where it happens" subtitle="Inside the chloroplast">
A chloroplast is a tiny sugar factory with two working areas:

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Thylakoid membranes | Capture light; split water; make energy carriers |
| Stroma (the fluid around them) | Use that energy to build sugar from CO₂ |

<Callout tone="neutral" title="Why leaves are green">
Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light well but reflects green — so the light the plant *doesn't* use is the color you see.
</Callout>
</Card>

<Card title="The two stages" subtitle="Light reactions first, then the Calvin cycle">
<Tabs>
<Tab title="Stage 1 — Light reactions">
**Where:** thylakoid membranes
**Needs:** light + water
**Makes:** ATP, NADPH, and oxygen

1. Chlorophyll absorbs a photon and passes the energy along.
2. That energy splits water: `2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂`.
3. The electrons travel a chain of proteins, pumping H⁺ ions to one side.
4. H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase — like water through a turbine — making **ATP**.
5. The electrons end up on **NADPH**, a loaded energy carrier.

<Callout tone="success" title="Output">
ATP and NADPH (the battery pack) plus O₂ released to the air.
</Callout>
</Tab>
<Tab title="Stage 2 — Calvin cycle">
**Where:** the stroma
**Needs:** CO₂ + the ATP and NADPH from stage 1
**Makes:** glucose

1. **Fixation:** the enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO₂ to a 5-carbon sugar.
2. **Reduction:** ATP and NADPH add energy and hydrogens, forming 3-carbon sugars.
3. **Regeneration:** most of those sugars are recycled to rebuild the starting molecule, so the cycle can run again.

Six turns of the cycle make one glucose.
</Tab>
</Tabs>
</Card>

<Card title="Follow the atoms" subtitle="A quick trace of where each piece goes">
<Steps>
<Step title="Light hits chlorophyll" status="done">Energy is captured; nothing is used up yet.</Step>
<Step title="Water is split" status="done">Its electrons power the chain; its oxygen is released as O₂.</Step>
<Step title="ATP and NADPH are made" status="done">Temporary energy currency, spent within seconds.</Step>
<Step title="CO₂ is fixed in the stroma" status="current">Carbon from the air becomes the backbone of sugar.</Step>
<Step title="Glucose is assembled" status="todo">Six turns of the cycle yield one glucose molecule.</Step>
<Step title="Sugar is used or stored" status="todo">Burned for growth, or packed away as starch.</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Card title="How fast it runs" subtitle="The main dials a plant can't control">
<Chart type="bar" title="Effect of light intensity on photosynthesis rate" x="Light" y="Rate (relative)">
| Light | Rate |
|---|---|
| Dark | 0 |
| Low | 25 |
| Medium | 60 |
| High | 90 |
| Very high | 95 |
</Chart>
The curve flattens because something else becomes the limit — usually CO₂ or temperature. That's the **limiting factor** idea.
</Card>

<Card title="What changes the rate">
<Compare title="Limiting factors compared">
| Factor | Effect of increasing it | Why it stops helping |
| Light intensity | Speeds up the light reactions | Photosystems saturate |
| CO₂ concentration | Speeds up the Calvin cycle | RuBisCO maxes out |
| Temperature | Speeds up enzymes, up to a point | Enzymes denature above ~40 °C |
| Water | Keeps stomata open and tissues turgid | Wilted leaves close their pores |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Card title="Why it matters" subtitle="Beyond the plant">
- **Food:** nearly every calorie you eat traces back to photosynthesis.
- **Oxygen:** the atmosphere's O₂ is its long-term leftovers.
- **Carbon:** it pulls CO₂ out of the air, which is why forests matter for climate.
- **Fuel:** coal, oil, and gas are ancient photosynthesis, stored.
</Card>
