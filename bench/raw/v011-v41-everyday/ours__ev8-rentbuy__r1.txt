# Rent or buy? The honest maths on £60k and £1,800/month

Short answer: at £1,800 rent you are already paying a mortgage-sized amount, so buying is likely cheaper *monthly* — but only if you can raise the deposit and stay put for 5+ years. Here's the working.

<Callout tone="info" title="The one number that decides this">
Your rent is £1,800. A £270,000 mortgage at 5% over 30 years costs about £1,450/month. So owning would be **cheaper each month** than renting — the barrier is the deposit and the fees, not the monthly payment.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Where £60k hurts you">
A 10% deposit on £300k is £33,500 — roughly 11 months of your entire take-home pay saved to zero. Lenders will also stress-test you at ~8%, and at that rate £270,000 costs £2,050/month. You'd pass, but with little slack. Under 10% deposit and the rates get worse.
</Callout>

## A plan you could actually follow

<Steps>
<Step title="Work out your real deposit" status="current" subtitle="This week">
Add up savings, premium bonds, help from family, and anything you could sell. Compare it to £33,500. If you're under £25,000, you're not ready yet and that's fine.
</Step>
<Step title="Get an agreement in principle" status="todo" subtitle="2 weeks">
Free from any broker or bank, valid ~90 days, and it tells you exactly what you can borrow rather than guessing at 4.5×. Do this before you view anything.
</Step>
<Step title="Fix your savings rate" status="todo" subtitle="6-18 months">
To reach £33,500 from a standing start at £1,200/month saved takes 28 months. At £1,800/month, 19 months. Put it in a cash ISA and a high-interest saver.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide the stay-put test" status="todo" subtitle="Before you offer">
If you can't honestly say you'll still be there in 5 years, keep renting and invest the difference instead.
</Step>
<Step title="Buy with a 10% deposit, 5-year fix" status="todo" subtitle="When ready">
A 5-year fix matches the break-even point. It protects you through the expensive early years when selling would lose money.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Card title="What I'd do in your shoes">
With £1,800 rent and £60k, you're paying someone else's mortgage. But you haven't told me your savings, so the decision hinges on one thing: **can you get to £33,500 without wiping yourself out?**

- If yes, and you'll stay 5 years: buy. You'd save ~£100/month immediately and build equity.
- If no: rent for now, save hard, and revisit in 18 months. Renting is not losing — it's buying flexibility, and at your rent level the gap is small.
</Card>
