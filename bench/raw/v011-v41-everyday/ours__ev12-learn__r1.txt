# Photosynthesis: How Plants Make Food From Light

Photosynthesis is the process where plants, algae, and some bacteria turn **light, water, and carbon dioxide** into **sugar (food)** and **oxygen**. It's the reason we have oxygen to breathe and food to eat.

<Card title="The Big Picture" subtitle="One equation explains it all">

<Math tex="6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light} \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2" label="Overall reaction" />

In words: **6 carbon dioxide + 6 water + light energy → 1 glucose + 6 oxygen.**

Plants pull CO₂ from the air through pores in their leaves, soak up water through their roots, and capture sunlight with a green pigment called **chlorophyll**. They use that energy to build glucose, and release oxygen as a leftover.

</Card>

<Card title="Where It Happens" subtitle="Inside the chloroplast">

<KeyValue title="The factory floor">
| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Chloroplast | The whole organelle where photosynthesis happens |
| Thylakoid membrane | Captures light energy (the "power plant") |
| Stroma | Fluid that builds sugar from that energy (the "assembly line") |
| Chlorophyll | The green molecule that absorbs light |
| Stomata | Tiny pores that let CO₂ in and O₂ out |

</KeyValue>

</Card>

<Card title="Two Stages, Working Together">

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Stage 1: Light Reactions">
Happens in the **thylakoid membrane**. Light hits chlorophyll, which energizes electrons. Water is split apart, and this does two things:

- Releases **oxygen** (the O₂ we breathe)
- Produces **ATP** and **NADPH** — energy carriers, like charged batteries

This stage needs light directly. No light, no energy carriers.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Stage 2: Calvin Cycle">
Happens in the **stroma**. This stage doesn't need light itself — it uses the ATP and NADPH made in Stage 1 to build sugar.

- CO₂ is captured and attached to existing molecules
- Energy from ATP and NADPH turns it into **glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)**
- The cycle repeats, over and over

Think of Stage 1 as charging a battery and Stage 2 as using that battery to assemble a product.
</Tab>
</Tabs>

</Card>

<Card title="Follow the Energy" subtitle="From sunlight to sugar">

<Steps>
<Step title="Light hits the leaf" status="done" subtitle="Photons absorbed by chlorophyll">
Sunlight strikes the leaf. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light (that's why leaves look green — green is reflected, not used).
</Step>
<Step title="Water is split" status="done" subtitle="Oxygen released">
Water molecules break apart. The oxygen is released into the air as a gas.
</Step>
<Step title="Energy carriers charge up" status="done" subtitle="ATP and NADPH made">
The light energy is stored in ATP and NADPH — portable energy the cell can spend.
</Step>
<Step title="CO₂ is captured" status="current" subtitle="Carbon enters the cycle">
Carbon dioxide from the air is pulled into the stroma and fixed into organic molecules.
</Step>
<Step title="Glucose is built" status="todo" subtitle="Sugar assembled">
Using ATP and NADPH, the plant builds glucose — its food and building material.
</Step>
<Step title="Oxygen leaves" status="todo" subtitle="Released through stomata">
The leftover O₂ exits through the leaf pores into the atmosphere.
</Step>
</Steps>

</Card>

Almost every food chain on Earth starts here. The glucose plants make feeds them — and feeds everything that eats them, including you.
