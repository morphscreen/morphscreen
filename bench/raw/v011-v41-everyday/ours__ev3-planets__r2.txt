# The planets of our solar system

Eight planets orbit the Sun, plus a dwarf planet or two. Here's the whole set, ordered outward from the Sun, with the numbers that make each one distinct.

<Card title="The eight planets at a glance" subtitle="Distance is average from the Sun; a year is one orbit">
| Planet | Type | Distance (AU) | Year | Day | Diameter (km) | Moons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | Rocky | 0.39 | 88 days | 176 days | 4,879 | 0 |
| Venus | Rocky | 0.72 | 225 days | 243 days | 12,104 | 0 |
| Earth | Rocky | 1.00 | 365 days | 24 hours | 12,756 | 1 |
| Mars | Rocky | 1.52 | 687 days | 24.6 hours | 6,792 | 2 |
| Jupiter | Gas giant | 5.20 | 11.9 years | 9.9 hours | 142,984 | 95 |
| Saturn | Gas giant | 9.54 | 29.5 years | 10.7 hours | 120,536 | 146 |
| Uranus | Ice giant | 19.2 | 84 years | 17.2 hours | 51,118 | 28 |
| Neptune | Ice giant | 30.1 | 165 years | 16.1 hours | 49,528 | 16 |
</Card>

<Callout tone="info" title="One AU = 149.6 million km">
The distance from Earth to the Sun. Mercury sits at 0.39 AU; Neptune at 30 AU — the solar system is mostly empty space.
</Callout>

## The four rocky planets

<Columns>
<Card title="Mercury" subtitle="Smallest, fastest, most cratered">
Closest to the Sun, with almost no atmosphere. Days are scorching (430 °C) and nights freezing (−180 °C). It completes an orbit in just 88 days.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Mercury">More on Mercury</Button>
</Card>
<Card title="Venus" subtitle="Hottest planet">
A runaway greenhouse effect: 465 °C at the surface, hotter than Mercury. Its thick CO₂ clouds make it the brightest planet in our sky. It spins backwards, and slower than it orbits.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Venus">More on Venus</Button>
</Card>
</Columns>

<Columns>
<Card title="Earth" subtitle="The only one with life">
The only planet with liquid water on the surface, plate tectonics, and an oxygen-rich atmosphere. 71% of its surface is ocean.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Earth">More on Earth</Button>
</Card>
<Card title="Mars" subtitle="The rusty one">
Iron oxide dust gives it the red colour. Home to Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the solar system (22 km), and Valles Marineris, a canyon 4,000 km long. Two small moons: Phobos and Deimos.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Mars">More on Mars</Button>
</Card>
</Columns>

## The four giant planets

<Card title="Jupiter" subtitle="Biggest, with the Great Red Spot">
More massive than all the other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth that has raged for centuries. It has 95 known moons, including Ganymede — the largest moon in the solar system, bigger than Mercury.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Jupiter">More on Jupiter</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="Saturn" subtitle="The ringed one">
Its rings are made of ice and rock, mostly a few metres thick but 280,000 km wide. Saturn is less dense than water — it would float. Its moon Titan has lakes of liquid methane.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Saturn">More on Saturn</Button>
</Card>

<Columns>
<Card title="Uranus" subtitle="Tipped on its side">
It rotates at a 98° tilt, so it essentially rolls around the Sun. Methane in its atmosphere gives it a pale cyan colour. Coldest atmosphere of any planet: −224 °C.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Uranus">More on Uranus</Button>
</Card>
<Card title="Neptune" subtitle="Windiest">
Supersonic winds reach 2,100 km/h. It was found by mathematics before anyone saw it — predicted from wobbles in Uranus's orbit, then spotted in 1846. One year there lasts 165 Earth years.

<Button ask="Tell me more about Neptune">More on Neptune</Button>
</Card>
</Columns>

<Card title="Rocky vs. giant" subtitle="What actually separates them">
<Compare title="Planet types compared">
| Feature | Rocky (Mercury–Mars) | Giant (Jupiter–Neptune) |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Solid, you could stand on it | No surface, just deepening gas |
| Main makeup | Rock and metal | Hydrogen, helium, ices |
| Rings | no | yes |
| Moons | 0–2 | 16–146 |
| Size | Small | 4–11× Earth's diameter |
| Distance | Inner solar system | Outer solar system |
</Compare>
</Card>

<Callout tone="neutral" title="And Pluto?">
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. It orbits at 39.5 AU, has five moons, and shares its zone with other icy bodies in the Kuiper Belt — which is why it lost full planet status.
</Callout>
