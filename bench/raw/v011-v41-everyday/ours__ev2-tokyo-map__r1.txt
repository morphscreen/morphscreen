# 5 Days in Tokyo for Two

A compact first-timer loop: west side (Shinjuku/Shibuya), old town (Asakusa/Ueno), a day trip to Nikko or Hakone, then markets and a farewell evening in Ebisu. Best months: late March–April (sakura) or October–November (clear skies, mild air).

TeamLab Planets, the Ghibli Museum, and Shibuya Sky all sell out days ahead and are timed-entry. Reserve those first, then build the days around them.
</Callout>

## The five days

<Steps>
<Step title="Day 1 — Arrive, Shinjuku" status="current" subtitle="Land, drop bags, easy first evening">
Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner into town, check in, then walk the Shinjuku skyscraper district at dusk. Dinner near Omoide Yokocho's yakitori alleys, then the free Metropolitan Government Building observatory (open to 22:00) if you still have legs.

<Button ask="Plan Day 1 in Shinjuku hour by hour, with dinner options">Plan Day 1 in detail</Button>
</Step>
<Step title="Day 2 — Shibuya and Harajuku" status="todo" subtitle="Crossings, cat street, Meiji Jingu">
Morning at Meiji Jingu shrine and Yoyogi Park, then Takeshita-dori and Cat Street in Harajuku. Afternoon in Shibuya: Miyashita Park, the scramble crossing, Shibuya Sky at sunset (book the 30 minutes before dusk). Evening: izakaya in Nonbei Yokocho.

<Button ask="Plan Day 2 in Shibuya and Harajuku, with sunset timing for Shibuya Sky">Plan Day 2 in detail</Button>
</Step>
<Step title="Day 3 — Asakusa and Ueno" status="todo" subtitle="Old Tokyo, temple and museum">
Senso-ji early (before 8:30 it is nearly empty), Nakamise-dori snacks, then the Sumida River walk to Skytree for the view. Afternoon in Ueno Park: Tokyo National Museum or the science museum, plus Ameyoko market for cheap seafood and fruit. Evening: yakatabune or a simple sumiyaki dinner in Asakusa.

<Button ask="Plan Day 3 in Asakusa and Ueno, including which museum to pick">Plan Day 3 in detail</Button>
</Step>
<Step title="Day 4 — Day trip: Nikko or Hakone" status="todo" subtitle="Pick one, both are full days">
Nikko for shrines, cedar avenues and waterfalls (Tobu limited express, ~2h). Hakone for the lake, Owakudani's sulphur vents and an onsen with Mt Fuji views (Odakyu Romancecar, ~1h25). Buy the Nikko All Area Pass or the Hakone Free Pass before you go.

<Button ask="Compare a Nikko day trip against Hakone for two people, with costs and timings">Compare Nikko vs Hakone</Button>
</Step>
<Step title="Day 5 — Tsukiji, Ginza, Ebisu" status="todo" subtitle="Market breakfast, then farewell dinner">
Breakfast at Tsukiji Outer Market (tamagoyaki, uni, grilled scallops), walk or metro to Ginza for the flagship stores and the free Tokyo Ginza Shiseido gallery, then the Hamarikyu garden teahouse for matcha over the water. Finish with yakitori or a small kaiseki in Ebisu, or the free Ebisu Garden Place observation floor.

<Button ask="Plan Day 5 around Tsukiji, Ginza and Ebisu, with a farewell dinner shortlist">Plan Day 5 in detail</Button>
</Step>
</Steps>

## Practical notes

<Card title="Before you go">
<Checklist title="Trip checklist" name="tokyo-prep">
- [ ] Passports valid 6+ months past return
- [ ] Suica added to Apple/Google Wallet, or a physical card on arrival
- [ ] Booked: TeamLab, Shibuya Sky, Ghibli (if wanted), any day-trip pass
- [ ] Pocket wifi or eSIM activated
- [ ] ¥30,000–50,000 cash for small shops
- [ ] Reserved Day 4 train seats (Nikko or Hakone)
- [ ] Checked which JR Pass, if any, actually pays off
</Checklist>
</Card>

<Details title="Etiquette worth knowing">
- No eating or drinking while walking on most streets; eat at the stall.
- Trains are quiet — no calls, phone on silent.
- Queue exactly where the platform markings say.
- Tipping is not expected and can confuse.
- Tattoos may bar you from some onsen; look for a private (kashikiri) bath.
</Details>

<Details title="If it rains">
Swap outdoor days: Ueno museums, the Edo-Tokyo Museum area, Ginza's department store food halls, TeamLab (mostly indoors), and the Shinjuku or Ikebukuro underground concourses, which connect whole neighbourhoods without going outside.
</Details>
