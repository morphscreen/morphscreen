# Neapolitan Pizza at Home

The real thing comes down to four things: a **very wet, slow-risen dough**, a **blistering hot oven**, **San Marzano tomatoes**, and **restraint with toppings**. Everything else is detail.

<Callout tone="info" title="The one hard limit">
A home oven tops out around 250–290°C. True Neapolitan bakes at 430–485°C for 60–90 seconds. You can't fully match that — but a pizza steel on the top rack, preheated 45–60 minutes, gets you 80% of the way there.
</Callout>

## The dough

<Card title="Dough formula" subtitle="4 balls × ~250g, 62% hydration">
| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour (W300) | 600 g | 00 or strong bread flour |
| Water | 372 g | Cool, ~18°C |
| Fine sea salt | 18 g | 3% of flour |
| Fresh yeast | 1.2 g | Or 0.4 g instant dry |
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Dissolve the salt" status="done">
Stir salt into the water until fully dissolved. It should taste like the sea.
</Step>
<Step title="Mix the flour in" status="done">
Add flour and yeast. Mix until no dry patches remain — a rough, shaggy mass is fine.
</Step>
<Step title="Rest 20 minutes" status="done">
Cover and leave. The flour hydrates and the gluten starts organising itself.
</Step>
<Step title="Knead briefly" status="current" subtitle="2–3 minutes, no more">
Fold the dough over itself a few times until smooth. It should be sticky but hold together.
</Step>
<Step title="Bulk rise 2 hours at room temp">
Covered. It won't double much — that's correct for this yeast level.
</Step>
<Step title="Ball it">
Divide into 4 × 250 g. Shape each into a tight ball with a smooth top, seam underneath.
</Step>
<Step title="Cold ferment 24–48 hours">
In a sealed container in the fridge. This is where the flavour and digestibility come from.
</Step>
<Step title="Warm up 3–4 hours before baking">
Balls at room temperature, covered. They should be relaxed and puffy, not collapsed.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="warning" title="If your dough is slack">
High hydration plus weak flour equals a dough that tears. Either drop to 58% water or use a flour with at least 12.5% protein. Don't add flour at balling time — it makes the crust tough.
</Callout>

## The bake

<Card title="Setup">
<KeyValue title="Equipment">
| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Pizza steel | Conducts heat far better than a stone |
| Top rack position | Closest to the grill element |
| Grill/broiler on | For the last 60 seconds, to blister the top |
| Metal peel | Slides under a wet dough better than wood |
</KeyValue>
</Card>

<Steps>
<Step title="Preheat 45–60 minutes" status="done">
Steel on the top rack, oven at maximum. Use the full time — a steel needs it.
</Step>
<Step title="Stretch, don't roll" status="done">
Press from the centre out with your fingertips, leaving a 2 cm rim untouched. Never use a rolling pin.
</Step>
<Step title="Top it lightly" status="current">
~80 g crushed San Marzano, a pinch of salt, torn fior di latte or mozzarella (pat it dry first), basil, a thread of olive oil.
</Step>
<Step title="Bake 4–6 minutes">
Slide onto the steel. Rotate at the halfway point. Switch the grill on for the final minute.
</Step>
<Step title="Finish and eat immediately">
Add fresh basil and a drizzle of raw olive oil after it comes out. Wait 2 minutes, then cut.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout tone="danger" title="Wet mozzarella ruins everything">
Buffalo mozzarella releases a lot of water. Tear it and drain it on paper towels for 30 minutes, or use a low-moisture fior di latte. A soggy centre is the most common home failure.
</Callout>

## What to change if it goes wrong

<Compare title="Troubleshooting">
| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
| Pale, dry crust | Oven too cool | Preheat longer; steel, not stone |
| Soggy middle | Too much sauce or wet cheese | Drain the mozzarella; less sauce |
| Dough springs back when stretched | Under-rested | Wait another 30 minutes |
| Dough tears | Over-proofed or weak flour | Shorten the cold ferment; stronger flour |
| Burnt base, raw top | Steel too low | Move to the top rack, use the grill |
| Flat, biscuity crust | Rolled, or no rim left | Fingertips only; keep the rim |
</Compare>

<Callout tone="success" title="The 62 / 24 sweet spot">
62% hydration with a 24-hour cold ferment gives you dough that's easy to stretch, deeply flavoured, and puffs properly in a home oven. Move from there once you've baked it a few times.
</Callout>

## Shopping list

<Checklist title="Before you bake" name="pizza-prep">
- [ ] Bread flour, 12.5%+ protein
- [ ] Fresh or instant yeast
- [ ] San Marzano tomatoes (whole, in tin)
- [ ] Fior di latte or low-moisture mozzarella
- [ ] Fresh basil
- [ ] Good olive oil
- [ ] Pizza steel or stone
- [ ] Metal peel
- [ ] Semolina or fine flour for dusting
</Checklist>
