# Rent or buy, on £60k with £1,800 rent

The honest answer: **on £60k alone, buying a typical flat is a stretch but not impossible — and renting is not automatically "throwing money away."** The decision turns on four numbers you can actually pin down: your deposit, the price of the flat you'd buy, how long you'd stay, and what your savings earn meanwhile.

<Callout tone="info" title="The rule of thumb that matters most">
Buying wins when you stay long enough to clear the upfront costs (stamp duty, legal fees, survey, mortgage fees) — usually **5+ years**. Below that, renting and investing the difference often comes out ahead.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="The bit people forget">
A £1,580 mortgage is *not* cheaper than £1,800 rent. Add service charge (£150–£250/month on a flat), buildings insurance, and maintenance, and the true monthly cost lands near £1,950–£2,050. You also pay the deposit, stamp duty and fees upfront.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="success" title="The short version">
- **If you'll stay 5+ years and can raise ~£33k:** buying a flat up to ~£300k is genuinely reachable on £60k.
- **If you might move, change jobs, or don't have the deposit yet:** keep renting, and invest the difference — you're not losing money, you're buying flexibility.
- **Either way:** don't let the mortgage payment alone decide it. The all-in monthly cost of owning is the number that matters.
</Callout>
