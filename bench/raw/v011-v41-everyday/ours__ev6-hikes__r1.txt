# Best day hikes near Vancouver

Vancouver is spoiled: real alpine terrain within an hour of downtown. Here are the hikes worth your boots, from gentle to genuinely hard.

<Callout tone="info" title="Before you go">
North Shore trails need a **parking pass** (free, book online) on weekends and holidays from May–September. Always carry the **10 essentials** — weather turns fast above 800 m.
</Callout>

<Card title="1. Lighthouse Park — the easy win">
A 6 km loop through old-growth Douglas fir to a rocky point with the Point Atkinson lighthouse and views across to Bowen Island. Flat-ish, family-friendly, and gorgeous in the rain.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Distance | 6 km loop |
| Time | 1.5–2 hours |
| Elevation gain | ~120 m |
| Best for | Kids, rainy days, sunset |
| Getting there | 25 min drive from downtown, West Vancouver |
</KeyValue>

<Card title="2. Quarry Rock — the classic North Shore stroll">
A 4 km out-and-back from Deep Cove up to a granite ledge over Indian Arm. Busy for good reason: the payoff view is huge for the effort. Grab a doughnut in Deep Cove after.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Distance | 4 km return |
| Time | 1.5 hours |
| Elevation gain | ~200 m |
| Best for | First hike, quick morning |
| Getting there | Deep Cove, North Vancouver |
</KeyValue>

<Button ask="Tell me more about Quarry Rock">More on Quarry Rock</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="3. Tunnel Bluffs — the best view-per-effort ratio">
Roughly 11 km return with about 500 m of gain, ending on a bluff above Howe Sound with the Sea-to-Sky highway curling below. Steep in places, roots and mud, but the panorama is one of the best near the city.

<KeyValue title="At a glance">
| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Distance | ~11 km return |
| Time | 3–4 hours |
| Elevation gain | ~500 m |
| Best for | A half-day with a big view |
| Getting there | Sea-to-Sky, near Lions Bay |
</KeyValue>

<Button ask="Tell me more about Tunnel Bluffs">More on Tunnel Bluffs</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="4. Joffre Lakes — turquoise water, three lakes">
About 2.5 hours up the Sea-to-Sky, but worth the drive: three glacier-fed lakes, each more absurdly blue than the last. 11 km return, ~400 m gain. **Day-use passes are required** and go fast.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Permits required">Book a free day-use pass in advance, especially for weekends and summer.</Callout>

<Button ask="Tell me more about Joffre Lakes">More on Joffre Lakes</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="5. St. Mark's Summit — the Howe Sound panorama">
A steady climb from Cypress Bowl to a bald summit with a 180° view of Howe Sound and the islands. 11 km return, ~600 m gain. Snow lingers into June.

<Button ask="Tell me more about St. Mark's Summit">More on St. Mark's Summit</Button>
</Card>

<Card title="6. Mount Brunswick — for strong hikers only">
The hardest on this list: 14 km return, ~1,600 m of gain, some scrambling, and a full day. The reward is a summit view over Howe Sound and the Tantalus Range. Not a casual outing.

<Callout tone="danger" title="Serious hike">Bring navigation, water, and start early. Turn around if weather closes in.</Callout>
