# 5 days in Tokyo for two

A balanced first-timer's route: old Tokyo in the east, the city's neon heart, a day trip west to the mountains, and a final sweep through markets and gardens. Built for two people, walking-heavy but with trains doing the heavy lifting.

<Callout tone="info" title="Two people, one card each">
Load a Suica or Pasmo on each phone (or a physical card each) — you can't tap two people through a gate on one card. Airport trains and most JR lines take it.
</Callout>

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Day 1 — East Tokyo">
<Card title="Asakusa, Ueno and the old city" subtitle="Arrival day, gentle pace">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Sensō-ji before the crowds" status="todo">
Arrive by 8am. Enter through Kaminarimon, walk Nakamise-dōri while the shutters are still going up, then the main hall. Omikuji fortune: 100 yen.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Sumida River and Skytree" status="todo">
Walk the riverside promenade to Tokyo Skytree. Skip the tower if skies are hazy; the Solamachi mall below is fine for lunch.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Ueno Park museums" status="todo">
Tokyo National Museum for swords and ceramics, or the Science Museum if it's raining. Ameyoko market for snacks on the way out.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: yakitori under the tracks" status="todo">
Ueno or Yurakucho. Order a set, share everything, expect 4,000–6,000 yen for two with drinks.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>

<Tab title="Day 2 — Neon Tokyo">
<Card title="Shibuya, Harajuku and Shinjuku" subtitle="The postcard day">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: Meiji Jingū forest" status="todo">
Start at Harajuku station, walk the gravel path through the shrine grounds. Quiet, free, and cool even in summer.
</Step>
<Step title="Midday: Takeshita-dōri and Omotesandō" status="todo">
Crepes and people-watching on Takeshita, then the tree-lined luxury strip of Omotesandō for architecture.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Shibuya Crossing and Miyashita Park" status="todo">
Cross at street level, then view it from above — Shibuya Sky needs a timed ticket booked ahead.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Omoide Yokochō and Golden Gai" status="todo">
Tiny alleys of six-seat bars in Shinjuku. Pick a place with an English menu if you'd rather not guess.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Tab title="Day 4 — Markets and gardens">
<Card title="Tsukiji, Ginza and the Imperial Palace" subtitle="Food first, then calm">
<Steps>
<Step title="Early: Tsukiji Outer Market" status="todo">
Go hungry. Tamagoyaki skewers, uni, grilled scallops, and a bowl of kaisendon. Budget 3,000–5,000 yen for two.
</Step>
<Step title="Late morning: Ginza" status="todo">
Itchōme and Chūō-dōri. On weekends the main street closes to cars — good for walking.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: Imperial Palace East Gardens" status="todo">
Free, no booking, closed Mondays and Fridays. Then walk to Tokyo Station for the red-brick facade.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Akihabara or a quiet izakaya" status="todo">
Akihabara if you want electronics and arcades; otherwise a neighbourhood izakaya near your hotel.
</Step>
</Steps>
</Card>
<Button ask="Plan day 4 around Tsukiji market and the Imperial Palace gardens">More on day 4</Button>

<Tab title="Day 5 — Last day">
<Card title="Flexible finish" subtitle="Shopping, then the airport">
<Steps>
<Step title="Morning: whatever you missed" status="todo">
Shinjuku Gyoen garden, teamLab if you booked ahead, or last-minute shopping in Shinjuku or Shibuya.
</Step>
<Step title="Afternoon: pack and ship" status="todo">
Luggage forwarding (takkyubin) to the airport takes a day, so send bags the night before if you can.
</Step>
<Step title="Evening: Narita or Haneda" status="todo">
Narita is ~60 min by Narita Express; Haneda ~30 min by monorail. Leave 3 hours before an international flight.
</Step>
</Steps>
