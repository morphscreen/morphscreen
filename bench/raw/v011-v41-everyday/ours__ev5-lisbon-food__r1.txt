# What to eat in Lisbon, and where

Lisbon eating splits into a few clear tracks: grilled fish and seafood, the cheap everyday classics, and the pastry counter. Below is what to order, where to sit, and a map you can walk.

<Callout tone="info" title="How to eat here">Lunch is the big meal, usually 12:30–15:00. Dinner starts late — 20:00 is early, 21:30 is normal. Many tascas close one day a week and shut by 22:00. Cash still helps in the old-school places.</Callout>

<Card title="The five things to order first" subtitle="If you only eat five meals in Lisbon">
| Dish | What it is | Where to have it |
|---|---|---|
| Sardinhas assadas | Grilled sardines on bread, salt and charcoal | A tasca in Alfama or Graça, June–September |
| Bacalhau à Brás | Shredded salt cod with egg, onion, matchstick potato | Any mid-range tasca, all year |
| Bifana | Pork cutlet in a crusty roll, mustard, sometimes a splash of broth | A cervejaria or a stand at a market |
| Pastel de nata | Custard tart, blistered top, cinnamon | Anywhere, but eat it warm |
| Ameijoas à Bulhão Pato | Clams in garlic, coriander and olive oil | A marisqueira, with white wine |
</Card>

<Columns>
<Card title="Seafood & grilled fish" subtitle="The main event">
- **Cervejaria Ramiro** — the famous one. Go early or put your name down and wait. Order the garlic prawns, then the steak sandwich to finish.
- **A Marisqueira do Lis** — quieter, excellent clams and crab.
- **Restaurante O Trigueirinho** — tiny, family-run, grilled fish by the kilo.
</Card>
<Card title="Cheap and everyday" subtitle="Where locals actually eat lunch">
- **A sandwich and a beer standing up** at a cervejaria counter — the cheapest good meal in the city.
- **Tascas in Graça and Campo de Ourique** — a *prato do dia* with soup, bread and wine for a fixed price.
- **Time Out Market** — convenient, touristy, but a good one-stop sampler if you're short on time.
</Card>
</Columns>

<Details title="Ordering words that get you further">
- **Prato do dia** — dish of the day, usually the cheapest and best.
- **Meia dose / dose** — half portion / full portion. Meia dose is often plenty for one.
- **Couvert** — the bread, olives and butter brought unasked. It is charged. You may send it back.
- **Bica** — an espresso. **Galão** — espresso with lots of milk.
- **À portuguesa** — with garlic, olive oil and coriander.
</Details>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Two traps">Couvert is not free. And in the most touristy streets of Baixa and Alfama, menus in five languages with photos usually mean mediocre food at high prices — walk two streets uphill and it improves.</Callout>
