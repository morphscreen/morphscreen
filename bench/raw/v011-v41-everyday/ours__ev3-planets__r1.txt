# The Solar System's Planets

Eight planets orbit the Sun, and they split cleanly into two families: the **rocky inner four** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the **giant outer four** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Here's the whole set at a glance.

<Tab title="How they formed">
<Steps>
<Step title="A cloud of gas and dust" status="done">A nebula collapses under gravity and starts to spin.</Step>
<Step title="The Sun ignites" status="done">The center gets hot enough for fusion; the rest flattens into a disk.</Step>
<Step title="Rock condenses close in" status="done">Near the Sun only metal and rock can stay solid, so the inner planets are small and dense.</Step>
<Step title="Ice and gas survive far out" status="done">Beyond the frost line, ices and gas build the giant planets.</Step>
<Step title="Leftovers remain" status="current">The asteroid belt, Kuiper belt, and comets are material that never formed a planet.</Step>
</Steps>
</Tab>
</Tabs>

<Callout tone="info" title="Pluto isn't on the list">
Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006: it shares its orbital zone with many other icy bodies, which is the test a planet must pass. Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake are dwarf planets too.
</Callout>
